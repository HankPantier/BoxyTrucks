Our first reader rig is a red 2017 4Runner TRD Off-Road from Columbia, South Carolina. Its owner, Sarah Chambers, calls it Clifford.

Sarah found it on Facebook Marketplace in 2025. "It was already lifted with a Prinsu roof rack and Gobi ladder," she told us. "I purchased it with cash and immediately drove it to Gulches ORV Park in Waterloo, SC to test it out."

## What she's done to it
The first owner did the rack and a lift. Since then Sarah has redone the suspension with a Dobinsons 3-inch lift and Dobinsons struts and shocks, put it on 35-inch tires and Fuel wheels, and replaced the inner and outer tie rods and the sway bar end links. Up front there's a steel bumper with an XRC Gen 3 winch, and the lighting is Baja Designs.

That's a smart order to do it in. On a [[4runner/5th-gen|5th gen 4Runner]], once you go past about 2.5 to 3 inches of lift, the front end needs attention. Fresh tie rods and end links are exactly the kind of parts that take the abuse first.

## Where it's been
"I've driven it to and from Kentucky, North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia, Florida, Tennessee, and Georgia," Sarah says. "It has seen all kinds of terrain, including off-road parks, national parks trailing, and more." Her favorite trip so far is Red River Gorge in Kentucky.

## Why we like it
Clifford is the 4Runner most people should build. It started as a TRD Off-Road, so it already had the factory rear locker. It's on a proven lift and sensible 35s, and it carries the recovery gear and lights that matter. Then it actually gets driven, in snow, fog, mud and seven states' worth of back roads. Sarah isn't building a show truck. She's using the truck.
