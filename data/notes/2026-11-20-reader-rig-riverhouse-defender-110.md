This one came in through Instagram. [Riverhouse Land Rover](https://www.instagram.com/riverhouselandrover/), a Land Rover dealer in Almen in the Netherlands, sent us a 1997 Defender 110 Station Wagon in Arles Blue with a limestone roof.

Two things up front. Riverhouse is a dealer, and this truck is for sale. Nobody paid us to run it. We're running it because it's a good example of how to build a Tdi 110, and because that blue is very hard to argue with.

## Where it came from
Riverhouse brought the truck up from Córdoba, in the south of Spain. That matters more than it sounds. A Defender's chassis, bulkhead and rear crossmember decide what it's worth, and a truck that has spent its life somewhere warm and dry starts with a big advantage over one from a salted northern road.

They describe it as "a matching-number truck with the bulletproof 300 Tdi engine." The engine has been fully serviced, with a new timing belt and a new intercooler, and the fuel pump has been tuned. Their listing adds a new water pump and puts the mileage at 193,000 km, about 120,000 miles.

## What they've done to it
The unglamorous work is the best part. The axle ends were pulled and fully rebuilt with new hubs, bearings, pins, seals, gaskets, rotors and calipers, and it has new shocks and springs. Leaking swivels and tired wheel bearings are what you inherit with most old Defenders, so starting from zero there is worth real money.

Outside it's wearing NAS 110 wheels with a deeper offset, painted to match the roof, a NAS step-up rear bumper and a Mantec swing-away spare wheel carrier. The body was repainted inside and out, according to the listing.

## Inside
There's a new nine-seat interior: two up front, three in the middle and four on jump seats in the back. The front seats are heated.

Then come the parts that will divide the room: a new air conditioning kit and a 10.1-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay. Purists will wince. Anyone who has done a long motorway day in a Tdi 110 in August will not.

## Why we like it
The order is right. This build spent its money on the engine service, the axles, the brakes and the suspension first, and on the screen last. Plenty of restored Defenders do it the other way round.

It also kept the engine it left Solihull with. For a 25-year import, the [[land-rover/defender-90-110|300Tdi]] is the simple, mechanical favourite: no engine computer, and nothing a decent diesel shop can't understand.

## If you're shopping
- **This truck.** It's a 1997, so it's old enough to import to the US under the 25-year rule. Riverhouse lists it at €69,900 with ocean freight included as we publish. Ask them to confirm what's in the price, and read our [importing guide](/importing/) for the paperwork and tariffs that come on top.
- **Any 300Tdi.** A "new timing belt" is good news, but ask which kit. Land Rover revised the tensioner, idler and bottom pulley because early belts wore out. Our [timing belt guide](/repairs/land-rover/defender-90-110/300tdi-timing-belt-with-revised-kit/) shows what to look for.
- **Any Defender.** Start with the chassis, the bulkhead and the rear crossmember, even on a truck from Spain. The [inspection checklist](/models/land-rover/defender-90-110/problems/) has the full list.
- **Which year?** [Every Defender year compared](/models/land-rover/defender-90-110/years/), and why the Tdi trucks are the ones importers chase.

We haven't seen or driven this truck. Everything here comes from Riverhouse and their listing, so do your own inspection, or pay someone to do it, before you send money across an ocean.
