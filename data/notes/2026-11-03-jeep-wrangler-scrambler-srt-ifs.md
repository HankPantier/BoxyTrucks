Every Wrangler ever built, back to the CJs it grew out of, has had a solid axle up front. As of this week's SEMA show in Las Vegas, there's one that doesn't.

Jeep and SRT rolled out the **Wrangler Scrambler SRT Concept**: a V8 (no output figures yet) under a vented hood, a pickup bed behind the cab, and the part that has the forums on fire, a long-travel **independent front suspension** developed with off-road racer Casey Currie. Out back it uses a rear axle inspired by trophy trucks. Jeep says the front end is there for steering precision, wheel control and stability at speed over rough ground. It's related to the setup in the Striker concept that Currie drove to a class win at this year's NORRA 500.

The name is borrowed from the [[jeep/cj-8|CJ-8 Scrambler]], the long-wheelbase CJ pickup Jeep sold from 1981 to 1986. There's no production date and no price. Hagerty thinks it's probably headed for showrooms eventually. Jeep hasn't said so.

## Why this is a big deal

The solid front axle *is* the Wrangler. It's why one will flex over rocks that leave other trucks on three wheels. It's why a lift is a weekend job and not a re-engineering project. And it's why there's an aftermarket the size of a small country. Even when the [[bronco/sixth-gen|new Bronco]] showed up in 2021 with IFS and beat the [[jeep/jl|JL]] on road manners, Jeep didn't budge.

So Jeep putting IFS on anything wearing a Wrangler badge is the moment the company admits the Bronco had a point.

## Why I'm not worried

Look at what this truck is for. A V8, long-travel IFS, a trophy-truck rear end and Fox shocks: that's a desert truck. It's built for whoops at 60 mph, the job a Bronco Raptor or a Ram TRX does, not for crawling a ledge at 2 mph. Jeep isn't swapping the Rubicon's front Dana for control arms. It's adding a second kind of Wrangler for people who want to go fast on dirt.

I've lived with both. I had two TJ Unlimiteds on solid axles, and I've had four 4Runners on IFS, including the 2024 in my driveway now. They're different tools. IFS is the one you want for the drive *to* the trail and for anything fast and rough. A solid axle is the one you want when the trail turns into a rock garden and you're about to lift the truck and put 37s on it.

## What it means if you're shopping

- **Rocks, cheap lifts and big tires:** nothing changes. A [[jeep/jl|JL]] or [[jeep/jl-unlimited|JL Unlimited]] Rubicon is still the answer, and so is a good [[jeep/jk-unlimited|JK Unlimited]] if your budget is smaller. Check our [Jeep best years](/models/jeep/best-years/) page before you buy.
- **Fast dirt, long highway miles and a better ride:** that's a [[bronco/sixth-gen|Bronco]] today, and maybe a Scrambler SRT one day. Our [Wrangler vs Bronco](/compare/wrangler-jl-vs-bronco/) comparison lays out the trade-offs.
- **If you want the original Scrambler:** a [[jeep/cj-8|CJ-8]] is a genuine classic now. Clean ones aren't cheap, and they won't be getting cheaper after this week.

My bet: if this goes into production, it'll be the most interesting Wrangler in years, and a solid-axle Rubicon will still sit right next to it in the showroom. That's the right answer.
