Takata is back. On November 19, Toyota [recalled 314,029 Toyota and Lexus vehicles](https://www.cars.com/articles/314000-plus-toyota-lexus-vehicles-recalled-for-exploding-takata-airbags-531475/) in the US for Takata front passenger airbag inflators (NHTSA campaign 26V748). Two of the trucks on the list are ours: 35,949 **2016–2017 4Runners** and 8,931 **2017 Lexus GX 460s**, according to [autoevolution](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/takata-psan-airbag-inflator-saga-returns-with-314000-vehicle-toyota-and-lexus-recall-in-the-us-278674.html). The rest are older Corollas, Tundras, Sequoias and a couple of Lexus cars.

The problem is the old Takata one. After years of high humidity, heat and temperature swings, the propellant can degrade. If it burns too hard, the inflator can rupture and throw metal fragments into the cabin. Toyota says these inflators are nearing the end of their estimated safe service life, and that it hasn't had any reports of ruptures from this group. Dealers will replace the passenger inflator or the whole airbag assembly for free. Owner letters go out between December 28, 2026 and January 11, 2027. There's no do-not-drive warning.

## Why it matters
Here's the bit that catches people out. The recall covers inflators fitted at the factory **and** inflators fitted as replacements under earlier Takata recalls. Earlier campaigns already covered 4Runners up to 2016, so plenty of these trucks have had the passenger airbag done once. They can be recalled again for the same seat. As [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.sg/already-had-your-toyota-airbag-replaced-314029-vehicles-now-face-another-recall-96432) puts it, an earlier repair doesn't prove the inflator you have now is outside this recall.

Not on the list: the Land Cruiser, the Lexus LX and the FJ Cruiser.

## My take
I owned a 2016 4Runner. The fifth gen is about as tough as a modern SUV gets, and the GX 460 is the same truck with a V8 and nicer seats. This doesn't change that.

What it changes is how you read a service history. A receipt that says "Takata recall done" used to close the question. On a 2016–17 4Runner or a 2017 GX, it doesn't anymore. The paper trail on these trucks is only as good as the last time someone ran the VIN.

The fix is free and it's a dealer visit, not a rebuild. Book it, and until then, keep kids and anyone small out of the front passenger seat.

## What it means if you're shopping
- **Looking at a 2016–17 4Runner or a 2017 GX 460?** Run the VIN at NHTSA and with a Toyota or Lexus dealer, even if the seller shows you an old Takata receipt. NHTSA's lookup can lag a new recall, so ask the dealer too. Our [[4runner/5th-gen|5th-gen 4Runner guide]] and [[lexus/gx460|GX 460 guide]] list the other recalls to check.
- **Don't walk away over it.** An open recall is a free repair, not a reason to pass on a good truck. Ask the seller to get it done before you buy, or book it yourself the week you take it home.
