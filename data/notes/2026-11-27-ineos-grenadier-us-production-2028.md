On November 24, Ineos [announced](https://www.motorbiscuit.com/ineos-is-bringing-grenadier-production-to-the-us-its-biggest-market/) that it will build the Grenadier and the Quartermaster pickup in the United States, starting in 2028. It has a shortlist of sites and says it will pick one "in the next few months." It hasn't said where, how much it will spend, or whether a US-built truck will differ from the French one.

The reason is simple: Americans buy about 70% of all Grenadiers, [per CarBuzz](https://carbuzz.com/ineos-grenadier-official-american-factory-announcement/). Every one of them comes from the former Smart plant in Hambach, France, where production started in late 2022. That makes them imports. CarBuzz says tariffs on EU-built vehicles pushed Ineos to raise prices by $3,800 on the Grenadier and $9,000 on the Quartermaster, and that prices have come down slightly since preliminary trade deals. The Quartermaster also pays the 25% "Chicken Tax" on imported pickups. A US plant would avoid both.

There's a second piece of news. Ineos is in exclusive talks to sell the Hambach plant to BYD. If the deal goes through, Ineos says both trucks will keep being built in France "for the medium term." So the US plant is an addition, not a replacement.

## Why it matters
The Grenadier is a small company's truck. Owners have already lived through recalls and a parts shortage that stopped the line from September 2024 to January 2025, when seat supplier Recaro Automotive went insolvent. "Will Ineos still be here in ten years?" is a fair question to ask before spending $70,000-plus on one. A factory in its biggest market is about the strongest "yes" a carmaker can give.

## My take
This is good news for anyone who owns a Grenadier or wants one. But it's 2028 at the earliest, and there's no site and no budget yet. New plants slip. I'd treat 2028 as a hope, not a date.

And don't expect a cheaper truck. A US plant takes away tariff costs, but there's no promise those savings go to buyers. The Quartermaster has the most to gain, because the Chicken Tax is the biggest cost it carries.
