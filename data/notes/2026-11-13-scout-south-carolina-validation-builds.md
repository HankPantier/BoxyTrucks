Scout Motors is building actual trucks. On November 12, [Scout said](https://www.motor1.com/news/815073/scout-builds-terra-traveler-prototypes-factory/) it has started building validation vehicles of the Traveler SUV and the Terra pickup at its new plant in Blythewood, South Carolina. Motor1 and Autonext report more than 750 robots joining about 160 parts per body, with around 5,000 weld points each. The body shop, paint shop and assembly hall are running, and Scout says most of the equipment is in and being commissioned.

Don't get too excited about your driveway yet. These are test trucks, and none of them will be sold. Validation builds run into 2027, then pre-production. Scout says full production starts in 2027, and deliveries are still slated for 2028. There's no new pricing; Scout's line is still "under $60,000" to start.

The reservation numbers are the interesting part. [Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/news/scouts-first-factory-built-vehicles-arrive-with-212000-reservations), citing CNBC, puts them at about 212,000, and roughly 87% are for the Harvester extended-range versions, which carry a gas engine that only charges the battery. CEO Scott Keogh says about 75% are for the Traveler. That's why the Harvester Traveler is expected to be built first, then the Terra, and only then the pure EVs.

## Why it matters
For three years Scout has been renderings, a reveal outside Nashville, and a $100 refundable deposit. Bodies coming off a weld line is the first point where it stops being a promise. Keogh put it well: "What we are building in Blythewood is no longer a promise on paper."

## My take
I love that it's being built in South Carolina. The original Scout was an American truck from an American farm-equipment company, built in Fort Wayne, Indiana, until the last Scout II rolled out in October 1980. Volkswagen could have built this anywhere. It put a $2 billion, 4,000-job plant in South Carolina instead, and that's the right home for a name like this.

The 87% number tells you something too. Buyers want a boxy, body-on-frame 4x4 with lockers at both ends, and they want a gas tank behind it for the long trip. Scout listened and changed its launch order. That's a good sign.

But a weld line isn't a delivery date. Until Scout publishes prices and a build schedule, all anyone has is a place in line.
