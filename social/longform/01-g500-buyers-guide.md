## 0:30 What it is
*Spec card: 5.0 V8 M113, ladder frame, three locking diffs, 1998–2008.*

The G 500 is a military truck that was asked to wear a suit and never took its boots off. Under the leather and wood it's the same ladder frame and the same three locking differentials that armies bought by the thousand.

It launched in 1998 with the five-litre M113 V8, the first regular-production V8 G-Class. Mercedes didn't sell it in the US until the 2002 model year, so the US trucks run from 2002 to 2008. In 2009 the G 550 replaced it.

## 1:15 Years to avoid, and the sweet spot
*Timeline graphic: 2002–04 in red, 2005–08 in green.*

If you remember one thing, make it this. The 2002 to 2004 trucks are the ones with the electrical gremlins: seat motors and switches, window regulators, door-lock actuators and the transfer-case actuator. They can all be fixed, but it adds up.

The sweet spot is 2005 to 2008. Those early electrical faults got better every year and were mostly gone by 2005 and '06.

Then you get to pick. The 2005 and 2006 trucks keep the simpler five-speed automatic. The 2007 and 2008 trucks get the seven-speed, HID headlights, a reversing camera and tire-pressure monitoring. Mine's a 2007.

## 2:15 The five things that break
*One card per item, with the repair cost from the guide.*

**One: the oil filter housing gasket.** On my 2007 this has been the biggest issue. Oil collects in the V at the front of the engine and runs down the block. The parts are cheap and it's a simple fix; it just takes time. Most of the bill is labor.

**Two: valve cover gaskets and breather hoses.** You'll see oil on the coil packs and the top of the exhaust manifolds. One owner did it for about [two hundred dollars] in parts against shop quotes near [two thousand]. It's a weekend morning if you're handy.

**Three: the harmonic balancer.** On the M113, listen at idle. A vibration, or a chirp that changes with revs, points at the balancer. Figure about [two-fifty to four-fifty] in labor.

**Four: front axle seals.** Look behind the front wheels. Gear oil on the inside of the wheel or on the brake caliper means the seals are leaking, and possibly the brakes are contaminated. That's [six hundred to fifteen hundred dollars].

**Five: highway wander.** Drive it at fifty-five to seventy. If it wanders or the wheel shakes, it's usually a tired steering damper and worn linkage. [Three to eight hundred dollars], and it's one of the cheapest fixes on the truck.

## 4:00 The lockers
*Close-up of the three locker switches; lock indicators.*

Three locking differentials are the whole point of a G. Test all three on a loose surface and watch the lights.

Here's the catch. Lockers that were "never used" by the last owner often don't work. On most of these trucks the fault is in the vacuum system, not the axles. I'm still working through the lockers on mine, and the most likely answer is that the system needs bleeding or the lines need replacing.

So budget for a vacuum diagnosis. Then use them, even once every few months. It keeps them working.

## 4:45 Two more things to check
*Quick cuts: VIN plate, headliner, tailgate edge.*

First, is it really an AMG? Plenty of standard G's have been dressed up with AMG badges. Check the VIN and the data card before you pay an AMG price.

Second, look up. A sagging headliner usually means blocked sunroof drains, and water can reach the fuse box and the climate actuators. While you're at it, check for rust where nobody cleans: the bottom of the tailgate, the door bottoms, the sills, the rear frame tubes and the inner wheel arches.

## 5:30 Tires
*Fitment table; side-by-side stock vs 33s.*

From the factory, US G 500s ran [265/60 R18s]. Owners fit a [275/70 R18], which is about a 33-inch tire, on an unlifted truck on the factory wheels. A [285/65] can rub slightly at full lock, and wheel offset matters as much as tire size.

When you go bigger, have the tire size re-coded so the speedometer and the stability control agree. Every size, with lift heights and the speedometer math, is on our G 500 tires page.

## 6:15 What to pay
*Price bars from the guide.*

Prices on Bring a Trailer have barely moved since 2018. That makes the 2002 to 2008 G 500 the cheapest way into a V8 G-Wagen, so condition and records matter far more than the year.

From fifty-five sales this year: project and high-mileage trucks around [eighteen to twenty-five thousand], good drivers [twenty-five to forty-five thousand], and excellent or low-mile trucks [fifty to eighty thousand and up].

My advice: spend more on the truck with the records, and keep a couple of thousand back for the gaskets, the seals and the lockers.
