Field Notes/PRICE CHANGE
Boxy Trucks Field Notes · November 26, 2026

The 2027 4Runner is the same truck with heated seats and a bigger sticker. Shop the leftovers.

Toyota announced the 2027 4Runner on November 25. The base SR5 now starts at $43,490 before destination, or $45,085 with it, and the TRD Pro passes $70,000. What you get for the money is heated front seats on the SR5, power front seats on three more grades, a new paint color and puddle lamps. Mechanically, nothing changes.

By Hank →

Toyota announced the 2027 4Runner on November 25. The headline number is the price. The SR5 starts at $43,490 before destination. With the $1,595 destination charge it’s $45,085, which CarBuzz says is the first time a 4Runner has started above $45,000. CarBuzz puts the SR5’s increase at $1,220 over the 2026.

Here’s what that money buys. Every grade now gets heated front seats and 8-way power seats for the driver and passenger. Per CarBuzz, the heated seats are new on the SR5, and the power seats are new on the SR5, TRD Sport and TRD Off-Road. The TRD Pro gets a new color called Hot Honey (a $695 option, per CarBuzz) and red front recovery hooks. The Platinum gets puddle lamps with the 4Runner logo. “Inked” replaces Black.

That’s the list. The engines are the same: the 2.4-liter turbo four with 278 hp, and the i-FORCE MAX hybrid with 326 hp and 465 lb-ft. So is the TNGA-F frame, and so is the 6,000 lb tow rating. CarBuzz’s trim table shows the grades that gained no equipment going up about $200 or less, and the hybrid TRD Pro now listing at $70,465 with destination.

Why it matters

The 4Runner used to be the sensible one, the Toyota you bought because the Land Cruiser was too much money. A $70,000 TRD Pro isn’t sensible anymore. At that price you’re shopping alongside the Land Cruiser 250 and the Lexus GX 550, which ride on the same platform.

My take

Heated seats on an SR5 are welcome. Nobody should have to pay extra for a warm seat in a truck built for snow and trailheads. But this is a model-year bump, not a new truck. If you were happy with a 2026 last month, a 2026 is still the same 4Runner, minus heated seats on the SR5 and power seats on a few grades.

I own a 2024 4Runner, the last year of the fifth gen, and I’ve owned three before it (2002, 2007 and 2016). Toyota knows the 4Runner sells whatever it costs. That’s exactly why you should make the dealer work for it.

What it means if you’re shopping

  • Want a new sixth gen? Ask about leftover 2026s before you sign for a 2027. Unless you need the seat heaters on an SR5, it’s the same truck. Our 6th-gen 4Runner guide covers the trims and what to check.
  • Eyeing a TRD Pro or Trailhunter? At $70,000, cross-shop the Land Cruiser 250 first. Our Land Cruiser 250 vs 4Runner comparison shows where the extra money goes.
  • Just want a 4Runner? A used fifth gen is still the value buy. Check our 4Runner best years page before you pick a year.
