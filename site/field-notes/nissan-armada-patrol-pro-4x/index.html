Field Notes/NEW MODEL YEAR
Boxy Trucks Field Notes · November 9, 2026

The Patrol name is back in America. It's a sticker, but the truck under it is real.

For 2027 the Armada PRO-4X becomes the Armada Patrol PRO-4X, the first Patrol badge sold in the US since 1969. The hardware hasn't changed, and that's the most useful thing to know if you're shopping.

By Hank →

Nissan just put the Patrol name back on a truck you can buy in America. For 2027, the Armada PRO-4X becomes the Armada Patrol PRO-4X. It’s the first Patrol badge sold here since 1969, when the 60 Series left the US market. Nissan says the Patrol was the first Nissan-badged vehicle sold in America, starting in 1962, and the name turns 75 this year.

Read the fine print, though. Armada stays the model name, and “Patrol” is the trim. The new badges sit on the center console and the lower door frames. The hardware carries over from the 2025–26 PRO-4X: the 425 hp twin-turbo 3.5-liter V6 and nine-speed automatic, an electronic locking rear differential (the only Armada that gets one), adaptive air suspension, a skid plate, an off-road front bumper with a better approach angle, and eight drive modes. New for 2027 are three dealer-installed NISMO parts: rock sliders, a roof rack, and NISMO wheels on BFGoodrich all-terrains. Production starts this month, and it debuts at the LA Auto Show on November 20. It’s $81,265 with destination.

Why it matters

For most of the world, the Patrol is the rival to the Land Cruiser. In the Gulf and in Australia it’s a legend, and the current Armada is built on the same Y63 Patrol. Americans just never saw the name. Putting it on the most capable Armada is Nissan telling you which one is the real off-roader, and making the Land Cruiser comparison out loud.

My take

I like it. A name should mean something, and Patrol means a locker, skid plates and a truck built for sand. Putting it on the one Armada with a locking rear diff is the right call, rather than on the whole range.

But be clear about what you’re paying for: a heritage badge on an evolution of a trim that already existed. This isn’t a solid-axle, go-anywhere Patrol like the Y61. It’s a 425-horsepower luxury SUV with independent suspension and the right off-road parts bolted on. That’s not a criticism; it’s what Americans actually buy.

What it means if you’re shopping

  • Want the hardware, not the badge? A 2025 or 2026 Armada PRO-4X has the same locker, air suspension and twin-turbo V6. If the price is right, the name is the only thing you give up.
  • Looking at an older Armada? The 2017–24 Armada is the Y62 Patrol underneath, with the 5.6-liter V8 but no rear locker. Our Y62 / Armada guide covers what breaks and which years to buy.
  • Want the Patrol America never got? The solid-axle Y61 is the cult one, and a project to import. Start with Patrol best years.
  • Into the history? The truck that carried the name here until 1969 is the Patrol 60 Series.
