The best boxy 4x4s under $30,000

Thirty grand is where boxy 4x4s get properly interesting. It's Land Cruiser money again, both the solid-axle 80 Series and the V8 100 Series, plus the Lexus twins that share their bones, a long-wheelbase Wrangler with factory lockers, a square-body K5 and, against all common sense, a V8 G-Wagen.

Every truck here has a driver-quality range in our October 2026 value checks that starts under $30,000. A few run past it, and the clean, low-mile examples have pulled well away, so read each price line as the band a decent driver trades in. If you'd rather spend half as much, our under-$15,000 guide starts with the 4th-gen 4Runner.

How we picked We started from the driver-quality price range in each model guide's October 2026 value check, which is built mostly from recent Bring a Trailer sales, and kept only trucks whose driver range starts under $30,000. Best years come from the buy and avoid flags on each guide's timeline, which follow owner-forum consensus and published buyer's guides. The watch line is the failure that most often turns a cheap truck into an expensive one. Every pick was sold new in the US.

  1. No. 1 · Best all-rounder

    Toyota Land Cruiser 100 Series

    YEARS 2003–07PRICE $15k–36k for a driver

    The Land Cruiser that went to finishing school: a 4.7 V8, independent front suspension and a cabin you could take to the opera. The purists sulked, and it turned out to be one of the most durable big wagons on the trail. Buy a 2003–07 truck with the five-speed A750F automatic; those are the years owners most often point buyers to, and the 2006–07 trucks add VVT-i.

    Rough 100s are still cheap, but clean, low-mileage, documented trucks now sell for 80 Series money: 20 Bring a Trailer sales from August to October 2026 ran from $6,350 to $65,000. A good driver sits comfortably inside this budget.

    Check first: Ask for the timing-belt receipt (the 90,000-mile service, ideally with the water pump and the brittle plastic heater-hose tees), and try more than one cold start, since a click with no crank means worn starter contacts.

  2. No. 2 · Best solid-axle Land Cruiser

    Toyota Land Cruiser 80 Series

    YEARS 1993–97 (FZJ80)PRICE $18k–34k for a driver

    Coil springs, solid axles, full-time four-wheel drive and, if you find the right one, three lockers. The 1993–97 FZJ80, with the 4.5 1FZ-FE, is the run owners recommend over the 3F-E trucks. 1993 is also when factory front and rear lockers became optional and ABS trucks got a full-floating rear axle.

    250 Bring a Trailer sales from January 2025 to October 2026 had a median of about $25,500, so it fits, but tightly. Clean, lockered 1993–97 trucks have become collector trucks, and excellent ones run $45k–90k+. Under $30k you're buying a driver you can actually wheel.

    Check first: Look for coolant weeping at the head-to-block joint, then pull the square knuckle fill plug: runny grease means the inner axle seals have let diff oil in.

  3. No. 3 · Best-value Land Cruiser

    Lexus LX 470

    YEARS 2003–05PRICE $16k–25k for a driver

    A 100 Series Land Cruiser with a hydraulic party trick: AHC that rises on its toes to clear a rock, then kneels so you can load the groceries. The 2003 facelift brought the five-speed automatic, and forum regulars call 2003–05 the years to get, mostly for that gearbox.

    Driver-quality LX 470s top out lower than driver 100 Series trucks in our checks ($25k against $36k), and the 20 most recent Bring a Trailer sales ran from $6,000 to $35,325. Prices hinge on the AHC, timing-belt records and miles.

    Check first: Cycle the AHC through Low, Normal and High and watch it level, and look for fluid at the actuators and lines.

  4. No. 4 · Best modern daily

    Lexus GX 460

    YEARS 2014–23PRICE $20k–33k for a driver

    Fourteen years, one V8, zero reinventions: the GX 460 is a Prado 150 with a 4.6 V8, standard KDSS and a locking centre diff. Buy 2014 or later; ClubLexus moderators say the secondary air-pump trouble that dogs 2010–13 trucks is far less common from 2014 on.

    30 Bring a Trailer sales from January 2025 to October 2026 had a median of about $26,000, but late low-mile trucks and Black Lines hold $45k–64k. Under $30k means an earlier facelift truck with miles, and GXOR's buying guide puts records ahead of mileage anyway.

    Check first: Look up at the transmission bell housing for pink crust from the coolant valley leak, and make sure oil near the filter isn't the timing cover, which dealers have quoted at $2,600–3,500.

  5. No. 5 · Most capable out of the box

    Toyota FJ Cruiser

    YEARS 2010–14PRICE $26k–37k for a 2010–13 driver

    The FJ40's face on a Prado-family frame, with a factory rear locker (standard on manuals, optional on automatics in Toyota's 2014 specs): few new trucks of the 2000s went further off the pavement without a single aftermarket part. 2010 brought the dual VVT-i V6 (259 hp on regular fuel) and a slightly larger rear diff, and owners usually steer buyers toward 2010–14. CarGurus says 2007s have far more reported problems than any other year.

    It's the tightest fit here. Our driver range for 2010–13 trucks starts at $26k, and Jalopnik found sub-100,000-mile FJs listed at or above their last new price. High-mile 2007–08 trucks are cheaper at $12.5k–17k, but those are the early trucks with the 2007 teething problems and the fender-apron cracks.

    Check first: Get under it with a hammer and a light: frame rot around the rear control-arm brackets is the FJ's big weakness in salt-belt states.

  6. No. 6 · Best Jeep

    Jeep Wrangler Unlimited (LJ)

    YEARS 2004–06; Rubicon from 2005PRICE $16k–27k for a driver

    The LJ is a TJ that grew ten inches of wheelbase, which turned out to be exactly the right number. Jeep sold it for barely two and a half model years. The Unlimited Rubicon arrived for 2005 with locking Dana 44s front and rear, the 4:1 Rock-Trac transfer case and 245/75R16 MT/Rs.

    Now everyone wants one. 55 Bring a Trailer sales from April 2025 to October 2026 had a median of about $20,500, and Unlimited Rubicons a median of about $25,250, so a good Rubicon is within reach.

    Check first: Probe the frame near the rear lower control-arm brackets, which Quadratec calls 'the TJ issue', and on a 2005–06 ask whether the oil pump drive assembly has been replaced.

  7. No. 7 · Best wildcard

    Mercedes-Benz G 500

    YEARS 2005–08PRICE $25k–45k for a driver

    A Cold War military design with leather, a V8 and three locking differentials. Handsome Motors says the early electrical faults got better every year and were mostly gone by 2005–06, so start there. 2005–06 trucks keep the simpler five-speed automatic; 2007–08 trucks get the seven-speed, HID lamps and a reversing camera.

    On Bring a Trailer, prices have barely moved since 2018: 55 sales of 2002–08 US-market G 500s from January 2025 to October 2026 had a median of about $35,000. Under $30k is the bottom of the driver range, where condition and records matter far more than the year. This is the pick with the biggest running costs, so keep a slice of the budget back.

    Check first: Engage all three locking differentials on a loose surface and watch the indicators; lockers that were 'never used' by the last owner often don't work.

  8. No. 8 · Best classic

    Chevrolet K5 Blazer (1973–1991)

    YEARS 1987–91, ideally 1989–91PRICE $15k–30k for a driver

    A half-ton pickup with the back seat where the bed should be and a roof you can unbolt, plus a small-block V8 you can fix with a wrench and axles that half the junkyard will fit. CK5 owners point to the 1987–91 throttle-body-injection trucks for drivability and economy, and rate 1989–91 best for the NP241 transfer case, 30-spline axle shafts and updated 700R4.

    The 15 most recent Bring a Trailer sales ran from $4,600 to $130,000 with a median of $29,500, but stock trucks mostly sold for $15,000–38,500. Prices depend more on the build than the year, so pay for good work, not for chrome.

    Check first: Unbolt the steering box if the seller will let you: the frame cracks around and behind its bolts, often on the inside of the rail where you can't see it.

Questions

Land Cruiser or Lexus?

Same bones, different priorities. The LX 470 is a 100 Series with AHC and more leather, and the LX 450 is an FZJ80 with cladding and softer springs. Our driver ranges: LX 470 $16k–25k against the 100's $15k–36k, and LX 450 $17k–30k against the 80's $18k–34k. The LX 470's catch is the AHC, which needs its fluid looked after.

Why isn't the 5th-gen 4Runner on this list?

It nearly is: our driver range for the 5th-gen 4Runner is $25k–46k. But our guide's best years are 2020–24, and 22 recent Bring a Trailer sales had a median of about $38,000, so the trucks we'd point you to mostly sit above this budget. An early high-mile truck ($15k–25k) is the way in under $30k.

Is a $30,000 G-Wagen a good idea?

It can be, if you buy the right one and keep money back. Stick to 2005–08 G 500s, when Handsome Motors says the early electrical gremlins were mostly gone, and check every window, lock and seat motor, the harmonic balancer and the front axle seals. Badge-check AMG claims against the VIN and data card, because plenty of standard Gs have been dressed as AMGs.

Will these trucks hold their value?

We report what's happened, not what will. In our October 2026 checks, G 500 prices on Bring a Trailer had barely moved since 2018, clean lockered FZJ80s had become collector trucks, and clean 100 Series trucks were selling for 80 Series money. None of that is a forecast, so buy the truck you want to drive.
