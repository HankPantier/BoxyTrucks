The best boxy 4x4s for overlanding

Overlanding is mostly driving, a bit of camping and, if you've chosen badly, a lot of waiting for parts. So the qualities that matter are dull ones: a big tank, payload left over once the truck is loaded, an engine that's known to run for hundreds of thousands of miles, and parts you can get in a small town or carry in a box.

That's why this list is heavy on Toyota and Lexus. It isn't brand loyalty; it's what owners and their forums keep proving. The Grenadier makes it in as the new-truck answer, and the FAQ explains why the Jeeps and Land Rovers you might expect aren't here.

How we picked We started from the driver-grade prices in our October 2026 value checks (mostly recent Bring a Trailer results), took the best years from the owner consensus in each truck's guide, and weighed what breaks against what it costs to fix far from a dealer. Range figures are simple tank-times-mpg arithmetic from published EPA or KBB numbers, so treat them as best case.

  1. No. 1 · Best all-rounder

    Toyota Land Cruiser 100 Series

    YEARS 2003–07PRICE $15k–36k for a driver

    The 100 is the default answer for a reason. It has the 4.7 2UZ-FE V8, full-time four-wheel drive with a lockable center diff, 9.8 in of ground clearance and, on a 2005, a 1,470 lb payload and a 6,500 lb tow rating. The 25.4-gallon tank at 14 mpg combined works out to roughly 350 miles on paper, which is about as far as anyone should trust a fuel gauge in the desert.

    Owners most often point buyers to the 2003–07 trucks for the five-speed A750F automatic, and some say the gearbox matters more than the extra VVT-i power the 2006–07 trucks get. The 1998–99 trucks are the only ones with the optional rear locker, but they also have the two-pinion front diff that fails most often when driven hard. Rough ones are still cheap; clean, documented trucks now sell for 80 Series money.

    Check first: Ask for the 90,000-mile timing-belt receipt, including the heater-hose tees most shops skip; with no paperwork, assume it's due.

  2. No. 2 · Best value

    Lexus GX 470

    YEARS 2003–09 (buy on condition, not year)PRICE $12k–25k for a driver

    Our value check calls the GX 470 the default budget overlander, and it's easy to see why: it's a Prado underneath, with the same 2UZ-FE V8 as the 100, a five-speed automatic, full-time 4WD with low range and a locking center diff. A 23-gallon tank at an EPA 15 mpg combined gives roughly 345 miles, and a 6,150 lb GVWR against a 4,740 lb base curb weight leaves about 1,400 lb on paper for people, water and gear. Towing went from 5,000 lb to 6,500 lb with a mid-2004 hitch upgrade.

    Our guide has no forum-consensus best year, so buy the truck rather than the model year. The 2005-on engine has VVT-i and 270 hp (restated as 263 hp from 2006), which is nice but not essential. What matters more is the deferred maintenance a cheap one usually comes with: timing belt, front-end joints and the rear air springs.

    Check first: Get under it with a light and a screwdriver: frame rust is the dealbreaker on salt-belt trucks, and rust on the rear air-spring perches is what kills the airbags.

  3. No. 3 · Best solid-axle expedition wagon

    Toyota Land Cruiser 80 Series

    YEARS 1993–97 FZJ80PRICE $18k–34k for a driver

    If you want solid axles, coil springs and the option of three lockers, the 80 is the one. The 1993–97 FZJ80 brought the 4.5 1FZ-FE, a full-floating rear axle with discs on ABS trucks and optional factory front and rear lockers, and it's the run owners recommend over the earlier 3F-E trucks. A 1997 is rated for a 1,719 lb payload and has 10.8 in of ground clearance, more than anything else on this list.

    The catch is thirst. KBB lists 12 city and 14 highway for a 1997, so the 25.1-gallon tank is good for roughly 300–350 miles before you start doing sums. Clean, locked trucks have become collector items; tired drivers are still cheap enough to use the way Toyota intended.

    Check first: Look for coolant weeping at the head-to-block joint and runny grease in the front knuckles, a sign the inner axle seals went long ago.

  4. No. 4 · Best V8 bargain

    Toyota 4Runner 4th Gen

    YEARS 2006–09, ideally the V8PRICE $8k–23k for a driver

    The fourth gen is the only 4Runner with a V8, and the 2UZ-FE version gets full-time 4WD with a lockable Torsen center diff and a 7,000 lb tow rating on 4WD trucks. It's built on Prado 120 bones, shares a lot with the GX 470 and usually costs less. The 23-gallon tank at the V8's EPA 15 city and 19 highway is good for roughly 350–440 miles.

    The 2006 refresh is the start of the years to buy: 2006-on V8s are the pick, and late-2006 V6s get the revised head gasket that 2003–05 V6s lacked. The 2009 Trail Edition added a factory rear e-locker, A-TRAC and Bilsteins. Low-mile V8s have broken away from the pack in price; everything else is still cheap.

    Check first: On a V8, ask for timing-belt records and scan for secondary air-injection codes, a failure that can cost $2,000–4,000.

  5. No. 5 · Best modern daily and trip truck

    Toyota 4Runner 5th Gen

    YEARS 2020–24PRICE $25k–46k for a driver

    Fifteen years of the same 4.0 V6 and five-speed made the fifth gen about as well understood as any truck on sale. Trail and TRD Off-Road trucks have KDSS, a rear locker and Crawl Control, and the aftermarket for this truck is enormous. Car and Driver saw 17 mpg at 75 mph in a 2022 TRD Pro, which makes the 23-gallon tank good for roughly 390 miles at a steady cruise.

    Autotrader recommends a late truck, preferably 2020–24, which brought Toyota Safety Sense-P and CarPlay. The limits are the 5,000 lb tow rating and the independent front suspension, which gets steep CV angles once it's lifted. Prices are firm: late low-mile TRD Pros still change hands near their original sticker.

    Check first: Look for wet KDSS cylinders or weeping lines and run the VIN for the airbag and fuel-pump recalls before you buy.

  6. No. 6 · Best for towing a trailer into the wild

    Toyota Land Cruiser 200 Series

    YEARS 2013–15, or laterPRICE $55k–75k for a 2013–18 driver

    The 200 is the one to pick if your overland rig includes a trailer: KBB lists 8,500 lb for 2008 and 8,100 lb for 2016–2021, behind a 5.7 3UR-FE V8, KDSS and Crawl Control. IH8MUD owners point US shoppers to 2013–15 trucks, which got Multi-Terrain Select, Multi-Terrain Monitor and Off-Road Turn Assist as standard. The 2016–21 trucks add the eight-speed and bigger front brakes.

    Read the payload figure before you build it: KBB lists just 1,320 lb for a 2021, because the truck already weighs 5,815 lb. A 24.6-gallon tank at 14 mpg combined gives roughly 345 miles. High-mile early trucks start around $20k, and late, low-mile 2021s and Heritage Editions trade at or above their original sticker.

    Check first: Look for dried pink coolant below the water-pump weep hole, and on a lifted truck check that both KDSS valves were closed again.

  7. No. 7 · Best late-model luxury pick

    Lexus GX 460

    YEARS 2014–23PRICE $20k–33k for a driver

    The GX 460 is a Prado 150 with a 4.6 V8, KDSS and a locking center diff, rated to tow 6,500 lb. The 2020 Off-Road package added Crawl Control, Multi-Terrain Select, a transmission cooler and a fuel-tank protector, which is most of what you'd add yourself. A 23-gallon tank at an EPA 16–17 mpg combined is good for roughly 370–390 miles.

    Buy a 2014 or later: ClubLexus moderators say secondary air-pump trouble is far less common from 2014 on, while it's a known problem on 2010–13 trucks. Watch the roof: the owner's manual limits the factory luggage carrier to 176 lb, so a rooftop tent may need an aftermarket rack. Late low-mile trucks and Black Lines hold close to new-car money.

    Check first: Look for pink crust on the transmission bell housing and oil around the filter area, which can mean the coolant-valley leak or the driver-side timing cover (dealers have quoted $2,600–3,500).

  8. No. 8 · Best if you're buying new

    Ineos Grenadier Station Wagon

    YEARS MY2026PRICE $56.5k–72k for a typical used 2024; $71,000–82,995 new (before destination)

    The Grenadier was built for exactly this job: solid axles, a ladder frame, a standard center diff lock (front and rear lockers come with the Rough Pack), about 1,680 lb of payload in US spec, 7,716 lb braked towing and a roof rated for 150 kg moving and 420 kg static. A 90-liter tank at an EPA 15 mpg gives roughly 355 miles.

    The MY2026 update is the reason to buy new: a variable-ratio steering box with more assist replaces the slow Bosch box reviewers called vague. The weak spot for remote travel is support. Ineos is a young brand with a small dealer network, and the factory stopped from September 2024 to January 2025 over a parts shortage. The powertrain is BMW and ZF, but body and chassis parts come through Ineos.

    Check first: Run the VIN for US recalls 23V828, 23V903 and 25V147 and ask the local dealer about parts lead times before you plan a long trip.

Questions

What's the cheapest good overlander?

A GX 470 or a fourth-gen 4Runner. Our October 2026 checks put drivers at $12k–25k for the GX and $8k–23k for the 4Runner, and both use the same 2UZ-FE V8 if you choose a V8 4Runner. Budget for the timing belt and front-end work either way, because cheap ones usually come with deferred maintenance.

How much payload do these trucks actually have?

Less than you'd think. KBB lists 1,470 lb for a 2005 100 Series, 1,719 lb for a 1997 80 Series and just 1,320 lb for a 2021 200 Series; a US-spec Grenadier Station Wagon carries about 1,680 lb. Bumpers, a winch, sliders, a roof rack, water and passengers come out of that number, so weigh the truck loaded before a long trip.

Why isn't a Jeep Wrangler on this list?

Because the numbers favor the trail over the long haul. A 2007 Wrangler JK is rated for 1,000 lb of payload and 2,000 lb of towing with an 18.6-gallon tank. Wranglers are superb on the rocks, which is why they lead our rock-crawling guide, but they run out of payload quickly once you add a fridge, a rooftop tent and a passenger.

What about a classic Defender?

It's a proper expedition truck, but parts and specialists are thinner in most of the US, and the prices have run away. Our checks put a good gray-market 90 or 110 Tdi driver at $30k–56k and a NAS Defender 90 driver at $40k–60k. If you want one, start with our Defender guide and its rust checks.

Do I need lockers for overlanding?

Usually not. Most overland routes are about clearance, tires and not breaking anything, and a locking center diff, which every truck on this list has, does most of the work. A rear locker is useful for getting yourself out of a bad spot, which is why the 4th-gen Trail Edition, 5th-gen Trail and TRD Off-Road trucks and the Grenadier's Rough Pack are worth looking for.
