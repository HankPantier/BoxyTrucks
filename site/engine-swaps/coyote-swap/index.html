Coyote 5.0 swaps in the early Bronco

A Ford Coyote V8 in a 1966–77 early Bronco is a bit like a fax machine with a fibre connection: it's the right brand, the right shape and wildly out of its era, and it's the engine behind many of the restomods at the top of the market. According to CJ Pony Parts, the Coyote arrived in 2011, and every engine Ford fitted to the Bronco was an overhead-valve design. An overhead-cam V8 in a truck that started life with an inline-six is a long way from the factory, but for a Ford owner who wants a modern engine, it keeps the badge on the valve covers honest.

It's also the hardest of the common early Bronco swaps. Wild Horses, which sells parts for everything from 289s to Explorer 5.0s, calls the Coyote the most involved option: custom mounts, crossmember and drivetrain changes, a standalone harness and ECU, fuel-system upgrades, and custom exhaust and cooling. This guide covers who makes the parts, what the emissions rules say, and what it costs based on supplier prices and builder figures.

Trucks it goes into

Ford Bronco (1966–1977)The Coyote's natural home, and the only boxy 4x4 on our site where we found a mature Coyote parts market. Throttle Down Kustoms sells Coyote mounts that bolt to the factory V8 mount locations ($199.99), and says almost every build needs at least a 2-inch body lift for hood clearance. It also sells a Coyote-specific replacement frame. B.C. Broncos sells the rest of the package, including a Coyote 10-quart oil pan (from $699.99), headers (from $1,246.44), a serpentine kit for the F-150 Coyote ($1,757.45) and a 2-into-1 6R80 exhaust ($699.99). Its 4WDrive build fitted a 2011 Coyote without cutting the frame, with a 4R70W, an Atlas 2 transfer case, a relocated oil filter, a slimmer IDIDIT column and a radiator with its outlets flipped. Advance Adapters makes a kit to put a Coyote and AX15 five-speed on the factory Dana 20 (27-3525C, $4,521) and a 6R80 crossmember for the early Bronco (716040, $254). In our October 2026 value check, Coyote-swapped shop-built restomods sold on Bring a Trailer for $122,000–166,500 in the middle of the range, with the best up to $325,000.

Why people do it

Power and drivability, in a Ford. Wild Horses says factory Coyote ratings range from about 360 hp to more than 480 hp, depending on the generation. That's a long way from the original six and the three-speed with no overdrive. CJ Pony Parts says F-150 Coyotes are easier to find in salvage yards, are usually cheaper, and are physically smaller, which helps in an old engine bay. Mustang Coyotes make more power and have more aftermarket support.

The other reason is the market. In our October 2026 early Bronco value check, Coyote-swapped shop builds sat in their own band above even excellent original trucks. Who built the truck now matters more than the year it was made. That's a description of what has sold, not a forecast.

What it takes

CJ Pony Parts' list of what a crate Coyote doesn't come with is the honest starting point: a transmission, motor mounts, a K-member, flywheel and clutch or flexplate, the Ford Performance control pack, an oil-filter adapter or relocator, a starter (or a way to rotate the solenoid), exhaust headers, a transfer-case adapter and a lift kit. The control pack includes the harness, OBD port, power distribution box, MAF sensor and PCM. Switching from a carburettor also means new fuel lines and an EFI-rated electric pump.

The B.C. Broncos build in 4WDrive shows where the engine fights the truck. The stock Coyote headers hit the frame, and the passenger side also hits the floor and inner fender. The oil filter hits the frame and has to be moved. The upper radiator hose angles into the air intake, so they used a radiator with its outlets flipped. The brake booster sits close to a valve cover, and the factory steering column crowds the engine, so they fitted a 2-inch IDIDIT column and smaller Borgeson U-joints. On ClassicBroncos, posters add that the usual Coyote starter solenoid hits the frame, and one recommends Ford's high-torque mini starter.

Transmission choice decides the transfer-case work. CJ Pony names the 6R80 as the popular choice and says the 4R70W also works. B.C. Broncos used a 4R70W. On ClassicBroncos, one poster says the 6R80 has to come from a 4x4 for the adapter to bolt on, and another weighs the 6R80 against a new controller and an Atlas adapter. Advance Adapters explains that the Bronco Dana 20 puts its front output on the driver's side, has a 6-spline input, and switched from a 'T' handle to a 'J' shifter in 1973. It recommends that J-shifter owners convert to a T-handle or a twin-stick. Advance Adapters also manufactures a 32-spline Dana 20 output shaft, designed by Wild Horses and sold through them, that it says beefs up the output shaft by up to 50% over stock.

Plan for a lift and springs. CJ Pony suggests about 2–3 inches of lift for clearance and new springs, because the Coyote is heavier. Throttle Down Kustoms says almost every build with its mounts needs a 2-inch body lift.

Parts and kits

Mounts and frames: Throttle Down Kustoms sells Coyote mounts, a Coyote frame and a skid plate. B.C. Broncos makes adapter mounts with replaceable rubbers, and ClassicBroncos posters name Tumblin as a cheaper second option, though one passed on it because he didn't want to cut his frame. Engine-side parts: B.C. Broncos sells oil pans, headers, serpentine kits, air tubes, exhaust and plumbing. A ClassicBroncos poster used Van's headers, 6R80 adapter and computer, and another advises buying everything from one vendor so it supports the whole package.

Transmission and transfer case: Advance Adapters' Coyote bellhousing and AX15/Dana 20 kit covers manual builds, and its 6R80 crossmember covers automatics. Wild Horses lists 17 parts in its 1966–77 Coyote section. For the engine itself, Ford Performance sells crate Coyotes, control packs and complete Coyote Power Modules.

Other boxy 4x4s: we didn't find a comparable Coyote kit market for the Land Cruiser, Jeep, Scout or Defender. Novak Conversions and Advance Adapters, the big Jeep swap houses we checked in October 2026, list LS kits for the CJ, XJ and TJ, but we didn't find Coyote packages in their catalogs, and Advance Adapters' Coyote parts are aimed at the early Bronco. ECD's Defender engine-swap page offers the LS3, and its Range Rover Classic engine options are the LS3, LC9, Cummins diesel and Rover V8. For those trucks, see our LS swap guide.

Emissions and registration

Federal rules: the EPA's 1991 engine-switching fact sheet says putting a light-duty engine in a different light-duty vehicle counts as tampering unless the result matches a certified configuration of the same or newer model year as the vehicle chassis. It also says a heavy-duty engine may never go into a light-duty vehicle. Any Coyote is newer than any early Bronco, but you still have to keep its emissions equipment.

California: BAR's Engine Change Guidelines require the engine to be the same model year or newer than the vehicle, the donor and recipient to share the same vehicle and engine classification, and every emissions system from the donor, including evaporative monitoring, to be installed. The truck must then pass a BAR Referee inspection and wear a Referee label. The Smog Check Reference Guide also lists 1975-and-older gasoline vehicles as exempt from Smog Check, which covers 1966–75 Broncos but not 1976–77 trucks.

Ford's emissions route is the Coyote Power Module, M-9000-M50AE. Ford Authority reported in 2023 that it is a Gen 3 Coyote paired with a 10R80 automatic, two catalytic converters and O2 sensors, intended for pre-1996 vehicles that need a 'CARB compliant solution'. Ford's instruction sheet limits it to pre-1996 vehicles with emissions equipment up to OBD I, not OBD II. It also requires the purge solenoid, EVAP canister, supplied induction tube and fuel-pressure regulator, keeps the O2 sensors in their stock locations, and calls for an emission label. The sheet doesn't give an executive order number, so ask Ford for it before you rely on it at a referee.

What it costs

CJ Pony Parts puts the total for a Coyote swap at up to about $20,000, not counting the truck, and says the extra parts cost at least as much as the engine. Ford Authority reported a $21,500 retail price for the Coyote Power Module in 2023, which includes the engine, a 10R80 automatic and its emissions parts.

These are October 2026 list prices for the parts that are specific to the early Bronco: Throttle Down Kustoms mounts at $199.99; Advance Adapters' AX15/Dana 20 Coyote kit at $4,521, including a new AX15 and bellhousing; its 6R80 crossmember at $254; and from B.C. Broncos, an oil pan from $699.99, headers from $1,246.44, a serpentine kit at $1,757.45, a Gen 3 air tube at $349.99 and a 2-into-1 exhaust at $699.99. Add a lift, driveshafts, fuel system, cooling and labour.

If you're buying one already built: in our October 2026 value check, Coyote-swapped shop-built early Broncos sold on Bring a Trailer for $122,000–166,500 in the middle of the range, against $41,500–70,000 for a stock or mildly modified driver.

Questions

F-150 or Mustang Coyote?

CJ Pony Parts says F-150 Coyotes are easier to find at salvage yards, usually cheaper and physically smaller, which helps in an early Bronco. Mustang Coyotes make more power and have more aftermarket support. Make sure your mounts, oil pan and headers match the engine you buy.

Can I keep the Dana 20?

Yes. Advance Adapters makes a kit that puts a Coyote and AX15 five-speed on the stock Bronco Dana 20, and Wild Horses sells a heavy-duty 32-spline output shaft for it. Many builders switch to an Atlas, as B.C. Broncos did in its 4WDrive build.

Do I have to cut the frame or lift the body?

Not necessarily the frame. B.C. Broncos' build used adapter mounts on the stock frame mounts, and Throttle Down Kustoms' mounts bolt to the factory V8 mount locations. One ClassicBroncos poster passed on Tumblin's parts because he didn't want to cut his frame. Do plan on a body lift: Throttle Down Kustoms says almost every build needs at least 2 inches, and B.C. Broncos used one inch.

Is a Coyote swap legal in California?

It can be. BAR requires the engine to be the same model year or newer, of the same vehicle and engine class, with all of the donor's emissions equipment fitted, and the truck must pass a BAR Referee inspection. BAR lists 1975-and-older gasoline vehicles as exempt from Smog Check. Ford markets the Coyote Power Module for pre-1996 OBD I vehicles that need a CARB-compliant solution, but confirm its executive order before you buy.

Has anyone Coyote-swapped a Land Cruiser, Jeep or Defender?

Probably, but we didn't find the kits or well-documented builds that would let us recommend it. For those trucks, the established route is the LS. See our LS swap guide.

Engine swaps change emissions compliance, insurance and sometimes legality. Check your state’s rules before you start. See the disclaimer.
