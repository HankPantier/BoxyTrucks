Compare/Land Rover Defender vs Ineos Grenadier vs Mercedes G-Class
Head to head

Land Rover Defender vs Ineos Grenadier vs Mercedes G-Class

Land Rover Defender (L663)Ineos Grenadier Station WagonMercedes-Benz G-Class (second generation)

The Defender is the best all-rounder and by far the cheapest used; the Grenadier is the traditional one, ladder frame and beam axles, for people who like to know how things work; the G-Class gives you three lockers and a ladder frame inside a luxury car, at luxury-car prices. Top Gear summed up the first two as 'mechanics versus electrics', and the G sits somewhere between.

The short answer

Buy the Land Rover Defender (L663) if

you want one truck for the commute, the highway and a hard trail, with air suspension, 900 mm of wading and the best used value of the three

Buy the Ineos Grenadier Station Wagon if

you want a ladder frame, beam axles and up to three lockers, plan to do your own work, and can live with a young dealer network (and pre-2026 steering)

Buy the Mercedes-Benz G-Class (second generation) if

you want three factory lockers and a 2.93:1 low range wrapped in real luxury, and the price of entry doesn't bother you

Off-road

On paper the Defender leads. A 110 on air gives up to 291 mm (11.5 in) of clearance, 38°/40°/28° angles and 900 mm of wading. The Grenadier has 264 mm, 35.5°/36.1°/28.2° and 800 mm; a 2019 G 550 has 9.5 in, 30.9°/29.9°/23.5° by Mercedes-Benz USA's figures, and 27.6 in. Top Gear ran a Defender against a Grenadier and found them 'pretty evenly matched' in capability: the Defender's electronics do most of the work, while the Grenadier asks more of the driver and gives more feedback.

Underneath, they couldn't be more different. The Defender is an aluminium monocoque with independent suspension, a lockable centre differential and a locking rear, but no front locker. The Grenadier has Carraro beam axles on coils, a 2.5:1 low range and three lockers with the Rough Pack. The G keeps a ladder frame and a rigid rear axle, adds independent front suspension, and has a 2.93:1 low range and three 100% locks on every gas model.

On-road and daily use

The Defender wins easily. Top Gear: 'For life's general tasks, the Grenadier can't hold a candle to the Defender.' The Grenadier's pre-2026 steering is a slow recirculating-ball box, nearly four turns lock to lock, which The Drive found wandered on the highway; MY2026 trucks got a variable-ratio box that GearJunkie called 'a big leap forward'. The 2018-on G moved to independent front suspension and electric rack-and-pinion steering and is far better on the road than the old W463.

All three are thirsty and all three tow about 3.5 tonnes. The EPA rates a 2026 Defender 3.0 six at 18 mpg city and 22–23 highway, the Grenadier at 15/15 on premium, and the 2019 V8 G 550 at 13/17 (17/19 for the 2025-on six). Towing is 8,200 lb for the Defender, 7,716 lb for the Grenadier and 7,700 lb for a 2019 G 550.

Reliability and what breaks

None is a Toyota. The Defender's faults are mostly electrical and software: DC-DC converter failures and recalls on the mild hybrids, steering knocks, coolant loss on the P400, and PM and EGR sensor codes on diesels sold outside the US. The Grenadier's are mostly electronic too, with module failures, false warnings, reflashes, sticky door buttons and water in the doors, plus three US recalls on early trucks; its BMW six and ZF gearbox are well proven elsewhere.

The G's early trucks carry several recalls, including the front-axle harness that can disable ABS and stability control (23V097), the diff-lock module (20V090) and rear door latches (21V353, 24V659). Owners also report transfer-case output-seal leaks and door hinge sag.

Running costs, parts and modifying

The Grenadier is the most conventional to work on, with a ladder frame, beam axles and bought-in BMW and ZF powertrain parts. Body and chassis parts come only through Ineos, though, and the factory stopped for four months in 2024–25 over a parts shortage. The Defender wants a diagnostic tool and a good specialist. Bolt-on armor for the G is scarce; Eurowise is one of the few making skids, sliders and a rear bumper for it.

Tires: the Defender takes a 275/60R20 (about 33 in) without a lift, the Grenadier a 285/75R17 (about 34 in) with washers under the spare carrier, and the G takes 33s on a 2.5 in lift kit. A Grenadier needs about 2 in of lift for 35s, and Lucky8 put 35s on a Defender 110 using lift rods, trimming and relocated intercoolers.

Buying one now

Depreciation makes the Defender the bargain: 2020–22 90s and 110s sold for $27k–42k on Bring a Trailer, with a $45,000 median across 2025–26. Used 2024 Grenadiers brought $56,500–92,500 (median about $65,750), not far under a new 2026 at $71,000–82,995 before destination. Early 2019 G 550s start around $72k, and AMG G 63s fetched a median of $146,500.

Buy a Defender with every recall closed, especially the DC-DC converter campaigns on mild hybrids. Buy a MY2026 Grenadier if you drive a lot of highway, or a 2024 with all three recalls done. Buy a G 550 with the harness, diff-lock and door-latch recalls confirmed by VIN. If you want the same ideas with simpler engineering, look at the old Defender 90/110 and the W463 G-Class.

Questions, answered

Which is the most capable off-road?

Stock, the Defender has the best numbers: up to 291 mm of clearance, 38°/40°/28° angles and 900 mm of wading on a 110 with air. Top Gear found the Defender and Grenadier evenly matched in practice. The Grenadier and G have more locking differentials, three each against the Defender's two, and the Grenadier's beam axles are the easiest to modify.

Which has the most lockers?

The G-Class and the Grenadier can both have three: the G has centre, rear and front locks on every gas model, and the Grenadier has a standard centre lock plus front and rear lockers with the Rough Pack (standard on the Trialmaster). The Defender has a lockable centre and a locking rear differential. The electric G 580 uses torque vectoring instead of mechanical lockers.

Which is cheapest to buy?

The Defender, by a lot. 2020–22 trucks sold for $27k–42k on Bring a Trailer in 2025–26. Used 2024 Grenadiers typically brought $56.5k–72k, and the cheapest 2019 G 550s about $72k.

Which tows the most?

The Defender, at 8,200 lb (3,500 kg). The Grenadier is rated at 3,500 kg braked (GearJunkie lists 7,716 lb) and a 2019 G 550 at 7,700 lb. Check payload as well: the Grenadier's is about 1,680 lb in US figures.

Can I get a diesel in the US?

Not in any of them. Land Rover's six-cylinder diesels, the Grenadier's BMW B57 and the G 350 d / G 400 d are all sold in other markets only.

Which is the most reliable?

None has a long, clean record yet. The Grenadier's BMW engine and ZF gearbox are proven, but owners report electronic gremlins; the Defender's weak spots are mild-hybrid electrics and sensors; the G's early trucks had several recalls. Buy the one with the best service and recall history, and keep a good dealer or specialist close.
