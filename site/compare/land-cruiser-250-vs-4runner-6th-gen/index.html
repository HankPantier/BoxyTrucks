Land Cruiser 250 vs 6th Gen 4Runner

Toyota Land Cruiser 250 SeriesToyota 4Runner 6th Gen

For most buyers the 4Runner is the better deal: a TRD Off-Road gets the same frame, the same hybrid option and a rear locker for less money, with more range and an optional third row. The Land Cruiser 250 justifies its premium with full-time 4WD and center and rear lockers as standard on every US grade.

The short answer

Buy the Toyota Land Cruiser 250 Series if

you want full-time 4WD with center and rear lockers on every grade, the stabilizer disconnect without buying a top trim, and the Land Cruiser name

Buy the Toyota 4Runner 6th Gen if

you want a cheaper way in, a non-hybrid option, regular fuel, a bigger tank, more ground clearance or a third row

Same bones, different badges

These two are closer than any other Land Cruiser and 4Runner pair. Both sit on Toyota's GA-F (TNGA-F) ladder frame with a 112.2-inch wheelbase, double-wishbone front suspension and a coil-sprung live rear axle, and both offer the 326-hp i-FORCE MAX 2.4-liter turbo hybrid with an eight-speed automatic. In the US, the Land Cruiser 250 comes only with the hybrid; the 6th-gen 4Runner also offers a non-hybrid 278-hp turbo four.

The differences are in how Toyota spec'd them. Every US 250 gets full-time 4WD with a locking center diff and a locking rear diff. Most 4Runners get part-time 4WD, and only the TRD Off-Road, TRD Pro and Trailhunter get the rear locker; the others have a limited-slip rear diff.

Off-road

The 250's standard hardware is the big advantage: center and rear lockers on even the cheapest 1958 grade, and a Stabilizer Disconnect Mechanism for front-axle articulation available on the Land Cruiser grade. GearJunkie's back-to-back test against a 4Runner TRD Off-Road Premium also credited the Land Cruiser with a better approach angle and quicker steering, at about 2.5 turns lock to lock against 3.25.

The 4Runner answers with clearance. Car and Driver quotes 9.2 inches and 32° and 24° approach and departure angles, the Trailhunter has 10.1 inches, and GearJunkie's spec comparison gave its test 4Runner 0.8 inch more clearance than the Land Cruiser. The TRD Pro and Trailhunter add the front anti-roll-bar disconnect as standard, plus Fox or Old Man Emu dampers. Neither truck has a factory front locker.

On-road and daily use

With the same hybrid, performance is a wash, and both are rated to tow 6,000 lb. GearJunkie found more front room, three-zone climate control and quicker steering in the Land Cruiser, and more rear legroom, a tighter turning circle and the roll-down rear window in the 4Runner. Only the 4Runner offers a third row, on the SR5 and non-hybrid Limited, and Car and Driver describes those rearmost seats as little more than jump seats.

Range is the 250's weak spot. It has a 17.9-gallon tank and wants 91-octane fuel, and at its 23-mpg combined rating that's about 410 miles in theory. The 4Runner has 19 gallons and runs on 87-octane regular, though owners on 4Runner6G.com still complain of about 280 miles between fill-ups in mixed use.

Reliability and early issues

Both are too new for long-term verdicts, but they share some early problems. Front brakes that squeal in reverse are covered by a Toyota TSB (T-SB-0046-25, since superseded by T-SB-0006-26) that applies to both trucks. Hybrid owners of either should keep the battery cooling intakes and filters clear.

250 owners also report 12-volt battery drain at camp and hybrid battery-fan warnings, and its recalls include an instrument-cluster software update and a 2024–2027 fuel-tube connector recall (NHTSA 26V673000). 4Runner owners report transmission temperatures climbing on long, slow climbs, which Car and Driver also saw in testing, and some early trucks needed a new steering shaft under warranty for a clunk. 2025 4Runners are in an instrument-panel software recall (25V-595). Run the VIN at NHTSA for either.

Running costs and modifying

Running costs are close. The 250's premium fuel adds up, and its 75W-85 GL-5 diff oil is something owners say dealer parts counters don't always stock. Because the platform is shared, much of the aftermarket thinking carries over. ICON sells 1.25–3 inch lifts for the 250 and Dobinsons a 2-inch kit; 4Runner owners have kits from ReadyLift, TORQ and Total Chaos, and upper control arms from Total Chaos, Peak and Westcott.

If range matters, the 250 has an answer the 4Runner doesn't yet: the tank on Slee Off-Road's AT37 build holds 29 gallons, while a 4Runner6G thread hadn't confirmed a long-range tank for the 4Runner. An ARB Air Locker up front makes either truck triple-locked.

Buying one now

The 4Runner is the cheaper way in. Car and Driver lists 2026 4Runners from $43,865 (gas) to $70,195 (top hybrid) before destination, while Toyota.com lists the 2027 Land Cruiser at $58,180 for the 1958 and $64,255 for the Land Cruiser grade. In GearJunkie's like-for-like comparison, the 4Runner TRD Off-Road Premium hybrid cost about $4,680 less than a Land Cruiser, and GearJunkie called the 4Runner 'a clear winner'.

On the used market, 250 First Editions that once sold over sticker have come back down: Bring a Trailer sales fell from $83,000 in May 2024 to $58,500 in May 2026. There's no meaningful used market for the 6th-gen 4Runner yet. The 1958 is the cheapest 250 and still gets both lockers; if you want a locked 4Runner, start at the TRD Off-Road.

Questions, answered

Are the Land Cruiser 250 and 6th-gen 4Runner the same truck?

They share the GA-F (TNGA-F) ladder frame, the 112.2-inch wheelbase, the suspension layout and the i-FORCE MAX hybrid. The 250 gets full-time 4WD and center and rear lockers as standard; most 4Runners are part-time 4WD, and only the off-road trims get a rear locker.

Does the 4Runner have full-time 4WD?

Only the Platinum and the hybrid Limited. Other 4WD 4Runners use a part-time system with low range. Every US Land Cruiser 250 has full-time 4WD with a locking Torsen center diff.

Which is better off-road?

Out of the box the 250 has more locking hardware, and SDM is available on the Land Cruiser grade. A 4Runner TRD Pro or Trailhunter has more ground clearance, a rear locker and the front bar disconnect as standard. With tires and armor, either will go a long way.

Which has more range?

The 4Runner, on paper: 19 gallons against 17.9, and it runs on regular fuel while the 250 wants 91 octane. GearJunkie estimated about 437 miles of average range for the 4Runner hybrid and 412 for the Land Cruiser.

Can I get a third row?

Only in the 4Runner, as an option on the SR5 and the non-hybrid Limited. The hybrid battery sits under the 4Runner's cargo floor, so no hybrid 4Runner has one, and the US Land Cruiser 250 is two rows only.

Is the Land Cruiser worth the extra money?

If you'd otherwise buy a top-trim 4Runner, the prices overlap and the 250's full-time 4WD and standard lockers make a good case. Against a TRD Off-Road with the same hybrid, GearJunkie found the 4Runner the better value at about $4,680 less.
