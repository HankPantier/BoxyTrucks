PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

By Mitsubishi's count, the first two Pajeros, including the second generation, had sold 1.26 million in about 170 countries. The threat was the crossover SUV: buyers wanted car-like manners on the road. Mitsubishi says the decision on how to answer it split the company. In the end it dropped the separate ladder frame and built a monocoque body with a frame structure welded into its floor. The new Pajero went on sale in Japan in the autumn of 1999 and reached most export markets as a 2001 model.

THE ARGUMENT THAT SPLIT MITSUBISHI

Mitsubishi's own history admits the trade-off. A monocoque is lighter, but a frame is better at the rigidity and durability that rough tracks demand. Its answer was the 'built-in frame' monocoque, and the company says that although the truck grew, it lost a lot of weight and met an internal target of body stiffness comparable to its Dakar prototypes. Wikipedia credits Mitsubishi's RISE safety structure for a cabin that held its shape well, and the fuel tank moved between the axles to help weight distribution and ground clearance.

The suspension changed just as much. Double wishbones and coil springs went in at the front, replacing torsion bars, and the rear became independent too, on a subframe. The idea came from the rally team: the Pajero Evolution had used independent rear suspension, and Wikipedia says that design set the pattern for the third generation. Rack-and-pinion steering replaced the old recirculating-ball box. Unsealed 4X4 called the Australian NM 'the first 4×4 wagon in its class with monocoque construction and a fully independent suspension system'.

SUPER SELECT II, GDI AND A NEW DIESEL

Super Select became Super Select 4WD II. The centre differential uses planetary gears and splits torque between 33:67 and 50:50 front to rear, and the modes are now switched electrically. You still get 2H, full-time 4H, and locked-centre high and low ranges. Engines were a mix of old and new: the 6G74 3.5 V6 carried over (as a GDI direct-injection engine in Japan), the 2.8 4M40 diesel stayed in some markets, and a new 3.2-litre 4M41 DI-D four arrived. Mitsubishi Japan lists the 3.2 DI and 3.5 GDI as the two engines developed for the new truck.

Australia got the NM in 2000 with a launch drive to the Lambert Centre that a freak downpour turned into a mud epic. Unsealed 4X4's Dean Mellor remembers the petrol V6s refuelling from jerry cans while the 2.8 diesels 'made it on the smell of an oily rag'. In 2002 the 3.2 DI-D replaced the 2.8 there, and it could be ordered with the five-speed automatic. A 3.8 V6 followed in 2003, and electronic traction control arrived in 2004, ahead of the Prado.

AMERICA: THE MONTERO LIMITED AND THE MOOSE TEST

The US got the long-wheelbase five-door only, as the Montero XLS and Limited, with the 3.5 V6 rated at 200 hp and 235 lb-ft. Its first year brought bad press. In May 2001 Consumer Reports put the Montero Limited through its short-course avoidance manoeuvre, and its test truck tipped up on two wheels in eight of nine runs at 36.7 mph or faster. CR rated it 'Not Acceptable', a verdict it had previously given only the Suzuki Samurai and the 1996 Isuzu Trooper and Acura SLX. Mitsubishi disputed the method, said it had no stability complaints from customers and quoted NHTSA's view that CR's test had no scientific basis.

For 2003 the Montero was facelifted and the 3.5 gave way to a 3.8-litre 6G75 rated by Bring a Trailer's listings at 215 hp and 248 lb-ft, with Mitsubishi's M-ASTC stability and traction control. Owners on Expedition Portal say the XLS was dropped after 2003, leaving only the Limited. The Montero was Mitsubishi's only American SUV with standard four-wheel drive rather than all-wheel drive, and it left the US market after the 2006 model year.

LEGACY

The third generation was replaced in Japan in October 2006, but the platform didn't die. The fourth-generation Pajero kept the monocoque-and-frame body and independent suspension, and Unsealed notes that the same basic structure was still on sale in Australia two decades after the NM. In China, GAC Changfeng built the third generation until 2011. Today it's a bargain: Bring a Trailer has sold US Monteros for under $20,000, and the Japanese 3.2 diesels are now old enough to import. It rides like a car and has the 100 Series's cabin space, according to Unsealed, though its IFS-and-IRS layout will always start an argument at the campsite.

SIDEBAR · DAKAR 2000–2005

Five wins in a row

Mitsubishi's works team switched to a T2 rally car based on the new Pajero for the 2000 Dakar. It kept the production monocoque, stretched the wheelbase, used four-wheel independent suspension and ran a 3.5 MIVEC V6. That year Jean-Louis Schlesser's buggy won, and Mitsubishi had to settle for third.

Then the streak began. In 2001 Jutta Kleinschmidt became the first woman to win the Dakar outright, in a Pajero, after Hiroshi Masuoka broke a half shaft fighting the Schlesser buggies. Masuoka won in 2002 and again in 2003, the second year in a tube-framed Pajero Evolution prototype whose body only looked like the road car. Unsealed 4X4 counts five consecutive wins from 2001 to 2005, and Wikipedia says Mitsubishi's final Dakar wins came in 2007.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The big change is structural: a monocoque with a ladder-type frame welded into the floor, not a body bolted to a separate chassis. The suspension is independent at all four corners on coil springs, steering is rack and pinion, and Super Select 4WD II gives full-time 4WD with a lockable planetary centre differential. US Monteros are long-wheelbase V6 automatics; elsewhere you'll find three-door short bodies and the 3.2 DI-D diesel.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Ask for the timing-belt receiptBoth US V6s use belts, and owners on Expedition Portal call them interference engines. One owner quotes the manual at 105,000 miles for 2001–2006 Monteros. If there's no proof, budget for a belt, tensioner, pulley, water pump and seals; owners quoted $700 at an independent shop to about $1,400 at a dealer.
  2. 02
    Test the brakes cold, and listen for the boosterThird-gen trucks use a hydraulic brake booster (HBB) with an accumulator. Recalls covered the accumulator in the US (01V254001), Australia and Hong Kong. Listen for an ABS-pump noise or tone, and check for a hard pedal on the first stop after the truck has been parked. One 2002 owner paid about $2,000 to replace the whole HBB system.
  3. 03
    Look under it for oil and ATF cooler leaksOwners on Expedition Portal chase leaks from the engine-oil and transmission-cooler hoses where they run along the frame and join the oil pan and cooler. Bring a Trailer's 2001 Limited had its oil-cooler lines replaced before sale.
  4. 04
    Cycle the Super Select leverGo through 2H, 4H, 4HLc and 4LLc on loose ground. CarsGuide's expert says a flashing 4WD light on these trucks usually points to the front-diff actuation system: solenoids, vacuum lines, actuator or diff sensor. BaT's 2001 Limited needed new transfer-case switches.
  5. 05
    Feel for driveline clunkAustralian NM owners report a clunk when going from off-throttle to on-throttle. One dealer replaced the diff centre and drive rods, which only helped a little. Owners say it didn't get worse with mileage, but factor it into the price.
  6. 06
    Check rear ride heightSeveral NM/NP owners on ProductReview say the rear sags with age, and an ExplorOz owner scraped the side steps and towbar until he fitted a 40 mm ARB kit. Look for a nose-up stance and worn rear bushings.
  7. 07
    Identify the engine, and don't expect a ladder frameJDMBUYSELL warns buyers that there's no separate chassis, that the 4M41 uses a chain while the V6s use belts, and not to accept a common-rail claim on a 1999 diesel without proof. Check the chassis code on the plate.
  8. 08
    Run the VIN for recallsUS recalls include the brake-booster accumulator (01V254001, 2001–02), a fuel-tank leveling valve that can leak (04V319000, 2002–04), and label campaigns. In Australia, REC-003136 covers 13,488 NM/NP trucks for the same accumulator.

Spotting the real thing

US Monteros are straightforward, but imported Pajeros come in many codes and engines, and the 1999 cut-over means some '99 Pajeros are second-generation trucks.

  1. ID
    Chassis codesJDMBUYSELL lists V63W, V65W, V68W, V73W, V75W and V78W. Bring a Trailer's imports include a V65W three-door 3.5, a V68W three-door 3.2 diesel and a V75W five-door 3.5. Confirm the full code on the frame or VIN plate.
  2. ID
    Second or third generation?A 1999 Pajero can be either. Third-generation codes start V6 or V7; BaT's 1999 'Wide Exceed' with a V45 code is a second-generation truck with a separate frame.
  3. ID
    US engine from the VINOn BaT's US trucks, the 3.5 Monteros carry R as the eighth VIN character (JA4MW51R…) and the 3.8s carry S (JA4MW51S…, JA4NW31S…).
  4. ID
    Pre-facelift or faceliftMitsubishi facelifted the truck for 2003; in the US that's also when the 3.8 and M-ASTC arrived. BaT's 2003 and 2004 Limiteds list M-ASTC.
  5. ID
    Grey-import tellsRight-hand drive, a fender-mounted mirror and a km/h speedometer (BaT's 1999 V68W has a 180 km/h speedo) mark a Japanese-market truck. Ask for the import paperwork.

Open recalls follow the VIN, not the model. Run yours through NHTSA before you buy, and ask the seller for dealer records showing recall work.

Service bulletins & known fixes
Hydraulic brake booster: spilled fluid can damage the pump motor ↗Mitsubishi HBB notice (reported by JDMBUYSELL; six chassis codes)Spilled brake fluid entering the pump motor caused malfunction and weak assist. Wipe up spills, never wash them away with water, and have a warning lamp, buzzer or poor braking checked with Mitsubishi's tester.

Importing & legality

Only the long-wheelbase V6 Montero was sold new in the US. Short-wheelbase three-doors, the 3.0 V6, the 3.5 GDI and the 3.2 DI-D diesel are gray-market trucks. NHTSA's exemption covers vehicles at least 25 years old by their own manufacture date (49 CFR 591.5(i)), so as of October 2026 trucks built up to about October 2001 qualify and the rest follow month by month until the last ones in 2031. Bring a Trailer has already sold a 1999 V68W 3.2 diesel and a 2000 V65W three-door with import documents.

The EPA exemption is separate: JDMBUYSELL notes it requires at least 21 years and the original configuration, or an equivalent or newer EPA-certified engine and emissions system. State titling and emissions rules vary, and California is the hardest. Canada's 15-year threshold doesn't make a truck legal to bring into the US.

Timing belt, tensioner and water pump (6G74 3.5 / 6G75 3.8) →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 10+ hours for a first-timerSHOP COST · Owners quoted about $700 (independent) to $1,400 (dealer) for the full job
SYMPTOMSUsually none before it fails. Owners on Expedition Portal call both US V6s interference engines and quote the manual's 105,000-mile interval for 2001–2006 Monteros. On a 2001 NM, an Australian owner's tensioner disintegrated at about 110,000 km.
THE FIXFollowing an Expedition Portal write-up: drain the coolant, remove the fan and shroud (the radiator can stay in if you protect it), then the accessory belts, accessories and main bracket. Hold the crank pulley and remove it, then the timing covers. Refit the crank bolt with spacers so you can turn the engine, and set TDC on the cam and crank marks. Remove the old tensioner and pulley, fit the new belt keeping the driver's side tight (large binder clips hold it on the cam gears), and fit the new tensioner without pulling its pin. Lever the pulley into the belt until the pin slides freely, torque the pulley bolt, rotate the crank two turns, wait five minutes and check the tensioner piston protrusion against the service manual. Replace the water pump, front seals and crank bolt while you're there, and the O-ring on the pipe behind the water pump, which owners say is often missed. One owner gives the crank-pulley bolt torque as 130 lb-ft; confirm it in the manual.

Diagnose the hydraulic brake booster (HBB) and accumulator →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 1 hour to check the recall; half a day to replace partsSHOP COST · Recall work is free; one owner paid about $2,000 for a complete HBB system
SYMPTOMSA hard pedal or slow brake assist on the first stop after the truck has been parked, an ABS-pump noise, or a warning lamp or buzzer. The US, Australian and Hong Kong recalls say a faulty accumulator can leak its nitrogen charge. A 2002 Limited owner on Expedition Portal had intermittent ABS tones, then a total loss of boost and a seized pump motor that drained the battery.
THE FIXRun the VIN for NHTSA 01V254001 (US 2001–02) or Australia's REC-003136 and have any open accumulator recall done first. JDMBUYSELL relays Mitsubishi's warning that spilled brake fluid can get into the pump motor: wipe up spills and never wash fluid away with water. A warning lamp, buzzer or weak assist needs Mitsubishi's tester to diagnose. The Expedition Portal owner's dealer blamed debris in the fluid, and owners there recommend regular brake-fluid flushes as these trucks age.
TOOLS & PARTSScan tool or dealer tester · brake bleeder · DOT 3 fluid · battery charger.

Replace leaking oil and ATF cooler hoses →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–4 hoursSHOP COST · OEM hoses, copper washers and fluid
SYMPTOMSOil or ATF drips under the engine, wet hoses along the frame, a low transmission level. Owners trace leaks to the joints at the oil pan and the oil cooler, and one found a line that had chafed on the frame.
THE FIXRemove the skid plate and the two brackets that hold the hoses to the frame. Soak the fittings in penetrating oil first; one owner twisted a hose at its fitting and had to cut it off with tin snips. Fit new OEM hoses with new copper washers (don't reuse them), and protect any line that touches the frame with a split piece of old hose and zip ties. Top up or refill with the right fluids: owners insist on Mitsubishi Diaqueen SP-III in the automatic.

Cure a flashing 4WD light (Super Select II) →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–3 hours to diagnoseSHOP COST · Usually a solenoid, hose, switch or sensor
SYMPTOMSThe 4WD indicator keeps flashing after you shift, or the front axle won't engage.
THE FIXCarsGuide's expert says it's 'likely to be a problem with the actuation system for the front diff' and lists the solenoids, vacuum line, actuator and diff sensor. Start with the cheap parts: check every vacuum hose to the front-diff actuator for cracks and loose ends, test the solenoids, then the actuator and sensor. Bring a Trailer's 2001 Limited had its transfer-case switches replaced, so check those and their wiring too. Afterwards, cycle through every mode on loose ground and use 4WD regularly.

Chase the on/off-throttle driveline clunk →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2 hours to inspectSHOP COST · From a grease gun to diff mounts and bushings
SYMPTOMSA heavy clunk from the driveline when you go from off-throttle to on-throttle. NM owners on ExplorOz report it from about 50,000–60,000 km, and say Mitsubishi called it a known issue with no fix at the time.
THE FIXRule out the simple causes first. Grease the tailshaft slip joint (one ExplorOz contributor found a dry slip joint caused the same clunk on another 4x4), then check the rear diff mounts, the rear subframe and control-arm bushings, and the CV joints for play. One owner's dealer replaced the diff centre and drive rods and got only a small improvement. Owners note that shifting to 4H removes the backlash, and that the clunk didn't get worse over 110,000–120,000 km.
TOOLS & PARTSJack and stands · pry bar · grease gun · flashlight.

Refresh tired rear springs, shocks and bushings →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ half a daySHOP COST · Springs, shocks and bushings, plus an alignment
SYMPTOMSA sagging rear, scraping side steps or towbar, and a floaty or clunky rear end. Several NM/NP owners say the rear sags with age, and one ExplorOz owner fixed scraping with a 40 mm ARB lift.
THE FIXSupport the body and rear subframe, remove the rear shocks, and drop the control arms enough to release the coil springs. Fit new coils matched to your load (Old Man Emu offers medium- and heavy-duty rear coils for the NM) and new shocks. Replace worn control-arm bushings while it's apart. Torque the suspension bolts at ride height, not hanging, then get a four-wheel alignment.

DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hoursSHOP COST · A starter and an hour or two

THE FIXCharge and load-test the battery and clean the terminals and earth strap first. Then disconnect the battery, remove the skid plate if fitted, unplug the solenoid lead and main cable, and unbolt the starter. Fit the new unit, torque the bolts, reconnect and test.

Asked on every forum

Pajero / Montero (Third Generation) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

About 33 inches if it's narrow. Owners on Expedition Portal run 235/85R16 and 255/85R16 (about 33 in) on stock suspension with no rubbing, and one '05 owner runs LT265/70R17. A 285/75R16 is a step too far: owners report it rubbing at full lock even with an OME lift. A veteran owner's advice is that '265/75R16 or 17 are the best fit on these rigs, even lifted.' Stock tires on BaT's 2001 Limited are 265/70R16.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL · BAT
Is it body-on-frame?

No. It's a monocoque with a frame structure welded into its floor, which Mitsubishi calls a built-in frame. Mitsubishi says the design was lighter than the old ladder frame and as stiff as its Dakar prototypes, but JDMBUYSELL warns that it's not for buyers who want a separate chassis.

SOURCE · MITSUBISHI JAPAN · JDMBUYSELL
Can I drive in 4WD on the highway?

Yes, in 4H. Super Select 4WD II's centre differential splits torque between 33:67 and 50:50 front to rear, so 4H works on pavement. 4HLc and 4LLc lock the centre for loose surfaces. The modes are switched electrically.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · MITSUBISHI JAPAN
Does it have a locker?

Not from the factory on US trucks. BaT's 2001 Limited has a limited-slip rear, and 2003-on trucks have M-ASTC traction control. ARB lists a rear Air Locker for the 9.5-inch IRS diff in 2001–2006 Monteros, and one Expedition Portal owner runs ARB lockers with 4.90 gears.

SOURCE · BAT · HURREYS · EXPEDITION PORTAL
Should I buy a 3.5 or a 3.8?

The 2001–02 3.5 is rated at 200 hp and 235 lb-ft, the 2003–06 3.8 at 215 hp and 248 lb-ft (BaT figures). The 3.8 trucks also get M-ASTC traction control, which owners rate highly. One owner says the 3.8 wants premium fuel and the 3.5 doesn't. Both are belt-driven and, owners say, interference engines.

SOURCE · BAT · EXPEDITION PORTAL
Is it the Montero that failed Consumer Reports' rollover test?

Yes. In 2001 CR rated the Montero Limited 'Not Acceptable' after its test truck tipped up on two wheels in eight of nine runs at 36.7 mph or faster in a short-course avoidance manoeuvre. Mitsubishi disputed the method and quoted NHTSA's view that CR's test had no scientific basis, and Mitsubishi quoted a static stability factor equal to a three-star NHTSA rating.

SOURCE · ROCKCRAWLER · DESIGN NEWS
How often does the timing belt need changing?

An owner quoting the manual says 105,000 miles for 2001–2006 Monteros, and owners say the engines are interference designs, so don't stretch it. Do the water pump, tensioner and seals at the same time. The 3.2 diesel uses a chain.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL · JDMBUYSELL
What transmission fluid does it take?

Owners insist on Mitsubishi Diaqueen SP-III and say other fluids cause slipping and torque-converter damage. Hyundai/Kia SP-III and Eneos SP are named as alternatives. AMSOIL lists 9.8 quarts total for the 2004 five-speed.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL · AMSOIL
What fuel economy should I expect?

CarsGuide's expert puts normal consumption at 10 to 15 L/100 km. An NP 3.2 DI-D automatic owner reports about 10.5 L/100 km around town and 11 towing a caravan. Another owner says the petrol's range drops sharply when towing.

SOURCE · CARSGUIDE · EXPLOROZ
Can I import a Japanese 3.2 diesel?

Yes, once it's 25 years old by its own build date. As of October 2026 that covers trucks built up to about October 2001, and BaT has already sold a 1999 V68W 3.2 diesel in the US. Later trucks become eligible month by month. The EPA exemption is separate and needs the original configuration.

SOURCE · 49 CFR 591.5 · JDMBUYSELL · BAT
What's a third-gen Montero worth?

Not much yet. Twenty-four third-generation Monteros and Pajeros sold on Bring a Trailer between February 2022 and August 2026 brought $3,750 to $19,777, with a median of about $12,500. Low-mileage Limiteds sit at the top.

SOURCE · BAT
Are there recalls I should check?

In the US: the hydraulic brake booster accumulator on 2001–02 trucks (01V254001), a fuel-tank leveling valve that can leak on 2002–04 trucks (04V319000), and some label campaigns. Australia's REC-003136 covers the accumulator on 13,488 NM/NP Pajeros.

SOURCE · NHTSA · VEHICLERECALLS.GOV.AU
