2nd Gen Pajero / Montero problems: 8 things to check

Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

2nd Gen Pajero / Montero problems to check before you buy

  1. Ask for the timing-belt receiptOn the V6s and the 4D56 the belt is a scheduled part, and owners say the 3.5 doesn't survive a snapped belt. Australian owners use about 100,000 km. With no proof, price in a belt, tensioner, idlers and water pump. The 4M40 uses a chain.
  2. Look hard at the V6 cooling systemCheck for milky oil, coolant loss and a temperature gauge that wanders. ExplorOz mechanics describe the V6 Pajeros as prone to head gaskets and say the heads crack. A combustion-leak test before purchase costs very little.
  3. Blue smoke on the overrunWorn valve stem seals are a known V6 complaint. One Australian owner says they 'normally need replacing at 200,000 km', and Montana Montero has a video on why Monteros burn oil. Watch the exhaust after a long idle and on the first blip of the throttle.
  4. Exercise the Super Select leverOn loose ground, go through 2H, 4H, 4HLc and 4LLc. The lights should stop flashing once each mode engages. A light that keeps flashing, or a front axle that won't engage, points to the vacuum system. Owners say the system misbehaves on trucks that rarely use 4WD.
  5. Check the steering linkageMitsubishi's pitman-arm notice covers every second-generation code, the Evolution included. Feel for play at the pitman and idler arms and inspect every boot. A cracked boot means the joint needs replacing.
  6. Measure the front ride heightTired torsion bars leave the nose low, and a bull bar makes it worse. Uneven ride height side to side or a nose-down stance means the bars need adjusting or replacing.
  7. Rust in the sills and chassisSteven Taylor's Mk2 Pajero video shows sill replacement and chassis welding on a 2.8. BaT's 1998 Montero needed its underside treated. Probe the sills, the rear chassis rails and the spring mounts.
  8. Run the VIN for recallsUS recalls include the 1998–2000 NADI driver airbag (20V035000), cracking front brake hoses on 1992–94 trucks (96V143), chafing brake lines on 1997–98 trucks (98V220) and a loose crank-pulley bolt on 1992–96 3.0s (00V311001). In Australia, NL airbag trucks are being bought back.

2nd Gen Pajero / Montero recalls and service bulletins

  • NADI driver airbag inflator ↗ 1998–2000 · NHTSA 20V035000Non-azide driver inflators can absorb moisture and rupture or under-inflate. In Australia, Mitsubishi offers to buy back affected NL Pajeros because no replacement inflator exists.
  • Crankshaft pulley bolt can loosen (3.0) ↗ 1992–1996 · NHTSA 00V311001On 3.0-litre Monteros the crank-pulley bolt can loosen and the pulley can fall off.
  • Front wheel-side brake hoses can crack ↗ 1992–1994 · NHTSA 96V143001At full lock and full suspension travel the front hoses can crack and leak fluid. 96V143002 covers Caribbean-market trucks.
  • Front lower brake hoses can crack ↗ 1994 · NHTSA 00V226001The front lower brake hoses can develop a crack, causing loss of brake fluid.
  • Front brake lines chafe through ↗ 1997–1998 · NHTSA 98V220000Front brake lines can develop pinholes from chafing on the edge of the inner-fender pass-through hole.
  • Accessory cargo mat stops third-row seats latching ↗ 1994–1998 · NHTSA 98V205000Accessory cargo mats in the third-row and cargo area can interfere with the folding third seats' latches.
  • Airbag warning labels too small ↗ 1998–2000 · NHTSA 04V428000The bilingual removable airbag warning labels didn't meet FMVSS 208 size requirements (Caribbean-market trucks).
  • Aftermarket Reese trailer hitch weld (equipment) ↗ 1991–1992 · NHTSA 93E023000The receiver weld on a Reese hitch can break. This is an equipment recall, not a Mitsubishi campaign.
  • Pitman-arm ball-joint inspection (Japan service notice) ↗ · Mitsubishi Japan notice (all V20 codes incl. V55W)Inspection procedure for old, hard-used trucks with integral ball-nut steering: check the stud for wear, use moly chassis grease, torque relay-rod nuts to 44 N·m, and replace boots every 5 years.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.
