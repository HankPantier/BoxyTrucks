PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Mitsubishi had built licensed Jeeps since 1953, but the licence wouldn't let it export them, and in Japan the Land Cruiser was taking its customers. In the summer of 1978 a tiny team led by Shinji Yokoyama began a 'preliminary project', because nobody would yet fund a real one. Designer Kiyoshi Honda had a Mercedes G and a Ford Bronco at the technical centre for reference. The open-top Pajero II concept was a hit at the 1979 Tokyo Motor Show, and the production Pajero went on sale in May 1982. Management planned on just 1,000 a month in Japan and 1,600 overseas.

A JEEP WITH A HEATED SEAT

The recipe was new for a Japanese 4x4: a ladder frame with double-wishbone independent front suspension on torsion bars, power steering, suspension seats and a turbo-diesel option. Interior designer Hiroshi Mizutani borrowed from aircraft cockpits and put an inclinometer in the middle of the dash, which he believes was the first three-dimensional inclinometer in any car. The short three-door came with a metal or canvas top. A five-door long wheelbase arrived in February 1983 on a 106.1-inch wheelbase, against 92.5 inches for the short one.

Engines included a 2.0 petrol (turbocharged in some markets), the 2.6 4G54 four, the 2.3 4D55 diesel and turbo-diesel, and from 1986 the 2.5 4D56 turbo-diesel. A four-speed automatic came in 1985. The 1987 Exceed added two-tone paint and heated seats. In 1988 the 3.0 6G72 V6 and an intercooled 2.5 diesel joined the range, and long-wheelbase trucks swapped leaf springs for three-link coil rear suspension. Every truck had part-time 4WD.

PARIS–DAKAR, 1983–1985

Mitsubishi worried buyers would see a comfortable 4x4 as a soft one, so it went to the Dakar. In January 1983 it entered four nearly standard canvas-top Pajeros with the 2.6 four. Andrew Cowan won the unmodified production class and finished 11th overall in a field of 385 entrants, and Georges Debussy was second in class and 14th overall. The overall winner was Jacky Ickx in a Mercedes 280 GE. In 1984 Cowan moved up to the modified class with a metal-top Pajero running the Starion's 2.6 turbo, up from 110 to 170 PS. He finished third overall behind a Porsche and Patrick Zaniroli's Range Rover.

Mitsubishi then signed Zaniroli, and for 1985 built a prototype with a carbon-Kevlar body, a longer wheelbase, three-link rear suspension and 225 PS from the turbo 2.6. Zaniroli and Jean Da Silva won the 22-day, 10,284 km rally from Versailles, with Cowan second, the first overall Dakar win for a Japanese manufacturer. A Toyota 60 Series was third. A privately entered Pajero won the unmodified production and marathon classes as well. Be clear what won, though: the 1985 winner was a works prototype, not a showroom truck.

MONTERO, SHOGUN AND DODGE RAIDER

The name became a problem abroad. 'Pajero' comes from the Pampas cat, but in Spanish it's a crude insult, so North America, Spain and most of Latin America got the Montero. In Britain it became the Shogun. US Monteros had a 2.6 G54B four rated at 109 hp and 142 lb-ft, and later the 3.0 6G72 V6 at 143 hp and 168 lb-ft. They came with automatic locking front hubs, an inclinometer pod and a suspension driver's seat.

Chrysler sold the short three-door as the Dodge Raider from 1987 to 1989, alongside the Ram 50 and the new Dakota, and never offered the five-door. The 2.6 was standard and the V6, with a 'V6' grille badge, followed a year later. A base Raider listed at $12,830 in 1989, cheaper than a Pathfinder or a 4Runner. CarSalesBase counts 19,539 US sales in 1987 and fewer than 10,000 in each of the next two years, and Dodge didn't sell another compact SUV until the 2007 Nitro.

LEGACY

The second generation took over in January 1991. Mitsubishi had underestimated the first: domestic sales started at 670 a month and reached 3,000 a month by the end, and Mitsubishi's designers credit it with starting Japan's RV boom. Hyundai built a licensed version as the Galloper from 1991 to 2003. Mitsubishi also built about 150 yellow short-wheelbase Camel Trophy Editions with the 4D56. The Japan Automotive Hall of Fame named the 1982 Pajero a Historic Car in November 2023, and designer Kiyoshi Honda says the Middle East called it the 'Mercedes-Benz of the Desert'.

SIDEBAR · THE DODGE THAT WASN'T

The Raider: a Pajero with a Dodge badge

The Raider was Mitsubishi-built and Chrysler-sold. It was a Pajero three-door with Dodge badges, covered by Chrysler's 3-year/36,000-mile warranty. The Truth About Cars says Dodge aimed it at the Nissan Pathfinder XE and the Toyota 4Runner, and undercut both on price. Owners added two-tone paint, stripes, bull bars and chrome wheels.

Today Raiders change hands cheaply. On Bring a Trailer they've sold for between $5,100 and $11,300 since 2024, with the low-mileage five-speeds at the top. Parts are the same as for the Montero, so shop by the Montero's engine and year.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The first Pajero is a ladder-frame truck with double-wishbone independent front suspension on torsion bars, unusual in a 4x4 of its day, and a live rear axle. That axle sat on leaf springs at first; long-wheelbase trucks got three-link coil springs from 1988. The 4WD is part-time with freewheeling front hubs (automatic on US trucks). Engines ranged from Astron fours and turbo-diesels to the 3.0 V6 from 1988.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust: windshield frame, roof and floorsBring a Trailer's 1989 Raider showed corrosion on the bonnet, the windshield surround and the roof, and its 1988 Montero Sport had corrosion and failing clearcoat. Check the windshield frame, the roof gutters, the floors, the rear wheel arches and the chassis rails.
  2. 02
    Steering linkageEvery first-generation chassis code is on Mitsubishi's pitman-arm notice. Check for play and inspect every ball-joint boot. A cracked boot means the joint needs replacing.
  3. 03
    Listen for chain rattle on the 2.6The 4G54 runs a timing chain and a balance-shaft chain. A rattle at cold start means a chain-and-guide job, so budget for both chains.
  4. 04
    Test the hubs and 4WDEngage 4H and 4L on gravel. The automatic hubs should lock and release cleanly. A truck that's been driven in 4WD on pavement may have a stressed driveline.
  5. 05
    Check the paperwork and the odometerTwo of the BaT trucks we read about had odometer problems: a Raider with a Carfax mileage inconsistency and a Montero Sport issued 'Not Actual Mileage' titles several times. Pull a history report.
  6. 06
    Know which engine and rear suspension you're gettingA 'V6' grille badge marks the 3.0. Leaf-sprung and coil-sprung rear ends take different parts. Any diesel in the US is a later import, so check its paperwork.
  7. 07
    Run the VIN for recallsUS recalls include front seat-belt buckle buttons (95V103: 1986–91 Montero and 1987–89 Raider), throttle icing on 1989 trucks (89V030 for the Montero, 89V027 for the Raider) and a leaking oil-pressure sender on 1984 Monteros (84V098).
  8. 08
    Budget for the small stuffBaT sellers regularly list air conditioning that doesn't work, a missing headliner, and replaced fuel pumps, shocks, wheel cylinders and power-steering lines. Most are simple fixes that add up.

Spotting the real thing

Short or long, leaf or coil, four or V6, Mitsubishi or Dodge: the first generation comes in many combinations, and grey-import diesels add more.

  1. ID
    Chassis codesMitsubishi's steering notice lists the first-generation codes: L041G, L043G, L044G, L046G, L048G, L049G, L141G, L144G, L146G and L149G.
  2. ID
    Raider or MonteroThe Raider is a Dodge-badged three-door only. A 'V6' grille badge marks the 3.0 V6 Raider. Mechanically it's the same as a three-door Montero.
  3. ID
    EngineUS trucks have the 2.6 G54B SOHC four (109 hp) or the 3.0 6G72 V6 (143 hp). A diesel in the US is a later import.
  4. ID
    Rear suspensionLeaf springs until 1988; LWB trucks then got three-link coils. BaT's 1988 Montero Sport short wheelbase still runs leaves.
  5. ID
    Camel Trophy EditionAbout 150 yellow short-wheelbase Camel Trophy Editions were built with the 4D56. Wikipedia says originals carry a yellow-and-black plaque and their own chassis code, and that replicas exist.

Importing & legality

NHTSA's import exemption covers vehicles at least 25 years old, counted from each vehicle's own manufacture date (49 CFR 591.5(i)). As of October 2026 that includes every first- and second-generation Pajero, so right-hand-drive Japanese-market trucks, the 2.5 and 2.8 turbo-diesels and the Pajero Evolution can all be imported. Bring a Trailer has sold dozens of them since 2016.

The EPA exemption is separate. It requires the vehicle to be at least 21 years old and in its original configuration, or fitted with an equivalent or newer EPA-certified engine and emissions system, so keep the import paperwork. State titling and emissions rules vary, and California is the hardest. Canada's threshold is 15 years, which doesn't make a truck legal to bring into the US.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

For the hands-on owner

Common repairs

The jobs owners actually end up doing, rated for a competent home mechanic with a decent tool kit. Always confirm torque figures, fluids and procedures against the factory service manual for your VIN, and work safely — support the truck on stands, never just a jack. You do this work at your own risk; see the disclaimer. Some parts links are affiliate links.

Inspect the pitman-arm ball joint and steering boots
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hoursSHOP COST · Free to inspect; a pitman arm or ball joints if worn
SYMPTOMSWander, play at the wheel, or split ball-joint boots. Mitsubishi Japan's notice lists every first-generation code (L041G–L149G). It warns that on old, hard-used trucks with integral ball-nut steering, a corroded pitman-arm ball joint can break and cause loss of steering.
THE FIXLift the truck and rock each steering rod and arm by hand. Look for play, loose mounts, bends and missing split pins. Replace any ball joint whose boot is cracked, because water has probably got in. To check the pitman joint, loosen the relay-rod nuts at the pitman and idler arms but leave them on, break the tapers, then remove the nuts and the rod (idler end first). Push the boot up, wipe off the grease and feel the stud where the boot lip runs. A dent or catch means a new pitman arm. If it's smooth, grease the boot lip with molybdenum-disulfide chassis grease, refit, torque the nuts to 44 N·m and grease through the nipple. Mitsubishi recommends new boots every five years on trucks used off-road.
TOOLS & PARTSJack and stands · ball-joint separator · torque wrench · grease gun · molybdenum-disulfide chassis grease.

Timing and balance-shaft chains (2.6 4G54)
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A weekendSHOP COST · A two-chain kit plus gaskets
SYMPTOMSA chain rattle from the front of the engine, worst at cold start. The 4G54 has Mitsubishi's 'Silent Shaft' twin balance shafts, and aftermarket timing kits for the Montero's 4G54 include two chains, tensioners and guides.
THE FIXDrain the coolant and remove the fan, radiator, accessory belts and crank pulley, then the timing cover. Set the engine at TDC on No. 1 and note how the balance-shaft and camshaft marks line up before you remove anything. Replace both chains, the sprockets if they're worn, the guides and the tensioner as a set. Follow the factory manual's alignment marks for both the balance-shaft chain and the cam chain; misaligned balance shafts will vibrate. Use new gaskets and seals, refill and bleed the cooling system.

Set the front ride height at the torsion bars
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1 hour plus an alignmentSHOP COST · Free to adjust
SYMPTOMSA nose-down stance, or one front corner lower than the other. Like every Pajero up to 1999, the first generation has double-wishbone front suspension on torsion bars.
THE FIXWith the truck on level ground, measure the front ride height on both sides. Turn the torsion-bar adjusters evenly to raise the low side, settle the suspension and measure again. Clean and anti-seize the adjuster threads. Get an alignment afterwards. If the bars are badly sagged or you carry a heavy bar and winch, replace them rather than winding them up further.

Check the automatic locking hubs and part-time 4WD
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hours to check; longer to rebuild a hubSHOP COST · Free to check
SYMPTOMS4WD that won't engage or won't release. US trucks have automatically locking front hubs as standard. Mitsubishi says the first generation's part-time system binds ('tight-corner braking') if you use 4WD on dry pavement.
THE FIXOn loose ground, shift into 4H and drive forward until the hubs engage, then try 4L. Shift back to 2H and reverse a few metres to release the auto hubs; Mitsubishi describes the old procedure of stopping and backing about a metre. If one side won't engage, pull the hub, clean it, check the internals, and regrease it with the correct hub grease. Never run 4WD on dry pavement in this truck.

Pajero / Montero (First Generation) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

We couldn't find a clear forum consensus for the first generation. Documented examples include a BaT 1990 Montero LS on 285/70R17 tyres (about 33 inches) on aftermarket 17-inch wheels; the listing doesn't mention a lift. Most first-generation trucks run 15-inch wheels. Measure your clearance at full lock and full stuff before you buy tyres.

SOURCE · BAT
Is the Dodge Raider the same as the Montero?

Yes. It's the three-door Pajero/Montero, built by Mitsubishi and sold by Dodge from 1987 to 1989, with the 2.6 four and, from 1988, the 3.0 V6. Dodge never sold the five-door.

SOURCE · TTAC · BAT
Did the Pajero really win the Dakar?

Yes. Patrick Zaniroli and Jean Da Silva won the 1985 rally overall, with Andrew Cowan second. It was the first overall win for a Japanese manufacturer, but the winning car was a works prototype. A production-based Pajero won its class on the 1983 debut, and Cowan was third overall in 1984.

SOURCE · MITSUBISHI · WIKIPEDIA
Can I drive it in 4WD on the road?

No. The first generation has part-time 4WD with no centre differential. Mitsubishi itself names tight-corner binding on pavement as the system's weakness, and it's why Super Select was developed for the second generation. Use 4WD on loose or slippery surfaces only.

SOURCE · MITSUBISHI · JDMBUYSELL
Which engine should I look for?

In the US: the 2.6 four (109 hp) or, from 1988, the 3.0 V6 (143 hp). The V6 is the better highway engine. Japanese and European imports may have the 2.5 4D56 turbo-diesel, which got an intercooler in 1988 and uses a timing belt.

SOURCE · BAT · WIKIPEDIA
What do they sell for?

On Bring a Trailer since 2024, US Monteros and Raiders have sold for $3,850 to $24,000. Clean, original V6 LS trucks are at the top, and Raiders have sold between $5,100 and $11,300. Imported Pajeros (2.5 turbo-diesels and V6s) have sold for about $3,850 to $16,250 on BaT since 2021.

SOURCE · BAT
What does 'Pajero' mean?

The name comes from the Pampas cat, Leopardus pajeros. Mitsubishi used Montero in North America, Spain and most of Latin America because the word is a crude insult in Spanish, and Shogun in the UK.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Can I import a diesel or JDM first-generation Pajero to the US?

Yes. Every first-generation truck is now more than 25 years old, which meets NHTSA's exemption. The EPA rules are separate: the vehicle must be in original configuration (or have an equivalent EPA-certified engine). Then check your state's titling rules.

SOURCE · 49 CFR 591.5 · JDMBUYSELL
What's the gauge pod on the dash?

An inclinometer, flanked by a voltmeter and an oil-pressure gauge. Interior designer Hiroshi Mizutani modelled it on aircraft and crane controls and believes it was the first three-dimensional inclinometer in a production car.

SOURCE · MITSUBISHI CAR DESIGN TRAILS · BAT
Leaf springs or coils?

Early trucks have leaf-sprung rear axles. Long-wheelbase trucks switched to three-link coil springs in 1988; short-wheelbase US trucks such as BaT's 1988 Montero Sport kept leaves. Check under the truck before you order springs or shocks.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · BAT
