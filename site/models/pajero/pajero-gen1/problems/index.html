1st Gen Pajero / Montero problems: 8 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the Mitsubishi Pajero / Montero (First Generation), the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Read the full 1st Gen Pajero / Montero guideSpecs & fluids

1st Gen Pajero / Montero problems to check before you buy

  1. Rust: windshield frame, roof and floorsBring a Trailer's 1989 Raider showed corrosion on the bonnet, the windshield surround and the roof, and its 1988 Montero Sport had corrosion and failing clearcoat. Check the windshield frame, the roof gutters, the floors, the rear wheel arches and the chassis rails.
  2. Steering linkageEvery first-generation chassis code is on Mitsubishi's pitman-arm notice. Check for play and inspect every ball-joint boot. A cracked boot means the joint needs replacing.
  3. Listen for chain rattle on the 2.6The 4G54 runs a timing chain and a balance-shaft chain. A rattle at cold start means a chain-and-guide job, so budget for both chains.
  4. Test the hubs and 4WDEngage 4H and 4L on gravel. The automatic hubs should lock and release cleanly. A truck that's been driven in 4WD on pavement may have a stressed driveline.
  5. Check the paperwork and the odometerTwo of the BaT trucks we read about had odometer problems: a Raider with a Carfax mileage inconsistency and a Montero Sport issued 'Not Actual Mileage' titles several times. Pull a history report.
  6. Know which engine and rear suspension you're gettingA 'V6' grille badge marks the 3.0. Leaf-sprung and coil-sprung rear ends take different parts. Any diesel in the US is a later import, so check its paperwork.
  7. Run the VIN for recallsUS recalls include front seat-belt buckle buttons (95V103: 1986–91 Montero and 1987–89 Raider), throttle icing on 1989 trucks (89V030 for the Montero, 89V027 for the Raider) and a leaking oil-pressure sender on 1984 Monteros (84V098).
  8. Budget for the small stuffBaT sellers regularly list air conditioning that doesn't work, a missing headliner, and replaced fuel pumps, shocks, wheel cylinders and power-steering lines. Most are simple fixes that add up.

1st Gen Pajero / Montero recalls and service bulletins

  • Front seat-belt buckle release buttons can break (Montero) ↗ 1986–1991 · NHTSA 95V103004Red 'PRESS' release buttons can break, and the pieces can stop the buckle working properly.
  • Front seat-belt buckle release buttons can break (Dodge Raider) ↗ 1987–1989 · NHTSA 95V103003Same buckle problem on the Raider, under Chrysler's campaign.
  • Throttle valve icing (Montero) ↗ 1989 · NHTSA 89V030000The throttle valve can ice up under certain conditions.
  • Throttle blade icing (Dodge Raider) ↗ 1989 · NHTSA 89V027000The throttle blade can ice during extended highway driving below 10 °F.
  • Oil-pressure sender can leak ↗ 1984 · NHTSA 84V098000The oil-pressure sending unit on the right side of the engine can leak oil, risking engine damage.
  • Aftermarket Reese trailer hitch weld (equipment) ↗ 1987–1989 Raider · 1991 Montero · NHTSA 93E023000The receiver weld on a Reese hitch can break. This is an equipment recall, not a Mitsubishi or Chrysler campaign.
  • Pitman-arm ball-joint inspection (Japan service notice) ↗ · Mitsubishi Japan notice (L041G–L149G)Inspection procedure for trucks with integral ball-nut steering; relay-rod nuts 44 N·m; replace boots every 5 years.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.
