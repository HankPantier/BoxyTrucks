PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Suzuki showed the third-generation Jimny at the 1997 Tokyo Motor Show, and it went on sale in Japan in January 1998 as the 1.3-liter Jimny Wide. While rivals were turning small 4x4s into crossovers, Suzuki kept the separate ladder frame, the solid axles and a two-speed transfer case. It then built the thing, mostly unchanged under the skin, for twenty years. It replaced the Samurai in most markets and carried the Jimny name around the world. America was the one big market that never got it new, and Suzuki stopped selling cars in the US altogether in 2012.

KEI CAR, WIDE CAR

There are really two trucks under one badge. The JB23 is the Japanese kei car: 3,395 mm long and 1,475 mm wide, with no fender flares, small bumpers and a 658 cc turbocharged K6A three-cylinder. It reached showrooms in October 1998, months after the bigger car, because it was designed around new kei rules that hadn't yet taken effect. Everything else is the 'Wide' car, with flares, bigger bumpers and a 1.3-liter four. That's the JB33 with the 80 PS G13BB, then the JB43 with the M13A from January 2000 in Japan. Japan revived the Sierra name for the 1.3 in January 2002, and Australia adopted it in 2007.

The family spread wide. Santana Motor in Spain built the Canvas Top from 1999 to 2009, and its soft tops kept the old G13BB until 2005. Europe got the JB53 diesel, a Santana- and Suzuki-built Jimny with Renault's 1.5 K9K, from 2004 to 2011, though never in Britain or Ireland. GM Colmotores assembled a 'Chevrolet Jimny' in Bogotá from 1999, and Mazda sold the kei car as the AZ-Offroad. Brazil kept building the facelifted 1.3 until 2022, when new rules caught up with the M13A.

COILS, VACUUM AND A DRY KNUCKLE

The mechanical recipe is old-school 4x4 at three-quarter scale. Both axles are solid beams located by radius arms, and apart from the late SJ 'Coily,' this was the first Jimny on coil springs at all four corners after decades of leaves. Steering is recirculating ball. Drive is part-time with no center differential, so 4WD is for loose surfaces only. Vacuum-locking front hubs let you shift from 2WD to 4WD at up to 100 km/h (62 mph), while low range needs a stop. From about 2005 a push-button, motor-driven transfer case replaced the lever. BigJimny's owners compare the layout to a shrunken Patrol Y61, which is fair, if generous.

The weak point is where the front wheels steer. Each front hub swings on a pair of tapered kingpin bearings inside a knuckle that BigJimny bluntly calls 'poorly sealed by design.' Water and grit get in, the lower bearing wears, the halfshaft seal follows, and the result is the notorious 'death wobble' at around 45–50 mph. Unlike a Land Rover swivel, the Jimny's is meant to run dry with grease, not oil. Fresh Koyo bearings, a new seal and good waterproof grease fix it, and owners who wheel hard do this job often.

THE M13A YEARS

The engine most export Jimnys carry is the M13A, a 1,328 cc all-aluminum DOHC 16-valve four with a timing chain, so there's no belt to budget for, unlike the G13BB it replaced. The original version made about 82 PS (60 kW). The 2005–06 update added variable valve timing for 85–86 PS (63 kW) and 110 Nm at 4,100 rpm, and from 2007 every petrol Jimny used the VVT engine. BigJimny's verdict is that the G13BB and M13A are 'generally very robust' and mostly suffer sensor and electrical niggles. The gearbox paired with the VVT engine, the R72, is a different story: early boxes, roughly 2005–2008, are known for bearing failures.

Suzuki kept freshening the outside. A deeper front bumper arrived around 2006 and cost some approach angle. In 2012 a squarer grille and a fake hood scoop appeared, and late cars added ESP, TPMS and a traction-control mode that only works in low range. Production ended worldwide in 2018, except in Brazil, as Suzuki retooled for the fourth generation.

RUST, AND HOW AN AMERICAN GETS ONE

The thing that kills Jimnys isn't the drivetrain. BigJimny's wiki says every generation left the factory with 'particularly poor rust prevention,' and lists the usual spots: body and spring mounts on the chassis, factory welds, the boot floor and the floor under the rear seats, the panels behind the plastic cladding, and the cavity behind each headlamp. Its rust article shows a ten-year-old car with only 60,000 km, mostly on salted alpine roads, and a chassis rail weakened enough to keep a truck off the road. Buy the driest one you can find, then rust-proof it.

For Americans, the clock is the attraction. NHTSA lets anyone import a vehicle that is 25 or more years old, measured from its date of manufacture. That covers every 1998–2001 Jimny today: the first JB33 Wides turned 25 around the start of 2023, the first kei JB23s in late 2023 and the first M13A JB43s in 2025, and each later truck qualifies on its own 25th birthday. That's why US auctions are full of right-hand-drive JB23 turbos, while the 70 Series follows the same path a few sizes up.

SIDEBAR · THE JIMNY WITH A MAZDA BADGE

Mazda AZ-Offroad

In October 1998 Mazda launched the AZ-Offroad, a rebadged kei JB23 sold through its Autozam small-car channel, which is where the 'AZ' comes from. It used the same 658 cc K6A turbo three, rated at 64 PS, with manual four-wheel drive, auto-locking front hubs, low range and an optional automatic. It carried its own model code, JM23, and production ended in early 2014.

Because it's a JB23 underneath, parts and know-how carry straight over, and it qualifies for import under the same 25-year rule. A 2000 AZ-Offroad sold on Bring a Trailer for $9,401 in July 2025, right in the middle of the market for JB23 Jimnys from the same years.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

A separate ladder frame carries a steel body that isn't structural. Both axles are solid beams on coil springs, located by radius arms and a panhard rod. Power goes to the rear wheels, and a lever (or, from about 2005, push buttons) adds the front axle and a 2:1-ish low range through a separate transfer case. There's no center differential, and the front hubs lock by vacuum.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Crawl under it before anything elseBigJimny's buying guide calls rust 'the main issue with most Jimnys.' Look at the body and coil-spring mounts on the chassis, the factory welds and the rear crossmember. A body-on-frame truck can look fine from above while the mounts are rotting. One forum owner's 2000 Jimny failed its UK MOT on two body mounts and the rear crossmember.
  2. 02
    Pull up the boot floor and the rear seatThe rear seat bench lifts out by hand. Check the floor around the rear seat-belt anchors, the tool well above the exhaust and the joint between the rear floor and the inner arches. BigJimny says this is the most common rot-through, and it's worse on soft tops.
  3. 03
    Look behind the headlamps and the claddingThe cavity behind each headlamp is a water drain that rarely drains properly, and the plastic sill and arch cladding hides rust until it's advanced. Pop the inner arch liners if the seller will let you.
  4. 04
    Listen for a clunk pulling awayA clunk from the rear in first gear points to loose or worn radius-arm mounts. The mounting holes can wear oval and the brackets can rust through, which needs welding.
  5. 05
    Drive it at 45–50 mphSteering shake in that band is the classic Jimny 'death wobble,' usually from worn kingpin bearings. Gear oil weeping onto the swivel balls means the halfshaft seal has gone too. It's fixable, but price it in.
  6. 06
    Engage 4WD on a loose surfaceTry 4WD and low range on gravel and listen for the front hubs clicking in. Erratic engagement on lever cars means vacuum leaks. On push-button cars, flashing 4WD lights usually point to the transfer-case actuator or its wiring.
  7. 07
    Abuse the transfer case, brieflyBigJimny's test: a hard launch in first. A loud machine-gun rattle with no movement means a stretched transfer chain. Used transfer cases are cheap, so it's a bargaining point rather than a deal-breaker.
  8. 08
    Importing? Read the build plateThe 25-year exemption runs from the date of manufacture, not the model year, so check the build date before you pay. Get the export certificate and import paperwork, and check how your state titles right-hand-drive kei imports before the truck lands.

Spotting the real thing

None of these were sold new in the US, so every American truck is an import, and the code on the paperwork should match the metal.

  1. ID
    Model code and VINBigJimny's manual guide notes the model is coded in the VIN: JB23 (Japanese kei), JB33 (G13BB Wide), JB43 (M13A Wide/Sierra) and JB53 (diesel). The VIN is in the engine bay below the windscreen, on top of the dash and stamped in the chassis rail behind a rear wheel. The Mazda version is coded JM23.
  2. ID
    Kei or WideA JB23 has no fender flares and small bumpers to stay within Japan's kei-car size limits (3,395 x 1,475 mm). The Wide/Sierra has flares, larger bumpers and is 1,600 mm wide.
  3. ID
    Lever or buttonsA transfer-case lever between the gearshift and handbrake means a pre-2005 truck. Three dash buttons (2WD, 4WD, 4WD-L) mean the later electric transfer case, used on every M13A VVT Jimny.
  4. ID
    Bumper generationsBigJimny dates the fronts: first bumper 1998–2005, a deeper second bumper 2006–2012, and a shallower third bumper with a hood scoop and new grille from 2012.
  5. ID
    Diesel and 2WD odditiesA JB53 diesel has a prominent fake intake bulge on the hood. Rare 2WD-only Jimnys exist, including the export JB33 2WD (1998–2000) and the Japanese Jimny L / J2 (JB23, 2000–2002); no transfer lever on an early-interior truck is the giveaway.

Importing & legality

Suzuki never sold the third-generation Jimny new in the United States, and it stopped selling cars here altogether in 2012. Every US example is a private import. Under NHTSA's rule (49 CFR 591.5(i)), a vehicle that is 25 or more years old can be imported without meeting the federal safety standards. In the 2022 Puerto Rico seizure of a newer Jimny, DOT said age is counted 'from the date of manufacture,' so check the build date, not the model year.

As of October 2026, that means trucks built up to about October 2001. The JB33 Wide (on sale from January 1998) became eligible around the start of 2023, the kei JB23 (from October 1998) in late 2023, and the first M13A JB43s (Japan, January 2000) in 2025. The window rolls forward a month at a time, so 2002 builds qualify through 2027. The last Japanese-built trucks, from 2018, won't qualify until 2043, and Brazil's, built until 2022, not until 2047.

EPA has a separate age-based exemption in 40 CFR 85.1511 for vehicles more than 20 years past their original production year. A 25-year-old Jimny clears that age too, but the EPA declaration still has to be filed alongside NHTSA's HS-7. Most US imports are right-hand-drive Japanese kei JB23s with the 658 cc turbo, plus some European left-hand-drive JB33s and Spanish-built soft tops. Titling, inspection and emissions rules are set by each state, so check yours before you buy.

Chase down a death wobble that isn't the kingpins →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–3 hours per itemSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSWobble remains after new kingpin bearings, or a sloppy, wandering feel at speed.
THE FIXWork through BigJimny's list in order: worn tie-rod ends (one standard and one reverse thread per rod), worn radius-arm bushes (most often the chassis end of the front arms), wheel balance (beads help with chunky mud tires), and rust on the hub face behind the brake disc that stops the disc and wheel seating square. Warped discs can mimic it too.
TOOLS & PARTSTie-rod end puller · bush press or drawbolt kit · wire brush for hub faces · alignment afterward.

Fix vacuum front hubs that won't engage →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hours to diagnoseSHOP COST · Often just hose or valve parts
SYMPTOMSThe 4WD light flashes, the front hubs don't click in, or 4WD works one day and not the next. Common on trucks that rarely use 4WD.
THE FIXThe system only pulls vacuum for a few seconds, so you can't hear a leak. Pull a wheel, disconnect the two hub hoses and apply vacuum with a large syringe to each spigot in turn: the hub should click. If it does, test again at the valve block. The pipe from the intake manifold to the valves is the most common failure, and a leaky 'unlock' valve can stop the 'lock' circuit working. If you're tired of chasing it, BigJimny documents converting to manual or fixed hubs.
TOOLS & PARTS100 ml syringe · vacuum hose · basic hand tools.
WHERE TO BUY PARTSVacuum Hub Tool · BigJimny ↗Amazon ↗
Revive the push-button transfer-case actuator →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–3 hoursSHOP COST · Free if cleaning fixes it
SYMPTOMSOn 2005-on trucks, the green 4WD light flashes (one owner counted 12 flashes) with a click, the 4L light may flash too, and 4WD won't engage even though the hubs lock when tested.
THE FIXFirst rule out the hubs and check the clutch is fully down (or the auto is in neutral). Then check the plugs and wiring on the transfer case. If they're fine, remove the round tin actuator cover at the back of the transfer case. One BigJimny owner found condensation and rust inside, cleaned and dried it, and it worked perfectly afterward. Watch for two springs that fly out when the armature comes out, and set the actuator's position relative to the selector shaft per Suzuki's procedure on reassembly. Change the transfer-case oil while you're there.
TOOLS & PARTSBasic hand tools · contact cleaner · service manual for the actuator alignment procedure.

Repair rotten floors, sills and radius-arm brackets →
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ Days, with weldingSHOP COST · Parts plus fabrication labor
SYMPTOMSHoles in the boot floor or under the rear seats, rust around the seat-belt anchors, MOT or inspection failures on body mounts, or a rear clunk from a corroded arm bracket.
THE FIXCut back to clean metal before judging it: forum regulars warn it always looks worse once cleaned up. Pressed repair floors are available in the UK, and sills and radius-arm brackets come as panels. Weld in new metal, seam-seal and paint, then wax the cavities. If the chassis rail itself or the spring and body mounts are badly rotted, BigJimny warns the truck may be beyond economical repair.

Rebuild a noisy R72 gearbox →
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A weekendSHOP COST · About £700 at a shop, per BigJimny
SYMPTOMSGrinding or whining when cruising in 3rd or 4th on 2005-ish to 2008 petrol trucks, and a furry drain plug or metal chunks in the oil.
THE FIXRemove the gearbox, split the three-piece case and replace the open deep-groove bearings with metal-shielded ones, as Suzuki's later parts do. BigJimny suspects early oil starvation (some trucks also had leaking prop-shaft center caps). Keep the synchro rings, clips and needle bearings in order. The procedure is in Suzuki supplementary manual 99501-81A10-01E. Do the clutch while the box is out.

Asked on every forum

Suzuki Jimny JB23 / JB43 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

Stock on the Wide/Sierra is 205/70R15 (about 27.1 inches). BigJimny's consensus is that 215/75R15 (about 27.7 inches) is the largest that fits an unmodified truck. 235/75R15 (about 28.8 inches) wants a 2-inch lift, and forum regulars say gen 3s still need some trimming even then, though a few owners run them with minor rubbing at full flex. Anything bigger than 215 really calls for regearing, or you'll cook the clutch or the automatic's fluid.

SOURCE · BIGJIMNY
Can I legally import one to the US?

Yes, if it's at least 25 years old from its date of manufacture, which is the NHTSA exemption in 49 CFR 591.5(i). As of late 2026 that covers trucks built up to about October 2001: JB33 Wides, early JB43s and kei JB23s, plus the Mazda AZ-Offroad. Each later truck becomes eligible on its own 25th birthday. EPA has its own age-based exemption, and your state decides how it's titled.

SOURCE · eCFR · NHTSA
What's the difference between JB23, JB33, JB43 and JB53?

JB23 is the Japanese kei car with the 658 cc turbo three and no fender flares. JB33 is the export 1.3 'Wide' with the G13BB. JB43 is the M13A-engined Wide or Sierra from 2000 to 2018. JB53 is the European diesel with Renault's 1.5 K9K. All four share the 2,250 mm wheelbase.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · BIGJIMNY
Which engine should I look for?

BigJimny rates both petrol engines as robust. The G13BB uses a timing belt with a 100,000 km interval, while the M13A uses a chain that doesn't need replacing. The M13A VVT (2005–06 on) is the strongest petrol at 85–86 PS, but its R72 gearbox had bearing problems in roughly 2005–2008 trucks. Avoid the early K9K 700 diesel, whose injection pump failures can wreck the engine.

SOURCE · BIGJIMNY
Which years are the best to buy?

BigJimny doesn't name a best year, and it rates both petrol engines as robust: the M13A has a timing chain that doesn't need replacing, while the earlier G13B has a cam belt. The ones to be careful with are the early K9K 700 diesels (2003 to about 2006), which it calls statistically very troublesome, and petrols from about 2005–2008 with the early R72 gearbox, whose bearings can fail (about £700 to fix). Late trucks from about 2014 add low-range traction control. Otherwise, rust decides it.

SOURCE · BIGJIMNY
Can I leave it in 4WD on the road?

No. There's no center differential, so 4WD on dry tarmac binds the driveline and can break CV joints, and Suzuki only sells the CV joint with the halfshaft. On push-button trucks, check you haven't knocked 4WD on by accident. Use 4WD on snow, mud, gravel, wet grass and sand.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · BIGJIMNY
How fast can I shift into 4WD?

The vacuum-locking front hubs let you go from 2WD to 4WD-High at up to 100 km/h (62 mph). Low range requires stopping, and on push-button trucks the clutch must be down with the gearbox in neutral.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
How much can it tow?

A 2017 New Zealand-market Jimny Sierra was rated at 1,300 kg (2,866 lb) braked and 500 kg (1,102 lb) unbraked. Bear in mind that it only has 110 Nm of torque.

SOURCE · CAREXPERT NZ
What fuel economy should I expect?

Suzuki claimed 7.1 L/100 km combined for the 2017 manual Sierra, about 33 US mpg, from a 40-liter (10.6-gallon) tank. Bigger tires, roof racks and the 4-speed automatic all make it worse.

SOURCE · CAREXPERT NZ
Does it have lockers?

Not from the factory: both axles have open differentials, and late trucks add a traction-control mode that only works in low range. BigJimny's owners rate the ARB Air Locker as the best aftermarket option because it's selectable, while Lockright and Kaiser are cheaper automatic lockers that can misbehave on the road.

SOURCE · BIGJIMNY
Should I fill the front swivels with oil like a Land Rover?

No. BigJimny warns that Land Rover-trained mechanics sometimes try it. The Jimny's knuckle is a greased, 'dry' joint, and its swivel seal acts more as a dust shield and steering damper than an oil seal. Corroded swivel balls aren't critical either: file them smooth and paint them.

SOURCE · BIGJIMNY
Are wheel spacers OK?

Forum regulars advise against them. They shorten the life of the kingpin and wheel bearings dramatically, to as little as six months in hard off-road use according to one experienced owner.

SOURCE · BIGJIMNY
