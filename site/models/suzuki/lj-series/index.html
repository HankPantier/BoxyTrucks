PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

In April 1970 Suzuki launched the Jimny LJ10, the first four-wheel-drive kei car to go into series production. It had a ladder frame, leaf springs on rigid axles, 16-inch wheels and a two-speed transfer case, all powered by a 359 cc air-cooled two-stroke twin making 25 PS. Suzuki pitched it as a 'professional's tool' for surveyors, foresters and anyone hauling goods up mountain roads. Nearly 5,000 sold in Japan in the first year, outselling the Mitsubishi Jeep, and the line that runs through the Samurai to today's Jimny started here.

A HOPE AND A KEI LOOPHOLE

The design wasn't originally Suzuki's. Hope Motor Company built the HopeStar ON360, a doorless two-seater with a Mitsubishi two-stroke, a Mitsubishi Colt rear axle and Mitsubishi Jeep wheels. It went on sale in April 1968, but Hope sold only a handful, about 15 in Japan and 30 for export by Wikipedia's count, before passing the design to Suzuki that year.

Suzuki fitted its own engine and a new body, and it put the spare wheel inside the truck to keep the length under the 3-metre kei limit, which made the first Jimny a three-seater. As a kei car it got Japan's tax and registration breaks. Early trucks were largely hand-built by a subcontractor, but demand forced Suzuki to set up its own assembly line, and more than 6,000 sold in 1971.

WATER COOLING, THEN THREE CYLINDERS

The May 1972 LJ20 switched to the water-cooled L50 twin with 28 PS, a vertical-slat grille and a steel-roofed van body. Suzuki says it would climb a 35-degree slope, and that it finished the Mexican 1000 race against 3- to 7-litre foreign rivals after 34 hours without an accident. Left-hand drive signalled export plans. In the US, an importer called International Equipment Co. sold it as the Suzuki Brute.

Kei rules changed in 1976, so the home-market Jimny 55 (SJ10) got the 539 cc LJ50 two-stroke triple. That engine was first used in export trucks from September 1975 with 33 PS, and the export LJ50 went to Australia and, from late 1976, was assembled in New Zealand. Suzuki says the SJ10 could climb 39.7 degrees.

LJ80: FOUR STROKES AND THE WORLD

The 1977 Jimny 8, the LJ80 abroad, was Suzuki's first car with a four-stroke engine. Its 797 cc F8A four made 41 PS, which allowed taller gearing, and the track was about 100 mm wider. It was also the first Jimny registered in Japan as a small car rather than a kei car, and only 1,799 sold there, because it was really built for export. In Germany it was meant to be called the Eljot until Disney's Pete's Dragon got in the way. Australia called it the Stockman.

Metal doors arrived in 1979, along with the LJ81 pickup on a 2,200 mm wheelbase. The LJ80 was also assembled in New Zealand and Indonesia. Suzuki says it made the Suzuki name known around the world as a four-wheel-drive maker. It was replaced in 1981 by the SJ410, the first of the SJ and Samurai generation.

SIDEBAR · THE FIRST JIMNY'S STORY

HopeStar ON360: the 4x4 before the Jimny

Hope Motor Company had built three-wheeled trucks before its ON360. The ON360 used a 359 cc Mitsubishi ME24 air-cooled two-stroke with 21 PS, Daihatsu brakes, a Mitsubishi Colt 1000 rear axle and Mitsubishi Jeep wheels. Top speed was 70 km/h, or 30 km/h in four-wheel drive.

Hope bought 100 engines from Mitsubishi but built very few trucks. When Mitsubishi declined to take over production, Hope sold the design to Suzuki in 1968. Two years later it became the LJ10.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

Every LJ uses the same basic truck. A ladder frame carries the body, and rigid axles hang on semi-elliptic leaf springs at both ends. A four-speed manual feeds a separate two-speed transfer case, and the brakes are drums all round. What changed was the engine: an air-cooled two-stroke twin, then water cooling, then a two-stroke triple, and finally a four-stroke four.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust in sills, corner posts and floorsThese are 45- to 55-year-old steel bodies. LJ80 specialist LJ80.de stocks repair panels for the corner posts, sills, side panels and footwell floors, which hints at where they rot. Probe those areas and the frame before anything else. On one BaT 1972 LJ20, the battery tray had a patch panel and rust.
  2. 02
    Which LJ is it really?Engines were swapped and bodies updated. The LJ10 has an air-cooled twin and a horizontal-slat grille. The LJ20 has a water-cooled twin and a vertical grille. The LJ50 has a three-cylinder two-stroke, and the LJ80 has a four-stroke four. Count the cylinders and check the cooling system.
  3. 03
    Two-stroke healthOn LJ10, LJ20 and LJ50 trucks, a two-stroke that won't idle or smokes heavily may need a full rebuild, and engine parts are scarcer than for the LJ80. Ask what has been done and how the engine is lubricated confirm in manual.
  4. 04
    Leaf springs and bushingsSagging springs and perished bushings are normal at this age. LJ80.de still sells complete front and rear LJ80 spring sets, bushing kits and shackle plates, so it's fixable.
  5. 05
    Brakes are drums all roundEvery LJ has four-wheel drum brakes. Check for seized wheel cylinders, a leaking master cylinder and contaminated shoes before driving one anywhere.
  6. 06
    Title and import paperworkSuzuki didn't sell the LJ in the US itself; LJ20s came in through an independent importer as the Brute. Most US trucks are later imports, like the LJ20 on BaT that was sold new in Australia. Make sure the title and import paperwork match the chassis.
  7. 07
    Originality affects valueOriginal LJ10 and LJ20 trucks are rare. BaT results show restored LJ20s and early Jimnys selling at about $10,000–11,500, ahead of rougher examples.

Chassis rust: inspect, treat and wax the frame →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ A weekend to clean and treat; welding is extraSHOP COST · Materials plus labor; frame welding is a specialist bill
SYMPTOMSFlaking scale on the frame rails and crossmembers, and rust around the body mounts and spring hangers. Classic Trader's LJ 80 guide calls rust 'a defining drawback', 'especially affecting the steel body panels and chassis', and tells buyers to check the sills, floor panels and frame.
THE FIXWash the underside and scrape off old underseal so you can see the metal. Tap and probe the rails, crossmembers, spring hangers and body-mount points. Asked on every forum

Suzuki Jimny LJ series questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

We couldn't find a reliable owner consensus. Suzuki fitted 16-inch wheels to the LJ10 and 15-inch wheels to the LJ20 van. With kei-sized wheel arches and tracks around 1,100–1,200 mm, staying close to the stock size is the safe choice.

SOURCE · SUZUKI · WIKIPEDIA
What do the LJ codes mean?

LJ stands for 'Light Jeep'. In Japan the trucks were also known by chassis codes: SJ10 (Jimny 55) and SJ20 (Jimny 8). Export markets mostly used the LJ names.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Which LJ is easiest to own?

The LJ80. It has a four-stroke engine, it was the most widely exported, and specialists such as LJ80.de still stock much of it. Two-stroke LJ10, LJ20 and LJ50 trucks are rarer and more of a collector's project.

SOURCE · SUZUKI · LJ80.DE
How fast is it?

Not very. Wikipedia gives the LJ10's top speed as 75 km/h, and about 97 km/h (60 mph) for the 539 cc Jimny 55.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Was the LJ sold in the United States?

Not by Suzuki. An importer, International Equipment Co., brought in LJ20s and sold them as the Suzuki Brute. The Samurai of late 1985 was the first four-wheeled vehicle Suzuki itself sold in the US.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · MOTORTREND
How capable is it off road?

Suzuki says the LJ20 would climb a 35-degree slope and the SJ10 39.7 degrees, and that an LJ20 finished the Mexican 1000 race after 34 hours. It's light, narrow and has low range, which counts for a lot.

SOURCE · SUZUKI
What gear ratios did it have?

Wikipedia lists transfer case ratios of 2.57 low / 1.56 high or 3.01 low / 1.71 high depending on the year, and axle ratios of 4.55, 4.87 or 5.66 for the later trucks.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Where do I get parts?

For the LJ80, LJ80.de in Germany stocks body repair panels, springs, bearings, brakes and service parts, and publishes a parts catalogue. Some parts are shared with the SJ410. Two-stroke engine parts are harder to find.

SOURCE · LJ80.DE
What is an LJ worth?

Bring a Trailer sales have mostly run from about $4,300 to $11,500, with restored LJ20s and early Jimnys at the top. An LJ80 bid to $18,003 in 2023 without selling.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER
Is it a kei car?

The 359 cc and 539 cc trucks were. The 797 cc Jimny 8 (SJ20/LJ80) was the first Jimny registered as a small car rather than a kei car in Japan.

SOURCE · SUZUKI
