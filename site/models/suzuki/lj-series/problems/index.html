Suzuki LJ Jimny problems: 7 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the Suzuki Jimny LJ series, the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Suzuki LJ Jimny problems to check before you buy

  1. Rust in sills, corner posts and floorsThese are 45- to 55-year-old steel bodies. LJ80 specialist LJ80.de stocks repair panels for the corner posts, sills, side panels and footwell floors, which hints at where they rot. Probe those areas and the frame before anything else. On one BaT 1972 LJ20, the battery tray had a patch panel and rust.
  2. Which LJ is it really?Engines were swapped and bodies updated. The LJ10 has an air-cooled twin and a horizontal-slat grille. The LJ20 has a water-cooled twin and a vertical grille. The LJ50 has a three-cylinder two-stroke, and the LJ80 has a four-stroke four. Count the cylinders and check the cooling system.
  3. Two-stroke healthOn LJ10, LJ20 and LJ50 trucks, a two-stroke that won't idle or smokes heavily may need a full rebuild, and engine parts are scarcer than for the LJ80. Ask what has been done and how the engine is lubricated confirm in manual.
  4. Leaf springs and bushingsSagging springs and perished bushings are normal at this age. LJ80.de still sells complete front and rear LJ80 spring sets, bushing kits and shackle plates, so it's fixable.
  5. Brakes are drums all roundEvery LJ has four-wheel drum brakes. Check for seized wheel cylinders, a leaking master cylinder and contaminated shoes before driving one anywhere.
  6. Title and import paperworkSuzuki didn't sell the LJ in the US itself; LJ20s came in through an independent importer as the Brute. Most US trucks are later imports, like the LJ20 on BaT that was sold new in Australia. Make sure the title and import paperwork match the chassis.
  7. Originality affects valueOriginal LJ10 and LJ20 trucks are rare. BaT results show restored LJ20s and early Jimnys selling at about $10,000–11,500, ahead of rougher examples.

How to fix them

The short version of each fix, as owners and specialists describe it. Confirm specs for your VIN before you start.

Sill, corner-post and floor rust repair

Remove the seats and trim, then probe everything. Brace the door openings before cutting, cut back to clean metal and weld in the repair panels. Seam-seal and protect the new metal, and renew the frame-to-body rubbers while the body is loose.

Tools and parts: MIG welder · cut-off wheel · spot-weld drill · clamps · seam sealer

Parts links and the full guide →
Front wheel bearings and hub seals

Remove the wheel, hub and drum. Pull the old bearings and seals, clean and inspect the spindle, and pack and fit new bearings with a new hub seal set. Adjust the preload and secure the castle nut with a new split pin.

Tools and parts: Socket set · bearing packer · seal driver · drift

Parts links and the full guide →
Transfer case reseal and mounts

Drain and remove the transfer case. Replace the bearings and gaskets with the rebuild sets, fit new rubber mounts and a new shifter boot, and refill with gear oil to the manual's spec.

Tools and parts: Transmission jack · puller · seal driver · gasket scraper
