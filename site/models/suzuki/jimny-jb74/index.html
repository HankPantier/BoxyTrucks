PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Production of the fourth-generation Jimny started at Suzuki's Kosai plant on 29 May 2018, the first time Jimnys had been built there, and the truck went on sale in Japan on 5 July, the first all-new Jimny in twenty years. Its styling looks back to the LJ and SJ/Samurai generations, with flat panels, round lamps and drip rails. Underneath, Suzuki kept the ladder frame, the front-engine rear-drive layout, the part-time 4WD with a low-range transfer gear, and three-link solid axles at both ends. Then demand outran supply in almost every market it entered, and it hasn't fully caught up since.

KEI JB64 VS SIERRA JB74

As with the JB23 generation, there are two widths. The JB64 is the kei car, sold only in Japan: 3,395 mm long and 1,475 mm wide, with no fender flares, tucked-in bumpers and a turbocharged 658 cc R06A three-cylinder, in XG, XL and XC trims. The JB74 is the one the world knows. Japan calls it the Jimny Sierra (JL and JC trims), and everyone else simply calls it the Jimny. It's 1,645 mm wide with bolt-on flares and has the 1.5-liter K15B.

Both share the 2,250 mm wheelbase, a reworked ladder frame with extra cross members that Wikipedia's development notes credit with about 1.5 times the torsional rigidity, a choice of five-speed manual or four-speed automatic, and standard brake-LSD traction control. That system brakes a spinning wheel so the one opposite can keep pulling, which is useful but no substitute for a locker. Suzuki Australia quotes 210 mm of ground clearance, with approach, breakover and departure angles of 36°, 27° and 47°.

THE K15B

The K15B replaced the JB43's M13A. It's a 1,462 cc all-aluminum DOHC 16-valve four with variable valve timing, a 74 x 85 mm bore and stroke, a 10:1 compression ratio and a timing chain. In Suzuki Australia's spec it makes 75 kW (102 PS) at 6,000 rpm and 130 Nm (96 lb-ft) at 4,000 rpm. That's a real gain over the old 1.3, but it's still only 1,095 kg of truck being pulled up hills by a naturally aspirated four.

Unsealed 4X4's staff, many of them Suzuki owners, are blunt about it. The engine is 'begging for a few more killer-wasps, or even better, a turbo.' The four-speed automatic is 'way too old to be put into a vehicle in this decade,' and next to it the manual 'feels like it has an extra 30kw available.' Gearing is the other complaint: the transfer case is 'geared way too high for anywhere near sizeable tyres,' so a set of lower gears tends to arrive alongside the first set of bigger tires.

THE QUEUE

Suzuki UK's entire 2019 allocation of 1,200 cars was sold by December 2018, with waits of up to a year, and some dealers stopped taking deposits. Suzuki blamed a bottleneck at the Japanese factory. In Australia the queue was still up to twelve months in mid-2021, and in Mexico the first 1,000 reserved units sold out in three days. At home, Japanese Jimny sales went from about 11,000–16,000 a year in the old truck's last decade to 30,281 in 2019 and roughly 40,000 a year from 2021. Maruti Suzuki began building Jimnys in India in January 2021 to help supply export markets.

Europe shows the other side of the coin. At 154–178 g/km of CO2, the Jimny dragged Suzuki's fleet average well above the EU target, so the passenger car was withdrawn from the UK in 2020 and returned as a two-seat light commercial vehicle. That too was withdrawn from European sale in 2025.

FIVE DOORS, AND THE AMERICAN QUESTION

The five-door Jimny (JC74) was revealed in India in January 2023 and launched there on 7 June 2023. Its wheelbase is 340 mm longer at 2,590 mm, and it's built only at Maruti Suzuki's plant in Gurugram for export to almost 100 countries. Australia got it in December 2023 as the Jimny XL. Japan got it on 30 January 2025 as the Jimny Nomade and took about 50,000 orders in four days, against roughly 1,200 cars a month allocated for Japan. Suzuki suspended orders, apologized, raised output to about 3,300 a month and reopened the books on 30 January 2026.

For Americans, none of this is available. Suzuki left the US car market in 2012, and the JB74 was never certified here. In September 2022, CBP seized a 2022 Jimny that a US citizen had bought in Mexico and driven across the border, because DOT ruled it ineligible: it was 'less than 25 years old from the date of manufacture.' The earliest JB74s turn 25 in mid-2043. Until then, the legal way into a small Suzuki in the US is a 25-year-old JB23 or JB43.

SIDEBAR · CO2 AND THE TWO-SEAT JIMNY

How Europe turned the Jimny into a van

In July 2020 Suzuki stopped taking new UK orders for the passenger Jimny. Even its cleanest version, the manual, emitted 154 g/km of CO2, 59 g/km above the fleet average Suzuki had to hit the following year, and the WLTP figure Autocar reported at launch was 178 g/km. Every Jimny sold made Suzuki's fines bigger.

Suzuki's answer, from September 2020, was the Jimny Commercial: the same truck re-homologated as an N1 light commercial vehicle, with no rear seats, a partition behind the front seats and up to 863 liters of load space. Vans face a looser target of 147 g/km rather than 95 g/km. It cost £16,796 plus VAT and kept the ladder frame, solid axles and ALLGRIP PRO low range. Wikipedia records it being withdrawn from European sale in 2025.

How it’s built

The fourth generation keeps the classic Jimny layout: a separate ladder frame, now with extra cross members, and rigid axles front and rear on coil springs, each located by three links. Drive is rear-wheel by default, with a part-time 4WD system Suzuki calls ALLGRIP PRO adding the front axle and a low range. There's no center differential, and traction control brakes spinning wheels instead of locking diffs.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Check the recall recordEU Safety Gate lists three Jimny recalls: fuel pumps whose resin impellers could swell and stall the engine (cars built 9 May to 22 November 2018, then a second campaign for 12 November 2018 to 7 September 2019), and door wiring harnesses that could break and stop the side and curtain airbags deploying (cars built to 2 December 2020). Ask a dealer to run the VIN.
  2. 02
    Get underneath with a flashlightBigJimny's wiki says every Jimny generation has 'particularly poor rust prevention' from the factory, and the usual spots are the chassis welds and the body and spring mounts. The JB74 is still young, but a truck from a salted-road region should show evidence of underbody protection.
  3. 03
    Drive it at 45–50 mphSteering shake in that band was the third generation's signature fault, from kingpin bearings in a poorly sealed knuckle. BigJimny expects the same on the JB74 because the design is so similar, so test-drive at that speed and rock each raised front wheel.
  4. 04
    Inspect any lift kit properlyA good Jimny lift includes caster correction, a crossmember drop, extended brake lines and often a panhard bracket. Pedders notes the factory headlight-leveling bracket can't simply be extended without risk of breaking. Missing pieces mean someone took shortcuts.
  5. 05
    Do the payload mathUnsealed puts the 3-door's factory payload at about 340 kg (750 lb), which a bull bar, winch, roof rack and two adults can use up. In Australia, check for a GVM upgrade plate or engineer's certificate if the truck is heavily accessorized.
  6. 06
    Big tires without gearing?Anything larger than 215/75R15 really needs lower gearing. Without it, BigJimny owners warn you'll quickly burn the clutch or overheat the automatic's fluid. Ask what ratio changes came with the tires.
  7. 07
    Manual or automaticUnsealed 4X4 couldn't see 'a viable reason to spend the three extra grand' on the four-speed auto in an XL. The manual is the enthusiast pick, and the autos are slower.
  8. 08
    In the US? Ask how it got hereA 2018-on Jimny can't be legally imported into the US until it's 25 years old. CBP's 2022 Puerto Rico seizure ended with an order to bring the truck into compliance, export it or destroy it. Don't buy one with 'paperwork in progress.'

Spotting the real thing

The JB74 is still new and everywhere outside North America, so identification is mostly about kei versus Sierra, 3-door versus 5-door, and the European two-seat van.

  1. ID
    JB64 or JB74The Japanese kei JB64 has no wheel-arch extensions and tucked-in bumpers, is 1,475 mm wide and uses the 658 cc R06A turbo three. The JB74 has bolt-on flares, is 1,645 mm wide and uses the 1.5 K15B.
  2. ID
    Model codeThe model codes are JB64W for the kei car, JB74W for the Jimny Sierra and global 3-door, and JC74W for the 5-door.
  3. ID
    3-door or 5-doorThe 5-door (Jimny XL in Australia, Nomade in Japan) has a 2,590 mm wheelbase versus 2,250 mm and is built only in India; the 3-door is built in Japan, and in India for some export markets.
  4. ID
    Commercial versionUK and European Jimny Commercial vans have no rear seats and a partition behind the front seats. They were homologated as light commercial vehicles.

Importing & legality

The fourth-generation Jimny has never been sold in the United States, and Suzuki hasn't sold cars here since American Suzuki's 2012 bankruptcy. It isn't certified to US safety or emissions standards, so it can't be imported as a new or late-model car.

The way in for older foreign cars, NHTSA's exemption for vehicles 25 or more years old (49 CFR 591.5(i)), runs from the date of manufacture. JB74 production began on 29 May 2018, so the earliest trucks become eligible in mid-2043, 2023-on 5-doors from 2048, and each truck on its own 25th birthday.

CBP's September 2022 Puerto Rico case shows what happens if you try sooner. A US citizen bought a 2022 Jimny in Mexico, where it has been sold new since November 2020, drove it across the border and shipped it to Puerto Rico. CBP seized it: DOT ruled it ineligible because it was 'less than 25 years old from the date of manufacture,' and EPA said it couldn't be certified because the Jimny isn't sold in the US. CBP said such vehicles 'must be brought into compliance, exported, or destroyed.' Until 2043, the legal small Suzuki for an American is a 25-year-old third-generation JB23 or JB43.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Asked on every forum

Suzuki Jimny JB74 / JB64 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

Stock is 195/80R15. The BigJimny consensus is that 215/75R15 is the biggest that suits the stock rims, though it's only slightly taller. Some Australian owners squeeze 235/75R15s on, but those exceed the stock rims' recommended limits. With a 2-inch lift, 235/75R15s fit with a little trimming of the front bumper. Go bigger and you'll want lower gearing.

SOURCE · BIGJIMNY
Can I import a new Jimny into the US?

No. It isn't certified for US safety or emissions rules, and NHTSA's exemption only covers vehicles 25 years old from their date of manufacture, which puts the earliest JB74s (built from 29 May 2018) at mid-2043. In 2022 CBP seized a 2022 Jimny bought in Mexico and taken to Puerto Rico, and ordered it brought into compliance, exported or destroyed.

SOURCE · CBP · eCFR
What's the difference between the JB64 and JB74?

The JB64 is Japan's kei car, with a 658 cc R06A turbo three, no fender flares and a width of 1,475 mm. The JB74 is the 1.5-liter K15B truck with flares and a width of 1,645 mm, sold as the Jimny Sierra in Japan and as the Jimny everywhere else. They share the 2,250 mm wheelbase and the drivetrain layout.

SOURCE · SUZUKI · WIKIPEDIA
Should I get the 5-door?

Unsealed 4X4 thinks the long-wheelbase versions of Suzuki 4x4s have always been 'far superior,' with more stability, better climbing and more room. The XL's 2,590 mm wheelbase drops breakover from 27° to 24°, but rear cargo space with the seats up goes from 85 to 211 liters.

SOURCE · UNSEALED 4X4 · SUZUKI AU
Manual or automatic?

Manual, if you can. The automatic is an old four-speed, and Unsealed says the five-speed manual feels like it has an extra 30 kW by comparison. Suzuki Australia rates the manual at 6.4 L/100 km and the auto at 6.9.

SOURCE · UNSEALED 4X4 · SUZUKI AU
How much can it tow and carry?

Suzuki Australia rates it at 1,300 kg (2,866 lb) braked and 350 kg (772 lb) unbraked, with a 1,450 kg GVM for the 3-door. Unsealed puts real payload at about 340 kg, which a bull bar and winch eat quickly. Towing anything bigger than a small trailer is 'not the droid you're looking for.'

SOURCE · SUZUKI AU · UNSEALED 4X4
Why is it so hard to get one?

Demand has run ahead of supply since launch. The UK's 2019 allocation sold out by December 2018, Australia had up to a 12-month queue in 2021, and Japan's 5-door Nomade drew about 50,000 orders in four days in 2025, forcing Suzuki to stop taking orders for a year.

SOURCE · CARSCOOPS · CARSGUIDE · RUSHLANE
Why was it pulled from Europe?

CO2. Even the cleanest manual emitted 154 g/km, far above Suzuki's EU fleet target, so the passenger car was withdrawn from the UK in 2020 and replaced by a two-seat commercial version facing a looser 147 g/km target. Wikipedia records that version leaving European sale in 2025.

SOURCE · CARSCOOPS · FLEET NEWS
Does it have lockers?

No. Both diffs are open, and 'brake LSD' traction control brakes a spinning wheel to send torque to the others. ARB lists Air Lockers for the 2019-on Jimny if you want the real thing.

SOURCE · SUZUKI · ARB
Is it safe?

Euro NCAP gave the 2018 3-door Jimny three stars, with 73% for adult occupant protection. Standard equipment varies by market and year. Australian cars now have six airbags, AEB and lane-departure warning, and the 2026 Australian 3-door update added adaptive cruise and front and rear parking sensors.

SOURCE · EURO NCAP · SUZUKI AU
What's 'little G'?

Japanese tuner DAMD sells 'little G.' body kits for the Jimny Sierra that restyle it as a miniature G-Class.

SOURCE · DAMD
