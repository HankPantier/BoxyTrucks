PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

On 14 January 2018, at the Detroit auto show, Mercedes revealed a G-Class that looked almost exactly like the old one and was almost entirely new. It was 121 mm wider, 53 mm longer and about 170 kg lighter, with a modern cabin and electric rack-and-pinion steering. The headline change was underneath: the solid front axle the G had used since 1979 gave way to independent double wishbones. The ladder frame, the low range and all three locking differentials stayed.

THE SAME SHAPE, A DIFFERENT TRUCK

The new front suspension bolts straight to the ladder frame with no subframe, and its lower wishbone mounts sit as high as possible to protect clearance. Out back, a rigid axle is located by four links per side and a Panhard rod. Mercedes quoted 9.5 inches of clearance between the axles, 27.6 inches of wading depth (up almost 4 inches), 31° approach, 30° departure and 26° breakover angles — better on every count than the solid-axle G 550 it replaced.

Low range got shorter, too: 2.93:1 against the old truck’s roughly 2.1:1, engaged at up to 25 mph. Engage it, or any locker, and the truck drops into G-Mode, which retunes the dampers, steering and throttle and holds gears. Development included the Schöckl, the mountain test course outside Graz with gradients up to 60%. The US G 550 kept the 4.0-litre twin-turbo V8 — 416 hp and 450 lb-ft — now through a nine-speed automatic and a 40:60 torque split.

SIX CYLINDERS AND HALF A MILLION Gs

The G kept selling as if nothing had changed. Graz built the 400,000th G-Class on 4 December 2020 and the 500,000th in April 2023 — a one-off in agave green with amber indicators, a tribute to a famous 1979 photo of a W460 240 GD in mid-air. The AMG G 63 carried on alongside with 577 hp.

For the 2025 model year the US G 550 traded its V8 for a turbocharged 3.0-litre inline-six with mild-hybrid assistance: 443 hp and 413 lb-ft. Aero tweaks cut the drag coefficient from 0.55 to 0.48, the cabin got twin 12.3-inch MBUX screens, and the Offroad Cockpit added a “transparent bonnet” camera view. The off-road hardware didn’t move: triple lockers, 9.5 inches of clearance and 27.6 inches of wading.

THE ELECTRIC G

Then came the one nobody expected to work. The G 580 with EQ Technology, revealed in April 2024, keeps the ladder frame, independent front and rigid rear axle, but drives each wheel with its own motor — 579 hp in total, fed by a 116 kWh battery with 200 kW DC charging. There are no mechanical lockers; torque vectoring provides ‘virtual’ ones. Each axle has a 2:1 low range, and it wades 33.5 inches — 5.9 more than the gas truck.

It also does tricks no Land Cruiser or Defender can: G-Turn spins the truck on the spot by driving the wheels on each side in opposite directions, and G-Steering pivots it around the inside rear wheel. The price is weight — more than three tonnes (Wikipedia quotes 3,085 kg) — and range: up to 294 miles on the European WLTP cycle, 239 miles on the EPA cycle. For 2025 the US got it only as the loaded Edition One.

SIDEBAR · THE 4×4² RETURNS

Portal axles, take two

In June 2022 Mercedes-AMG revived the 4×4² on the new truck: portal axles combined with independent front suspension, 585 hp from the twin-turbo V8, and 325/55R22 Pirelli tires. It went on sale for the 2023 model year at $350,050.

The numbers: 13.8 inches of ground clearance (4.3 more than a standard G), 35.8 inches of wading depth, and 41.3° approach and 36.8° departure angles. At 6,315 lb it weighs 379 lb less than the original G 500/G 550 4×4² — though it can’t match that truck’s 17.2 inches of clearance.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The second-generation G keeps the old truck’s architecture where it matters off-road and modernizes everything else. The body still sits on a ladder frame, the rear axle is still rigid, and the transfer case still has a proper low range. The front, though, is now independent, the steering is electric rack-and-pinion instead of a recirculating-ball box, and the nine-speed automatic sends 40% of the torque forward and 60% rearward.

G-MODE

Engage low range or any differential lock and G-Mode switches on automatically, whatever drive program is selected. It adjusts damping, steering and throttle response and suppresses unnecessary upshifts; a small ‘G’ lights up in the instrument cluster. The off-road screen shows gradient, inclination, compass, steering angle and locker status.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Check recall 23V09726,116 US 2019–2021 G 550s and AMG G 63s were recalled because the front-axle wiring harness can chafe on the oil cooler, killing ABS and stability control. Confirm by VIN that the inspection and sleeve or harness were done.
  2. 02
    Test lockers and low rangeSelect low range and each lock on a loose surface. The ‘G’ icon should appear and every locker indicator should confirm. Most of these trucks have never been asked.
  3. 03
    Look under the middleOwner reports point to transfer-case output-seal leaks on 2013–2023 trucks: oil spots under the centre, wet housing, whine. Check the driveshaft flanges for wear marks too.
  4. 04
    Open every doorThe doors are heavy; owners report hinge sag. Check that each door closes cleanly with that famous bank-vault clack and lines up with its neighbors.
  5. 05
    Drive it through a full shift cycleHarsh or jerky 9-speed shifts are often an adaptation issue fixable with a dealer tool — but get it documented before purchase.
  6. 06
    Exercise the screens and camerasInfotainment freezes, reboots and blank camera feeds are reported; make sure software is current and the 360° camera works — you’ll want it on the trail.
  7. 07
    Check the wheels and badgesMany trucks wear 21- or 22-inch wheels and AMG bodywork. Factory G 550 sizes are 275/55R19 or 275/50R20; budget for an 18- or 20-inch all-terrain setup, and check the VIN before paying a G 63 premium.
  8. 08
    Rear door latchesOpen and close both rear doors from inside and outside, and make sure each one latches fully. Then confirm that latch recalls 21V-353 and, for trucks that got a replacement latch, 24V-659 have been completed.

Spotting the real thing

Many G 550s are dressed up as G 63s, and the 2019-on trucks look a lot like the old ones. The VIN shows what you're looking at.

  1. ID
    VIN characters 4–8Bring a Trailer examples decoded with NHTSA's vPIC tool: 2019 G 550 = WDCYC6BJ…, 2019 AMG G 63 = WDCYC7HJ…, 2023 G 550 = W1NYC6BJ…, 2025 G 550 (inline-six) = W1NWH1AB…, 2025 G 580 = W1NWM0AB…. A 'G 63' with a YC6 VIN is a G 550 in AMG clothing.
  2. ID
    WDC versus W1NThe 2019 examples start WDC and the 2023–2025 examples start W1N. Both are Mercedes-Benz manufacturer prefixes, and vPIC decodes both as Graz-built G-Class.
  3. ID
    Model year (10th character)K = 2019, L = 2020, P = 2023, S = 2025. The V8 G 550 runs through 2024; 2025-on G 550s are inline-six mild hybrids.
  4. ID
    'W463A' isn't official'W463A' is an enthusiast and media label. Mercedes kept the W463 chassis code for the 2018-on truck, and the 2024 update has been reported as W465.
  5. ID
    Electric G 580The G 580 has no mechanical lockers. It uses per-wheel torque vectoring with a 2:1 low range in each axle's transmission.

Asked on every forum

G-Class (second generation) questions, answered

What’s the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

We couldn’t verify a firm owner consensus for the 2019-on truck. What is documented: factory G 550 sizes are 275/55R19 and 275/50R20 (about 31 in), Mercedes offered 18-inch all-terrain tires as an option, and a 2.5-inch lift kit is rated for up to 33-inch tires. On earlier Gs, about 32 inches is the commonly cited no-rub ceiling — treat it as a starting point and test-fit at full lock. [TO VERIFY FOR 2019+]

SOURCE · TIRESIZE.COM · TFLTRUCK · EUROWISE · EXPEDITION PORTAL
Does it still have three locking differentials?

Yes — centre, rear and front, 100% locks, on every gas model. The electric G 580 drops mechanical lockers in favor of per-wheel torque vectoring.

SOURCE · THE SHOP · TFLCAR
Is it still body-on-frame with a solid axle?

It’s still on a ladder frame — even the EV. The rear axle is rigid; the front is now independent double wishbones, the first time a G has had independent front suspension.

SOURCE · THE SHOP · TFLTRUCK
What’s the low-range ratio?

2.93:1, versus about 2.1:1 on the previous G. It engages at up to 25 mph with the transmission in neutral, and you can shift back to high range at up to 43 mph.

SOURCE · THE SHOP
How much ground clearance and wading depth?

9.5 inches between the axles (10.6 inches to the front axle), 27.6 inches of wading depth, and 31° / 30° / 26° approach, departure and breakover angles. The G 580 EV has 9.8 inches and wades 33.5 inches.

SOURCE · THE SHOP · SLASHGEAR · MOTOR AUTHORITY
What fuel economy should I expect?

The V8 G 550 is EPA-rated 14 mpg combined for 2019–2020; the mild-hybrid inline-six G 550 is rated 18 mpg combined for 2025–2026.

SOURCE · EPA VIA MPG BUDDY
V8 or inline-six?

The 2019–2024 G 550’s 4.0-litre twin-turbo V8 makes 416 hp and 450 lb-ft; the 2025-on 3.0-litre mild-hybrid six makes 443 hp and 413 lb-ft and is rated about 4 mpg better combined. Same lockers, same clearance.

SOURCE · TFLCAR · EPA
What can the electric G 580 do off-road?

Climb 100% grades on suitable surfaces, wade 33.5 inches, crawl in three settings, and turn on the spot with G-Turn. It has a 2:1 low range in each axle’s transmission.

SOURCE · MOTOR AUTHORITY · TFLCAR
What does “W463A” mean?

It’s an enthusiast label for the 2018-on truck. Mercedes-Benz doesn’t use it officially.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Are there recalls I should know about?

Recall 23V097 covers 26,116 2019–2021 G 550s and AMG G 63s whose front-axle harness can chafe on the oil cooler and disable ABS/ESC. Check any VIN with NHTSA.

SOURCE · NHTSA VIA MOTORVERSO
Can I use the diff locks on pavement?

No. Locked diffs bind the driveline on high-grip surfaces. Use them on dirt, sand, snow or rock at low speed and unlock before tarmac.

SOURCE · BOXYTRUCKS
