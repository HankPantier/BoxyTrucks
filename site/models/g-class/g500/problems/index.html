Oil filter housing gasket

On my 2007 G500 the classic oil filter housing / oil cooler gasket leak has been my biggest issue. It's a simple, inexpensive fix; it just takes some time to complete. The video walkthrough I recommend ↗

Lockers

I'm still working through the lockers on mine. It's most likely that the system needs to be bled or the lines need to be replaced.

Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

G500 G-Wagon problems to check before you buy

  1. Test all three locking differentialsEngage each lock on a loose surface and watch the indicators. Lockers that ‘were never used’ by the last owner often don’t work — budget for the vacuum diagnosis below.
  2. Is it really an AMG?Plenty of standard Gs have been dressed as AMGs. Check the VIN and data card against the badges before paying an AMG premium.
  3. Work every window, lock and seatEspecially on 2002–04 trucks. Handsome Motors lists seat motors and controls, window regulators and motors, and door-lock actuators as the early trucks' electrical gremlins, along with the transfer-case actuator. Try every switch, every door from inside and out, and shift the transfer case.
  4. Listen at idleVibration or a chirp that changes with revs on an M113 points at the harmonic balancer — a known failure on early-2000s Mercedes V8s.
  5. Look for oil on top of the engineWetness at the front of the V points at the oil filter housing seals; oil on the coil packs and exhaust manifolds points at valve cover gaskets or breather hoses. Neither is serious if fixed early, and both are reasonable DIY jobs (see repairs).
  6. Look behind the front wheelsGear oil on the inside of the wheels or the brake calipers means leaking front axle seals — and possibly contaminated brakes.
  7. Drive it at 55–70 mphWander or steering shake at highway speed usually means a tired steering damper and worn linkage.
  8. Rust where nobody cleansCheck the tailgate’s lower edge, door bottoms, sills, rear frame tubes, inner wheel arches and around the rear bumper.
  9. A sagging headliner means waterUsually blocked sunroof drains. Water can reach the fuse box and climate actuators, so check those too.

G500 G-Wagon recalls and service bulletins

  • Front turn signals behind grille cover don't meet FMVSS 108 ↗ 2002–2008 (campaign covers 2002–2010 G-Class) · NHTSA 10V366000The grille cover over the front indicators can cut their visibility over time; dealers fit improved turn-signal units.
  • Incorrect software calibration (SCN) coding after a workshop visit ↗ 2003 · NHTSA 08V303000Wrong coding could affect fuel gauge, check-engine light and speedometer, and stop the fuel pump cutting off in a crash; dealers recode.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about G500 G-Wagon reliability

Is the G 500 reliable?

The frame, axles and V8 are tough; the age-related jobs are predictable — harmonic balancer, vacuum lines for the lockers, axle seals, steering damper, sunroof drains. A truck with records of these is a far safer buy than a low-mileage one that’s never been touched. On 2002–04 trucks, add the electrical gremlins to the list: windows, door locks, seat motors and the transfer-case actuator.
