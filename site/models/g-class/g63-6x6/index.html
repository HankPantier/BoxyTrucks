PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The G 63 AMG 6×6 is a three-axle, four-door G-Class pickup that Mercedes built in small numbers at Magna Steyr in Graz from 2013 to 2015. It takes the G 63 AMG's 5.5-litre biturbo V8 and seven-speed automatic, adds portal axles at all six corners, five locking differentials and an onboard compressor that inflates the 37-inch tyres from inside the cab, and gives it a designo leather interior. Mercedes called it 'the most spectacular cross-country vehicle of its time'. It began as a show car, but the response was strong enough that Mercedes sold slightly more than 100.

AN ARMY TRUCK WITH AN AMG ENGINE

The 6×6 was developed from a military truck. In 2007 Mercedes-Benz became the preferred tenderer for Australia's Land 121 Overlander project to replace the army's Land Rover Perenties. Australia eventually bought 2,268 G-Wagens in ten variants, from the working W461 range, including a six-wheeler. The Australian army was the first military customer for the 6×6, which Wikipedia describes as the first six-wheeled military vehicle Mercedes had built since 1941. Autocar reported that the military 6×6 used a 3.0-litre V6 diesel of up to 220 bhp and the older five-speed automatic.

MotorTrend reported that most of the civilian truck's cab, its three-axle frame and its retuned transfer case came from the Australian military G. The engine, the seven-speed and the diamond-quilted leather came from the G 63, the biturbo successor to the G 55 AMG. The new parts were 18-inch two-piece beadlock wheels, 37-inch tyres, Öhlins gas bypass shocks, stiffer springs, heavier bump stops and, most expensively, the geared portal hubs. Australian magazine 4×4 Australia credits those hubs to the German company LeTech, which designed and builds them.

FIVE LOCKERS, ONE COMPRESSOR

The transfer case has a 0.87:1 high range and a 2.16:1 low range. Torque is normally split 30:40:30 between the front, middle and rear axles, and an extra shaft drives the rearmost axle. Three dashboard switches lock five differentials in stages. The first locks the transfer case and the through-drive, the second adds both rear axles, and the third adds the front. MotorTrend gave the locked split as 31/42/27 and found, unlike on a normal G, that the locks would engage in high range. Mercedes quotes 460 mm (18.1 in) of ground clearance and a metre of wading depth. A Mercedes display board for the truck gives approach and departure angles of 50° and 45°.

The tyre system is what sets it apart. A compressor feeds four 20-litre tanks hidden in the rear arches. Switches in the overhead console set pressures separately for the front axle and the two rear axles, and Mercedes quotes 0.5 bar for sand to 1.8 bar for the road in under 20 seconds. The beadlocks let the tyres run very soft without coming off the rims. The truck weighs about 3,850 kg, and it carries two fuel tanks totalling 159 litres.

SOLD OUT, THEN COLLECTED

Autocar quoted £370,000 in the UK, making it the second most expensive Mercedes road car of the time after the SLS Electric Drive. MotorTrend calculated a little under $625,000 at 2013 exchange rates and noted that it was not sold in the United States. Mercedes declared it sold out in February 2015, and the last customer truck left Graz that May. A few right-hand-drive trucks were converted for buyers in South Africa, and Brabus built its own B63S-700 6×6 version. The 6×6 also appeared in Jurassic World.

It also led to later trucks. The G 500 4×4² put a long-wheelbase G body on a shortened 6×6 chassis with portal axles, and the Maybach G 650 Landaulet used the 4×4²'s running gear. In the US, the 6×6s on Bring a Trailer are legal only through NHTSA's Show or Display exemption, which limits them to 2,500 miles a year. Sale prices since 2020 have run from $500,000 to $1.17 million. That makes this the most expensive G-Wagen on the site to use off-road, and the one most likely to get through whatever you point it at.

SIDEBAR · BUYING ONE IN AMERICA

Show or Display, not a normal title

Mercedes never sold the 6×6 in the US. Trucks there were imported and modified for EPA compliance by G&K Automotive Conversion of Santa Ana, California, and are registered under NHTSA's Show or Display exemption. Bring a Trailer listings explain the conditions: NHTSA must approve every change of ownership, each new owner applies for a new exemption, mileage is capped at 2,500 a year, and the truck must be available for inspection until it is 25 years old.

Under the 25-year rule, the oldest 2013 trucks won't be importable without restrictions until 2038. Until then, read the exemption letter before you read the service history.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The 6×6 is built from G-Class and military G parts and assembled on the same Graz line as every other G. It has a ladder frame and three rigid portal axles. Each axle is located independently so it can articulate on its own. Five differential locks and a tyre-pressure system do the rest. The engine and gearbox are the same as in the G 63.

WHY PORTAL AXLES

In a portal axle, a gear set in each hub drops the wheel centre below the axle tube. That lifts the axle and differential housings, which are the lowest parts of a solid-axle truck, and the hub gears also multiply torque at the wheel. MotorTrend reports that Mercedes originally designed these hubs for a future armoured military G. You pay for it in extra gear oil to check at every wheel and a slight lag that you feel when pulling away from a standstill on rocks.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Factory truck or conversion?Aftermarket 6×6 conversions of ordinary G 550s, G 63s and G 65s exist and have sold on Bring a Trailer. Factory trucks sold there carry chassis numbers such as WDB4632721X…, plus overhead tyre-pressure controls and a VDO auxiliary fuel gauge. Ask for Mercedes build paperwork.
  2. 02
    US paperworkIn the US, check the NHTSA Show or Display exemption letter, the EPA conversion label and whether NHTSA has approved the transfer. A clean title alone doesn't make it legal to drive.
  3. 03
    Tyre inflation systemRun the pressures down and back up. Mercedes quotes under 20 seconds from 0.5 to 1.8 bar. Slow inflation or a pressure drop overnight points to rotary seals or lines; one Bring a Trailer truck had its CTIS seals and lines replaced.
  4. 04
    Portal hubs and sealsLook for gear oil at each of the six hubs and the pinion seals. Ask when the hubs, differentials and transfer case were last serviced. Bring a Trailer service records mention a replaced centre-axle planetary gear and pinion seal on one truck.
  5. 05
    All five lockersEngage all three stages and confirm every indicator. The locks are supposed to engage quickly, even in high range, and slow or partial engagement needs investigating.
  6. 06
    The G 63 engine underneathIt's an M157, so check the same things as on a G 63: oil consumption, coolant at the turbo lines, oil in the cam-magnet connectors and plug or coil misfires.
  7. 07
    Modifications and tunesBrabus, Weistec and other tuners have modified these trucks. Find out exactly what has changed, including engine, exhaust, suspension and tyre size, and whether the original parts come with the truck.
  8. 08
    Tyre age37-inch beadlock tyres in the right size are a special order. Read the date codes; a low-mileage truck can easily have tyres ten years old.

Spotting the real thing

Aftermarket six-wheel conversions of ordinary W463s exist and have sold publicly, so prove a truck is a factory 6×6 before paying factory-6×6 money.

  2. ID

Importing & legality

Mercedes never sold the G 63 AMG 6×6 in the United States, and MotorTrend said so at launch. Trucks in the US were imported and modified for EPA compliance by G&K Automotive Conversion of Santa Ana, California, and run under NHTSA's Show or Display exemption.

Bring a Trailer listings spell out the conditions: NHTSA must approve every change of ownership, the buyer must apply for a new Show or Display approval with current photos, mileage is limited to 2,500 a year, and the truck must be available for NHTSA inspection until it is at least 25 years old. The earliest 2013 trucks reach 25 years in 2038.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Asked on every forum

G 63 AMG 6×6 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

It comes with 37-inch tyres on 18-inch beadlocks, and Bring a Trailer trucks show 37×12.50R18 tyres. One owner went up to 38×13.50R18 Toyo Open Country M/Ts, along with KW coilovers. There's no known need for anything bigger.

SOURCE · AUTOCAR · BRING A TRAILER
How many were built?

Slightly more than 100 between 2013 and 2015, according to Wikipedia citing GT Spirit. Mercedes declared it sold out in February 2015 and delivered the last customer truck in May 2015.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · GT SPIRIT · AUTOBLOG
Is it street legal in the US?

Only with restrictions. Mercedes never sold it there. Trucks in the US were federalized by G&K Automotive Conversion and run under NHTSA's Show or Display exemption, capped at 2,500 miles a year, with NHTSA approval needed for each sale.

SOURCE · MOTORTREND · BRING A TRAILER
How many locking differentials does it have?

Five, worked by three switches: first the transfer case and through-drive, then both rear axles, then the front. Locked, MotorTrend calculated a 31/42/27 torque split.

SOURCE · NEW ATLAS · MOTORTREND
How fast does the tyre inflation work?

From 0.5 bar (sand) to 1.8 bar (road) in under 20 seconds, according to Mercedes. A compressor fills four 20-litre tanks in the rear arches, and front and rear axles are set separately from the overhead console.

SOURCE · MERCEDES-BENZ · AUTOCAR
How much ground clearance and wading depth?

460 mm (18.1 in) of ground clearance and 1,000 mm (39.4 in) of wading depth, according to Mercedes. A Mercedes display board for the truck lists 50° approach and 45° departure.

SOURCE · MERCEDES-BENZ · MOTORTREND
How heavy is it, and what does it drink?

About 3,850 kg (8,488 lb), according to Mercedes. Wikipedia lists 15.6 L/100 km (about 15 mpg US) on the European cycle. Two tanks hold 96 + 63 litres (159 L in total), and the auxiliary tank is switched on from the overhead console.

SOURCE · MERCEDES-BENZ · NEW ATLAS · WIKIPEDIA
Is it the same as the Australian Army 6×6?

No. It's derived from it. The cab, three-axle frame and transfer case come from the military G. The army truck used a 3.0-litre V6 diesel and a five-speed automatic, while the civilian truck has the AMG V8, the seven-speed, portal axles, beadlocks and tyre inflation.

SOURCE · MOTORTREND · AUTOCAR
What did it cost new?

£370,000 in the UK, according to Autocar. MotorTrend calculated a little under $625,000 at 2013 exchange rates.

SOURCE · AUTOCAR · MOTORTREND
What are they worth now?

Bring a Trailer sales since 2020 run from $500,000 (a 56k-mile 2014 in May 2026) to $1.17 million (an 8k-mile Brabus B63S-700 in 2022). Several have failed to sell at bids of $800,000–831,000.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER
How is it different from the 4×4²?

The G 500 4×4² is the four-wheel follow-up. It puts a long-wheelbase G body on a shortened 6×6 chassis with portal axles and uses a 4.0-litre V8, but it has four wheels and three lockers rather than six and five.
