G 500 4×4²

The story

The 4×4² — say it “four-by-four squared” — is what happened when Mercedes took the running gear of its six-wheeled G 63 AMG 6×6, removed an entire axle and bolted the result under an ordinary long-wheelbase G. The result stands 88 inches tall, clears 17.2 inches of rock and wades through 39.4 inches of water. Mercedes-Benz USA called it the most capable G-Class ever, and charged $225,000 to prove it.

FROM SIX WHEELS TO FOUR

It started as a show car. At the Geneva Motor Show in March 2015 Mercedes displayed the G 500 4×4² with no commitment to build it, saying production would depend on the reaction. The reaction was enthusiastic, and the truck went on sale in Europe in late 2015. Underneath it is the body of the five-door long-wheelbase W463 on a shortened version of the 6×6’s chassis.

The trick is the portal axle. A gear drop at each hub puts the wheel centre below the axle tube, so the diffs and axle housings ride far higher than on a normal G — 450 mm (17.7 in) of ground clearance in European spec, against 9.3 inches for a standard G 550. The track grew too, by 299 mm, filled out with detachable carbon-fibre wheel-arch flares and 325/55R22 tires. For the 2017 model year the truck came to America as the G 550 4×4², at $225,000 plus destination.

TWO SPRINGS PER CORNER

Each wheel gets two coil-over struts in parallel — one with fixed damping, one adjustable — and Mercedes claimed the switch between Comfort and Sport took 15 milliseconds. Two stabilizer bars help keep it upright, and the drivetrain is pure G: permanent four-wheel drive split 50:50, a low-range transfer case and three 100% differential locks. Power came from the 4.0-litre twin-turbo M176 V8, rated 416 hp and 450 lb-ft in the US, driving through a seven-speed automatic.

Mercedes-Benz USA’s numbers are absurd in the best way: 51.6° approach, 43.8° departure, a breakover of 44.6° at maximum load in the spec sheet (47.4° in the launch release), 69-inch-plus track widths, a 46.9-foot turning circle and a 6,694-lb curb weight. It returned 11 mpg city, highway and combined, according to the same sheet. Expedition Portal, not given to gushing about concept trucks, said Mercedes had knocked it “straight out of the park.”

THE END, AND WHAT CAME NEXT

With the second-generation G due in 2018, Mercedes announced it would stop taking 4×4² orders at the end of October; in Germany it had cost €231,693. Its portal axles and raised suspension lived on in the Mercedes-Maybach G 650 Landaulet, and in 2022 the idea returned as the G 63 4×4² — now with independent front suspension, 585 hp and a $350,050 sticker, and 379 lb lighter than the old truck.

Today the original is a collector’s off-roader that people actually use. Bring a Trailer regularly lists them — one 2017 G 550 4×4² with 39k miles came on Pirelli Scorpion ATRs, with plugs, a coil and transmission service in its records — and shops now build lookalikes: Houston’s Banyan Motors converted a 2000 G 500 with a LeTech dual-suspension setup, portal axles and beadlocks. Rivals? Nothing from the factory, though the new Defender and Grenadier owners will happily argue.

SIDEBAR · BY THE NUMBERS

4×4² versus a 2016 G 550

Ground clearance: 17.2 in vs 9.3 in. Wading depth: 39.4 in vs 23.6 in. Approach angle: 51.6° vs 30°. Departure: 43.8° vs 30°. Height: 88.0 in vs 76.9 in. Curb weight: 6,694 lb vs 5,724 lb. Price: $225,000 vs $119,900.

Same engine, same seven-speed, same three lockers and the same 112.2-inch wheelbase. Everything that changed is below the axle line — which is precisely where off-roaders want it to change. (All figures from Mercedes-Benz USA.)

How it’s built

The 4×4² keeps the G-Class fundamentals — ladder frame, rigid axles front and rear, full-time four-wheel drive and three lockers — and changes the geometry around them. Portal hubs lift the axle housings; a wider track and dual-spring suspension keep the tall truck stable. Mercedes-Benz USA noted that the portal design let it leave suspension geometry, steering links and driveshafts essentially unchanged from other G-Class models while reducing driveshaft load.

WHY PORTAL AXLES

On a normal axle the differential sits level with the wheel centre, so the diff housing is the lowest thing under the truck. Portal hubs add a gear set at each wheel that moves the drive down to a lower hub, raising the whole axle — and buying ground clearance without a giant lift or ever-bigger tires. The cost is weight, complexity and more gears to service.

Buying one: what to check

  1. 01
    Make sure it’s a real oneShops build 4×4² lookalikes from ordinary Gs — some, like Banyan Motors’ portal-axle G 500, are serious builds, but they aren’t factory trucks. Confirm the VIN, window sticker and data card.
  2. 02
    Price the tires before the truckFactory fitment is 325/55R22 on 9.5×22 wheels — big, heavy and not cheap. Check tread depth and date codes, and whether the spare matches.
  3. 03
    Test the adjustable dampersSwitch between Comfort and Sport on the drive. You should feel the difference immediately; a fault light or no change means a damper or control problem.
  4. 04
    Test all three lockersEngage centre, rear and front on a loose surface and watch the indicators. Trucks that never left pavement often have lazy lockers.
  5. 05
    Inspect the carbon flares and roofThe detachable carbon-fibre arch extensions and roof-mounted lights live high and wide — look for cracks, chips and scratches (one BaT example noted roof scratches).
  6. 06
    Ask for the service fileSpark plugs, ignition coils, transmission fluid and filter, brake fluid — the BaT example’s history shows the kind of records to expect. Ask specifically about portal-hub and axle oil changes.
  7. 07
    Measure your garageIt’s 88 inches tall and 86.2 inches wide with mirrors. Parking structures and automatic car washes are now hostile territory.
  8. 08
    Rust where nobody cleansIt’s still a W463: check the tailgate lower edge, door bottoms, sills, rear frame tubes and strut mounts.

The 4×4² shares its engine, gearbox and lockers with the 2016–2018 G 550, so the drivetrain basics are the same — but the portal axles add hub reduction gears that a regular G doesn’t have, and many shops have never serviced one. Baseline everything, and confirm portal-axle specs with a Mercedes dealer.

Common repairs

The jobs owners actually end up doing, rated for a competent home mechanic with a decent tool kit. Always confirm torque figures and procedures against the factory service manual for your VIN.

Asked on every forum

G 500 4×4² questions, answered

What’s the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

It doesn’t need one — the portal axles are the lift. Stock tires are 325/55R22, about 36 inches tall. When Mercedes showed the truck, it said 37×12.5×18 mud-terrains would be available for production trucks, and the Geneva show car wore 37-inch mud-terrains on beadlocks. Check caliper clearance before going to 18-inch wheels, and re-code the tire size.

SOURCE · PRACTICAL MOTORING · EXPEDITION PORTAL
Was the 4×4² sold in the US?

Yes, for the 2017 model year, as the G 550 4×4². Mercedes-Benz USA listed it at $225,000 plus a $925 destination charge, with deliveries from early 2017.

SOURCE · MBUSA · TFLCAR
How much ground clearance and wading depth?

17.24 inches at maximum load and 39.4 inches of fording depth in US spec — up 7.9 and 15.8 inches respectively over a 2016 G 550. European figures are 450 mm and 1,000 mm.

SOURCE · MBUSA · CAR MAGAZINE
What are its approach and departure angles?

51.6° approach and 43.8° departure. Mercedes quoted breakover as 47.4° in its launch release and 44.6° at maximum load in the spec sheet.

SOURCE · MBUSA
Is it just a G 63 6×6 with an axle missing?

Partly. It uses a shortened version of the 6×6’s chassis and portal running gear, but with the five-door long-wheelbase body, the G 500/G 550’s 4.0-litre twin-turbo V8 and a seven-speed automatic.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · KBB
What fuel economy does it get?

Mercedes-Benz USA’s spec sheet lists 11 mpg city, 11 highway and 11 combined. The fuel tank holds 25.4 gallons.

SOURCE · MBUSA
Will it fit in my garage?

Maybe not. It’s 88.0 inches tall, 86.2 inches wide including mirrors and 177.1 inches long, with a 46.9-foot turning circle.

SOURCE · MBUSA
How heavy is it?

6,694 lb curb weight — almost a thousand pounds more than a regular G 550.

SOURCE · MBUSA
How much can it tow?

Mercedes-Benz USA’s 2017 spec sheet listed towing as “TBA,” so check the owner’s manual and hitch rating for your specific truck rather than assuming the regular G 550’s figure.

SOURCE · MBUSA
Can I use the diff locks on pavement?

No. Locked diffs bind the driveline on grippy surfaces. Lock up on loose ground at low speed and unlock before tarmac.

SOURCE · BOXYTRUCKS
How is it different from the newer G 63 4×4²?

The 2022-on G 63 4×4² is built on the second-generation G with independent front suspension, makes 585 hp and costs $350,050. It has less clearance (13.8 in) and wading depth (35.8 in) than the original, and weighs 379 lb less.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
