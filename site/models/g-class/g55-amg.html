The story

The G 55 AMG is the G that made speed part of the G-Wagen's reputation. AMG took the ladder-framed, solid-axle W463, fitted its own 5.4-litre V8 and later a supercharger, and sold it through the regular showroom from 1999 into 2012. It still has full-time four-wheel drive, low range and three locking differentials. It also has 700 Nm of torque and a top speed limited to 210 km/h, which is about as fast as anyone should drive something with this shape.

AMG'S SECOND TRY

AMG had tuned a G before. In 1993 it turned some of the 446 500 GEs into 6.0-litre specials for individual customers. The G 55 was different because anyone could order one. Introduced in 1999, it was the most powerful G yet: 354 PS (349 hp) and 525 Nm from AMG's naturally aspirated M113 V8, with 0–100 km/h in 7.4 seconds. That made it about four seconds quicker than the 500 GE to 100 km/h.

Mercedes treated it as a regular series model rather than a limited edition. When Mercedes-Benz USA took over G distribution from Europa International for the 2002 model year, the G 55 AMG launched alongside the G 500 as the only other G-Class in American showrooms. Hagerty credits the AMG with pushing US G sales higher in the following years.

THE KOMPRESSOR YEARS

In 2004 (the 2005 model year in the US) AMG added a supercharger. The M113K, the same supercharged 5.4 used in the era's CL 55 and SL 55, made 476 PS (469 hp) and 700 Nm, cutting 0–100 km/h to about 5.6 seconds. From 2006 it was rated at 500 PS. For 2009 a new engine control unit raised it to 507 PS (500 hp), with torque unchanged.

The 2009 update also brought a three-bar grille, Kompressor lettering, polished 19-inch AMG wheels on 275/55 R19 tyres, an anthracite Alcantara headliner, designo leather, COMAND APS and a stronger ESP with hill-start assist. All G 55s used the five-speed 722.6 automatic. Some enthusiasts prefer that well-proven gearbox to the seven-speed fitted to later Gs.

HOW IT ENDED

The G 55's last US model year was 2011. For 2012 the US G-Class range was just the G 550. In 2012 AMG replaced the supercharged 5.4 with a 5.5-litre biturbo V8 and renamed the truck G 63 AMG, arriving in the US for 2013. That made the G 55 the last AMG G with a supercharger and the five-speed automatic.

Values have settled well above ordinary G 500s. autoevolution's study of 2019–2022 auctions found average Kompressor prices around $60,000 in 2021–22, with low-mileage 2011 trucks reaching $72,500 and $94,000. High-mileage 2005 and 2007 trucks sold for $27,000–40,000. For an off-roader, those cheaper, well-serviced trucks are the interesting ones. You don't need perfect paint to use the lockers.

SIDEBAR · NATURALLY ASPIRATED OR KOMPRESSOR?

Two very different G 55s

The early G 55 (2002–2004 model years in the US) uses AMG's naturally aspirated M113 5.4: 349 hp, about 400 lb-ft, 0–100 km/h in 7.4 seconds. The Kompressor (US 2005–2011) has a supercharger and an intercooler, with 469–500 hp and 516 lb-ft, and gets there in about 5.5 seconds.

For trail use the trade-off is simple. The naturally aspirated truck has fewer parts to fail: no supercharger, no intercooler pump, and less heat under the hood. The Kompressor is quicker and newer and has the later interior updates, but it needs its supplemental cooling system kept in good order. German Cars For Sale Blog found the naturally aspirated trucks commanding a big premium over a contemporary G 500 for low-mileage examples.

How it’s built

Beneath the side pipes, the G 55 is still a W463. The body sits on a separate ladder frame. Both axles are solid and coil-sprung. A two-speed transfer case drives all four wheels all the time, and there are three lockers on the dash. AMG changed the engine, the brakes, the wheels and arches, and the cabin. The driveline layout that wins off-road stayed the same.

KEEP THE KOMPRESSOR COOL

The supercharged engine cools its intake charge through an intercooler fed by an electric circulation pump. When the pump fails the engine heat-soaks and loses power, which hurts most at low speed in hot weather — exactly how a G gets driven off-road. Treat the intercooler pump as a wear item.

Buying one: what to check

  1. 01
    Is it really an AMG?Plenty of G 500s and G 550s have been dressed as AMGs, and autoevolution even describes one G 55 that was itself given a G 63 nose. Check the VIN and the data card, not the badges.
  2. 02
    Naturally aspirated or supercharged?US 2002–2004 trucks are naturally aspirated; 2005–2011 are Kompressors. Make sure the model year, VIN and engine all match the price.
  3. 03
    Heat soak and the intercooler pumpOn a Kompressor, ask when the intercooler pump was replaced. A truck that pulls hard when cold and goes flat when hot is the classic symptom.
  4. 04
    Listen at idleVibration or an RPM-dependent chirp on the M113 points to the harmonic balancer. Don't drive far on a failing one.
  5. 05
    Mounts and suspensionCommenters on German Cars For Sale Blog advise checking engine and transmission mounts and suspension wear. With 700 Nm going through them, mounts wear out faster.
  6. 06
    Test all three lockersEngage the centre, rear and front locks on loose ground. Lockers that 'were never used' by the last owner often don't work.
  7. 07
    Rust, even on the fancy oneOwners warn G 55s rust like any G, especially near the coast. Check the tailgate's lower edge, door bottoms, sills and rear frame tubes.
  8. 08
    Gear oil behind the front wheelsOil on the inner wheels or the large AMG calipers means front axle seals, and possibly contaminated brakes.

After you buy it

Baseline everything the week you buy it. The G 55 adds an AMG engine, and from 2005 a supercharger with its own low-temperature intercooler circuit, to a W463 chassis that still needs knuckle, axle and diff-lock care. Note which engine you have: 2003–04 US trucks are naturally aspirated, 2005-on are Kompressor (M113K), and several parts differ.

The jobs owners actually end up doing, rated for a competent home mechanic with a decent tool kit. Always confirm torque figures and procedures against the factory service manual for your VIN.

Harmonic balancer (M113)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 2.5 hoursSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSVibration at idle, a wobbling crank pulley, cracked or bulging rubber, chirp or rattle that changes with RPM. If the outer ring lets go you lose the belt drive.

Diff locks won't engage
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoonSHOP COST · Diagnosis-dependent
SYMPTOMSOne or all locks refuse to engage or drop out. On mid-2000s W463s the centre lock is vacuum-operated and the axle locks hydraulic, so one vacuum leak can disable the whole sequence.
THE FIXConfirm vacuum at the transfer-case hose, then work back through the pump, reservoir and switchover valve block with a tee-in gauge. Asked on every forum

G 55 AMG questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

About 33 inches on 18-inch wheels. Owners of unlifted W463s on Expedition Portal run 275/70R18 with no rubbing on sensible offsets, and 285/65R18 with a slight rub at full left lock. Fitment data lists 285/55R18 for 2004 and 2008 US G 55s, so 18s clear those brakes. One owner warns 18s won't clear G 63 brakes, so test-fit before buying 18s for a 19-inch 2009–2011 truck. Re-code the tire size so the speedometer and ESP stay accurate.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL · GOODYEAR · TIRESIZE.COM
What size are the factory tires?

285/55R18 on US trucks through 2008 (Goodyear and TireSize.com fitment data). The 2009 update moved to 275/55R19 on 9.5-inch-wide AMG wheels.

SOURCE · GOODYEAR · AUTOBLOG
Which years are supercharged?

In the US, 2002–2004 G 55s are naturally aspirated (349 hp) and 2005–2011 are Kompressors (469 hp, later 493 and 500 hp). In Europe the supercharger arrived in 2004.

SOURCE · GCFSB · WIKIPEDIA
Does it have low range and lockers?

Yes. It's a full W463 with permanent four-wheel drive, a two-speed transfer case and centre, rear and front locking differentials. AMG didn't take any of that out.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · MBWORLD
How much can it tow?

7,000 lb with a braked trailer, according to the spec data on a 2005 example. Check your owner's manual and trailer brakes.

SOURCE · GERMAN CARS FOR SALE BLOG
What fuel economy should I expect?

About 12 mpg city and 14 highway for a 2005 Kompressor, according to the spec data on a German Cars For Sale Blog listing. Bigger tires and a rack will make it worse.

SOURCE · GERMAN CARS FOR SALE BLOG
G 55 or G 550?

An MBWorld owner who drove both found the gap smaller than expected: the G 550's 382 hp feels quick, but the G 55 has power in reserve at any speed, feels tighter and is much louder. For trail use they're mechanically the same truck; the G 55 adds supercharger cooling to maintain.

SOURCE · MBWORLD
When did the G 55 end?

The last US model year was 2011. The 2012 US range was only the G 550, and the 5.5-litre biturbo G 63 AMG replaced the G 55 in 2012 (the 2013 model year in the US).

SOURCE · CARVANA · WIKIPEDIA
Is the G 55 reliable?

The frame, axles and V8 are strong. Common jobs are predictable: the intercooler pump on Kompressors, the harmonic balancer, mounts, front axle seals, the steering damper and locker vacuum lines. Owners also say the windshield behaves like a consumable. Buy on service records, not low mileage.

SOURCE · VRP · AU7O · GCFSB
What are they worth?

autoevolution's 2019–2022 auction study found Kompressor averages around $60,000 in 2021–22, with high-mileage 2005–2007 trucks at $27,000–40,000 and low-mileage 2011s up to $94,000. Deep Black and Fire Opal Red cars sold best.

SOURCE · AUTOEVOLUTION
