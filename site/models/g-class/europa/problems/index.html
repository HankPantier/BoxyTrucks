Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

Europa Gray-Market G-Wagon problems to check before you buy

  1. US VIN and Europa paperworkA real Europa truck has a US VIN and federal compliance, not a foreign VIN cleared later by an independent lab. Match the VIN, the compliance label and any Europa invoices.
  2. Which G 320 engine?1998 was the changeover year, so a G 320 can have the M104 inline-six or the M112 V6. Check the engine code, since the two have different weak points.
  3. M104 head gasketLook under the right rear of the inline-six for oil seeping from the head gasket. Owners say a proper repair, about $2,000 at a dealer in 2002, usually lasts.
  4. Front axle service historyAsk when the front knuckles and bearings were last repacked. Europa recommended every 40,000 miles; owners say it's definitely due by 75,000.
  5. Test all three lockersEngage centre, rear and front on loose ground and watch the indicators. Lockers that 'were never used' often don't work.
  6. Ignition and key systemGerman Cars For Sale Blog warns that the electronic ignition (EIS) and smart key fitted to late Europa trucks (around 2000, possibly a year or two earlier) can strand you without warning and are expensive to replace. Make sure every key starts the truck.
  7. Rust from snowy statesScott Brady's 1996 Europa truck had two corrosion spots from its time in the Northeast. Check the tailgate's lower edge, door bottoms, sills and rear frame tubes.
  8. Originality paysHagerty says enthusiasts will pay up to 30 per cent more for an all-original G. Keep the factory parts if you modify one.
