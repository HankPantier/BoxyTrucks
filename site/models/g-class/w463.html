G-Class W463

“Twenty-eight years and engines from four to twelve cylinders, all on the same ladder frame and two solid axles. It became a rapper's accessory and stayed a real off-roader the whole time. Buy one for the three lockers and keep a budget for the vacuum lines.”

The story

The W463 is the G-Class most people picture: square, upright, faintly absurd, and still built on a ladder frame with a solid axle at each end. Between 1990 and 2018 it went from a farmer's tool with a wood dash to a twin-turbo V12 AMG with more than 600 PS, a 6×6 cousin and a Maybach landaulet. Mercedes fitted it with more engine types than any other model it has built, and it never lost the three locking differentials that make it so good off-road.

FRANKFURT, 1989: THE G GETS A LIVING ROOM

For the G's tenth birthday, Mercedes showed a new version at the 1989 Frankfurt show, and production started the following April. It had a new chassis number, W463, and a new mission. Permanent four-wheel drive replaced the W460's part-time system, the diff locks became electrically switched from the middle of the dash, ABS was available, and for the first time the cabin got wood and leather. Autocar's 1991 test said permanent 4WD and ABS made it 'a much more convincing tool on the road.'

The working trucks went elsewhere. The W463 dropped the panel vans and barn doors, and they carried on in the utilitarian W461 for armies and agencies. Early W463s used carryover engines, with petrol 230 GEs and 300 GEs and diesel 250 GDs and 300 GDs. In 1994 the line was renamed the G-Class, the manual gearbox disappeared, and ventilated front discs and a driver's airbag became standard.

V8s, AMERICANS AND AMG

Mercedes started testing what the G could carry. The 1993 500 GE squeezed in a V8 for a run of 446 trucks. In 1996 an electronically controlled five-speed automatic arrived, and the 1997 update brought V6 engines and dual airbags. The G 500 made the V8 a regular-production option in 1998, and in 1999 the G 55 AMG became the most powerful G yet.

America came next, by a back door. Mercedes didn't sell the G in the US, so Europa International of New Mexico started certifying and selling them in 1993. Those Europa trucks proved the demand, Mercedes bought the distribution rights, and the G launched officially in the US with the 2002 model year. That facelift brought ESP, Brake Assist and the four-wheel electronic traction system, 4ETS.

LUXURY, LOCKERS AND A LOT OF CYLINDERS

From there the W463 kept adding power. The G 55 AMG got a supercharger in 2004, the 5.5-litre M273 V8 created the US G 550 for 2009, and in 2012 came the twin-turbo G 63 and the V12 G 65. Mercedes sold just over 100 G 63 6×6s, and the portal-axled G 500 4×4² followed in 2015. Ninety-nine Maybach G 650 Landaulets closed out the run. By the end there were 22 paint colours and engines from four to twelve cylinders, naturally aspirated, supercharged, turbo and twin-turbo.

Underneath, almost nothing changed. The frame, the solid axles and the three lockers were the same on a 2018 G 550 as on a 1990 230 GE. Hagerty quotes 36°/31° approach and departure angles, 80% grades and 30° side slopes for the G. Handsome Motors, a dealer who has owned more than 200 Gs, estimates that each truck still took roughly 100 hours of hand labour in Graz.

HOW IT ENDED

In late 2017 Mercedes announced the end of the first-generation W463. Its replacement, revealed at Detroit in January 2018, kept the shape, the ladder frame and three lockers. It also brought independent front suspension, rack-and-pinion steering and the first formal crash test of any G. The second generation is the better road car by a wide margin.

For the trail crowd, though, 2018 marks the last year a new G came with a solid front axle. That's why clean W463s, from the 1990s diesels to the last G 550s, are now bought as off-roaders as much as status symbols.

SIDEBAR · THE G THAT WOULDN'T DIE

How the US Marine Corps saved the W463

Mercedes announced that 2005 would be the G-Class's last year in America, naming the new GL-Class as its successor. It shipped 500 farewell Grand Edition G 500s and G 55 AMGs, and rumours spread that W463 production would end entirely: emissions and pedestrian-safety rules were tightening, and the military contracts were complete.

Then the US Marine Corps ordered 157 G-based Interim Fast Attack Vehicles, and enthusiasts protested loudly. On 11 November 2005 Dieter Zetsche announced the G would continue. At the 2006 Paris show Mercedes promised production through 2015, then extended Magna Steyr's contract to 2017. The 'replacement' GL is long gone, and the G is still in production.

How it’s built

Mercedes never changed the W463's hard parts. A separate ladder frame carries the body, coil springs suspend a rigid axle at each end, and the recirculating-ball steering box comes from the same family as the W460's. Drive goes to all four wheels all the time through a two-speed transfer case. The three locks are where the complexity lives: an electronic controller enforces the order (centre, then rear, then front), vacuum engages the centre lock and switches the valves, and hydraulic slave cylinders on the axles slide dog sleeves into the front and rear diffs. An auxiliary vacuum pump backs up the engine's own vacuum.

Buying one: what to check

  1. 01
    Rust, rust, rustHandsome Motors lists the places to check: the roof around the sunroof, the metal under the tail-lights and rear door, every door hinge, the rear spring perches, the windscreen gasket, the mirror bases, the 'dog-leg' cavities inside the doors and every body seam. Autocar adds the inner wheel arches and the area around the rear bumper.
  2. 02
    Test all three lockersEngage centre, rear and front in order on loose ground and watch the indicators. Lockers that 'were never used' often don't work. Handsome Motors says to have the hydraulics flushed before chasing parts; vacuum leaks are the next suspect.
  3. 03
    Drive it at 55–70 mphJudder or shaking can mean a failing propshaft (Autocar budgets £1,500 or more). Harsh 2–3 shifts, flares or speed-sensor codes on the five-speed 722.6 point to old fluid or a failing conductor plate.
  4. 04
    Early-2000s electricsHandsome Motors says 2002–04 trucks suffer seat-motor, window-regulator and door-lock-actuator gremlins, along with transfer case motor and actuator faults, and that these improved each year until 2005–06. Test every switch.
  5. 05
    A sagging headliner means waterThe 2003-on fabric headliner sags with age, and Autocar warns it can also signal water getting in. Check the sunroof drains, the rear-door hinge seals and the fuse box area for damp.
  6. 06
    Listen for a thumpCoil springs crack, especially in cold weather, and a loud thump over bumps is the sign. Autocar puts the fix at about £120 per corner.
  7. 07
    Engine-specific weak spotsM113 (G 500, early G 55): harmonic balancer and viscous fan. M273 (G 550, 2009–15): starters every 50–60k miles, cam magnets and intake swirl flaps. M176 (2016–18): a failed crankcase-vent oil separator can cause rear-main and valve-cover leaks, with $3–5k in labour for the rear main seal (Handsome Motors). M157 G 63: inspect the cooling system and water pump.
  8. 08
    Is it really an AMG?Autocar warns about standard Gs converted to look like AMGs. Check the VIN and the factory data card, not the badges.

Lockers won't engage: find the vacuum leak
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · 2–4 hoursSHOP COST · Often a few dollars in fittings; vacuum reservoir ≈$74 (Expedition Imports)
SYMPTOMSOne or more lock lights never come on, the centre lock drops out (and takes the rear with it), or locks release when engine revs fall.
THE FIXFollow the MBWorld owner who fixed his 2005 G 500. Pull the vacuum hose at the transfer case and test for vacuum there. Then work back toward the source: intake manifold, the vacuum reservoir in the front-left wheel well, then the switchover valve block on the firewall, teeing a gauge into each segment. His leak was a split plastic connector inside a rubber Y-piece under the brake booster. Replace brittle hoses and weak check valves as you go, then flush and bleed the hydraulic side.

Asked on every forum

G-Class W463 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

About 33 inches. A 275/70R18 on the factory 18s fits an unlifted G 500/G 550, according to Expedition Portal owners. JWO designed its 4-inch springs specifically to clear tires bigger than 33 inches without rubbing. Some owners squeeze in more, such as one G 500 on 315/75R16s with no lift, and offset ET63 wheels help, but 35s on stock springs will usually rub somewhere at full compression or lock.

SOURCE · OWNER CONSENSUS · EXPEDITION PORTAL · EUROWISE/JWO
Which W463 engine is the most reliable?

Handsome Motors ranks it G 500 → G 55 → G 550 → G 63 for mechanical reliability, and calls the 2002–2015 V8s the safest bets. In Europe, Autocar names the G 320 CDI diesel V6 the best all-rounder. Avoid neglected G 63s unless you've budgeted for big brakes and cooling-system work.

SOURCE · HANDSOME MOTORS · AUTOCAR
What's the low-range ratio?

2.16:1 through the two-speed transfer case.

SOURCE · AWDWIKI
How do the three lockers work, and in what order?

You press the dash buttons and a controller engages them in sequence: centre first, then rear, then front. Each one locks after the truck has rolled a short distance. Vacuum operates the centre lock and the valves; hydraulics move the front and rear locks. Unlock everything before you're back on pavement.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL · MBWORLD · AWDWIKI
Was the W463 sold in the US before 2002?

Yes, but not by Mercedes. Europa International of New Mexico federalised and sold them from 1993, and became the exclusive US importer of the G 500 with small-volume-manufacturer status. Those trucks have US VINs. Mercedes took over with the 2002 model year.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
When did the manual gearbox disappear?

In 1994, when the 200, 230 and 300 GE were dropped. From 1996 the automatic was an electronically controlled five-speed. The seven-speed came in for 2007 on the US G 500.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · HANDSOME MOTORS
Which years have ESP and traction control?

ESP, Brake Assist and the four-wheel electronic traction system (4ETS) arrived with the 2001–02 update that coincided with the official US launch. Earlier W463s have ABS but rely on the lockers alone for traction.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
How often should I service the automatic?

Toolsource cites a 40–60k-mile interval for the five-speed 722.6, using MB 236.14 ATF. Handsome Motors suggests every 40–50k miles for the seven-speed. If the history is unknown, do it now.

SOURCE · TOOLSOURCE · HANDSOME MOTORS
Are the 2016–2018 G 550s with the twin-turbo V8 a good buy?

Handsome Motors says yes. The M176 is a lower-stressed relative of AMG's M177, and by those years the early electrical gremlins were gone. Watch for a failed crankcase-vent oil separator causing rear-main-seal and valve-cover leaks. The same dealer personally recommends 3–5k-mile oil changes.

SOURCE · HANDSOME MOTORS
What does 'W463A' mean?

It's an enthusiast label for the 2018-on second-generation G, which kept the W463 chassis code. Mercedes doesn't use the term itself.

SOURCE · BOXYTRUCKS
How do I spot a fake AMG?

Check the VIN and the factory data card. Bumpers, flares, wheels and badges are easy to bolt on, and Autocar specifically warns about Gs converted to look like AMGs.

SOURCE · AUTOCAR
Can I use the diff locks on pavement?

No. Locked diffs stop the wheels turning at different speeds, so on grippy surfaces the driveline binds and the tires scrub. Use them on dirt, sand, snow or rock at low speed.

SOURCE · BOXYTRUCKS
