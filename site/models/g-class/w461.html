PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

When the G-Class split in two at the end of the 1980s, the W463 got the leather, the wood and the attention. The W461 got the work. It kept the W460's body and chassis, gained the W463's drivetrain, and spent three decades carrying soldiers, medics, foresters and border police where roads give out. It's the G-Class with the luxury removed and the useful parts kept: barn doors, panel vans, cab-chassis trucks, three locking diffs and, on military trucks, a 24-volt electrical system.

TWO Gs FROM ONE

Mercedes showed the luxurious W463 in 1989, and W460 production ended in 1991. For buyers who needed a tool rather than a status symbol, the W460 became the W461 in 1990, initially as the 230 GE and 290 GD, and the series was formally introduced in 1992. Wikipedia sums up the formula as the W460's chassis and body with the W463's powertrain, built specifically for militaries, public authorities and NGOs.

The split shows in the details. The W463 got no panel vans and no barn doors; those stayed exclusive to the W461. The W461 kept the narrow body, the plain dash and, on government trucks, 24-volt electrics. Some military trucks carried the 461 designation even earlier: the Argentine Army began using a 'model 461' in 1981.

THE WOLF AND ITS PACK

In 1992 the Bundeswehr took delivery of 12,500 'Wolf' G-Wagens. Expedition Motor Company, which restores them, describes the 250 GD Wolf as an ex-military W461. By its definition a true Wolf is a two-door convertible with a fold-down windscreen and frameless windows, and it left the factory with a diesel engine.

Other armies joined the pack. Canada ordered 1,159 G-Wagons from late 2003 as its Light Utility Vehicle Wheeled, replacing the VW Iltis, with turreted, non-turreted and military-police versions. The US Marine Corps bought 157 Interim Fast Attack Vehicles based on a modified 290. Norway, Greece and Finland ran Gs from 240 GDs to armoured 270 CDIs. Australia replaced its army Land Rovers with W461s, and a 2010 Australian tender produced a 6×6 version of the 461 range.

DIESELS THAT GOT SERIOUS

The engines started out painfully slow. The 1992 290 GD made 95 PS from a naturally aspirated 2.9-litre five-cylinder and took more than 25 seconds to reach 100 km/h. In 1998 the 290 GD Turbodiesel replaced it, with direct injection, about 120 hp, a driver's airbag and an automatic gearbox only. Common rail arrived with the G 270 CDI in 2001. For 2007 the G 280 CDI brought the 3.0-litre V6 diesel, detuned to 135 kW and 400 Nm so it could cope with poor-quality fuel.

CIVILIANS ALLOWED, BRIEFLY

For the G-Class's 30th birthday in 2009, Mercedes sold the W461 to civilians as the G 280 CDI EDITION.30 PUR. It was a long-wheelbase wagon with four hard-wearing seats, rubber floors, drain holes in the footwells, a walk-on bonnet, flexible wheel-arch flares, headlamp grilles and barn doors. It sold well enough to spawn the G 280 CDI Professional in 2009 and the G 300 CDI Professional in 2010, offered as a wagon, panel van, cabriolet and cab-chassis.

Australia, where the army had adopted the W461, got the cab-chassis in 2016 and the wagon in 2017. Civilian production ended in 2019. Military trucks continued until 2022, when the W464 replaced them; Mercedes announced it on 1 October 2021. The W461 is the bridge between the W460 and today's working Gs, and the closest thing to a new W460 anyone could buy in the 2010s.

SIDEBAR · CANNING STOCK ROUTE

The day the plain G rescued the posh ones

To demonstrate what the G-Class could do, seven of them set out from Halls Creek to Wiluna along the Canning Stock Route, one of the country's most notorious tracks. On day seven its corrugations broke the shock absorbers on six of them: five civilian G 350s and one military model.

Mercedes-Benz Australia flew replacement shocks to Perth and then by light plane to a remote Aboriginal settlement near Well 33, about 1,000 km from Wiluna. The truck that went to fetch them was the only one still running: a G-Professional, the W461.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

Under the plain body the W461 is a W460 chassis carrying W463 running gear. That means a separate ladder frame, coil-sprung solid axles front and rear, and permanent four-wheel drive through a two-speed transfer case with three individually selectable locking differentials. In late G 300 CDI Professional form, the result is a 41:1 crawl ratio and a cab-chassis that can carry more than two tonnes.

Buying one

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

What they cost

Prices move; checked recently. See live listings below.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Know which W461 you're looking atEx-military trucks such as the Bundeswehr Wolf, the civilian EDITION.30 PUR and the G 280/300 CDI Professional are all W461s, but they differ in electrics, equipment and paperwork. Check the VIN and the Mercedes data card rather than the seller's description.
  2. 02
    24-volt systemsMilitary W461s run 24 V, and parts follow suit. Expedition Imports sells separate 12 V and 24 V heater blower motors, for example. Make sure accessories, lights and chargers match the system, and that no previous owner has half-converted it.
  3. 03
    Rust, even on the 'safer' GAutocar calls the W461 a safer bet than the W460 for avoiding corrosion and costly repairs, but these are still steel bodies on a steel frame. Check the windscreen frame, door sills, door hinges, rear spring perches and the metal under the tail-lights and rear door.
  4. 04
    Cardan shaft recall (late diesels)Mercedes recalled G 280 CDI and G 300 CDI models built between 4 April 2016 and 13 September 2021: the cardan shaft can strike the clamping body, causing power loss and transmission failure. Confirm the recall work has been done.
  5. 05
    Lockers and low rangeEngage low range, then each lock in turn on loose ground. If a lock won't engage, specialists say to flush the hydraulic system first before chasing parts.
  6. 06
    Springs and shocksAutocar notes that G-Class coil springs crack, especially in cold weather, and a loud thump over bumps is the clue. Ex-fleet trucks may have had hard lives, so check the dampers and the bushings on the radius arms and Panhard rods.
  7. 07
    Set your expectations on paceA naturally aspirated 290 GD makes 95 PS and needs 25–27 seconds to reach 100 km/h. The 1998–2001 turbodiesel, the CDIs and the G 300 CDI are far happier at highway speed.

Military trucks run 24 V, and glow plugs, bulbs and blower motors must match the system. Specs vary by engine, from the 290 GD to the OM642 CDIs, so confirm capacities and fluid approvals for your VIN.

Locker won't engage
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · 2–4 hours (estimate)SHOP COST · Hoses ≈$28; slave kits ≈$143 (Expedition Imports)
SYMPTOMSLock indicator stays dark, a lock drops out under load, or one lock works and another doesn't.
THE FIXHandsome Motors, which has owned more than 200 Gs, says to have the hydraulic system flushed before anything else. Check the flex lines; on many W461s the rear locker line is paired with the front brake hose, so replace both. Bleed the system and retest. If a lock still won't move, rebuild the slave cylinder on that axle and check the indicator switch and its boot.

Springs and shocks
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hours per corner (estimate)SHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSA thump over bumps (a cracked coil), sagging ride height, a wallowing or bouncy ride, oily shock bodies. Corrugated roads kill shocks: see the Canning Stock Route sidebar.
THE FIXSupport the chassis on stands, take the axle's weight on a jack, remove the shock and sway-bar link, and lower the axle carefully until the spring can be lifted out, without overextending the brake hoses and locker lines. Asked on every forum

G-Class W461 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

The G 300 CDI Professional comes on 265/75R16s, about 31.6 inches. On narrow-body Gs, an Expedition Portal owner runs 235/85R16s (about 32 inches) on the standard steel wheels, which is a sensible no-lift ceiling. Beyond that you're into lift springs: GwagenParts' ROGUE spring listing talks about 33- and 35-inch tires, and warns that bigger tires can trigger ABS and ESP warnings.

SOURCE · 4X4 AUSTRALIA · OWNER REPORTS · GWAGENPARTS
What's the difference between a W461 and a W463?

The W461 uses the W460's narrow body and chassis with W463 running gear, plain trim, and barn doors and panel-van bodies you can't get on a W463. Military trucks also have 24-volt electrics. The W463 is the luxury civilian G with wood, leather and more electronics.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Is the 250 GD 'Wolf' a W460 or a W461?

Expedition Motor Company, which restores them, calls the 250 GD Wolf an ex-military W461, and Wikipedia lists the 1990–91 Wolf in the W461 table. The line between late military W460s and early W461s is blurry, so check the VIN and data card on the truck in front of you.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION MOTOR COMPANY · WIKIPEDIA
Could civilians buy a new W461?

Yes, in limited form. Mercedes sold the G 280 CDI EDITION.30 PUR in 2009, followed by the G 280 CDI Professional (2009) and the G 300 CDI Professional (2010–2019). In Australia the G 300 CDI Professional cab-chassis launched in 2016 at A$119,900, and the wagon followed in 2017 at A$109,900.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · WHICHCAR · 4X4 AUSTRALIA
How much can it carry and tow?

4X4 Australia's figures for the G 300 CDI Professional cab-chassis: 2,410 kg kerb weight, 4,490 kg GVM, about 2,080 kg payload and 2,210 kg towing capacity, with a 6,700 kg GCM.

SOURCE · 4X4 AUSTRALIA
Is it full-time four-wheel drive?

Yes. The G 300 CDI Professional has permanent all-wheel drive with a 50:50 split, a two-speed transfer case and three locking diffs selectable on the move. Unusually, you can unlock the centre diff even in low range.

SOURCE · WHICHCAR · 4X4 AUSTRALIA
Does it really run on 24 volts?

Military W461s do. Wikipedia lists the 24-volt system as one of the W461's defining features, and parts suppliers stock 24 V components such as blower motors. The Australian civilian Professional wagon is described as carrying two 12-volt batteries. Check what your truck actually has before wiring in accessories.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · EXPEDITION IMPORTS · WHICHCAR
Which engine should I look for?

For highway use, the 1998–2001 290 GD Turbodiesel or later: the G 270 CDI (156 PS, 400 Nm) and the OM642 V6 in the G 280/300 CDI (184 PS, 400 Nm). The naturally aspirated 290 GD (95 PS) and the 250 GD Wolf are charming and simple but very slow.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · MERCEDES-BENZ
How big is the fuel tank?

96–97 litres on the G 300 CDI Professional, depending on whether you read WhichCar or 4X4 Australia.

SOURCE · WHICHCAR · 4X4 AUSTRALIA
Why does the late W461 have a bonnet bulge?

It clears an air pump that's part of the emissions equipment, according to 4X4 Australia. The W460 doesn't have one.

SOURCE · 4X4 AUSTRALIA
Is the W461 still built?

No. Civilian production ended in 2019, and military production continued until 2022, when the W464 took over. Mercedes announced the W464 on 1 October 2021.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
