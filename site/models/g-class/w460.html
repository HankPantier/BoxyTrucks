G-Class W460

“The original Geländewagen: built for a Shah, bought by armies, blessed by a Pope and slower up a motorway on-ramp than most tractors. It is the purest G there is, and it will happily outlive you if you keep the rust and the hydraulics in check.”

The story

Every G-Class that has ever crushed a trail or a valet stand descends from this one. The W460 is a ladder frame, two coil-sprung rigid axles, a lever for the front axle and a choice of engines that ranged from leisurely to geological. Mercedes engineered it, Steyr-Daimler-Puch hand-built it in Graz, and for twelve years it was sold mainly to people who needed it: border guards, soldiers, farmers and the occasional explorer. Nothing about it was designed to impress the neighbours, which is exactly why off-roaders still want one.

A SHAH, A WOODEN MOCK-UP AND GRAZ

Development began in 1972 under a cooperation agreement between Daimler-Benz and Steyr-Daimler-Puch, pushed along by the Shah of Iran — then a major Mercedes shareholder — who pre-ordered some 20,000 military vehicles. Design studies were built in wood in 1973, Erich Ledwinka led the design, and prototypes were tested everywhere from the Arctic Circle to the Sahara. The Shah lost his throne in 1979, the year production began, and the order never happened.

The W460 was launched at a press event on the off-road proving ground in Toulon, France, and went on sale in September 1979 with three engines and five body variants on two wheelbases: 2,400 mm and 2,850 mm. In Austria, Switzerland, Yugoslavia and the COMECON countries it was badged Puch G, and in France Peugeot built a licensed military version, the P4, with its own engine and gearbox. The French Army ordered 15,000, later cut to 13,500.

BORDER GUARDS, A POPE AND THE DAKAR

Customers arrived quickly. In 1980 Germany's Federal Border Guard and riot police took delivery of short-wheelbase 230 Gs and long-wheelbase 280 GEs, and Mercedes built a Popemobile on a 230 G cabriolet for Pope John Paul II's first visit to Germany. The Argentine Army started using the military model in 1981, and in 1982 Mercedes won the Swiss Army tender.

Then it went racing. In 1983 Jacky Ickx and Claude Brasseur won the car category of the Paris–Dakar Rally in a 280 GE, which ended any argument about whether this was a real off-roader. In 1986 Porsche ran Gs as Dakar support trucks, and one, fitted with a Porsche V8, finished second overall.

SLOWLY GETTING CIVILISED

The W460 picked up comforts gradually. In 1981 came an automatic gearbox, air conditioning, an auxiliary fuel tank, headlamp grilles and a cable winch. Fuel injection arrived with the 230 GE in 1982, along with better seats, wider tyres and fender flares. From 1985 front and rear differential locks, central locking and a tachometer were standard on every W460, and by 1986 more than 50,000 G Models had been built.

The engines barely changed. The 300 GD's five-cylinder OM617 diesel was the most popular, the 280 GE's straight-six was the most powerful, and although Mercedes put turbodiesels in its saloons, it never fitted one to a W460. Mercedes didn't sell the truck in America either. Grey-market importers federalised a few in the 1980s until the 1988 Motor Vehicle Safety Compliance Act made that much harder and more expensive.

HOW IT ENDED — AND DIDN'T

At the 1989 Frankfurt show Mercedes revealed the G for the next era: permanent four-wheel drive, a wood-trimmed cabin and optional ABS. That truck became the W463, and it took the G-Class upmarket. The W460 ended production in 1991, but its job didn't. Its body and chassis carried on as the W461, a working G for armies and agencies built with W463 running gear.

That makes the W460 the last G built purely as a tool. Today it is a classic with a strong following, and Classic & Sports Car notes that a pre-1990 soft-top in show condition can make £75,000 or more. Rust-free W460s are rare, because most spent their lives in wet European winters.

SIDEBAR · OTTO

The 300 GD that drove around the world, for 26 years

In 1989 Gunther Holtorf, a former Lufthansa manager, left his job to spend 18 months exploring Africa in his 1988 G-Wagen, a 300 GD he called Otto. He didn't come home for a quarter of a century. Holtorf and his partner Christine travelled through Africa, South America, North America, Australia and Asia, mostly sleeping in the truck with the rear seats removed, and Holtorf became the first Westerner to drive in North Korea.

By the time Otto came home in 2014 it had covered roughly 560,000 miles (about 900,000 km) across 215 countries and territories. In some of them Otto was the first private car a foreigner had been allowed to bring in. It now sits in the Mercedes-Benz Museum in Stuttgart, about as good a reference as any classic truck could have.

How it’s built

The W460 is about as simple as a serious 4×4 can be. A separate steel ladder frame carries the body, and coil springs hang a rigid axle at each corner, located by radius arms and a Panhard rod. Drive goes to the rear wheels until you engage the front axle through the VG 080 transfer case, which also has low range. The front and rear locks are mechanical-hydraulic: a dash control drives a dedicated master cylinder, and slave cylinders on each axle shift the locks.

Buying one: what to check

  1. 01
    Rust first, everything else secondClassic & Sports Car says rust starts in the rear body corners. Check the inner wings, the bottom of the rear door and the shock top mounts visible in the wheel arch. Auto Bild adds the sills, the area under the windscreen, the spring mounts and the fuel tank. Most W460s lived in salty European winters, so a rot-free one costs more for good reason.
  2. 02
    Work the front-axle lever and the lockersEngage 4WD and low range, then lock the rear and front diffs on a loose surface and listen for clean engagement. On a W460 the locks are hydraulic and can take a few wheel turns to bite. A lock that never engages usually points to a tired master cylinder, slave cylinders or flex lines.
  3. 03
    Overheating historyClassic & Sports Car warns that overheating and cylinder-head warping are common, and that an old radiator is probably clogged. Check the coolant for oil, look for weeps, and budget for a new radiator if the original is still fitted.
  4. 04
    Steering box and springsLook for oil leaking from the bottom of the steering box, broken coil springs, leaking dampers and signs of hard off-road abuse. Excess play at the wheel usually means box, tie-rod or drag-link wear.
  5. 05
    Water where it shouldn't beWiper spindles, the heater plenum and the rear-door hinge seals all leak. Lift the mats and look for stains and soft metal under them.
  6. 06
    Originality and paperworkHagerty, quoting Club G-Wagen co-founder Mike Serpe, says buyers pay up to 30% more for an all-original G and that any grey-market truck needs an expert inspection. Mercedes keeps a data card for each truck listing how it left the factory, so ask for it.
  7. 07
    Know what you're buying intoA 300 GD makes 88 PS and tops out around 127 km/h (79 mph). An IH8MUD owner says even his mildly built OM617 ran out of breath around 70 mph. If you need it to keep up on the interstate, buy a different G.
Post-purchase baseline

After you buy it

Assume nothing was done. Classic & Sports Car tells buyers to ask about oil changes for the engine, gearbox, transfer box and diffs, and most W460s are now 35 to 45 years old. Change every fluid, fix the cooling system and get the locker hydraulics working before the first trail trip. Factory capacities vary by engine and gearbox, so check the manual for your truck's numbers.

For the hands-on owner

Common repairs

The jobs owners actually end up doing, rated for a competent home mechanic with a decent tool kit. Always confirm torque figures and procedures against the factory service manual for your VIN.

Front axle rebuild (bearings, kingpins, swivel seals, CVs)
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · A full weekend (estimate)SHOP COST · Kit $610 plus bronze bushing (Expedition Imports)
SYMPTOMSOil or grease weeping from the steering ball (the big swivel housing behind the hub), play at the wheel, clicking on full lock, torn CV boots.
THE FIXDo the whole corner at once, as specialists recommend, because bearings, kingpins and CVs wear together. Lift and support the axle and pull the wheel and brake caliper. Remove the hub nut and lock with the W460 hub-nut socket, then pull the hub and wheel bearings. Remove the kingpin caps and bearings, lift off the knuckle, and withdraw the axle shaft and CV. Replace the wheel bearings and seals, kingpin bearings and seals, steering-ball gasket and wipe seals, inner axle seal and bronze bushing, then repack and reassemble. Ask the supplier for written instructions, which Expedition Imports provides on request.

Diff locks won't engage (hydraulic system)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · Half a day (estimate)SHOP COST · Rebuild kits ≈$142 each (Expedition Imports)
SYMPTOMSThe dash control moves but the lock indicator never lights, locks engage then drop out, or fluid is low or weeping at an axle.
THE FIXCheck the reservoir and look for fluid at the slave cylinders on each axle and at the flex lines. On a W460 the rear locker line and front brake hose are a matched pair of flex lines, so replace aged ones together. Bleed the system, then test on loose ground. If it still won't hold, rebuild the dash master cylinder and the slave cylinders, and check the indicator switch and its rubber boot on the axle.

Steering box leak
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · Half a day (estimate)SHOP COST · Seal $12.50; bearing set $111 (Expedition Imports)
SYMPTOMSPower-steering fluid on the frame rail under the box, low reservoir, groaning on lock, or play in the steering.
THE FIXClassic & Sports Car lists steering box leaks as a standard W460 check. A likely culprit is the inexpensive sector-shaft seal at the bottom of the LS2 box. Disconnect the drag link, remove the pitman arm and replace the seal. Bad play or notchiness calls for the three-bearing set, which means removing and opening the box. While you're under there, check the steering damper and tie-rod ends.

Cooling system refresh
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 4 hours (estimate)SHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSTemperature creeping up on climbs or in traffic, weeping radiator end tanks, coolant smelling of oil. These engines warp heads when they overheat.
THE FIXReplace the radiator before it fails. An old one is likely clogged, and overheated iron-block, alloy-head engines warp their heads. Fit a new thermostat and hoses, check the fan and its shroud, and pressure-test the system. Aluminium W460 radiators are made in manual-gearbox and automatic versions; the automatic version has the transmission cooler.

Asked on every forum

G-Class W460 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

About a 32-inch 235/85R16 on the stock steel wheels. An Expedition Portal owner runs 235/85R16s on standard steel wheels on a short-wheelbase G. The original fitment was a skinny 205 R16, and many owners stay close to 215/85R16 because a naturally aspirated diesel struggles to turn a bigger tire up hills. For 33s and up, plan on lift springs.

SOURCE · OWNER REPORTS · EXPEDITION PORTAL · LONGSTONE
Is the W460 full-time four-wheel drive?

No. It's part-time: you engage the front axle by lever through the transfer case, with high and low range. Permanent 4WD arrived with the 1989–90 W463. Don't drive a W460 in 4WD on dry pavement.

SOURCE · AWDWIKI · WIKIPEDIA
Do all W460s have locking differentials?

Not all of them. Mercedes made front and rear locks standard on every W460 from 1985, so earlier trucks may not have them. Look for the locker control on the dash and the slave cylinders on the axles. A W460 never had the W463's third (centre) lock.

SOURCE · MERCEDES-BENZ G-CLASS MILESTONES
Which engine should I buy?

The 300 GD was the most popular W460 and the 280 GE the most powerful. Auto Bild names the 230 GE and the 300 GD, with their long-lived manual gearboxes, as the ones to have. The OM617 five-cylinder in the 300 GD has a reputation for reaching huge mileages, but it's slow and was never turbocharged in a W460.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · AUTO BILD
How fast will it cruise?

Not very. Factory figures for a 300 GD are 88 PS, 0–100 km/h in about 27 seconds and a 127 km/h (79 mph) top speed. An IH8MUD owner says his mildly modified OM617 ran out of power around 70 mph. A 280 GE is good for about 165 km/h.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · IH8MUD
What fuel economy should I expect?

Mercedes' cycle figures for the 300 GD run from 11.7 to 18.8 L/100 km, roughly 12.5–20 mpg US. The 280 GE is thirstier, at 14.0–22.4 L/100 km (about 10.5–17 mpg). Big tires, a roof rack and a lift make both worse.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA (FACTORY FIGURES)
Was the W460 sold in the US?

Not by Mercedes. Grey-market specialists federalised some in the 1980s until the 1988 Motor Vehicle Safety Compliance Act made it much harder. Official US sales didn't begin until the 2002 model year, and before that Europa International imported later W463s.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · HAGERTY
W460, W461 or W463 — what's the difference?

The W460 (1979–1991) is the original, with part-time 4WD and two lockers. The W461 kept the W460's body and chassis but used W463 running gear, and was built for armies, authorities and NGOs. The W463 is the civilian luxury G from 1990, with permanent 4WD, three lockers, ABS and wood trim.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Are parts hard to find?

In the US they're scarce and expensive, according to IH8MUD owners. In Europe, Auto Bild says Mercedes dealers still stock most parts at high prices and used parts are plentiful. Specialists such as Expedition Imports stock W460 axle, locker, steering and cooling parts, and some components are shared with Mercedes cars and vans.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · AUTO BILD · EXPEDITION IMPORTS
What is a W460 worth?

Classic & Sports Car's 2023 guide put a pre-1990 hardtop at £5,000 as a project, £14,000 average and £27,000+ for a show truck. Soft-tops run £12,000 / £30,000 / £75,000+. For a US example, a rusty, re-engined 1986 300 GD project sold for $13,000 on PCARMARKET in January 2022.

SOURCE · CLASSIC & SPORTS CAR · PCARMARKET
Can I use the diff locks on pavement?

No. A locked diff stops the wheels turning at different speeds, so on grippy surfaces the driveline binds and the tires scrub. Lock on dirt, sand, snow or rock at low speed, and unlock before you're back on tarmac.

SOURCE · BOXYTRUCKS
