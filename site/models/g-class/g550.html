PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The G 550 is the old G-Class at the height of its contradictions. By 2009 the boxy W463 cost north of a hundred grand, wore a navigation screen and was more likely to be photographed outside a gym than in a riverbed. Yet nothing that mattered had been diluted: a separate ladder frame, solid axles at both ends, permanent four-wheel drive with low range and three fully locking differentials. For the US the name changed and the engine grew; the truck itself carried on until 2018, when an all-new G with independent front suspension finally replaced it.

A NEW NAME, A BIGGER V8

For 2009 the US-market G 500 became the G 550, swapping the 5.0-litre M113 for the 5.5-litre M273 V8: 382 hp and 391 lb-ft. Mercedes had begun phasing the 722.9 seven-speed automatic into the US G 500 during 2007, so the G 550 arrived with seven ratios from the start. The 2008 update had also brought a new grille with three thicker horizontal fins and a COMAND APS system with its own off-road menu.

A later update lifted the dashboard and centre console from the ML-Class and added LED daytime running lamps, new mirrors, DISTRONIC cruise and blind-spot monitoring. Along the way came special editions — the 2011 Edition Select, a Japan-only G 550 Night Edition of 100 trucks, and a 30th-anniversary G 500/G 550 with designo leather — none of which changed the hardware underneath.

TWIN TURBOS AND A LONG GOODBYE

For 2016 the naturally aspirated V8 gave way to the 4.0-litre twin-turbo M176, with its turbochargers tucked between the cylinder banks: 416 hp and 450 lb-ft. Mercedes-Benz USA quoted 0–60 mph in 5.8 seconds and an electronically limited 130 mph — for a 5,724-lb brick with the drag coefficient of a filing cabinet. The 2016 G 550 started at $119,900.

The off-road numbers barely moved over the decade: 9.3 inches of ground clearance, 23.6 inches of wading depth, 30° approach and departure angles and a 24° breakover, per Mercedes-Benz USA. The same twin-turbo engine went into the outrageous portal-axle G 550 4×4² for 2017. Then, at Detroit in January 2018, Mercedes revealed the second-generation G — and the solid front axle was gone.

WHY OFF-ROADERS STILL WANT ONE

A solid front axle is a simple, strong, articulating thing, and it makes the G 550 the most modern G you can lift and tire the old-fashioned way. One Expedition Portal member put BFGoodrich KO2s in 275/70R18 on his stock-height 2014 G 550, with no rubbing even when flexing the axles in opposite directions, then added a Gobi roof rack — which knocked his fuel economy from about 12–13 mpg to 10–11.

The same owner later dropped the truck into a sinkhole in a creek. Recovery took about five hours, three tow services and, finally, a family member’s tractor; he noted the doors hadn’t leaked a drop. That’s the G 550 in a sentence: tougher than its reputation, and nowhere near as clever as a lifted 80 Series at avoiding trouble in the first place. Its contemporaries — the Land Cruiser 200, LX 570 and Range Rover L322 — were all softer, newer designs with independent fronts.

SIDEBAR · ONE TRUCK, TWO HEARTS

5.5-litre or 4.0 twin-turbo?

2009–2015 trucks use the M273 5.5-litre V8, a naturally aspirated engine shared with the S- and E-Class of the era. Its known weak points are well mapped: the map-controlled thermostat (the single most common failure, according to FCP Euro), intake-manifold swirl-flap levers, crankcase-breather seals and oil wicking up the camshaft-magnet connectors. The infamous idler-gear wear problem is attributed to early 2004–2008 production engines, which puts the G 550 largely after it — but scan for cam-timing codes anyway.

2016–2018 trucks get the M176 4.0-litre twin-turbo: 34 more horsepower and 59 more lb-ft, but turbos, intercooling and more heat in a tight engine bay. EPA fuel economy didn’t reward the change — every G 550 from 2010 to 2018 is rated 13 mpg combined — so choose on budget and service history, not on the window sticker.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The G 550 is the end of the line for the original engineering recipe. The body bolts to a separate steel ladder frame; both axles are rigid, located by links and sprung on coils; steering is a hydraulically assisted recirculating-ball box. Power flows through a two-speed transfer case to all four wheels all the time, split 50:50, and three 100% differential locks — centre, rear, front — are engaged from switches on the dash.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Decide on the engine first2009–2015 trucks have the naturally aspirated 5.5-litre M273; 2016–2018 trucks have the 4.0-litre twin-turbo M176. Same chassis, same lockers, different maintenance profile — and the 2016-on trucks command more money.
  2. 02
    Test all three lockersEngage centre, rear and front in order on a loose surface and watch the dash indicators. A truck whose lockers ‘were never used’ often has a vacuum or actuation fault waiting.
  3. 03
    Watch the temperature gauge (M273)A slow-to-warm or erratic coolant temperature, or codes P0597–P0599, points at the map-controlled thermostat — the most common M273 failure.
  4. 04
    Scan for swirl-flap codes (M273)P2004, P2005 or P2006 mean the intake-manifold flap mechanism has failed. Don’t ignore it: broken flap pieces can be ingested by the engine.
  5. 05
    Look behind the front wheelsGear oil on the inner wheels, calipers or rotors means leaking front axle seals — and possibly contaminated brakes.
  6. 06
    Drive it at 55–70 mphWander, wheel shake or a vague on-centre feel usually means a worn steering damper and tired linkage — common on the solid-axle G.
  7. 07
    Rust where nobody cleansTailgate lower edge, door bottoms, sills, rear frame tubes, strut mounts, inner wheel arches and around the rear bumper. Salt-belt trucks deserve a lift and a flashlight.
  8. 08
    Check the headliner and the badgesAutocar warns that a sagging headliner can mean water has got in, so check the sunroof drains and seals. And plenty of G 550s wear AMG bumpers and badges — check the VIN before paying a G 63 premium.

Spotting the real thing

Many G 550s wear AMG bumpers, badges and wheels, and the M273 and M176 trucks look much the same. The VIN separates them.

  1. ID
    VIN characters 4–8Bring a Trailer examples decoded with NHTSA's vPIC tool: 2010–2014 G 550 (5.5 V8) = WDCYC3HF…, 2016 G 550 (4.0 twin-turbo) = WDCYC3KF…, 2018 G 550 = WDCYC3KH…. A 2015 G 63 AMG reads WDCYC7DF…, so a 'G 63' with a YC3 VIN is a dressed-up G 550.
  2. ID
    4×4² versus regular G 550The 2017–2018 G 550 4×4² decodes as WDCYC5FF… / WDCYC5FH…, not YC3. A portal-axle truck without a YC5F VIN is an aftermarket conversion.
  3. ID
    Model year (10th character)The 10th character gives the model year (A = 2010, C = 2012, E = 2014, G = 2016, J = 2018). 2009–2015 trucks have the M273 5.5; 2016–2018 have the M176 twin-turbo.
  4. ID
    Engine bayThe M273 is a naturally aspirated V8; the M176 has twin turbos inside the vee. A quick look settles which engine it is.

Thermostat (M273)
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2 hoursSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSEngine slow to warm up or running cool, erratic temperature gauge, poor heater output, codes P0597, P0598 or P0599. Note that 80–95 °C can be normal on a working map-controlled thermostat.
THE FIXDrain enough coolant to drop the level below the housing, unplug the thermostat’s electrical connector, release the hoses and unbolt the housing. If the history is unknown, replace the radiator hose at the same time — old hoses often seal poorly at the O-ring on the new part. Refill with Mercedes-approved coolant and bleed the system. FCP Euro suggests treating it as a roughly 60,000-mile wear item.

Intake manifold swirl flaps (M273)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · Most of a daySHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSCheck-engine light with P2004, P2005 or P2006; sometimes a lumpy idle or flat spot. The operating lever on the flap shaft breaks.
THE FIXRepair kits for the lever exist, but FCP Euro recommends replacing the complete manifold assembly because the flaps can also fail internally and send pieces into the cylinders. G 550 questions, answered

What’s the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

About 33 inches — a 275/70R18 on 18-inch wheels. A 2014 G 550 owner on Expedition Portal ran BFGoodrich KO2s in that size at stock height with no rubbing at full flex, and a G 500 owner rubbed only on aftermarket ET63-offset wheels (ET43 was fine). A 285/65R18 can rub slightly at full left lock; a 265/65R18 (about 31.6 in) fits with room to spare. Later trucks came on 19s–21s, and owners warn 18s don’t clear every caliper — check before buying wheels, and have the tire size re-coded.

SOURCE · OWNER CONSENSUS · EXPEDITION PORTAL
What size are the factory tires?

A 2012 G 550 came on 265/60R18; optional sizes listed for that year run 275/55R19, 275/50R20 and 295/40R21. An Expedition Portal owner measured his 2017 G 550’s stock tires at about 30.5 inches tall.

SOURCE · SIZEMYTIRES · AUTOFILES · EXPEDITION PORTAL
How much can it tow?

7,000 lb with a braked trailer for a 2012 G 550. Check your own truck’s manual and hitch rating.

SOURCE · AUTOFILES
What fuel economy should I expect?

Bad. EPA figures for 2012 are 12 mpg city, 15 highway, 13 combined on premium — and every G 550 from 2010 to 2018 is rated 13 combined, twin-turbo or not. A roof rack cost one owner a couple more mpg.

SOURCE · EPA VIA MPG BUDDY · EXPEDITION PORTAL
How big is the fuel tank?

25.4 gallons, on both the 2012 G 550 and the 2017 4×4² spec sheets.

SOURCE · AUTOFILES · MBUSA
Should I buy a 5.5-litre or a 4.0 twin-turbo?

The 2016–2018 M176 twin-turbo has 416 hp and 450 lb-ft versus 382 hp and 391 lb-ft for the 2009–2015 M273, but EPA economy is identical. The M273’s faults are well documented and DIY-friendly; the twin-turbo is quicker and newer. Service history matters more than the engine choice.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · FCP EURO · EPA
Does the M273 balance-shaft/idler-gear problem affect the G 550?

It’s associated with early-production 2004–2008 M272 and M273 engines, which largely predates the 2009 G 550. If you see codes such as P0059, P0060, P0064, P0272, P0275 or P0276, or Mercedes codes 1200/1208, have it checked anyway.

SOURCE · FCP EURO
Can I use the diff locks on pavement?

No. Locked differentials stop the wheels turning at different speeds, so on grippy surfaces the driveline binds and tires scrub. Lock on dirt, sand, snow or rock at low speed, and unlock before you’re back on tarmac.

SOURCE · BOXYTRUCKS
How much ground clearance and wading depth does it have?

9.3 inches of ground clearance and 23.6 inches of wading depth, with 30° approach and departure angles and a 24° breakover, per Mercedes-Benz USA for the 2016 G 550.

SOURCE · MBUSA
Will it fit in my garage?

A 2016 G 550 is 76.9 inches tall, 80.9 inches wide and about 187.6 inches long, on a 112.2-inch wheelbase. Add a roof rack and measure again.

SOURCE · MBUSA
Is the G 550 reliable?

The frame, axles and drivetrain are tough; the age-related jobs are predictable — thermostat and swirl flaps on the 5.5, axle seals, steering damper, locker actuation, rust and sunroof drains. A truck with receipts for those beats a low-mileage one that’s never been touched.

SOURCE · FCP EURO · AU7O · AUTOCAR
