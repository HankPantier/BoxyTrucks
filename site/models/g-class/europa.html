Europa International Gs

300 GEG 320G 500US VIN · 50-STATE LEGAL

“A small company in Santa Fe spent years and a lot of money proving to Washington that the G was legal, and ended up building the US market that Mercedes then bought from it. The trucks are simpler, plainer and often the better G to own.”

The story

Before 2002 you couldn't walk into a Mercedes dealer in America and buy a G-Class. You could buy one from Europa International in Santa Fe, New Mexico. Europa took the civilian W463, crash-tested and certified it at its own expense, and sold 300 GEs, G 320s and eventually V8 G 500s with real US VINs. They were 50-state legal, not gray-market. In practice these are the first American G-Wagens.

AMERICA SAYS NO

The G was never planned for the US. In the 1980s gray-market specialists brought W460s in anyway, but tighter import rules in 1987 and the 1988 Motor Vehicle Safety Compliance Act largely ended that. Hagerty notes that gray-market buyers had always paid a steep premium, and Mike Serpe of Club G-Wagen warns that some of those dealers weren't entirely trustworthy. Anyone who wanted a G legally needed someone to do the certification properly.

According to the German Cars For Sale Blog, that was Dave Holland's idea. His Santa Fe company, Europa, decided to certify the truck to federal emissions and safety standards, crash tests included. Dealer Ferraris Online's account says the programme took five years, finished in October 1993 and cost $500,000. Wikipedia says Europa started selling Gs that year.

FROM CONVERTER TO MANUFACTURER

The early trucks were European Gs modified to US specification. Hagerty says Europa later persuaded Mercedes to build them to DOT standards at the factory. Then came the bigger step. Ferraris Online says that after almost two years and more than $1 million, Europa gained US Small Volume Manufacturer status, making the trucks legal in all 50 states. Wikipedia ties that status to the arrival of the V8 G 500 in 1998 and says it made Europa the exclusive US importer and distributor.

The line-up followed Europe's. It started with the 3.0-litre inline-six 300 GE, then the 3.2-litre inline-six G 320. In 1998 the G 320 switched to a V6, so both engines exist for that year. The 5.0-litre V8 G 500 joined at the top, and Europa also certified a handful of short-wheelbase Cabriolets. Volumes were small and margins were large. Hagerty reports that some trucks sold for as much as $135,000.

WHAT A EUROPA G IS LIKE

From outside, a 2000 Europa G 500 looks almost the same as a 2002 Mercedes-Benz USA truck. Jalopnik points to the black-painted bumpers, where later trucks have body-colour ones. Inside it's plainer: the steering wheel and instrument cluster come from the pre-facelift W210 E-Class, there are no radio buttons on the wheel, and the 722.6 five-speed automatic has no tip-shift gate. The dash-top screen on some trucks is Mercedes' optional navigation.

Under the skin it's still a full W463. Overland Journal's Scott Brady bought a 1996 Europa truck for the Expeditions West fleet. It had ABS, airbags and three dash buttons for the centre, rear and front locks, and Brady praised the early Europa Gs for their simple systems, durable six-cylinder engines and late-1990s build quality. German Cars For Sale Blog likes them for the same reason: fewer US-specific electronics to go wrong.

THE HANDOVER

Europa's success was what ended its import business. Mercedes-Benz bought the US distribution rights and launched the G officially for the 2002 model year, with the G 500 and G 55 AMG. Europa moved into service, parts and accessories, and kept importing a few models America never got officially, such as the three-door Cabriolet. A related company, Europa G-Wagen LLC of Santa Fe, still appears in title histories as late as 2013. The importer itself is a footnote now, but its trucks are not: a Europa G is the sweet spot for anyone who wants a 1990s G with proper US paperwork.

SIDEBAR · THE NEIMAN MARCUS G

A G-Wagen in the Christmas catalogue

In 1999 Europa and Neiman Marcus put a special-edition G 500 in the department store's famous Christmas Book at $160,000. It had a two-tone interior and Swedish birch trim. According to German Cars For Sale Blog, six were sold, probably built to order, which makes it one of the rarest US-market Gs.

It also shows who Europa was selling to. These weren't farm trucks. The trucks were expensive and luxurious, and they were still capable off-road.

How it’s built

Mechanically, Europa trucks are civilian W463s from the 1990s: a ladder frame, coil-sprung solid axles front and rear, permanent four-wheel drive with a two-speed transfer case, and three differential locks on the dash. The differences are in the engines and the amount of electronics. The sixes and the M113 V8 run without the COMAND and smart-key systems that came with Mercedes-Benz USA's 2002 trucks.

HOW TO SPOT ONE

A pre-2002 G with a US-format VIN and a federal compliance label is the basic sign. On a G 500, look for the W210 E-Class steering wheel and cluster, no radio buttons on the wheel, and black-painted bumpers. Ask for any Europa sales or service paperwork too.

Buying one: what to check

  1. 01
    US VIN and Europa paperworkA real Europa truck has a US VIN and federal compliance, not a foreign VIN cleared later by an independent lab. Match the VIN, the compliance label and any Europa invoices.
  2. 02
    Which G 320 engine?1998 was the changeover year, so a G 320 can have the M104 inline-six or the M112 V6. Check the engine code, since the two have different weak points.
  3. 03
    M104 head gasketLook under the right rear of the inline-six for oil seeping from the head gasket. Owners say a proper repair, about $2,000 at a dealer in 2002, usually lasts.
  4. 04
    Front axle service historyAsk when the front knuckles and bearings were last repacked. Europa recommended every 40,000 miles; owners say it's definitely due by 75,000.
  5. 05
    Test all three lockersEngage centre, rear and front on loose ground and watch the indicators. Lockers that 'were never used' often don't work.
  6. 06
    Ignition and key systemGerman Cars For Sale Blog warns that the EIS and smart-key system on later trucks can strand you without warning, and one Europa G 320 needed a new ignition tumbler. Make sure every key starts the truck.
  7. 07
    Rust from snowy statesScott Brady's 1996 Europa truck had two corrosion spots from its time in the Northeast. Check the tailgate's lower edge, door bottoms, sills and rear frame tubes.
  8. 08
    Originality paysHagerty says enthusiasts will pay up to 30 per cent more for an all-original G. Keep the factory parts if you modify one.
Post-purchase baseline

After you buy it

Start by confirming exactly what you bought: a Europa truck can have the M103, M104 or M112 six or the M113 V8, a 4- or 5-speed automatic, and drivetrain fluids that changed through the 1990s. Then reset everything — these are 25-plus-year-old grey-market imports, and service records rarely made the trip.

Asked on every forum

Europa International Gs questions, answered

Is a Europa G a gray-market truck?

No. Europa certified the trucks to US emissions and safety standards, including crash testing, sold them with US VINs, and gained Small Volume Manufacturer status, making them 50-state legal. Gray-market Gs were private imports cleared one at a time.

SOURCE · FERRARIS ONLINE · WIKIPEDIA · GCFSB
What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

About 33 inches. An Expedition Portal owner runs 275/70R18 on an unlifted 2000 G 500 and only rubbed on aftermarket ET63 wheels. Another runs 285/65R18 with a slight rub at full left lock. Another owner says up to about 32 inches should clear with no rubbing. Have the tire size re-coded with Mercedes diagnostics so the speedometer and ESP stay accurate.

SOURCE · OWNER CONSENSUS · EXPEDITION PORTAL
Which Europa G should I buy?

Forum regulars usually prefer the G 500 for its much greater power at similar mileage. German Cars For Sale Blog prefers the 300 GE or G 500 to the inline-six G 320 because of its head-gasket issue. One owner found the V6 G 320 more economical than a G 500, though the gap narrows when towing.

SOURCE · PEACHPARTS · GCFSB · EXPEDITION PORTAL
Do Europa trucks have all three lockers?

Yes. Scott Brady's 1996 Europa truck had dash buttons for the centre, rear and front locks, plus ABS and airbags.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL · SCOTT BRADY
How is a Europa G 500 different from a 2002 Mercedes-Benz USA G 500?

It looks nearly the same outside, apart from black-painted bumpers. Inside it has a pre-facelift W210 E-Class steering wheel and cluster, no wheel-mounted radio controls, older climate control, and a 722.6 automatic without the tip-shift gate. It also avoids the COMAND electronics that German Cars For Sale Blog blames for early MBUSA trucks' electrical problems.

SOURCE · GCFSB · JALOPNIK
How much did they cost new?

Hagerty says some sold for up to $135,000. The 1999 Neiman Marcus Christmas-catalogue G 500 was $160,000, and six were sold.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · GCFSB
What are they worth now?

Usually about the same as 2002-on trucks. Recent asks include a 202,750-mile 2000 G 500 at $32,000 (Jalopnik) and a well-documented 1998 G 320 at $47,500 (German Cars For Sale Blog), which the blog called high but understandable. Clean Europa G 320s come up only every few years and tend to sell quickly.

SOURCE · JALOPNIK · GCFSB
How many did Europa import?

We couldn't find a reliable total. The rarest variant is better documented: fewer than ten short-wheelbase Cabriolets were certified as US models, according to Ferraris Online.

SOURCE · FERRARIS ONLINE
How often should the front axle be serviced?

Europa recommended repacking the front axle (knuckles, wheel bearings, CV boots) every 40,000 miles. One owner thought that was early but agreed it's needed by about 75,000. Expect to pay around $1,500–2,000 at a specialist (2002 figures).

SOURCE · PEACHPARTS
Are they good overland trucks?

Yes, by most accounts. Scott Brady put a 1996 Europa G into the Expeditions West fleet, and its previous owner had driven it from the Northeast to Labrador and Newfoundland with a fridge, compressor and winch. Brady expected it to last another 10–15 years without major problems.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL
