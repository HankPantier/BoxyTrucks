Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

Lexus LX 570 problems to check before you buy

  1. AHC leaks and behaviorCycle the truck through Low, Normal and High and make sure it levels evenly. The IH8MUD AHC sticky includes a Toyota diagram of leak-prone junctions. Fluid at those points means you're about to learn a lot about hydraulics. A healthy system also lifts an extra inch or so in some low-range recovery situations, one owner reports.
  2. Radiator (2008–2010)Owners of early trucks report cracks in the top plastic tank, next to the molded platform. Look for a wet line or dried pink coolant along the top. Later radiators carry a different part number.
  3. Water pumpForum members say 2008 trucks had water-pump problems that have since been fixed. Look for pink crust at the pump weep hole and ask whether it's been replaced. One high-mileage owner paid about $700.
  4. Valley coolant leakPink coolant dripping from the top of the transmission hump at the back of the engine usually means the valley leak: the heat-exchanger cover and union pipe under the intake. It's not hard to fix, but it takes a lot of labor.
  5. Driveshaft clunkA 'thunk' when taking off or stopping often points to dry slip yokes and U-joints. Ask when the driveshafts were last greased. One owner paid about $780 for a propeller-shaft assembly to cure it.
  6. StarterSeveral owners have had the starter solenoid fail with no warning and leave them stranded. On a high-mileage truck, proactive replacement is common.
  7. Records by VINThe IH8MUD FAQ points out that the Toyota and Lexus owner sites show dealer service history once you enter the VIN, which is a good check before buying.
  8. HVAC recirculation doorIH8MUD members warn that the recirculation door hinge can be broken by a careless cabin-filter change. Check that the recirc control actually changes the airflow.

Lexus LX 570 recalls and service bulletins

  • Seat-belt tension sensor harness ↗ 2008–2019 · NHTSA 18V-887The harness can break and switch off the front passenger, knee and seat-mounted side airbags. Dealers modify or replace the belt assembly.
  • Incorrect airbag fitted ↗ 2008–2013 · NHTSA 17V-548Some trucks may have the wrong driver or passenger airbag. Dealers replace the affected assemblies.
  • Accelerator pedal entrapment by floor mat ↗ 2008–2011 · NHTSA 11V-113The pedal can stick wide open if trapped by an unsecured or incompatible mat. Dealers modify or replace the pedal and the mats.
  • Low-pressure fuel pump ↗ 2014–2015 · NHTSA 20V-012The fuel pump may fail and stall the engine. Dealers replace it.
  • Low-pressure fuel pump (expansion) ↗ 2018–2019 · NHTSA 20V-682Same pump failure on later trucks. Dealers fit an improved assembly.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

What to bring when you look at a Lexus LX 570

Six inexpensive tools that catch most of the problems above before you hand over the money. Or book a specialist: see how to get a pre-purchase inspection.

  • Bluetooth OBD-II scannerReads stored and pending codes, and shows the readiness monitors. If several say "not ready", someone may have cleared the codes just before you arrived.
    Amazon ↗
  • Paint thickness gaugeThick readings on one panel mean body filler or a respray, which usually means an accident or rust repair the seller hasn’t mentioned.
    Amazon ↗
  • Inspection borescope cameraFits through drain holes into frame rails, rockers and sills, where these trucks rust from the inside out.
    Amazon ↗
  • Bright LED flashlightFor the underside: leaks at the axle seals, transfer case and rear main, and the frame behind the rear wheels.
    Amazon ↗
  • Telescoping inspection mirrorShows the back of the engine, the top of the fuel tank and the inner fenders without crawling under.
    Amazon ↗
  • Compression testerOn a high-mileage or long-parked engine, uneven compression is the cheapest way to spot a tired engine before you pay for it. Diesels need a diesel-rated tester.
