PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The LX 470 is a 100 Series Land Cruiser, sharing its floor pan and most of its body panels, with Lexus fitting the expensive suspension from day one. Underneath are a ladder frame, a solid rear axle, torsion-bar independent front suspension, full-time four-wheel drive with a lockable center differential, and Toyota's 4.7-liter 2UZ-FE V8. On top of that sits Adjustable Height Control (AHC), a hydraulic system that raises the truck for trails and lowers it for loading, plus Adaptive Variable Suspension (AVS) for the dampers. The Land Cruiser didn't get AHC even as an option until 2006. Every LX 470 had it from launch.

A V8 AND A NEW FRONT END

Critics had called the LX 450 underpowered, so its replacement got a V8. The LX 470 debuted at the Los Angeles Auto Show in December 1997, and sales began in the second quarter of 1998. The 2UZ-FE made 230 hp from launch. It is a quad-cam, 32-valve engine with a timing belt, which shapes the ownership experience. The bigger change was up front. The live axle gave way to independent suspension with torsion bars, a first for the Land Cruiser line, and Wikipedia's Land Cruiser article says that change limited off-road capability and durability.

The lockers went too. The LX 450's optional electric front and rear lockers disappeared from the US LX. A rear locker stayed available in Canada for 1998 and 1999. In their place came the transfer case's locking center differential and, from 2000, A-TRAC brake-based traction control with VSC. Also in 2000, the front differential went from two pinion gears to four. One IH8MUD veteran warns that pre-2000 front diffs "can grenade with little stress."

THE HYDRAULIC TRICK

AHC replaces most of the conventional springing with hydraulics. There are actuators at each corner, gas-charged accumulators (owners call them globes or spheres), a pump and a reservoir. On IH8MUD, owners compare the settings like this: Normal sits at roughly Land Cruiser height, Low is a little over an inch lower, and High is 2–3 inches above a Land Cruiser. AVS picks from 64 damper settings in under 2.5 milliseconds at each wheel, with normal, comfort and sport modes. Both High and Low drop back to Normal above about 20 mph, one owner notes.

It works brilliantly while the fluid is fresh and the globes are charged. Lexus recommends an AHC fluid flush every 60,000 miles, according to the IH8MUD how-to thread, and owners who do it report a dramatically softer ride. When the globes go the ride turns harsh, and the usual test is to watch the reservoir level change between High and Low. Owners also take the cheap way out. They adjust the AHC height sensors for about 2 in of lift, or remove the system entirely and fit conventional springs and shocks, as at least one BaT truck has.

YEAR BY YEAR

The LX got the clever features early. The 2001 trucks got DVD navigation and a Mark Levinson option, and in 2002 Night View infrared became an option, a head-up display that projected a thermal image onto the windshield. The 2003 refresh was the big one. It brought a new front end, the A750F five-speed automatic, Variable Gear Ratio Steering, 275/60R18 tires, side curtain airbags, 235 hp and stronger driveshafts with bigger U-joints. For 2006, VVT-i raised output to 275 hp and LED taillights arrived. The last hurrah was a 2007 Limited Edition of 400 trucks in Black Onyx with stone leather.

Not everything aged gracefully. Lexus recalled about 39,000 2003–2007 trucks in 2010 because a hard pothole hit could unseat a snap ring on the steering shaft. The 2UZ also has a habit of loosening its spark plugs, which IH8MUD members have documented in detail. The early tick is often mistaken for a cracked exhaust manifold, which on these engines is also common.

HOW IT ENDED

US sales were 14,732 in calendar 2000 and slid steadily to 2,468 in 2007, when the 200 Series-based LX 570 arrived with a 383-hp 5.7-liter V8. The LX 470 had collected J.D. Power initial-quality awards (2000, 2002 and 2004) and was named the most reliable luxury SUV over three years in 2005. Its little brother, the GX 470, had arrived for 2003 with the same 2UZ-FE V8.

Two decades on, the LX 470 is a bargain for anyone who can wrench. High-mileage trucks with full records are common. IH8MUD signatures list LX 470s past 290,000 miles, and parts, armor and how-tos for the 100 Series fit the LX almost without exception. The AHC is the one thing that scares buyers off. Kept up, it's a genuine off-road advantage. If you'd rather not keep it up, the conversion is well documented.

SIDEBAR · THE TOYOTA THAT WAS A LEXUS

Land Cruiser Cygnus

In Japan, the LX 470 was sold as a Toyota: the Land Cruiser Cygnus. Wikipedia notes that the 100 Series 'formed the basis for the Lexus LX 470, also sold in Japan as the Toyota Cygnus.' DrivingLine describes the Cygnus as Japan's luxury version of the LX 470.

The Cygnus and the LX shared the gadgets. In 2002, Night View, the infrared camera system that projected a thermal image onto the windshield, arrived on both the Cygnus and the LX 470.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The LX 470 is built on the UZJ100 ladder frame. It has torsion-bar independent front suspension, a solid rear axle, and full-time 4WD through an HF2A two-speed transfer case with a lockable center differential and no viscous coupler. Lexus added hydraulic Adjustable Height Control and Adaptive Variable Suspension from launch. IH8MUD members note the brakes use a high-pressure pump instead of a vacuum booster, so a brake fault is something to diagnose with a scan tool, not ignore.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    AHC healthCycle the truck through Low, Normal and High and watch it level. Look for fluid at the actuators and lines. Check the reservoir level in High and in Low. One IH8MUD member says that if pressures and heights are in spec, a 7-graduation difference means the globes are shot. A harsh, jiggly ride points the same way. Find out when the fluid was last flushed.
  2. 02
    Timing belt recordsThe 2UZ-FE uses a timing belt with a 90k-mile service. Forum regulars say to confirm the 90k or 180k service has been done if the truck is near those mileages, and to have the water pump, idlers and tensioner done at the same time.
  3. 03
    Ticks at cold startA tick that fades as the engine warms is often a cracked driver's-side exhaust manifold, which CarSpec calls common on the 2UZ. IH8MUD members warn that the same sound can be a spark plug working loose, so check the plugs before blaming the manifold.
  4. 04
    Brake warning lightsThe 100 Series uses a high-pressure brake booster pump and accumulator instead of a vacuum booster. ABS and BRAKE lights plus a warning tone, or a pedal that sinks, mean a scan-tool diagnosis, not a guess. IH8MUD ran a poll on total brake failures, so treat it seriously.
  5. 05
    Steering and front endCheck for power-steering leaks at the rack, play in the steering and torn CV boots. Lexus issued a ball-joint inspection TSB in 2002. Confirm that the 2010 steering-shaft recall (2003–2007 trucks) was done.
  6. 06
    1998–1999 throttle gremlinsSlee lists accelerator and throttle position sensor faults on 1998–1999 trucks that can leave the truck not responding to the throttle. Make sure the throttle response is clean on the test drive.
  7. 07
    Rust and useForum members report plenty of rusty trucks from the northern US. Get underneath with a light. Like any Land Cruiser, a high-mileage truck with full records beats a low-mileage truck with none.
  8. 08
    Starter healthA slow or intermittent crank usually means worn starter contacts. The starter sits under the intake manifold, so it's a big job, and owners usually do it with the timing belt.

Spotting the real thing

The LX 470 shares its drivetrain with the 100 Series Land Cruiser, but some equipment differs, and Lexus fitted certain features earlier than the Land Cruiser got them.

  1. ID
    VINUS trucks start JTJHT00W. NHTSA's vPIC decoder reads them as the LX 470 with the 2UZ-FE engine, built in Toyota City. The 10th character is the model year (W = 1998, X = 1999, Y = 2000, 1 = 2001 through 7 = 2007).
  2. ID
    LX vs Land Cruiser 100Quad multi-reflector/projector headlamps, body cladding on the doors and quarter panels, backlit gauges, different wheels, and AHC with AVS standard from 1998.
  3. ID
    Front differential1998–1999 trucks have the weaker 2-pinion front diff. 2000-on trucks have the 4-pinion unit.
  4. ID
    Steering-shaft recallOn 2003–2007 trucks, confirm recall 10V-346 was done. Lexus's VIN lookup or a dealer can confirm it.
  5. ID
    2007 Limited Edition400 built, all in Black Onyx with stone leather and special badging and scuff plates. Check for all three before paying a premium.

AHC fluid flush and bleed
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1 hour (owner report)SHOP COST · About 2 cans of Toyota AHC fluid (part 08886-01805, $22/can in 2005) plus about $20 in supplies
SYMPTOMSA harsh or bouncy ride, dark or metallic-gray fluid in the reservoir, sluggish leveling, or a flush that's overdue (owners say Lexus recommends one every 60k miles).
THE FIXFollowing the IH8MUD how-to: park on level ground, select Low and switch off. Remove the old fluid from the reservoir with a turkey baster or fluid evacuator and refill with new AHC fluid. Fit clear tubing to the bleeder on the driver's front actuator, open it until that corner settles, close it, then start the truck and let it re-level. Repeat for the passenger front, driver rear and passenger rear, topping up as you go. Keep cycling until clean fluid comes out of every corner. Soak the rear bleeders in penetrating oil the day before, because they rust and round off.

AHC height-sensor lift
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoon plus an alignmentSHOP COST · A can of AHC fluid, scrap aluminum flat stock and an alignment
SYMPTOMSYou want more ground clearance in Normal without giving up AHC.
THE FIXMeasure the ride height at each corner first. With the ignition off, shorten the front height-sensor links to raise the front about 2 in. Going to 2.5 in means trimming the heim joint, and one side is reverse-threaded. The single rear sensor gives about 1.75 in with its mount slid to the top. More than that needs a 2-in extension bracket. Top up the AHC fluid, do a diff drop if you don't have one, and get an alignment. ATS4X4's advice, quoted in the thread, is to keep at least 70 mm of front droop so the CV boots survive. Lift too far and High mode will refuse to stay up.

Timing belt, water pump and 90k service
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · A weekend (about 15 leisurely hours in one write-up)SHOP COST · About $600 in parts for a full refresh in one owner's write-up. One owner's 180k service invoice ran about $1,900 in labor, including a snorkel install.
SYMPTOMSThe mileage says 90k, 180k or 270k with no proof the belt was done, or there's a weeping water pump or a rough idler bearing.
THE FIXDrain the coolant, protect the radiator with cardboard and remove the fan, shroud and belts. Hold the crank and break the crank-pulley bolt loose with a long breaker bar, then pull the pulley. Spark plug check and replacement
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoonSHOP COST · A set of 8 plugs (Denso or NGK) plus coil boots
SYMPTOMSA tick-tick-pop at cold start that can sound like an exhaust leak, a misfire or coil failure. In the worst case a plug blows out of the head and takes the threads with it.
THE FIXBlow out the plug wells before loosening anything. Remove the coils, check every plug for tightness and replace them using genuine parts. Don't use anti-seize. Toyota's 2UZ-FE spec is 13 ft-lb (17.5 N·m) in the engine section of the factory repair manual, which lists 15 ft-lb (20 N·m) in its ignition section. One veteran IH8MUD poster uses 18 ft-lb. Fit new coil boots and seals and torque the coil bolts to 66 in-lb. He recommends checking the plugs whenever the tick appears and at every timing-belt service.

DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · A full day, with a friendSHOP COST · About $530 for a new OEM rack bought through a friend in 2013 (retail $700+), plus coolant, oil, power-steering fluid and an alignment
SYMPTOMSPower-steering fluid leaking from the rack boots, play in the steering or a heavy feel.
THE FIXIn the IH8MUD write-up, the oil filter housing comes off first to make room. It has coolant lines, so be ready to catch about half a quart of oil and half a gallon of coolant. Then unbolt the column coupler, both hard lines and the rack mounts, spin the rack off the tie-rod ends and work it out through the frame. Install the new rack centered, reconnect everything, refill and bleed, and get an alignment.

Asked on every forum

Lexus LX 470 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

The IH8MUD 100 Series FAQ answer is 285/75R16 or 285/65R18, both about 33 in, with no lift. An owner with a stock '99 found 295/75R16 a tight fit that might not clear at full bump and full lock. Stock sizes were 16-in wheels to 2002 and 275/60R18 from 2003.

SOURCE · IH8MUD 100-SERIES FAQ · SLEE OFF-ROAD
How much can an LX 470 tow?

6,500 lb with the towing package.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What fuel economy should I expect?

The EPA rates a 2002 truck (four-speed) at 12 city, 15 highway and 13 combined. Five-speed trucks are rated 12 city, 15–16 highway and 13 combined. All run on regular fuel.

SOURCE · FUELECONOMY.GOV
Is it a timing belt or a chain?

A belt. The 2UZ-FE is due every 90k miles. Owners replace the water pump, idlers, tensioner and thermostat at the same time, and often the starter or its contacts too, since the intake is already half apart.

SOURCE · IH8MUD 90K THREADS · SLEE OFF-ROAD
Should I keep the AHC?

If it's healthy, yes. It gives you adjustable ground clearance a spring truck can't match, and a fluid flush is a one-hour job. Owners whose AHC failed have switched to conventional suspension instead. One IH8MUD member went to Old Man Emu 2.5-in heavy springs and called it much cheaper than replacing AHC sensors and actuators, which another member described as very expensive.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
How do I lift an LX 470?

The cheapest way is to adjust the AHC height-sensor links. That gives about 2 in up front (2.5 in with a trimmed heim joint) and about 1.75 in at the rear, or more with an extended bracket. Keep at least 70 mm of front droop for CV boot life, add a diff drop and get an alignment. The other route is removing the AHC and fitting conventional torsion bars, springs and shocks.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
Does it have lockers?

US trucks have a lockable center differential, plus brake-based A-TRAC from 2000. A factory rear locker was only offered on Canadian LX 470s in 1998–1999. Aftermarket air lockers are the usual upgrade.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · SLEE OFF-ROAD
Which years should I buy?

2003–2005 is the closest thing to a consensus. An Expedition Portal regular calls 2003–05 'THE years to get', and IH8MUD members single out the 2003-on five-speed automatic and redesigned dash. 2000 added VSC, A-TRAC and an improved front differential, so 2000–02 trucks are a cheaper runner-up. 2006–07 trucks get VVT-i and more power but also an air-injection system one member warns can be costly to fix. 2003–2007 trucks should have had the steering-shaft recall done.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL · IH8MUD · WIKIPEDIA
What's different from a 100 Series Land Cruiser?

The LX has the same drivetrain but different styling and equipment. It had AHC and AVS standard from 1998 (the Land Cruiser only got AHC as an option in 2006), front and rear automatic climate control, a power tilt and telescoping column, projector lows and fogs, body cladding and its own gauges and wheels.

SOURCE · SLEE OFF-ROAD
Are there recalls I should check?

Yes. The big one is the July 2010 recall of about 39,000 2003–2007 LX 470s, which replaced the steering-shaft snap ring and added a retainer. There was also a 2004 US recall that added a resistor to dim daytime running lights that were too bright. In Australia, front rotor bolts and a dash wiring harness were recalled too.

SOURCE · IH8MUD LX 470 TSB THREAD (LEXUS / NHTSA)
What's that tick from the engine?

It could be either of two common 2UZ problems. A cracked driver's-side exhaust manifold ticks when cold and fades as it warms. A spark plug working loose makes a similar sound and is far more dangerous, because it can blow out of the head. IH8MUD members recommend checking the plugs first.

SOURCE · CARSPEC · IH8MUD
