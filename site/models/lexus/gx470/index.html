PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The Lexus GX 470 is a Land Cruiser Prado 120, the mid-size Land Cruiser the rest of the world got and North America never did, dressed for a suburb it was always overqualified for. Underneath the wood trim are a ladder frame, a four-link solid rear axle with a lateral rod, full-time four-wheel drive with low range and a lockable center differential, plus Toyota's 4.7-liter 2UZ-FE V8. It was sold to people who wanted a nice SUV. It is now bought by people who want to cross Nevada on dirt roads.

A PRADO FOR AMERICA

Development started in 1999, two years after the J120 Prado program it was built on, and design work was finished in the first half of 2000. Lexus showed the GX at the 2002 North American International Auto Show as a 2003 model, and sales began in November 2002. It was slotted underneath the LX 470, which was itself a 100 Series Land Cruiser in a suit.

The GX had company on the Tahara assembly line. Expedition Portal notes it was built on the same line as the Prado and the 4th-gen 4Runner, and the 4Runner shares its suspension layout, rear air springs included on some trims. That family resemblance is useful. Forum regulars routinely borrow 4Runner parts, tire-fitment data and even used steering racks for their GXs.

THE 2UZ AND THE GADGETS

At launch the 2UZ-FE made 235 hp and 320 lb-ft. Variable valve timing (VVT-i) arrived for 2005 and pushed output to 270 hp. A change in SAE testing procedures restated that as 263 hp from 2006, with no actual change to the hardware. Midway through 2004, a stronger frame-bolted tubular hitch raised the tow rating from 5,000 to 6,500 lb. The transmission also became a sealed unit with no dipstick that year.

Lexus being Lexus, the clever hardware came with complications. The rear axle rides on air springs with ride-height control, and the dampers are driver-adjustable through Adaptive Variable Suspension. Downhill Assist Control handles steep descents, and KDSS, a hydraulically linked anti-roll system, became an option late in the 2004 model year. All of it works brilliantly until it gets old. Then the air springs leak, the compressor wears itself out chasing them, and the truck sits on its bump stops.

WHY OVERLANDERS LOVE IT

Four Wheeler magazine named the GX its Four Wheeler of the Year for 2003 and 2004, which was unusual praise for a vehicle with a Mark Levinson stereo option. Expedition Portal's project truck called the J120 "often considered the pinnacle of the body-on-frame era" and called the V8 "one of the most reliable engines ever sold in the USA." It also pointed to a 109-inch wheelbase and payload higher than the 4Runner's.

The weak points are well mapped. Owners report aging steering racks, CV boots, lower ball joints and cracked exhaust manifolds (often the driver's side), plus the timing belt that someone eventually has to pay for. Lexus ran a warranty enhancement program for cracked or sticky dashboards on 2003–2008 trucks. None of these problems scares off people who own a floor jack, which is why aftermarket support is now deep: lifts, rear coil conversions, skids, sliders and bumpers built specifically for this truck.

HOW IT ENDED

US sales peaked at 35,420 in 2004 and slid to 6,235 in 2009. A 2008 facelift brought a dark metallic grille, graphite-finish wheels and more chrome. The GX 460 replaced it after debuting in November 2009, followed by the boxy GX 550, which shares its bones with the 250 Series Land Cruiser.

The GX 470's afterlife has been kinder than its retirement. Cheap examples with high mileage and full service records are the starter overland rig of choice, because the V8 lasts and the chassis was always a Land Cruiser. The suspension complexity is optional. You can retire it with a coil conversion and a Saturday afternoon.

SIDEBAR · THE PROJECT TRUCK

Expedition Portal's GX 470 build

When Expedition Portal turned a GX 470 into a project vehicle, it kept the factory rear airbags for load leveling, added a 60 mm Air Lift 52420 spacer and fitted Old Man Emu BP-51 shocks. It rebuilt the steering rack, replaced the CV axles with factory Toyota parts and added Timbren bump stops, an ARB Summit bar, a Warn Zeon 10-S winch and 255/80R17 Toyo A/T III tires, which measured just over 33 inches.

Two lessons from that build are worth noting. First, an airbag failure left the truck unable to be driven out, and it needed a tow. Second, an editor's note pointed out that ARB doesn't list the GX as an approved application for its products, so fitting ARB parts to a GX may void ARB's warranty.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The GX 470 is a J120 Prado underneath: a high-strength ladder frame, coil-over independent front suspension and a four-link solid rear axle with a lateral rod. Full-time 4WD runs through a two-speed transfer case and a helical limited-slip center differential that can be locked, with open differentials front and rear and brake-based A-TRAC traction control to cover for them. Lexus then layered on rear air springs with height control, adjustable dampers and, as an option from 2004, KDSS.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Frame rustForum veterans call this the biggest concern on trucks from the salt belt. Get underneath with a light and a screwdriver, or ask for close-up photos of the frame. Rust on the rear air-spring perches is also what kills the airbags.
  2. 02
    Rear air suspensionLook for a rear end that sags overnight, a blinking height-control light, a harsh ride or a compressor that runs constantly. Spray soapy water where each airbag folds over its lower perch. If the system is already converted to coils, ask whose kit was used.
  3. 03
    Timing belt historyThe 2UZ-FE uses a belt that Toyota schedules at 90,000 miles, and the water pump runs off it. If there's no receipt, budget roughly $1K at a shop or a weekend of your own time. Engine Builder classes all belt-driven Toyota V8s as interference engines, so don't gamble on an unknown belt.
  4. 04
    Cold-start tickA tick that fades as the engine warms is the classic sign of a cracked exhaust manifold, often on the driver's side. The repair is a big one, so treat it as a price negotiation point.
  5. 05
    Steering rackLook for wet rack boots and power-steering fluid on the crossmember, and listen for creaking or whining at full lock. One ClubLexus owner's rack began creaking and whining loudly at about 130k miles.
  6. 06
    Front end wearCheck for torn lower ball-joint boots and grease slung from the CV boots, and listen for clunks over bumps. Many trucks need lower control arms or ball joints, CV axles and front bearings at this age.
  7. 07
    KDSS or not?Turn the key and watch for a momentary KDSS light, or look for the oversized front sway bar. KDSS improves stability and articulation, but some skids and sliders need KDSS-specific versions or modifications.
  8. 08
    DashboardHeat cracks and sticky dashboard surfaces are common enough that Lexus ran a warranty enhancement program for 2003–2008 GX 470s. Check whether the dash was already replaced, and confirm with a dealer whether any coverage still applies.

Spotting the real thing

Every US GX 470 is a Tahara-built Land Cruiser Prado 120 underneath, so the questions are about options, not authenticity: KDSS, the hitch rating and whether the air suspension is still there.

  1. ID
    VINUS trucks start JTJBT20X. NHTSA's vPIC decoder reads the series as UZJ120L, the engine as the 2UZ-FE and the plant as Tahara, Japan. The 10th character is the model year (3 = 2003 through 9 = 2009).
  2. ID
    KDSSOptional from late in the 2004 model year and standard on the 2005-on Sport. Look for a momentary KDSS light at key-on and the noticeably large front sway bar.
  3. ID
    Hitch rating2003 trucks are rated for 5,000 lb. The stronger frame-bolted tubular hitch arrived during the 2004 model year with a 6,500 lb rating, so check what's actually bolted on.
  4. ID
    Sport modelFrom 2005: KDSS, smoked headlamp bezels, extra chrome trim and black birdseye maple wood.
  5. ID
    Facelift trucks2008–2009 trucks have the dark metallic grille and liquid-graphite wheels.
  6. ID
    Rear suspensionRear air suspension shares its layout with the air-sprung 4th-gen 4Runner. Many trucks have been converted to coils, which is worth knowing before you look for the height-control switch.

SYMPTOMSThe rear sits low or sags overnight, the ride turns harsh, the height-control light blinks, the compressor runs nonstop, and in the worst case the truck is sitting on its bump stops.
THE FIXFirst confirm the leak with soapy water where each bag folds over its perch. To keep air, replace both springs, and the compressor if it has worn out chasing leaks. DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ a full weekend for a first-timerSHOP COST · About $950–$1,770 at shops, as quoted by owners on ClubLexus (the higher figure included a radiator and plugs)

THE FIXRemove the fan and shroud, accessory belts and crank pulley, then the timing covers. Watch for P0011, P0016, P0018 or P0021 afterward, which can point to timing misalignment.

SYMPTOMSUsually nothing until it breaks. GXOR warns that the original passenger-side front differential support can fail catastrophically, which can take out nearby parts, the oil pan included, and the engine with it.
THE FIXLexus revised the passenger-side front differential support for 2006, and some 2005s got the new part at the factory. On a 2003 to early-2005 truck, get under the passenger side of the front diff and read the number on the support. The revised part is ordered as 52380-60021 but stamped 52391-60021. If yours is the old one, take the diff's weight on a jack, unbolt the old support, fit the revised one and torque the bolts to factory spec.

DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · A long day. The intake manifold has to come off (GXOR).SHOP COST · A mechanic quoted one ClubLexus owner about $2,000

One owner said the pump was covered by a 10-year/150,000-mile extension, which has long expired on most trucks.

Asked on every forum

Lexus GX 470 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

The stock size is 265/65R17. On a stock or nearly stock GX 470, the safe consensus is about 31.5 inches. Owners report 265/70R17 working with little or no rubbing (one reports a slight rub at full lock in reverse), and 255/75R17 with zero rubbing. Going to 33 inches (285/70R17, or the narrower 255/80R17) generally means a 2–3 in lift and some liner trimming.

SOURCE · CLUBLEXUS · IH8MUD · EXPEDITION PORTAL
How much can a GX 470 tow?

2003 models are rated for 5,000 lb. Midway through the 2004 model year, Lexus fitted a stronger frame-bolted tubular hitch and raised the rating to 6,500 lb.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What fuel economy should I expect?

The EPA rates it at 14 mpg city, 18 highway and 15 combined (2007), and it calls for premium fuel. Bigger tires typically cost owners another 1 to 1.5 mpg.

SOURCE · EPA VIA MPGBUDDY · IH8MUD
Is it a timing belt or a chain?

It's a belt. Toyota's interval for UZ V8s is 90,000 miles, and the belt drives the water pump, so most owners replace both together.

SOURCE · ENGINE BUILDER · CLUBLEXUS
What fluids and capacities does it take?

Per the 2005 factory repair manual: engine oil 5W-30, 6.5 US qt (6.2 L) with filter. The A750F automatic takes Toyota Genuine ATF WS only, about 3.2 qt (3.0 L) per drain-and-fill and 11.0 qt (10.4 L) dry. Transfer case: 1.5 qt (1.4 L, about 3 pints) of GL-5 75W-90. Front diff 1.48 qt (1.40 L) and rear diff 3.22 qt (3.05 L), both GL-5 SAE 90 (80W-90 below 0°F). Coolant is about 13.6 qt (12.9 L) of Toyota Super Long Life. Confirm against your own manual for your model year.

SOURCE · 2005 GX 470 REPAIR MANUAL (RM1164U)
When should I lock the center diff?

Lock it whenever you're off pavement with loose traction. Many owners leave it locked for all trail driving, because it cuts down how much A-TRAC has to work on climbs. Unlock it for very tight turns on grippy surfaces and back on pavement. VSC is disabled while the center diff is locked.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL FORUM
Should I keep the rear air suspension?

It works well while healthy, and Expedition Portal's build kept it for load leveling. But aging bags leak where they fold over rusty perches, compressors wear out chasing those leaks, and a failure can leave you on the bump stops. Many owners convert to coils for well under the cost of new air parts.

SOURCE · CARSPEC · OFF-ROAD.COM · CLUBLEXUS
How do I tell if a GX 470 has KDSS?

Watch for a momentary KDSS light when you turn the key, or look for the noticeably large front sway bar. It was optional from late in the 2004 model year. It helps both stability and articulation, but it complicates some armor installs.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL FORUM · WIKIPEDIA
Which years should I buy?

There's no consensus year. Every GX 470 has the same 2UZ-FE V8 and A750F five-speed automatic; 2005 added VVT-i (270 hp, restated as 263 hp from 2006). Some Expedition Portal members hold out for a VVT-i truck, but the thread's own starter calls it a mixed bag, one ClubLexus owner who drove both says you can't tell the difference to 60 mph, and that thread's starter reckons quality is much the same across the model years. Trucks from mid-2004 get the 6,500 lb hitch and 2008–2009 the facelift. In practice, rust, timing-belt records, air suspension, steering rack and manifold matter more than the build date.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL · CLUBLEXUS · WIKIPEDIA
What lift options are there?

Common choices are Icon's 0–3.5 in systems (sold by Metal Tech 4x4), ToyTec kits, and Old Man Emu BP-51 coilovers, which Expedition Portal used. ARB doesn't list the GX as an approved application, so check warranty terms first. Pair any front lift with a rear coil conversion or an air spacer to level it.

SOURCE · METAL TECH 4X4 · TOYTEC · EXPEDITION PORTAL
Is it really just a 4Runner?

Not quite. It's a Land Cruiser Prado 120 underneath. The 4th-gen 4Runner shares its suspension layout and was built on the same Tahara line, so many parts and much of the forum knowledge carry across. The GX had the V8 and full-time 4WD with a center lock as standard.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · EXPEDITION PORTAL
