PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The LX 450 was Lexus's first SUV, and it was barely disguised. Take a late 80 Series Land Cruiser, give it a new grille, body cladding, different wheels, softer suspension and extra sound insulation, and you have it. Underneath sit a ladder frame, coil-sprung solid axles front and rear, full-time four-wheel drive with a lockable center differential and Toyota's 4.5-liter 1FZ-FE straight-six. Electric front and rear lockers were optional. It was sold in the US for just two model years, 1996 and 1997, and it remains the only LX with a solid front axle.

A TRUCK BORN OF A TRADE WAR

The LX 450 exists because of a tariff threat. In the mid-1990s trade fight between the US and Japan, Washington threatened 100 percent tariffs on Japanese luxury cars, but SUVs weren't on the list. A rebadged Land Cruiser gave Lexus a flagship that was exempt, and it needed very little development money. Production began in November 1995, the first truck came off the line that December, and US sales started in January 1996. The tariffs never happened, because the two governments reached a gentlemen's agreement.

Lexus aimed it squarely at the Land Rover crowd, meaning the Range Rover and the Discovery. The ad line was "It's everything a Lexus is, and everything a Lexus isn't." Canada got it from 1997. Toyota had pulled the Land Cruiser from Canada in 1996, so for a while the LX was the only way Canadians could buy an 80 Series new.

WHAT $7,000 BOUGHT YOU

At launch the LX 450 listed at $47,995, about $7,000 more than a base Land Cruiser at $40,678. A Land Cruiser optioned to match cost $46,968, so the real premium was closer to a thousand dollars. Slee Off-Road's 80 Series guide lists the differences. The LX got a revised stereo with a subwoofer in the center console, an optional factory cell phone, softer suspension, cladding on the doors, fenders and tailgate, bigger rear bumper corners, a plastic cover over the rear crossmember, two-tone mud flaps, automatic climate control, softer leather and more sound deadening.

Mechanically it was the 1995–1997 FZJ80 drivetrain. That means the 24-valve 1FZ-FE (212 hp and 275 lb-ft by most period spec sheets, although Wikipedia gives 215 hp), the A343F four-speed automatic, 4.11 axle gears and 275/70R16 tires. The only factory options were a console CD changer, a moonroof and the electric lockers. Those lockers are the option to look for today. On an Expedition Portal thread, forum regulars valued them at about $2,500 on a used truck.

THE SLOW-SELLER THAT WASN'T

Lexus expected to sell around 4,000 in 1996. It sold more than 5,000, sold out its first allocation and had a two-month waiting list. Sales passed 9,000 in 1997, and the truck topped J.D. Power's Initial Quality Survey among full-size SUVs. The weak spot was the engine. Critics thought 212 hp wasn't enough for a 5,000-lb truck, so the model cycle was cut short. For 1998 the 100 Series-based LX 470 took over, with a V8, independent front suspension and hydraulic height control.

That changeover is the reason the LX 450 has a cult following now. It was the last LX with a live front axle, and the LX 470 that replaced it dropped the factory lockers in the US. Every 80 Series part, lift and armor kit fits, and Metal Tech 4x4 sells sliders and rear links labeled for both the 80 Series and the LX 450. You pay a little more for the badge, and in return you usually get a truck that spent its first decade on a country-club driveway.

LIVING WITH ONE NOW

The newest LX 450 is now nearly 30 years old, so condition matters far more than spec. The forums agree on the big three. The 1FZ-FE head gasket becomes a concern past about 150,000 miles. The front knuckles and birfields need a rebuild when their seals start weeping. And there's the PHH, short for "pesky heater hose": a 3-inch coolant hose under the intake that can strand you when it pops. None of these is exotic. Each one is documented on IH8MUD and Slee's site, and each is the kind of weekend job the owners' community has turned into a rite of passage.

SIDEBAR · LOCKED OR NOT?

Factory lockers and the hidden center-diff switch

The factory lockers were electric, front and rear, and were the most valuable option on the LX 450. Slee's buying advice is to make sure they actually engage by turning them on in low range on a low-traction surface. Slee also says the usual cause of a locker that won't engage is the actuator, which can be rebuilt. Retrofitting factory lockers to an unlocked truck needs the locker ECU, harnesses and new or re-splined axle shafts, plus some welding, so it is rarely worth it. ARB Air Lockers are the usual alternative.

The center differential is a separate story. US trucks from 1993 on lock the center diff automatically in low range but had no switch for high range, even though the wiring is there. Slee's plug-in center-diff-lock switch for 1995–1997 trucks (TOY1002) cost $79 and took Off-Road.com's tester less than an hour to install.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The LX 450 is an FZJ80 underneath. It has a ladder frame, coil springs on solid axles at both ends and full-time four-wheel drive through a two-speed transfer case with a viscous-coupled center differential. US trucks lock the center diff automatically in low range, which also switches off the ABS. The 1995–1997 trucks got the A343F four-speed automatic, airbags, OBD-II diagnostics (fully compliant from 1996) and in-line catalytic converters. Electric front and rear lockers were a factory option.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Head gasket historyLook for coolant at the head-to-block joint and ask whether the gasket has been replaced. One Expedition Portal regular calls 1FZ-FEs past 150k miles prone to failure and those past 200k very prone. Others think the worry is overblown. Either way, receipts for a properly done head gasket job add real value.
  2. 02
    Knuckle sealsCaked grease and oil on the outside of the steering knuckles means the inner axle seals failed a while ago. Slee suggests pulling the square fill plug on the knuckle. If the grease inside is runny, diff oil is getting into the knuckle.
  3. 03
    Lockers that workIf the truck has the factory lockers, turn them on in low range on a loose surface and make sure they engage. A dead locker is often a seized actuator, which can be rebuilt.
  4. 04
    Cooling system and PHHA replaced radiator or deposits in the overflow bottle can point to past overheating, which is bad news for the head gasket. Ask whether the PHH and the bypass hoses under the intake have been replaced. Use Toyota red coolant.
  5. 05
    Accident and rust historySlee's checklist covers overspray, filler (check with a magnet), uneven panel gaps and missing VIN stickers. Late 80s had a sticker on every body panel. Get underneath and check the frame for pull marks and rust.
  6. 06
    Records over milesForum regulars put service records ahead of mileage. If the driveline, diffs, birfields and bearings haven't been serviced within 150k miles, one Expedition Portal member says to budget about $2,000.
  7. 07
    Hidden wiringLook under the dash below the steering wheel and at extra wires on the battery terminal. A rat's nest of accessory wiring usually means the rest of the work wasn't done properly either.
  8. 08
    Drive itCheck that the transmission shifts smoothly, the brakes don't pull or pulse, the steering is free of noises from lock to lock, and there's no vibration at highway speed. Blue or white smoke on acceleration means walk away.

Spotting the real thing

An LX 450 is an 80 Series Land Cruiser in a suit, so the real questions are whether it's a genuine LX and what options it left the factory with.

  1. ID
    VINUS trucks start JT6HJ88J. NHTSA's vPIC decoder reads the series as FZJ80L with the 1FZ-FE 4.5 I6, built in Toyota City. The 10th character is the model year (T = 1996, V = 1997).
  2. ID
    LX vs Land CruiserFrom the outside, only the grille, the side body cladding and the wheels differ from an FZJ80. Inside, look for the LX's leather and extra sound insulation.
  3. ID
    Factory lockersElectric front and rear lockers were one of only three factory options. Trucks that have them have locker controls on the dash. Test both in low range on a loose surface.
  4. ID
    Center diff lockThe center diff locks automatically in low range. US trucks from 1993 lost the high-range switch but kept the wiring, so a plug-in CDL switch can add it back.
  5. ID
    Lug nutsFrom 1995 the 80 Series switched from conical to washer-type lug nuts, so make sure the nuts match the wheels on a truck with aftermarket rims.

Recalls & service bulletins

Open recalls follow the VIN, not the model. Run yours through NHTSA before you buy, and ask the seller for dealer records showing recall work.

Front knuckle rebuild and birfield repack
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · Varies. Allow a weekend for a first rebuild.SHOP COST · Rebuild kit about $126–$130 (Cruiser Corps, Low Range Off-Road). Wheel bearings are extra.
SYMPTOMSGrease and oil caked on the outside of the knuckle balls, runny grease at the knuckle fill plug, and diff oil migrating into the knuckles.
THE FIXFollowing Jim Phillips's IH8MUD write-up: pull the wheel and caliper (hang it, don't let it dangle), then the dust cap, snap ring, flange nuts, cone washers and locknuts, and slide off the hub and rotor. Unbolt the spindle, unplug the plastic ABS sensor gently, set the steering straight ahead and pull the axle and birfield. Pull the spring out of the inner seal lip before prying the old seal out, so it can't drop into the axle tube. Clean, repack and reassemble with new gaskets and seals. Set wheel bearing preload to 43 ft-lb, rotate, back off, then 3 ft-lb, lock nut to 47 ft-lb, and check for 6–12 lb on a spring scale. Flange nuts take 26 ft-lb and caliper bolts 90 ft-lb. Trunnion bearings are worth doing at the same time.

DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoon. It's easier if the starter is already out.SHOP COST · About $20 in Gates hose and clamps, or a bypass kit for about $45 (owner reports)

THE FIXFollow the hard metal heater pipe from the heater valve down the driver's side of the engine. Asked on every forum

Lexus LX 450 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

The stock size is 275/70R16. On a stock-height 80 Series or LX 450 with healthy springs, the usual answer is 285/75R16, about 33 in. Owners report it works, with some rubbing possible at full flex or full lock. Anything bigger starts rubbing. Slee's guide notes that 35s are possible with a 2-in Old Man Emu lift, but expect to rub when crawling unless you lower the bump stops.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · SLEE OFF-ROAD
How much can it tow?

A 1997 spec sheet lists a 2,268 kg (5,000 lb) maximum trailer weight. Slee warns that the four bolt holes in the black rear crossmember (covered by trim on the LX) aren't rated for towing in the US, so use a frame-mounted receiver hitch.

SOURCE · AUTO123 SPEC SHEET · SLEE OFF-ROAD
What fuel economy should I expect?

The EPA rates both years at 12 mpg city, 14 highway and 13 combined on regular fuel. Bigger tires and a roof rack push that lower.

SOURCE · EPA VIA MPGBUDDY
What fluids and capacities does it take?

According to AMSOIL's 1997 LX 450 lookup: engine oil 7.8 qt with filter (10W-30 above -18°C, 5W-30 below 10°C). Coolant 14.8 qt with the front heater only, or 15.9 qt with front and rear heaters. The A343F transmission takes 11.6 qt total. The transfer case takes 3.6 pt of GL-4 or GL-5 gear oil. The front diff takes 5.5 pt (with locker) or 5.9 pt (without) and the rear diff 7 pt, both GL-5 90-weight. Power steering uses DEXRON-III ATF.

SOURCE · AMSOIL APPLICATION GUIDE
Does it have lockers?

Every LX 450 has a center differential that locks automatically in low range. Electric front and rear axle lockers were a factory option, and BaT listings describe triple-locked trucks. If yours has them there are locker controls on the dash. Test them in low range on a loose surface before you buy. For unlocked trucks Slee recommends ARB Air Lockers, which cost less than retrofitting the factory units.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · SLEE OFF-ROAD · BAT
Can I lock the center diff in high range?

Not from the factory. US trucks from 1993 on had the switch deleted but kept the wiring. A plug-in CDL switch (Slee TOY1002 for 1995–1997) adds high-range locking in under an hour.

SOURCE · OFF-ROAD.COM · SLEE OFF-ROAD
How worried should I be about the head gasket?

Worried enough to budget for it, but not enough to panic. Expedition Portal regulars say 1995–1997 1FZ-FEs are the ones to watch, and that risk climbs past 150k and especially 200k miles, with overheating in hot climates as a trigger. A properly done gasket job with receipts adds about $1,500–$2,000 in value. Others note plenty of unreplaced gaskets go past 200k without trouble.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL FORUM
Should I regear for bigger tires?

The 1FZ-FE trucks come with 4.11 gears. Slee suggests regearing to 4.56, 4.88 or 5.29 when you go up in tire size. Opinions vary on the right ratio, so its advice is to drive a truck with the setup you're considering first.

SOURCE · SLEE OFF-ROAD
What's the difference between an LX 450 and a 1996–1997 Land Cruiser?

Mechanically, very little. The LX has softer suspension, extra sound deadening, softer leather, auto climate control, a console subwoofer, body cladding, larger rear bumper corners and two-tone mud flaps. The LX mud flaps don't swap onto a Toyota. Parts and aftermarket kits for the FZJ80 fit both.

SOURCE · SLEE OFF-ROAD · WIKIPEDIA
What lift options are there?

Old Man Emu coil-and-shock kits are the classic answer. Slee's guide mentions OME 2-in lifts with 35s, and owners run OME 850/864-series coils. Metal Tech 4x4 sells 1.5-in and 2-in front spacer plates, adjustable rear upper and offset lower links, sway-bar drop brackets and caster plates for the 80 Series and LX 450. If you lift the truck, readjust the load-sensing brake proportioning valve.

SOURCE · SLEE OFF-ROAD · METAL TECH 4X4
Which year should I buy?

There are only two, and they're nearly the same. The 1996 and 1997 trucks both have the A343F, airbags and OBD-II. Buy the one with the best records, the healthiest knuckles and working lockers. Factory lockers add about $2,500 in value according to forum regulars, so a locked truck is worth the wait.

SOURCE · SLEE OFF-ROAD · EXPEDITION PORTAL FORUM
