PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

On 12 May 1994 Mitsubishi replaced the cab-over Delica Star Wagon with something that looked like a minivan and drove like a Pajero. The Delica Space Gear moved the engine out from under the driver's seat and into a short nose, sat on the second-generation Pajero platform, and kept a proper 4WD system with high and low range. Japanese magazine Motor-Fan sums it up as a minivan with off-road ability inherited from the Pajero. Thirty years later, that's exactly why North Americans are importing them.

A PAJERO UNDERNEATH

Motor-Fan says the fourth-generation Delica was developed on the second-generation Pajero platform, with a Pajero-derived built-in frame for crash safety and a flat floor from the front seats to the cargo area, so you can walk through the cabin. Wikipedia describes the body as monocoque, unlike the Pajero, with no separate chassis. Underneath it's familiar Pajero hardware: double wishbones and torsion bars at the front, a five-link coil-sprung rear axle, and discs all round, according to the Japanese catalogue data on Gulliver.

At launch the 4WD versions came with a 2.4-litre petrol four, the 3.0-litre 6G72 V6 or the 2.8-litre 4M40 intercooled turbo-diesel. There were rear-drive versions too, with a 2.4, a 2.5 diesel and the V6. Bodies came in standard and long wheelbase, on 2,800 mm and 3,000 mm wheelbases, with an Aero Roof, a High Roof, twin sunroofs or the glass-panelled Crystal Lite Roof. Launch prices were ¥2.408 million for a 2.8 diesel manual and ¥2.80 million for the V6 automatic.

SERIES 1, SERIES 2

Japanese catalogue data lists the early 4M40 at 125 PS and 30 kgf·m. From the mid-1997 facelift it's listed at 140 PS and 32 kgf·m, and Wikipedia notes the diesel got an electronically controlled distributor-type injection pump at the same time. The facelift also changed the lights and front panel and replaced the three-section bull bar with a moulded bumper. Owners call these Series 1 and Series 2 vans. Grades ran from XR and XG through Exceed, Super Exceed and Royal Exceed, plus Chamonix and Jasper specials. By mid-1998 the 4WD diesel manual had left the Japanese catalogue.

A final facelift arrived in August 2002, and the catalogue thinned out after it. The rear-drive versions were gone, the 2.8 diesel last appears in the April–October 2004 listings, and the last vans were 3.0 V6 Chamonix and Active Field Edition models with the four-speed automatic. Mitsubishi's Japanese sales were down to 1,451 in fiscal 2006, according to JDMBUYSELL. The catalogue ends in January 2007, when the Outlander-based Delica D:5 arrived with an electronically controlled 4WD system in place of Super Select.

THE MONTERO WITH A VAN BODY

The Space Gear was never sold new in North America, but Canada's 15-year import rule let Canadians bring them in long before the US 25-year rule caught up. The Delica Canada forum, much of it based in British Columbia and with threads going back to 2006, has the best advice: tell the parts counter it's a Montero with a van body. One long-time member replaced bearings, seals, brakes, U-joints, CV boots, the pitman arm and ball joints using 1995 Montero parts.

That doesn't always work. Another member bought a Montero head-gasket kit for a V6 Delica and found the head gasket's water passages didn't match. The Drive's 1994 Space Gear owner gives the same advice: cross-reference parts by chassis code, not VIN. In return you get a tall, right-hand-drive, eight-seat van with low range, and the Delica Canada forum is full of owners taking them down gravel and bush roads.

SIDEBAR · THE SPACE GEAR THAT NEVER DIED

Starex, Starwagon and a Chinese van that's still in production

Mitsubishi's L400 lived on well beyond Japan. Hyundai used the Delica as the base for the first-generation Starex, built from 1997 to 2007. In Australia the WA-series Starwagon passenger van ran from September 1994 to 2003 and the Express cargo version until 2005. In Taiwan, China Motor Corporation built the Space Gear and gave it its own facelift in 2005.

Wikipedia says Taiwan's Space Gear tooling then went to Dongfeng Liuzhou, whose Fengxing Lingzhi started life as a rebadged Space Gear. Wikipedia lists Chinese production as continuing to the present. In Europe, the passenger version was simply the Mitsubishi Space Gear and the commercial version the L400.

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The Space Gear has a monocoque body with a built-in frame, on running gear taken from the second-generation Pajero. The front suspension is independent, with double wishbones and torsion bars, and there's a five-link coil-sprung live axle at the rear. 4WD versions use Mitsubishi's Super Select transfer case, which lets you choose 2WD, full-time 4WD, locked high range or locked low range. The front axle is engaged by a vacuum actuator.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Crawl under it with a flashlightRust is the van-killer. One Delica Canada owner photographed it on the frame rails, under the battery compartment, in the hole in front of the battery tray, along the seam back to the firewall and behind the rear bumper where the spare sat. Another warned that rebuilding a rotten L400 cost so much time and money he'd never do it again.
  2. 02
    Look up, tooOwners warn about the roof and the windshield surround. Crystal Lite Roof vans are said to be prone to rust and electrical problems, and one owner was quoted about $1,000 for windshield-frame rust repair.
  3. 03
    Confirm it's actually 4WDRear-drive Space Gears exist (PA, PB and PC codes). A 4WD van has a Super Select lever and a chassis code such as PD6W, PE8W, PD8W, PF6W or PF8W. Match the code on the frame number to the engine before you pay.
  4. 04
    Cycle the Super SelectShift through 2H, 4H, 4HLc and 4LLc on loose ground. A front-wheel light that keeps flashing usually means a sticking vacuum solenoid pair or a seized front-diff actuator; a flashing centre-diff light points to the transfer-case position switches.
  5. 05
    Cold-start a diesel yourselfArrive early so the seller can't pre-warm it. Hard starting points to glow plugs or the glow-plug controller, and smoke can be a clogged EGR, injectors or an injection pump that's on its way out. The early mechanical pump is the one owners say leaks.
  6. 06
    Check the cooling system and headAn owner told Field Mag that overheating and cracked heads are common on L400s. Look for coolant loss, bubbles in the reservoir and white smoke, and ask whether the van has ever overheated. A Delica Canada member advises crack-testing the head if the gasket has gone.
  7. 07
    Test the automatic properlyDrive it until it's warm and make sure it shifts through all four gears. A van that holds third and flashes the neutral light has dropped into limp mode, which can be a sensor fault or a failing transmission.
  8. 08
    Ask how it got hereGet the export certificate, the import paperwork and the build month. In the US the 25-year clock starts at the build date, and RHD insurance and registration rules vary by state and province.

Spotting the real thing

Every Space Gear in North America is a right-hand-drive gray import, and the same body was sold with rear-wheel drive, several engines and a dozen grades. The chassis code tells you what you're looking at.

  1. ID
    Chassis code4WD wagons include PD4W (2.4 petrol), PD6W (3.0 V6), PD8W and PE8W (2.8 diesel) and the long-wheelbase PF6W and PF8W. Rear-drive codes include PA4W (2.4), PA5W (2.5 diesel) and PB6W (3.0 LWB). Bring a Trailer listings show frame numbers such as PD6W0400925 and PE8W0600311.
  2. ID
    Type-code prefixJapanese papers show prefixes such as E-, KD-, KH-, GF- and GH- in front of the chassis code (for example KH-PE8W or GH-PD6W). JDMBUYSELL explains these as emissions-regulation markers, so they help date a van.
  3. ID
    Series 1 vs Series 2Series 1 vans (1994–mid-1997) have the three-section bull-bar front; Series 2 vans have a moulded bumper and revised lights. Diesel Series 2 vans have the electronically controlled injection pump, which matters when it needs work.
  4. ID
    Is it really 4WD?Look for the Super Select lever and display. On the Delica Canada forum, a member ordering lower ball joints was twice sent a 2WD-type joint, and the factory manual shows the 2WD and 4WD joints mounting differently, so check the code before you buy the van or its parts.
  5. ID
    RoofGrades came with an Aero Roof (about 1,965 mm tall on SWB 4WD), High Roof (2,060 mm), twin sunroofs or the glass Crystal Lite Roof; long-wheelbase Crystal Lite vans are 2,070 mm tall. Check roof height before you buy for a garage or a roof-top tent.

Importing & legality

The Space Gear was never sold new in the US. NHTSA's import exemption covers vehicles at least 25 years old, counted from each vehicle's build date (49 CFR 591.5). As of October 2026 that covers vans built up to about October 2001: every Series 1 and the early Series 2s, but not the post-2002 facelift models. The EPA exemption is separate. It requires the vehicle to be at least 21 years old and in its original configuration, or fitted with an equivalent or newer EPA-certified engine and emissions system, so keep the import paperwork and leave the EGR system in place.

Federal eligibility doesn't settle registration. Titling, safety inspections and emissions rules for right-hand-drive gray imports vary by state, and California is the hardest. Many US vans come through Canada, where the threshold is 15 years. Canadian provinces have their own inspection and RHD rules, and Delica Canada's importing forum covers headlight compliance and out-of-province inspections. A Canadian-registered van still has to meet the US 25-year rule.

DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–3 hoursSHOP COST · Often just cleaning; solenoids and hoses if not
SYMPTOMSThe green front-wheel lights on the Super Select display keep flashing in 2H, or the van won't fully engage or release 4WD. A flashing yellow centre light is a different fault.
THE FIXFirst try backing up a few van lengths and shifting in and out of 4H. Then check the vacuum hoses and the vacuum can at the front diff for cracks or loose hoses. Delica Canada's Big-Bird says the most common cause is the pair of vacuum solenoids behind the engine-bay fuse box: test them for resistance and vacuum hold, and clean or replace them. One owner's dealer fixed it by cleaning a gummed-up solenoid. If the lights still flash, check that the front-diff actuator rod moves freely. A flashing yellow centre-diff light means one of the five shift-position switches on the transfer case is failing.

THE FIXRemove the axle, clean and inspect the joint, and fit new boots, packing grease well up into the ribs (the area most likely to crack, says a Delica Canada member). Clean and check the EGR (4M40) →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1 hourSHOP COST · A gasket or two

THE FIXOn the Delica Canada forum, one owner calls the EGR valve the first suspect on an imported diesel: it's two bolts and a plate. Another adds that blanking it isn't the first step. Pull the valve, clean the carbon from the valve and its passage, and test the diaphragm with a vacuum tester before you condemn anything. Check the intercooler pipes for oil while you're there; oil in them pointed to failing turbo seals in one owner's 2002 van.

Fix a leaking 4M40 injection pump →
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ 6–8 shop hours to remove and refit (one Calgary quote), plus bench timeSHOP COST · Calgary owners were quoted CA$1,600–1,800 in 2016, including removal
SYMPTOMSDiesel weeping around the pump, hard or rough cold starts, irregular shifting on automatics, smoke.
THE FIXFind out which pump you have. Series 1 (to 1997) vans use a mechanical pump, which owners say is prone to leaks but can be resealed and recalibrated in North America. Series 2 vans use an electronically controlled pump, which a shop told one owner no one in North America can calibrate. One owner's rough cold starts and irregular shifting cleared up after a pump overhaul, and a Delica Canada member says a new pump front shaft seal 'does wonders'. Mark the pump timing before removal and have any rebuild bench-calibrated.

Stop the frame rust →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ A messy day, then yearlySHOP COST · A Fluid Film kit with gun and two gallons came to about CA$230 for one owner
SYMPTOMSScaling on the frame rails, rear axle and driveshaft; bubbling around the battery tray and rear bumper.
THE FIXPressure-wash the underside and let it dry for a few days. Take off the side steps and bumper tube to wire-brush and paint the worst spots. A Winnipeg owner says a wire wheel is enough and grinding isn't needed. Then spray an inhibitor into every frame and drain hole, using a wand on a flexible extension. Remove the spare tire first, because one van that was undercoated with the spare left in place rusted behind the rear bumper. Redo it every year.

Asked on every forum

Delica Space Gear questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

Not much bigger than stock. Japanese 4WD vans came on 225/80R15s, which are about 29 inches, on 15x6 wheels with about 4.25 inches of backspacing. A long-time Delica Canada member says 245/75R16 may fit unlifted, but only with a lot of trimming, and you'll need a lift to clear the wheel wells at full articulation. Even with a 2-inch lift, owners report 31x10.5s rubbing on compression in turns after trimming. One said he should have gone with 30s.

SOURCE · GULLIVER · DELICA CANADA · EXPEDITION PORTAL
Can I import one to the US?

Yes, once each van is 25 years old, counted from its build date (49 CFR 591.5). As of October 2026 that covers vans built up to about October 2001: every Series 1 and the early Series 2s. The EPA has a separate 21-year exemption that requires the original configuration. State titling is a separate step, and RHD rules and California smog checks vary. Canada's threshold is 15 years.

SOURCE · JDMBUYSELL · ECFR
Diesel or V6?

Most Space Gears sold on Bring a Trailer in 2025–26 were 4M40 2.8 turbo-diesels, which have a timing chain. Watch it for overheating and injection-pump leaks, and it needs a diesel mechanic. A Delica Canada owner of a long-wheelbase V6 says the 3.0 is quieter with more top-end power, and because the 6G72 was also used in North American Monteros, some engine parts are easier to find. It has a timing belt.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER · DELICA CANADA
What's the difference between Series 1 and Series 2?

Series 1 vans (1994 to mid-1997) have the three-section bull-bar front and a mechanical injection pump on the diesel, rated at 125 PS. The 1997 facelift brought new lights, a moulded bumper and an electronically controlled pump, which took the 4M40 to 140 PS. A final facelift followed in August 2002.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · GULLIVER
Is it a real 4x4?

The 4WD versions use the Pajero's Super Select system with a two-speed transfer case. You can pick 2WD, full-time 4WD, locked high range or locked low range. Japanese catalogue ground clearance is 190–195 mm (about 7.5 in). It's a monocoque van, though, and Delica Canada members warn that lifted vans used hard can break upper control arms.

SOURCE · MOTOR-FAN · GULLIVER · DELICA CANADA
Why are the Super Select lights flashing?

A flashing green front-wheel light usually means the vacuum solenoid pair behind the engine-bay fuse box is sticking, or the front-diff vacuum actuator, can or hoses are faulty. A flashing yellow centre-diff light points to the shift-position switches on the transfer case. Try reversing a few van lengths first.

SOURCE · DELICA CANADA
Where do I get parts?

Ask for parts for a Montero with a van body. Owners have fitted Montero bearings, seals, brakes, U-joints, CV boots, steering parts and ball joints. Match by part number from a genuine catalogue searched by your frame number, because some parts look the same but aren't. Importers also sell used parts, and Delica Canada has a parts trader.

SOURCE · DELICA CANADA · THE DRIVE
How bad is the rust?

Bad enough to end a van. Owners watch the frame rails, the battery-tray area, the firewall seams, the rear bumper, the roof and the windshield surround. Undercoat as soon as you buy it, and every year after that.

SOURCE · DELICA CANADA · JDMBUYSELL
What fluids does it take?

From one owner's manual: 5W-30 or 10W-30 engine oil (4.5 L for the V6, 8.3 L for the diesel), 9.3 L of ATF SP-II M, 2.5 L of 75W-85 GL-4 in the transfer case, GL-5 hypoid oil in the diffs (0.9 L front, 1.6–2.6 L rear), Mitsubishi ATF II in the power steering and about 11 L of coolant.

SOURCE · DELICA CANADA
How do I lift one?

The common setup is about 2 inches from the torsion-bar adjusters and spacers or stiffer coils at the rear, plus an alignment. Members put a basic kit at about CA$700, plus $400–500 to install. Lift blocks on the ball joints top out around 1.5 inches before camber and tire wear suffer. Heartland in Japan sells 4-, 6- and 8-inch kits that modify the spindles. Expect to replace ball joints more often.

SOURCE · DELICA CANADA
What fuel economy should I expect?

Mitsubishi's 2004 Japanese list rates the 2.8 diesel at 8.2–8.7 km/L (about 19–20 US mpg) in the 10-15 lab cycle, depending on weight. Real-world figures with a tall roof box and all-terrain tires will be lower. The tank holds 75 L (about 20 US gal).

SOURCE · JDMBUYSELL · GULLIVER
