Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

Delica L300 Star Wagon problems to check before you buy

  1. Rust is the deal-breakerEvery buyer's guide and forum thread says the same thing: buy the rust-free van, not the one with the better engine. Check the pinch welds along the bottom of the body, the box sections behind the front steps, under the sliding-door step and the rear corners, then the windscreen pillars and roof gutters, which a Delica.ca veteran says are usually the first to go. A bubble outside means far more inside.
  2. Pull up the carpetsWet footwells are a known L300 problem. Water gets in through the front panel seams above the headlamps, the windscreen seal, gutter seams, the antenna base and the washer jets, and one owner found his driver's floor and a seat-mount bolt rusted through from underneath. Feel the carpet and look for that 'wet dog' smell.
  3. Crystal Lite and high roofsGearJunkie warns that Crystal Lite glass roofs are more prone to leaks and rust, and Delica.ca has threads about rusty roofs on 1993 Crystal Lite vans. Check every glass panel seal and the gutters, and look at the roof from a ladder.
  4. Ask about the head and the beltsHead gaskets and cracked heads are the 4D56's known weakness in this van, usually after overheating. Ask for records of the timing belt, balance-shaft belt and water pump. Watch the temperature gauge on a long climb, look for bubbles or pressure in the coolant overflow and check for white smoke.
  5. Test 2H, 4H and 4L, and both hubsEngage 4H and 4L and drive on loose ground to make sure both front hubs actually lock. One Delica.ca member points out that the clunk of engagement doesn't prove both hubs are working. Automatic hubs need cleaning and greasing, and a seized one is a common find.
  6. Don't trust the LSD stickerA limited-slip rear diff was standard on some grades, and many vans have a door-jamb LSD sticker. Forum members warn that stickers go missing and appear on vans without an LSD. Jack up one rear wheel and spin it: if the other wheel turns the same way, it's a limited slip.
  7. Check the paperwork and your stateGet the import entry documents (CBP and EPA forms) and a clean title. Then confirm your state will register it. Maine revoked Delica registrations in 2021 and Michigan sent owners letters in 2024. Crankshaft Culture also says many mainstream insurers won't cover a Delica at an agreed value, so get a quote first.
  8. Drive it like it'll be drivenA healthy 4D56 is slow but should pull cleanly without heavy black smoke or a clattery 'tractor' noise at idle. That noise can mean a timing belt that has jumped a tooth. On automatics, a harsh 1–2 shift under power can mean a blocked filter screen, and a missing kickdown action can mean a broken kickdown cable.

Owner questions about Delica L300 Star Wagon reliability

Why do L300 head gaskets fail, and how do I prevent it?

Owners blame heat. The 4D56 has an aluminium head and a 21:1 compression ratio, and it has no intercooler. Let it warm up until the temperature needle moves, keep the cooling system healthy, and back off on long climbs. Several owners fit an EGT gauge; one keeps exhaust gas temperature under 1,000 °C, and another under about 1,400 °F. Watch the temperature gauge, not just the speedometer.
