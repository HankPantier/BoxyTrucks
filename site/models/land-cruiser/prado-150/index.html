PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The fourth-generation Prado went on sale in Japan on 14 September 2009 and stayed in production until October 2023, which is long enough to outlast most of the cars it was meant to compete with. Underneath is Toyota's F2 ladder-frame platform, shared with the 5th-gen 4Runner and the FJ Cruiser. America never got a Toyota-badged one. It got the GX 460 instead, which is the same truck with a V8, softer leather and a Lexus price.

MORE ELECTRONICS, SAME FRAME

Toyota's launch release talked about two things: a stiffer version of the traditional full frame, and a lot of new off-road electronics. The 150 introduced KDSS with a new electric accumulator to free up the anti-roll bars, Crawl Control with five speed settings, Multi-Terrain Select, and what Toyota called the world's first Multi-Terrain Monitor, a set of cameras that show the ground around the wheels. A display in the gauges even tells you which way the front wheels are pointing when you've lost track on a rock step.

There were five-door and three-door bodies (the short one is the J155), and the engine list depended on where you lived: the 2.7-litre 2TR-FE four and 4.0-litre 1GR-FE V6 petrols, and the 3.0-litre 1KD-FTV turbo-diesel. Japan dropped the three-door at launch. Australia kept it as the SX and ZR until the 2013 facelift, when slow sales killed it. Some markets put the spare under the floor and others hung it on the tailgate, freeing space for a 63-litre auxiliary tank alongside the 87-litre main one.

THREE FACELIFTS AND A NEW DIESEL

The first facelift arrived in Japan on 10 September 2013 and in Australia that October: a bigger grille, new bumpers and lamps, LED low beams on upper grades, retuned suspension and a fifth Multi-Terrain Select mode. In June 2015 the 1KD-FTV was replaced by the 2.8-litre 1GD-FTV (130 kW and 450 Nm in Australian trim), the V6 gained dual VVT-i, and both moved to the six-speed Aisin AC60F automatic. The 1GD also brought a diesel particulate filter, which became the defining problem of this generation.

The second facelift landed on 12 September 2017 in Japan, Europe and Australia, with a new face and, in Japan, LED headlights and Toyota Safety Sense as standard. Australia kept the V6 until that point. The last big update came in August 2020: a ball-bearing turbo and revised internals took the 1GD-FTV to 150 kW and 500 Nm, braked towing went to 3,000 kg, the manual gearbox was dropped, and a 9-inch screen finally brought Apple CarPlay and Android Auto.

THE DPF YEARS

On paper the 1GD was the better engine. In practice, Prados, HiLuxes and Fortuners with the new diesels built in the DPF era gained a reputation for filters that failed to regenerate, clouds of white smoke and a foul smell. Toyota made a manual regeneration switch standard from June 2018 and developed a hardware and software repair in May 2020, but owners went to court anyway. The case ran for seven years (see the sidebar) and ended in a $750 million settlement agreed in October 2026.

The older engine has its own story. Unsealed 4X4 says the 1KD-FTV's injector seat washers could fail and, combined with weaker piston crowns than earlier engines, crack a piston. Toyota's advice became: don't tune it, service the injectors around every 100,000 km and don't work it hard for long periods. Both stories are known, though, and both have fixes, which is why a well-documented 150 still sells quickly.

HOW IT ENDED

By the time the 150 bowed out, Toyota Australia said it had sold more than 320,000 Prados there since 1996, and the Prado had been the country's best-selling large SUV in ten of the previous twelve years. It was replaced by the boxy J250 Prado, sold in the US as the 250 Series Land Cruiser, which moved to the TNGA-F platform.

That leaves the 150 as the last Prado on the old F2 frame, and the most common one on the touring trails of Australia, Africa and the Middle East. In America it's still a gray-market curiosity. The earliest trucks don't turn 25 until late 2034, so for now the closest you can legally get is a GX 460.

SIDEBAR · WILLIAMS V TOYOTA

The seven-year DPF class action

The class action began in 2019 on behalf of buyers of 2.8-litre 1GD-FTV and 2.4-litre 2GD-FTV HiLux, Prado and Fortuner models, 264,170 vehicles sold between October 2015 and April 2020. In April 2022 Justice Michael Lee of the Federal Court found the vehicles weren't of acceptable quality under Australian Consumer Law and put the loss in value at 17.5% of the average retail price. Carsales reported that this could mean more than $7,000 per owner.

The Full Federal Court cut that to 10% in March 2023. In November 2024 the High Court overturned the cut and sent the case back for reassessment. In October 2026 Toyota agreed to a $750 million settlement, subject to court approval, covering the original claim and a second action for later private buyers. If you're shopping for a 2015–2020 diesel, ask whether the 2020 exhaust and software fix has been done.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The 150 uses a ladder frame (Toyota's F2 platform) with independent front suspension and a coil-sprung solid rear axle, the same layout as the GX 460 and 5th-gen 4Runner. Full-time 4WD runs through a two-speed transfer case and a Torsen centre differential that can be locked. Higher grades add KDSS, adaptive dampers and rear air springs with self-levelling. The headline electronics were Crawl Control, Multi-Terrain Select and the Multi-Terrain Monitor camera system.

PART 03

Buying one

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Which diesel, and what's been doneA 1KD-FTV (2009–2015) needs its timing belt at 150,000 km, and Unsealed 4X4 recommends servicing the injectors every 100,000 km. Listen for a cold-start knock or rattle and look for black smoke, which can mean the injectors are going. A 1GD-FTV (2015-on) has a chain but a DPF. Paperwork matters more than the odometer.
  2. 02
    DPF history on 2015–2020 dieselsAsk whether the truck has the manual DPF regeneration switch (standard from June 2018) and the free 2020 exhaust hardware and software fix. Heavy white smoke, a foul smell, limp mode or rising fuel use point to a filter that isn't regenerating. A truck used only for short city trips is the riskiest.
  3. 03
    Dust in the 2.8's intakeUnsealed 4X4 says the 1GD's airbox seal can let fine dust past the filter and coat the MAF sensor, which puts the truck in limp mode. Pull the filter and look for dust on the clean side.
  4. 04
    Early brakesUnsealed 4X4 reports that pre-2012 trucks could get a spongy or very hard pedal with a hiss from the booster. Toyota revised the booster and master cylinder, and the common fix is to fit the later parts.
  5. 05
    Suspension bushes and rear airLower shock bushes and lower control-arm bushes wear, especially if aftermarket suspension was tightened with the truck hanging on a hoist. Kakadu rear air springs leak with age. Auto Buyers Guide says factory shocks are often tired by 100,000 km.
  6. 06
    Tailgate and dashThe tailgate-mounted spare can make the door sag, so open and close it. Auto Buyers Guide also reports cracked dash tops on early trucks that lived in the sun.
  7. 07
    2009 and 2017 electronicsUnsealed 4X4 flags the launch year and the 2017 facelift year for electrical gremlins, including airbag recalls on 2009 trucks and head-unit, ESC-light and shift complaints on 2017s. Look for evidence that they've been sorted.
  8. 08
    Underbody and towing lifeCheck sills, bash plates and chassis rails for off-road damage and coastal rust, and ask what it has towed. Many 150s have pulled caravans at or near their 2,500–3,000 kg limit for years.

Spotting the real thing

The 150 was sold under several names and with several engines, and its US twin wears a Lexus badge. The model code tells you what you're looking at.

  1. ID
    Model codesKDJ150/155 = 1KD-FTV 3.0 diesel. GDJ150 = 1GD-FTV 2.8 diesel. TRJ150/152 = 2TR-FE 2.7 petrol. GRJ150 = 1GR-FE 4.0 V6. GRJ152 = 3.5 V6 (China). URJ150 = Lexus GX 460. A 155 suffix is the short three-door.
  2. ID
    Market namesIn the UK the J150 was sold as the Land Cruiser LC3, LC4 and LC5 by equipment level. Australian grades were GX, GXL, Altitude (from 2012), VX and Kakadu, plus the three-door SX and ZR until 2013.
  3. ID
    Facelift tells2013-on trucks have the larger grille and new lamps. 2017-on trucks have the second face with the vertical-bar grille. 2020-on Australian trucks have the 9-inch CarPlay screen.
  4. ID
    DPF countermeasuresOn a 2015–2020 diesel, look for the manual DPF regeneration switch (standard from June 2018) and ask a Toyota dealer whether the 2020 remedy is recorded against the VIN.
  5. ID
    Spare and sub-tankTrucks with the spare on the tailgate keep the 63-litre sub-tank. Under-floor spare versions give it up.

Importing & legality

Toyota never sold the Prado 150 in the US. Americans got the Lexus GX 460 instead. NHTSA lets vehicles at least 25 years old be imported without meeting federal standards, and the first 150s were built in late 2009, so they don't become eligible until late 2034.

Until then, a US buyer who wants a J150 should buy a GX 460. Many Prado-specific parts, from the front end to bumpers, have been fitted to GXs by owners on IH8MUD.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Asked on every forum

Land Cruiser Prado 150 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

Owner consensus on the Lexus GX 460, the US twin with the same body and suspension, is about 32 inches. 255/70R18 fits with no rubbing, 265/70R18 touches the plastics at full lock, and on 17s a 275/65R17 shows only a slight liner rub at full turn. Prado bumpers and grades differ, so test-fit before you buy. 33s need a lift, and 35s need a 2–2.5 in lift plus upper control arms, a body-mount chop and trimming.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · GXOR (GX 460)
Is it the same as a Lexus GX 460?

Underneath, yes. Wikipedia lists the Lexus GX 400/460 as the 150's twin. The US-market GX 460 uses the 1UR-FE 4.6 V8, which no Toyota-badged Prado got. The Prado's engines are the 2.7 and 4.0 petrols, the 1KD-FTV 3.0 and 1GD-FTV 2.8 diesels, and a 3.5 V6 in China.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
How much can it tow?

Early trucks are rated at 2,500 kg braked. After the August 2020 update, Toyota Australia quotes a maximum of 3,000 kg braked. Carsales' tow test recommends staying under about 2,500 kg in practice.

SOURCE · TOYOTA AUSTRALIA · CARSALES
Timing belt or chain?

The 1KD-FTV 3.0 diesel (2009–2015) has a belt, due at 150,000 km. The 1GD-FTV 2.8 diesel (2015-on) has a chain.

SOURCE · UNSEALED 4X4
Does my 2.8 have the DPF problem?

Diesels sold in Australia between October 2015 and April 2020 were covered by the class action. Toyota made a manual regeneration switch standard from June 2018 and has offered a free hardware and software fix since May 2020. Ask a dealer to check the VIN.

SOURCE · 4X4 AUSTRALIA
Which years are best?

Unsealed 4X4's summary: 2009–2012 are cheaper but have the early issues, 2013–2015 are a sweet spot for many buyers, 2015–2019 get the 2.8 but in the early DPF era, and 2020-on are the most desirable, with 150 kW and CarPlay. It also flags 2009 and 2017 for electrical gremlins.

SOURCE · UNSEALED 4X4
What fuel economy should I expect?

The 2020 2.8 diesel is rated at 7.9 L/100 km (ADR combined). Carsales saw 7.2 L/100 km on easy freeway running and 13.4 L/100 km towing a 2,300 kg caravan.

SOURCE · TOYOTA AUSTRALIA · CARSALES
How much fuel does it carry?

150 litres: an 87-litre main tank and a 63-litre sub-tank. On trucks with the spare under the floor instead of on the tailgate, the spare takes the sub-tank's place.

SOURCE · TOYOTA AUSTRALIA · CARSALES
What is KDSS and which grades have it?

The Kinetic Dynamic Suspension System hydraulically links the front and rear anti-roll bars. It firms them up on the road and frees them off-road for more articulation. The 150 introduced it with an electric accumulator. In Australia it's on the VX and Kakadu.

SOURCE · TOYOTA · AUTO BUYERS GUIDE
Can I import one to the US?

Not yet. Toyota never sold the Prado 150 in the US, and NHTSA's 25-year exemption means the first trucks (built from October 2009) don't become eligible until late 2034. Check your state's emissions rules even then.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · THE DRIVE
Is the 1KD engine reliable?

Mostly, if it's been serviced. Its known weakness is failing injector seat washers, which can crack a piston, so injector servicing is part of normal maintenance. Unsealed 4X4 notes that only a small percentage of Australian Prados were affected.
