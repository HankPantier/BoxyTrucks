Land Cruiser 70 Series

“The 70 is the Land Cruiser Toyota forgot to stop building, and nobody who needs one has ever complained. Leaf springs, a part-time transfer case, manual hubs and a cabin with all the sound deadening of a grain silo: it is a farm tool that happens to cross continents.”

PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The 70 Series arrived in 1984 to replace the 25-year-old 40 Series as the Land Cruiser that does the hard work, while the 60 Series wagon line drifted toward comfort and, eventually, the 80 Series. More than four decades later it is still in production. It has been built in Japan, Portugal, Venezuela and Colombia, and it has served as a troop carrier, a mine truck and a military patrol vehicle. What it has never been is a new car you can buy at an American Toyota dealer.

A 40 SERIES IN A SQUARER SUIT

The styling kept a family resemblance to the 40 but went more angular, and the range came in every length a farmer could want. Wikipedia's breakdown is the one owners use: 70 and 71 were the short wheelbase, 73 and 74 the medium, 75 and later 78 and 79 the long-wheelbase pickups and troop carriers, and 76 and 77 the semi-long four-door wagons. The letters in front give the engine away, so an FJ70 has the 3F petrol six, an HJ75 the 2H diesel, and an HZJ75 the 1HZ.

There were lighter-duty 70s as well, sold as the Bundera, the Land Cruiser II and eventually the Prado. That branch grew softer with each update, and by 1997 the Prado had become the 90 Series with no real connection to the workhorse. Some markets got odd variants, too: Portugal, Spain and Italy received a BJ73 with a VM Motori five-cylinder turbo-diesel, built by Salvador Caetano in Portugal.

LEAVES, COILS AND A V8

The big mechanical change came with the August 1999 facelift. The front axle moved from leaf springs to coils with leading arms, the rear leaves got longer for more articulation and a better ride, wheels went from six studs to five, and the long-wheelbase models were renamed 78 (troop carrier) and 79 (pickup). The short-wheelbase truck disappeared from most markets, and Japanese sales ended in June 2004 because the truck couldn't meet Japan's NOx and particulate rules.

In 2007 Toyota fitted the 4.5-liter 1VD-FTV turbo-diesel V8. It needed a wider chassis, a wider front axle and a new nose that finally did away with the flat fenders every commercial Land Cruiser had worn since the 40. The four-door 76 wagon was added to the range, and a dressed-up GXL trim arrived. Petrol 70s swapped the 1FZ six for the 1GR-FE V6 in 2009, and a double-cab 79 with a 130-liter tank followed in 2012.

THE TRUCK THAT WON'T RETIRE

In Australia, where the 70 is part of the furniture, the V8 picked up a diesel particulate filter and piezo injectors in late 2016 to meet Euro 5. The same update gave the single-cab ute curtain and driver's-knee airbags and a thicker, stronger chassis, which earned it five ANCAP stars. In 2023 came a 2.8-liter four-cylinder turbo-diesel with a six-speed automatic, LED headlights and a 40 Series-style gauge cluster. Japan, which had already given the 70 a one-year 30th-anniversary run in 2014–15, put it back on sale in November 2023.

The V8 didn't survive. Toyota Australia ended production of the V8 wagon and troop carrier in September 2024, citing 'changing regulations and community expectations,' and kept only the GXL pickups going into 2025. The four-cylinder makes 201 hp and 369 lb-ft (500 Nm) with the automatic, and from October 2024 it was also offered with a five-speed manual, detuned to 332 lb-ft. Unsealed 4X4's verdict is that plenty of enthusiasts still prefer the pre-DPF 2007–2016 V8s, mainly because they're mechanically simpler.

HOW AN AMERICAN GETS ONE

Toyota never offered the 70 in the United States. Canada got a few BJ70s for the 1985–87 model years with the 3B and 13B-T diesels, and as of 2022 Canadian mines could still buy them through Toyota Industrial Sales Canada. For everyone else in North America, the route is the gray market. Since 1989 NHTSA has exempted vehicles more than 25 years old from the rules it administers, so each year a new batch of older 70s becomes legal to import.

That's why the IH8MUD 70-Series Tech forum is full of American owners with BJ74s, HJ75s and 1HZ troopies, and why the 80 Series and the 70 often share a driveway. If you want the 80's toughness without the 80's comfort, the 70 is the purer version. It also makes the 100 Series and 200 Series look positively soft.

SIDEBAR · THE 70 GOES TO WAR

The Al-Thalab and other militarized 79s

The same qualities that make the 79 a good station truck — a heavy-duty chassis, simple mechanicals that can be fixed in the field and little reliance on electronics — made it the base for a long-range patrol vehicle. In 2000 the UK firm Jankel, working with Jordan's King Abdullah Design and Development Bureau, started designing the Al-Thalab on the Land Cruiser 79 chassis.

In base form it carries up to four crew and 1,700 kg of equipment, with three firing positions and two radio stations. Jordan's army ordered it in 2004 and now runs more than 200, Mauritania has used it since 2006, and British special forces were seen in Al-Thalabs in Syria in 2016. More recently, Babcock built its GLV on the 79 chassis and France's Technamm Masstech T4 used the HZJ76.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

Under the boxy body is a heavy-duty ladder frame, in more wheelbases than any other Land Cruiser line, with live axles at both ends. Every heavy-duty 70 has a leaf-sprung rear axle. The front ran on leaf springs until 1999 and on coils with leading arms after that. Drive goes through a part-time transfer case with high and low range and manual locking hubs, and Toyota offered selectable front and rear lockers in some markets.

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Check the A-pillars for rustUnsealed 4X4 says rust usually starts from the inside, so look for bubbling and flaking paint and run a magnet over suspect areas. Coastal trucks rot in the A-pillars and let water in. The magazine knows of one owner who paid nearly A$15,000 to replace both pillars, so its advice is to walk away.
  2. 02
    Look at the rear spring hangersTrucks that regularly ran over GVM on rough roads can crack at the weld where the rear spring hanger meets the frame. Look for cracks, fresh welds or reinforcement plates, and listen for a clunk under load.
  3. 03
    Open the airboxOn the V8, a weak airbox seal lets dust past the filter. Unclip the lid and check the clean side of the filter: if there's dust there, Unsealed says the engine is on borrowed time.
  4. 04
    Shift through every gearCrunching between second and third points to worn synchros. Unsealed 4X4 blames GL-5 oil, whose extreme-pressure additives attack the yellow-metal synchro rings, and says to use GL-4 75W-90. Other spec sheets list GL-4 or GL-5, so check what your manual calls for.
  5. 05
    Test the clutch on tuned trucksThe stock clutch is built for stock power. If the revs rise without the truck accelerating, or it bites at the very top of the pedal, budget for a heavy-duty clutch.
  6. 06
    Jack up the front wheelsGrab each front tire at 12 and 6 o'clock and rock it. Play or a hum that rises to a howl with speed points to front wheel bearings, which work hard on heavy, big-tired 70s.
  7. 07
    Ask about the DPF (late-2016-on V8s)Look for DPF warning lights, smoke or a history of limp mode, especially on trucks that mostly did short trips.
  8. 08
    Importing? Check the build dateFor a US import, NHTSA's exemption is based on age: the truck must be more than 25 years old. Check the build plate before you pay, and remember that a lot of imports have spent their lives working on farms or mine sites.

Spotting the real thing

The '70' badge covers forty years of engines, wheelbases and body styles, and the US never sold one new. Every truck here is a gray import, so make sure the code on the paperwork matches the metal.

  1. ID
    Model code = engine + wheelbaseThe prefix letters name the engine: FJ = 3F petrol, FZJ = 1FZ petrol, BJ = 3B diesel, HJ = 2H, HZJ = 1HZ, PZJ = 1PZ, LJ = 2L family, KZJ = 1KZ, VDJ = 1VD-FTV V8, GRJ = 1GR V6, GDJ = 1GD. The numbers give the body: 70/71 short wheelbase, 73/74 medium, 75/78/79 long (pickup and Troop Carrier), 76/77 four-door semi-long wagons.
  2. ID
    Pre- or post-1999From the August 1999 facelift, trucks have a coil-sprung front axle and 5-stud wheels. Earlier trucks have leaf springs at the front and 6-stud wheels. The three-bar grille with upturned ends also dates from that facelift.
  3. ID
    Not every '70' is a heavy-duty 70From 1985 to 1996, Toyota also sold lighter-duty models under the 70 designation, called Bundera, Land Cruiser II and later Prado. They grew into the separate 90-series Prado, so don't pay heavy-duty money for one.
  4. ID
    V8 front clip2007-on V8 trucks lose the flat front fenders and get a wider front track and chassis. If a 'VDJ' still has the old flat-fender nose, look harder.
  5. ID
    LockersSelectable front and rear diff locks were factory options in some markets, not standard everywhere. Confirm working factory lockers rather than trusting the ad.

Importing & legality

Toyota never sold the 70 Series new in the United States. Canada got small numbers of BJ70s for the 1985–87 model years, and later trucks only through Toyota Industrial Sales for the mining industry. Every US 70 is a gray import.

Since 1989, NHTSA has exempted vehicles over 25 years old from its rules, so the window rolls forward each year. That's why US auction results are full of 1980s and 1990s FJ, BJ, LJ, HZJ and FZJ trucks, and why late-1990s and 2000-ish HZJ79s and HZJ76s now appear. California's smog rules make registering an overseas classic difficult there even when the federal 25-year rule is met, so check your state's titling and emissions rules before you buy.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Common repairs

The jobs owners actually end up doing, rated for a competent home mechanic with a decent tool kit. Always confirm torque figures, fluids and procedures against the factory service manual for your VIN, and work safely — support the truck on stands, never just a jack. THE FIXThe 70 uses the same tapered-roller hub design Toyota has used since the 40 Series. Remove the locking hub, take off the lock nut with a 54 mm socket and tighten the bearing back up. SYMPTOMSCrunching shifts, especially between second and third.
THE FIXDrain the gearbox and refill with GL-4 75W-90, which is what Unsealed specifies for the V8 trucks (confirm the grade in your manual, since some spec sheets allow GL-4 or GL-5). Unsealed warns that the extreme-pressure additives in GL-5 attack the yellow metal in the synchros. Pause briefly mid-shift and avoid forcing shifts when the box is cold. If it still crunches, plan a rebuild before the gear teeth get damaged, and do the clutch at the same time.
TOOLS & PARTSDrain and fill plug wrenches · fluid pump · GL-4 75W-90 gear oil.
WHERE TO BUY PARTSRed Line MT-90 75W-90 GL-4 gear oil · Red Line ↗Amazon ↗

Asked on every forum

Land Cruiser 70 Series questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

The IH8MUD consensus for a stock-height long-wheelbase 70 is 235/85R16, about 31.7 inches. 285/75R16 (33 inches) will go on, but on factory steel wheels owners report it rubs on the inside at full lock unless you change wheel offset. Short-wheelbase owners in the same thread treat 255/85R16 (about 33 inches) as the sensible upper limit, and the one running them has a lift. Sagging old springs make everything rub sooner.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
Can I buy one in the US?

Not new. Toyota never sold the 70 in the United States. Canada got a few BJ70s for the 1985–87 model years and, as of 2022, Canadian mining companies could still buy them. Americans import older trucks under the rule NHTSA set in 1989 exempting vehicles more than 25 years old.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What do 76, 78 and 79 mean?

Body and wheelbase. Since 1999, 78 is the long-wheelbase troop carrier and 79 the long-wheelbase pickup (single and, from 2012, dual cab); the 76 is the four-door wagon. Earlier, 70/71 were short wheelbase, 73/74 medium, 75 long, and 77 a semi-long wagon.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Which years and engines are best?

Unsealed 4X4 says many enthusiasts prefer the 2007–2016 V8s, which don't have a DPF, but that service history matters more than the year. For US imports, the age rule means older trucks, typically with the 1HZ, 2H, 3B or 13B-T diesels or the 3F and 1FZ petrol sixes.

SOURCE · UNSEALED 4X4 · WIKIPEDIA
Did it come with lockers?

Selectable front and rear lockers were factory options in some markets. Otherwise ARB Air Lockers and Harrop electric lockers are the common add-ons; one IH8MUD owner runs Harrop E-lockers in a BJ74.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · IH8MUD
Is there an automatic?

Rarely. Most 70s are manual, but a four-speed auto was offered on a few versions: FJ73 (1985–1992), BJ74, HZJ73/77 (1990–1999) and HZJ74/76K (1999–2004). The 2.8 L four that arrived in 2023 came with a six-speed automatic.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Does it have a DPF?

Australian V8s got a diesel particulate filter from September 2016 to meet Euro 5. Earlier V8s don't have one. Unsealed recommends a good highway run every 1,000–2,000 km to let it regenerate.

SOURCE · UNSEALED 4X4
Why are the rear wheels inboard of the fronts on V8 trucks?

To make room for the 1VD-FTV, Toyota fitted a wider front axle and frame in 2007, which left the front and rear tracks different. Superior Engineering's coil conversion is one of the aftermarket fixes Unsealed mentions for equalizing them.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · UNSEALED 4X4
Is the V8 really gone?

In Australia, yes. V8 wagon and troop carrier production ended in September 2024, and the GXL pickups were the last V8s, shipping until late 2025. The 2.8 L four replaced it: 201 hp and 369 lb-ft with the six-speed automatic, or 332 lb-ft with the five-speed manual added in October 2024.

SOURCE · MOTOR1
Is it reliable?

Overall, yes, says Unsealed: a strong engine and a tough driveline, with many trucks running very high mileage. The known weak points are mostly about use and comfort: DPFs on late V8s, clutches on tuned trucks, gearbox synchros, front wheel bearings, dust past the airbox, leaky door seals, weak AC and frame cracks on overloaded pickups.

SOURCE · UNSEALED 4X4
Leaf springs or coils up front?

Leaf springs up to the 1999 facelift, then coils with leading arms. The rear stayed on leaf springs.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
