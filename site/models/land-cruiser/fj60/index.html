PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Toyota introduced the 60 Series in 1980, in the middle of the second oil crisis, to compete in a market that was just starting to call itself 'SUV.' It replaced the FJ55 Iron Pig and kept the old recipe underneath: ladder frame, leaf springs and solid axles. What changed was the cabin. Wikipedia lists air conditioning, a rear heater and an upgraded interior. Toyota UK adds that it was the first Land Cruiser with sound insulation and wall-to-wall upholstery, plus a front anti-roll bar and softer springs.

A WAGON FOR THE EIGHTIES

The new body was rounder, with front wings integrated into the bodywork for the first time and a wide-set grille. An optional high-roof version arrived in 1981, the same year Land Cruiser sales passed one million. Toyota offered five-, six-, eight-, nine- and ten-seat layouts in different markets, and Flexmotor says the 60 was the first Land Cruiser offered in named trim levels (STD, GX and, later, VX). Silodrome compares the step up in comfort to a Range Rover of the period, which is generous, but not by as much as you'd think.

American FJ60s kept the 4.2-liter 2F straight-six from the FJ40, carbureted and rated at 135 hp and 210 lb-ft, with a four-speed manual and a two-speed, part-time transfer case. Other markets moved faster. Wikipedia dates the five-speed to 1982 (Toyota UK says 1983), and says the 4.0-liter carbureted 3F replaced the 2F outside North America in 1984 (as the FJ61), and the 12H-T turbo-diesel appeared in 1985. US buyers got none of that and stuck with the 2F until the FJ62 arrived for 1988.

LIVING WITH ONE

On the road the FJ60 is slow; Corsetti Cruisers says it tops out around 65 mph. In the dirt it is the same tough truck as the 40, with closed-knuckle Birfield front axles and plenty of clearance. The weak points are age and the smog system. Corsetti lists carburetor rebuilds, tangled vacuum lines, knuckle sludge, steering wobble on lifted trucks, worn engines past 200,000 miles and, above all, rust. Its rust list starts with the windshield corners and ends with the rear wheel wells, which it calls the place every 60 starts to rot.

1981–87 trucks also carry a High Altitude Compensation system that, Corsetti says, advances ignition timing above about 3,930 feet. Many owners have replaced the factory carburetor with a Weber, but Corsetti recommends sticking with the factory carb because the Weber bypasses HAC and brings emissions and jetting headaches. The other common upgrade is a five-speed. In April 1985 Toyota lengthened the H42 four-speed with a spacer where the H55F's fifth gear sits, so on later trucks the five-speed is close to a direct swap.

THE COLLECTOR'S 60

The FJ60 gave way to the fuel-injected, automatic FJ62 for the 1988 US model year, and Toyota UK says the 60 Series ended Japanese production in December 1989 after 406,700 were built (Wikipedia says 1990), and Venezuela kept assembling them until 1992. For years they were cheap old wagons. Hagerty now calls the round-headlight, manual FJ60 the more collectible of the two, and its wagon-values piece (around 2020) put the median quote for FJ60s and FJ62s at $18,500, with demand apparently overtaking that for Jeep Grand Wagoneers.

That interest has spawned a restomod industry. Shops such as Corsetti Cruisers build FJ60s with modern engines and interiors, while IH8MUD's 60 Series Wagons forum remains the place to learn how to keep a stock one running. The 80 Series replaced the whole line in 1990, swapping leaf springs for coils, but the FJ60 has its own following of owners who like its simplicity.

SIDEBAR · THE ONE AMERICA DIDN'T GET

HJ61: the 60 with the turbo-diesel

Outside North America, the 60 that people talk about is the HJ61. Wikipedia dates the 12H-T direct-injection turbo-diesel to 1985, and Silodrome describes it as a 3,980 cc straight-six with an 18.6:1 compression ratio and 7 psi of boost, making 134 bhp and about 315 lb-ft. Silodrome calls the GX with this engine the most desirable 60.

None were sold new in the US. Under the 25-year import rule they can now be brought in as grey imports. Silodrome says the 12H-T was offered with a five-speed manual or an automatic, and that collectors prefer the manual.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The FJ60 is a 40 Series drivetrain wearing a family wagon's clothes. It keeps the ladder frame, live axles on leaf springs and closed-knuckle front axle with Birfield joints, and adds a front anti-roll bar, softer springs and power steering. US trucks use the carbureted 2F and an H42 four-speed manual feeding a two-speed, part-time transfer case.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rear frame rails firstCorsetti calls it the most important check: drop the spare if you have to and look at the rear frame rails. If the frame is rusted through, walk away. IH8MUD members agree the rear channel near the spare is usually the worst frame spot.
  2. 02
    The other rust spotsCheck the windshield gasket corners (peel the rubber back a little), roof drip rails, tailgate hinge areas, inner wheel wells behind the rear seat, front fenders, rockers and rear wheel wells. A missing or uneven mud flap can hide a patched fender, and a magnet finds filler.
  3. 03
    Floor under the rear seatIH8MUD buyers' threads add the floor pan under the back seat to the list, along with behind the mud flaps.
  4. 04
    Compression test high-mileage trucksOn trucks past 200,000 miles, or that have sat or had several owners, do a compression test. Corsetti's numbers for the 2F: around 160–165 psi is excellent, under 120 means caution, under 100 means don't buy, with no more than 10 percent spread between cylinders.
  5. 05
    Smog equipment and vacuum linesIH8MUD members call emissions equipment the biggest headache. Check that it's all there, look for cracked or disconnected vacuum hoses, and expect to rebuild the carburetor.
  6. 06
    Knuckles, steering and power-steering leaksSludge at the front knuckles means seals are due. Drive it and feel for wobble, especially on a lifted truck. A shiny, wet driver's-side axle and spring usually means the power-steering box or pump is leaking.
  7. 07
    Budget for rubberHoses, seals, bushings and diaphragms go hard with age, and most will need replacing. One IH8MUD member put catch-up maintenance at several thousand dollars.
  8. 08
    Which four-speed?Look for a spacer between the transmission and transfer case. If it's there, it's a post-4/1985 'long' H42, and a five-speed swap is straightforward.

Spotting the real thing

Plenty of '60s for sale' are FJ62s, imported diesels or five-speed conversions. Know what the US FJ60 actually came with.

  1. ID
    US drivetrainUS FJ60s had the 4.2 L 2F carbureted six and a four-speed H41F/H42F, a gearbox Toyota sold only in the US. A five-speed US truck has been converted.
  2. ID
    Altitude system1981–87 US trucks have High Altitude Compensation emissions gear that works above 3,930 ft. A Weber carb means that system has been bypassed.
  3. ID
    Build date splitSuspension and other parts change at April 1985 production. Check the build date before ordering springs or a lift kit.
  4. ID
    FJ60 vs FJ62The FJ60 is the pre-facelift truck with round headlights and a carburetor. The FJ62 (1988–90) has square headlights, the fuel-injected 3F-E and an automatic.
  5. ID
    DieselsBJ60, HJ60 and HJ61 diesels and the FJ61 with the 3F were never sold new in the US. US-titled examples are imports.

Recalls & service bulletins

Open recalls follow the VIN, not the model. Run yours through NHTSA before you buy, and ask the seller for dealer records showing recall work.

Check a VIN at NHTSA ↗
Notable recalls
Steering tie rod can fracture ↗1981 · NHTSA 81V117000Sustained driving over 50 mph on very rough roads can resonate and crack the tie rod; Toyota replaced the tie rod and relay rod free.
Rear lap belt may not retract ↗1982–1988 · NHTSA 89V155000The rear lap belt can stay partly extended and lock; the fix was a belt guide or sheath.
Fuel tank welds can crack ↗1980–1990 · NHTSA 91V110000Heat and load can pressurize the tank and crack its welds, leaking fuel that can catch fire near an ignition source; the remedy was an improved tank (38,500 vehicles).

Importing & legality

The US got only the gasoline FJ60 (1981–87) and FJ62 (1988–90). BJ60, HJ60 and HJ61 diesels, high-roof wagons and FJ61s are gray-market. Japanese 60 Series production ended in 1990 and Venezuelan production in 1992, so every 60 is now more than 25 years old, which NHTSA measures from the date of manufacture. Corsetti Cruisers warns that California may refuse to register an import without a 50-state-compliant emissions system, so check your state's rules before buying an HJ61.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Asked on every forum

Land Cruiser FJ60 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

33x9.50 fits on stock suspension; one IH8MUD member runs them on a stock FJ62 with 240,000 miles. 33x10.50 might fit, but 33x12.50s won't without a lift: one '87 FJ60 on a 2.5-inch BDS lift still rubbed its inner wheel wells at full stuff. Corsetti runs 31x10.50R15s on stock springs and says not to go wider than 10.5 in or taller than 33 in on a factory rim; Cruiser Corps also advises against tires wider than 10.5 in on the 15x5.5 OEM steel wheel.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · CORSETTI · CRUISER CORPS
FJ60 or FJ62?

The FJ60 (US 1981–87) has round headlights, the carbureted 4.2 L 2F and a four-speed manual. The FJ62 (1988–90) has fuel injection, an automatic, square headlights and a plusher cabin. Hagerty calls the FJ60 the more collectible. The FJ62 is easier to live with in traffic.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · CORSETTI
Did US FJ60s get a five-speed?

No. US trucks used the H41F/H42F four-speed. The H55F five-speed went into other markets from 1982–83, and swapping one in is close to direct on post-April-1985 US trucks.

SOURCE · OFF-ROAD.COM · IH8MUD
What's the High Altitude Compensation system?

It's emissions equipment on 1981–87 trucks that Corsetti says advances ignition timing above about 3,930 feet. A Weber carburetor bypasses it and costs performance, so Corsetti recommends keeping the factory carb.

SOURCE · CORSETTI
How much can it tow?

Corsetti lists 3,500 lb.

SOURCE · CORSETTI
What fuel economy should I expect?

Corsetti lists 11 mpg city and 18 highway with a 23.8-gallon tank. In practice, bigger tires and a roof rack make it worse.

SOURCE · CORSETTI
How do I tell if the engine is tired?

Do a compression test. Corsetti's benchmarks for a 2F are about 160–165 psi when excellent, caution under 120 and walk away under 100, with no more than 10 percent spread. The 2F reportedly reaches 300,000 miles with care.

SOURCE · CORSETTI
What lift should I run?

The classic setup is an Old Man Emu 2-inch kit with greaseable shackles and a steering damper. Kits are split by build date (before or after April 1985) and load rating. Corsetti fits an OME steering damper even on unlifted trucks.

SOURCE · CRUISER CORPS · CORSETTI
Can I import a diesel HJ61?

None were sold new in the US, but the 25-year rule now covers every 60 Series. Silodrome calls the 12H-T-powered GX the most desirable 60. Check EPA and state registration rules before buying.

SOURCE · SILODROME · JDM BUY SELL
What's an FJ60 worth?

Hagerty's wagon-values piece (around 2020) put the median quote for FJ60s and FJ62s at $18,500, called the FJ60 the pick for collectibility and said demand appeared to be overtaking Jeep Grand Wagoneers. Rust-free restored and restomod trucks sell for much more.

SOURCE · HAGERTY
