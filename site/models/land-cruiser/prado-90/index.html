PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

On 8 May 1996 Toyota launched a Land Cruiser that most Americans have never seen in the metal. The second-generation Prado, the J90, was the mid-size Land Cruiser: a ladder-frame wagon with full-time four-wheel drive, coil springs at both ends, and the independent front suspension it shared with the Hilux Surf, the truck North America bought as the 3rd-gen 4Runner. It went on sale in Japan, Australia, Britain and Latin America, but never in the United States. That held for a quarter of a century, until the 25-year import rule started letting them in.

A LAND CRUISER FOR PEOPLE

The first Prado had been a lighter-duty offshoot of the 70 Series. Toyota made the J90 a series of its own and, in its own words, pointed it toward a more passenger-oriented four-wheel drive. There were two bodies: a three-door, five-seat short wheelbase on 2,370 mm and a five-door, eight-seat long wheelbase on 2,675 mm. Toyota gave the short one a sportier face and the long one a more rugged one. The drivetrain switched from part-time to full-time 4WD, and the suspension (double wishbones at the front, a four-link coil-sprung axle with a lateral rod at the back) was the same as the Hilux Surf's. 'Prado', Toyota notes, is Portuguese for a meadow or field.

FOUR ENGINES AND NO V8

At launch you had a choice of two engines. The new one was the 3.4-liter 5VZ-FE V6 with 185 PS, the same family of V6 Americans got in the 4Runner. The carry-over was the 3.0-liter 1KZ-TE four-cylinder turbo-diesel, which gained an intercooler and 140 PS. A 2.7-liter 3RZ-FE four with 150 PS followed in April 1997. In July 2000 Toyota replaced the 1KZ-TE in Japan with the 1KD-FTV, its new common-rail D-4D: 170 PS and 352 Nm, and the first vehicle that engine ever went into.

Market by market, the line-up varied. Australia launched the Prado in July 1996 with petrol engines only and didn't get a diesel until March 2000, when the 96 kW 1KZ-TE arrived with a five-speed manual; an automatic followed that October. 4X4 Australia's buyer's guide, written with hindsight, advises steering clear of the 2.7 four. Britain called the truck the Land Cruiser Colorado, to tell it apart from the bigger 'Amazon', and Venezuela sold the three-door as the Toyota Merú.

THE 1999 FACELIFT

Toyota gave the J90 its minor change on 9 June 1999. Both bodies got big multi-reflector headlights and clear-lens lamps front and rear, plus a new grille, bumper, door trim and instruments, including a newly offered Optitron cluster and a Field Monitor display that switched to color. Underneath, Toyota added 'Active TRC' traction control for loose and muddy surfaces, and from August 1999 vehicle stability control on the main automatic grades. A TX Limited special edition arrived at the same time with roof rails, wood-grain trim and the Optitron gauges. Five-door trucks grew slightly, to 4,690 mm long.

WHY AMERICANS ARE BUYING THEM NOW

The J90 was replaced in October 2002 by the Prado 120, although Sofasa kept assembling it in Colombia until 2009. In the US it stayed off-limits until each truck turned 25, counted from its month of manufacture, so the first J90s became legal imports in 2021. In January 2022 IH8MUD started a 90 Series FAQ because 'Prado 90's [were] finally available for US import'. By 2026 Bring a Trailer had a model page for them, and most of the trucks sold there went for $10,000 to $16,000.

Living with one in America is easier than you'd think. The front suspension and plenty of hardware are shared with the 3rd-gen 4Runner. IH8MUD members have fitted 4Runner front rotors (Toyota 43512-35210), the rear rotors match the 80 Series, and US Land Cruiser specialists now stock 9x Prado ball joints, brakes and axle kits. The rest is a job for a Japanese parts catalogue and a VIN or frame number. Just remember the Prado has rear discs where many 4Runners have drums, and that its rear axle shafts have a different bolt pattern.

SIDEBAR · THE PRADO ON TRACKS

The 1999 Prado Crawler

On 22 October 1999 Toyota announced a factory 'Crawler' version of the Prado (and a matching Hilux Surf) through its TECS special-vehicle program. It went on sale on 1 November. Each road wheel was replaced by a triangular, rubber-belted crawler unit, turning a 3.4 V6 TZ into a snow vehicle for passengers and cargo. Araco built the bodies, and the crawler units could be swapped back for wheels outside the snow season.

Because the controls were unchanged, Toyota said the Crawler could be driven on an ordinary Japanese licence, unlike purpose-built tracked snow vehicles. The tracks added 440 kg (2,420 kg against the donor's 1,980 kg) and raised ground clearance from 230 mm to 330 mm. A speedometer switch corrected the reading between tracks and wheels. Launch price was ¥5,240,000 before tax.

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The J90 is a body-on-frame wagon with double-wishbone independent front suspension and a four-link, coil-sprung live rear axle with a lateral rod, the same layout as the Hilux Surf and 3rd-gen 4Runner. Unlike those trucks, every Prado has full-time four-wheel drive with a lockable center differential. Wheels are 6 on 139.7 mm with M12 x 1.5 nuts.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Ask when the lower ball joints were doneAustralia recalled a range of five-door Prados in May 2005 (PRA 2005/7749) because a scratched joint could wear and separate from the knuckle. AustralianCar.Reviews says joints still fail after the recall, and IH8MUD owners replace them preventively with genuine Toyota parts, because a joint can feel tight and still let go.
  2. 02
    Diesel: hunt for signs of overheatingOn the 1KZ-TE, overheating is how heads crack. Look for coolant bubbling into the overflow bottle, coolant loss or discoloration, and a gauge that climbs on hills or in traffic. Known causes include a tired viscous fan hub, a blocked radiator and a seized wastegate actuator. On a 260,000 km truck, one IH8MUD member's verdict was that 'the head on the 1kz has already been done', so ask for the receipt.
  3. 03
    Check the ATF for coolantThe automatic's oil cooler sits inside the engine radiator, so a crack lets coolant into the transmission. Pink, milky fluid on the dipstick is a deal-breaker, or at least a new radiator and a transmission rebuild.
  4. 04
    Rear axle flange recall (early trucks)Prados built from 17 April 1996 to 6 January 1999 were recalled in Australia (PRA 2006/8499) because the rear axle-shaft flange could crack after repeated hard driving on winding roads, and in the worst case let the wheel come off. A Japanese import won't have the paperwork, so inspect the flanges.
  5. 05
    Know which engine you're buyingThe model code tells you: VZJ = 3.4 V6, RZJ = 2.7 four, KZJ = 1KZ-TE diesel, KDJ = 1KD-FTV diesel (July 2000 on, Japan). '90' is the three-door, '95' the five-door. The V6 has a timing belt, and the 2.7 a chain.
  6. 06
    Timing belt historyHD Automotive gives the 1KZ-TE belt interval as 100,000 km or 5–6 years, and the 5VZ-FE V6 is a 90,000-mile belt. With no receipt, budget for the belt and water pump on day one.
  7. 07
    Import paperworkA US truck should come with proof that it was at least 25 years old on entry (the date runs from manufacture), the customs entry and the NHTSA HS-7 and EPA 3520-1 declarations. Check that your state will title and register it before you buy, especially California.
  8. 08
    Test the sub tank and CV bootsOn trucks with a sub tank, check that the switch-over works: AustralianCar.Reviews warns the mechanism can seize and the fuel go stale if it was never used. While you're underneath, look for split CV boots and grease on the inside of the front wheels.

Spotting the real thing

Every US J90 is a gray import, and the model code tells you what you're buying. Make sure the code on the paperwork matches the engine and body in front of you.

  1. ID
    Model code = engine + bodyVZJ = 3.4 V6, RZJ = 2.7 four, KZJ = 1KZ-TE diesel, KDJ = 1KD-FTV diesel. 90 is the three-door, 95 the five-door. Japanese codes carry a 'W' suffix (e.g. VZJ95W).
  2. ID
    1KZ or 1KD?Toyota fitted the common-rail 1KD-FTV from July 2000 in Japan, so a KDJ95 should be a mid-2000-or-later truck. 4X4 Australia lists the Australian J90 diesel as the 1KZ-TE, from March 2000.
  3. ID
    Pre- or post-faceliftTrucks built after the June 1999 minor change have large multi-reflector headlights, clear-lens indicators and a revised grille and bumper. Five-doors grew from 4,675 mm to 4,690 mm.
  4. ID
    Japanese gradesJapanese three-door grades start with R (RZ, RX) and five-door grades with T (TZ, TX, TX Limited). Australian five-doors were sold as RV, RV6, GXL and VX Grande.
  5. ID
    LockersEvery J90 has a center-diff lock. Australian trucks had no factory axle lockers, so an ad claiming 'factory lockers' needs proof.

Importing & legality

Toyota never sold the J90 in the United States; America got its platform twin, the 3rd-gen 4Runner, instead. Every US Prado 90 is a gray import. NHTSA says a vehicle at least 25 years old can be lawfully imported without regard to whether it meets US safety standards, and that the 25 years run from the date of manufacture. A manufacture label, a first-sale invoice or an old registration document can prove it.

J90 production began in May 1996, so the first trucks became eligible in May 2021, and the last 2002 builds qualify during 2027. On the EPA side, Form 3520-1 code E covers vehicles at least 21 years old in original, unmodified configuration (an engine swap only qualifies if it's the same engine model). That makes a J90 federally straightforward. State titling, inspection and emissions are separate, and California is the hardest, so check your state's rules before you buy.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Replace a cracked 1KZ-TE cylinder head
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A weekend for a capable home mechanicSHOP COST · Parts plus a head; AustralianCar.Reviews recommends a genuine Toyota head because some non-genuine heads have had casting faults
SYMPTOMSBubbles or coolant pushing into the overflow bottle, coolant loss, rapid overheating, rough running and lost power. A pressure test may not show the crack.
THE FIXFix whatever made it overheat first (radiator, fan hub, wastegate, hoses), or the new head will crack too. Remove the timing belt, intake and exhaust, injector lines and turbo oil and coolant lines, then the head. Have the block deck checked, fit a genuine head gasket, and reassemble with a fresh timing belt. An IH8MUD member's 'lessons learned' thread is a reminder to put the washers back under the head bolts.
TOOLS & PARTSFull metric socket set · torque wrench and angle gauge · straight-edge and feeler gauges · factory or Gregory's manual for the head-bolt sequence

5VZ-FE timing belt and water pump (V6 trucks)
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ 8–14 hours the first time (AdventureTaco, on the same engine in a Tacoma)SHOP COST · Parts plus a long day
SYMPTOMSNone until it snaps. The 5VZ-FE is non-interference, so a broken belt strands you but shouldn't bend valves. Plan on about 90,000 miles.
THE FIXIt's the same job as on a 3rd-gen 4Runner. Drain the coolant, remove the fan, shroud and drive belts, then the upper timing covers. Hold the crank pulley, break the bolt, pull the pulley and lower cover, and set cylinder 1 to TDC. Replace the belt, idlers, hydraulic tensioner, cam and crank seals, water pump and thermostat. Rotate the engine twice to confirm the marks, then torque the new crank bolt. The 4Runner part numbers below are the common ones, but check them against your Prado's frame number in an EPC.

Rebuild the rear axle seals and bearings
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ A day for both sidesSHOP COST · Seals, bearings and retainers; Cruiser Outfitters sells a complete kit
SYMPTOMSGear oil on the inside of the rear wheel or brakes, a low diff level, or a clunk in reverse after the diff has run low.
THE FIXDrain the diff, remove the wheel and rear brake, unbolt the axle-shaft retainer and pull the shaft. Replace the inner seal in the housing. Once a seal has leaked, the bearing's grease has usually washed out, so IH8MUD members replace the bearing too, which means pressing it and its retainer collar off and on. If the seal is the same superseded part as on a 3rd-gen 4Runner, look up the 'retainer flip' method: the new seal sits further inboard. Refit with new gaskets and O-rings and refill the diff.

Diagnose a sub tank that won't switch
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hours to diagnoseSHOP COST · Usually one valve
SYMPTOMSYou flick the sub-tank switch, the sub-tank light comes on, but the main-tank gauge keeps falling.
THE FIXOn the 1KZ-TE, the lift pump is part of the injection pump, so the lines between tank and pump are under vacuum. An IH8MUD member suggests starting at the fuel-tank switch-over valve (he found it listed as Toyota 77710-60040): check that it gets power when you flick the switch, and that it actually changes over. AustralianCar.Reviews warns that a sub tank that was never used can seize its switching mechanism and leave stale fuel behind, so drain old fuel before you trust it.

Asked on every forum

Land Cruiser Prado 90 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

265/75R16 (about 31.6 in). An IH8MUD member who has owned both a 90 and a 95 says it won't rub on stock suspension unless the springs have sagged, and ExplorOz members add that it needs the 7-inch rims of a GXL-type truck, because it's too wide for the 6-inch rims on base models. 285/75R16 rubs with or without a lift, on the upper control arm and a rear pinch weld. For 33s, the same IH8MUD member suggests a lift and 255/85R16s.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · EXPLOROZ
Can I legally import and register one in the US?

Yes, once it's 25 years old. NHTSA lets a vehicle at least 25 years old in without meeting US safety standards, counting from its date of manufacture, and EPA exempts vehicles at least 21 years old in original configuration. J90 production began in 1996, so the first trucks qualified in 2021, and the last 2002 builds qualify during 2027. Titling and emissions are up to your state, and California is the hardest.

SOURCE · NHTSA · EPA
Is it just a 3rd-gen 4Runner?

Under the skin, nearly. Toyota says the Prado and Hilux Surf (the 4Runner) shared their suspension: double wishbones at the front and a four-link coil rear axle. 4Runner shocks, front rotors and lift kits bolt on. The differences that matter: every Prado has full-time 4WD, it has rear disc brakes, it has a different body, and it offers diesels the 4Runner never got in the US.

SOURCE · TOYOTA · IH8MUD
Which engine should I buy?

The 1KZ-TE diesel is simple but cracks heads if it overheats. The 1KD-FTV (Japan, July 2000 on) has 170 PS and a 16-valve head that an IH8MUD member says is far less crack-prone, but its common-rail injectors cost much more to replace. The 3.4 V6 is the 4Runner's proven 5VZ-FE. 4X4 Australia's advice is to avoid the 2.7 four.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · 4X4 AUSTRALIA
Does it have lockers?

Every J90 has full-time 4WD with a center-diff lock. ExplorOz members say Australian J90s had no factory front or rear axle lockers. On some manual trucks the center diff locks mechanically whenever you select low range, while others have a dash switch. ARB Air Lockers are the usual retrofit.

SOURCE · EXPLOROZ
When is the timing belt due?

HD Automotive gives the 1KZ-TE belt as 100,000 km or 5–6 years (Roughtrax's UK schedule says 60,000 miles or 60 months). The 5VZ-FE V6 belt is about 90,000 miles, and that engine is non-interference. The 2.7 3RZ-FE has a timing chain.

SOURCE · HD AUTOMOTIVE · ROUGHTRAX · WIKIPEDIA
What fluids and capacities does a KZJ95 take?

Per Roughtrax's KZJ90/95 sheet: engine oil 7.7 L with filter (5W-40, API CF); coolant 9.5 L, or 11 L with a rear heater; manual gearbox 2.2 L and transfer case 1.2 L of 75W-90 GL-4/5; front diff 1.1 L and rear 2.9 L (2.6 L of LSD oil if you have an LSD); automatic 3.0 L per drain-and-fill; power steering 0.8 L of Dexron II/III. Confirm against your manual.

SOURCE · ROUGHTRAX
What are the lift options?

Prado-specific kits exist: Dobinsons sells an IMS kit for the Prado 90/95, and an IH8MUD member notes Ironman made one too. Because the front end is the same as a 3rd-gen 4Runner's, 4Runner kits fit. One owner found a 2.5 in OME 4Runner kit gave about 3.5 in on his 90, and he needed a diff drop and stronger front axles. Above 2–3 in, members warn, the CV joints suffer.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
Where do I get parts in the US?

Many wear parts cross over from 3rd-gen 4Runners and Tacomas. For the rest, IH8MUD members look up genuine part numbers by frame number in an online EPC such as Partsouq, Amayama or ToyoDIY. Cruiser Outfitters (CruiserTeq) in Utah stocks 9x Prado ball joints, brakes and axle kits.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
What's one worth?

On Bring a Trailer, 19 J90s sold between December 2024 and October 2026, mostly for $10,000–$16,000. The extremes were $9,500 for a 1996 KZJ90 and $34,500 for a 1997 KZJ90. Diesels dominate the listings.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER
