PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The 20 Series is where the Land Cruiser stopped being a police jeep and became a product. Toyota had renamed its BJ 'Jeep' the Land Cruiser in June 1954, after Willys objected to the use of its trademark, and the second-generation truck that followed in 1955 was redesigned for civilian buyers. Toyota's own lineage pages date the launch to November 1955 (its UK history says August). It got rounder bodywork, a roomier cabin and, within months, the 3.9-litre F straight-six. The short-wheelbase FJ25 is the one everyone remembers, but the family also included the longer FJ28, early B-engined BJ trucks and a run of vans and fire engines.

FROM POLICE JEEP TO EXPORT HERO

The name came first. Wikipedia quotes technical director Hanji Umehara explaining that Toyota's real rival in export markets was Land Rover, so the new name had to sound no less dignified than 'Land Rover.' Toyota's UK history says the first export shipment went to Pakistan in 1954 and Saudi Arabia followed in 1955. That makes the J20 the first Land Cruiser built with export in mind: Toyota says the 20 Series was the generation that began shipping to North America and other overseas markets in large volume. It came with export suspension uprated over the Japanese spec, because, as Toyota UK puts it, Japan had no highways at the time.

Toyota's 75-year history tracks the numbers. Land Cruiser exports went from 98 in 1955 to 518 in 1956, then jumped to 2,502 in 1957, when Toyota tried to make up for weak domestic sales overseas. Venezuela took 52 in autumn 1956 and 795 in 1957 for oilfields and cattle ranches. Burma got 22 in July 1956 as war reparations, and Australia got a dozen in August 1957. Toyota UK adds that after US military trials in 1957, Land Cruisers made up 38.2 percent of all vehicles exported from Japan that year, across every brand. The cheap little Toyota had become Japan's most important export.

THE F ENGINE AND A VERY SIMPLE DRIVELINE

At launch the J20 offered two petrol sixes: the 3.4-litre B from the BJ and the 3.9-litre F, which had previously been used only in the fire-engine chassis. Toyota says the range was cut to the F alone in mid-1956. Toyota's lineage page rates the FJ25's F at 105 PS (net) at 3,200 rpm. A period US dealer checklist posted on IH8MUD pitched the FJ25 against the CJ-5 as having 'almost twice the horsepower at no extra cost.' Wikipedia says the engine was also moved 120 mm forward to free up cabin space, and that the ride improved with longer leaf springs adapted from Toyota's light truck.

The rest is crude by modern standards. Toyota describes the four-wheel-drive J20 as having a one-speed transfer case: no low range, which didn't arrive until the FJ40 in 1960. A four-speed floor-shift manual (as listed by Bring a Trailer, RM Sotheby's and Barrett-Jackson for surviving trucks) did the work instead. Toyota also built a rear-drive version for police patrol work. Wheelbases were 2,285 mm (FJ25) and 2,430 mm (the longer FJ28), with a 2,650 mm chassis added near the end in 1959. Brakes were drums all round.

AMERICA, AUSTRALIA AND BRAZIL

America came late and slowly. Autoblog's visit to the Land Cruiser Heritage Museum describes the first Land Cruiser sold in the US: a 1958 FJ25 ordered by Rose Toyota and delivered at the port, because the dealership wasn't finished yet, to a Long Beach schoolteacher. It was the only Land Cruiser Toyota sold in America that year, alongside 287 Toyopet Crowns. The import figures compiled in IH8MUD's FJ2X registry show 61 Land Cruisers in 1959 and 162 in 1960.

Australia was a bigger deal. Wikipedia credits B&D Motors with importing FJ25/28 cab-chassis trucks from 1959 and says the Land Cruiser was the first Japanese vehicle regularly exported to Australia. A handful went to work for Thiess on the Snowy Mountains Scheme. In Brazil, Toyota began building the FJ25L in São Bernardo in May 1959. Its 75-year history calls this its first full-fledged overseas knockdown production. Brazil switched to Mercedes-Benz diesels and the Bandeirante name in 1962 and kept building the 25-based truck until 1968. Japan replaced the J20 with the 40 Series in 1960.

THE COLLECTOR'S TRUCK

Today the FJ25 is a collector's piece first and an off-roader a distant second. IH8MUD's FJ25 Owners Group runs a worldwide count of surviving 20 Series trucks and a VIN registry, and warns about 'Frankencruisers': FJ40 tubs wearing an FJ25 frame number. Most of the FJ25s sold on Bring a Trailer since 2019 were restored in Colombia or Costa Rica and imported later. On Bring a Trailer, FJ25s sold for $14,001 to $39,000 between 2019 and 2026, and RM Sotheby's got $70,000 for a restored 1960 soft-top in 2019.

SIDEBAR · THE NAME

Why it isn't called a Jeep

Toyota's prototype of 1951 was literally the 'Toyota Jeep BJ', named for its B engine and the American vehicle it imitated. Toyota says the National Police Reserve preferred Mitsubishi's licence-built Willys, so the BJ went into civilian production in 1953. It was renamed in June 1954 because 'Jeep' was a Willys-Overland trademark.

Umehara's reasoning, as Wikipedia quotes him, was aimed at Britain rather than America: 'In England we had another competitor – Land Rover.' The name had already been used on a Studebaker, which ended production the same year. The J20 was the first truck designed from the start to wear it, and the first to carry it abroad in numbers. The Series Land Rover it was named against is still its natural rival in a vintage line-up.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The J20 is a 1950s utility truck in its purest form: a ladder frame, leaf springs on solid axles, a big slow-revving six and a transfer case that only connects the front axle. Many engine parts are shared with early FJ40s, but the chassis, body and much of the trim are unique, which is why surviving FJ25 owners trade parts among themselves rather than at the parts counter.

Buying one

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Prove it's really a 25IH8MUD's FJ25 group has seen FJ40 tubs on rebuilt frames wearing FJ25 VIN plates. Real FJ25 tells include the fuel tank under the seat, round rear wheel cut-outs, a flat floor with no hump for an offset transfer case, a centered rear differential and an FJ25-specific steering center link. Photograph the frame from underneath, especially the crossmembers and spring hangers, before you pay a 25 price.
  2. 02
    Match the numbersThe data plate lists frame and engine numbers, and FJ25 VINs read like 9FJ25-12645L (year digit, model, serial, L for left-hand drive). Registry members note engines weren't installed in strict numerical order, but the block number should sit in the right range for the frame. Check the block stamping against the plate; Bring a Trailer listings photograph both.
  3. 03
    Know where it was restoredMost FJ25s sold in the US recently were refurbished in Colombia, Costa Rica or elsewhere in Latin America and then imported. That's fine, but restoration quality varies. Look for cracked paint at panel joints, swapped seats and later running gear. Get the title and import paperwork in order before you pay.
  4. 04
    Original engine or a 2F?Swaps are common, and the market notices. In June 2025 a 2F-powered 1959 FJ25 sold on Bring a Trailer for $14,250. In July 2026 a refurbished F-powered 1959 sold there for $37,500. A later 2F head on an F block is a 'Frankencruiser' sign that IH8MUD members flag.
  5. 05
    Wheels and FJ25-only partsFJ25 wheels are scarce. IH8MUD members report that 15-inch disc-brake wheels hit the tie-rod ends, 15-inch drum wheels won't clear the FJ25's drum rivets, and most other 16-inch wheels have too small a center bore. Rear spring hangers and bolts, top bows and the pre-1960 toolbox lid (diagonal ribs, five catches) are FJ25-specific, so missing pieces are expensive to find.
  6. 06
    Brakes and hydraulicsSpecter Off-Road lists its FJ25 brake and clutch master-cylinder kits (1-inch bore, cast iron) as discontinued, so check that the hydraulics work before you buy. Some restored trucks have front disc conversions, which make them safer but less original.
  7. 07
    Year details matter to puristsOwners report that 1958-and-earlier trucks (and some 1959s) use a three-bolt front-fender-to-frame pattern, that the 1959 wears the 'Thunderbird' emblem rather than the oval, and that the cradle-style front engine mount and the old toolbox lid both changed around 1960. If you're paying for originality, get the details checked by someone from the owners' group.

Spotting the real thing

FJ25s are rare and valuable enough that FJ40s get dressed up as them. IH8MUD's FJ25 Owners Group keeps a VIN registry, so check against it.

  1. ID
    VIN plate formatOwners' plates read like 9FJ25-12645L or 0-FJ25-21848: a year digit, the model code, a serial number and an L suffix for left-hand drive. The plate also lists the engine number.
  2. ID
    Frame and chassis tellsIH8MUD members point to the under-seat fuel tank, a flat floor with no hump for an offset transfer case, round rear wheel cut-outs, a centered rear differential, FJ25-specific rear spring hangers and an FJ25 steering center link.
  3. ID
    Engine numbersThe registry shows F engines weren't fitted in strict numerical order, but the block number should fall in the right range for the frame. A 2F head or valve cover on an F-numbered block means parts have been mixed.
  4. ID
    Year detailsOwners report three-bolt front-fender mounting on 1958-and-earlier trucks (and some 1959s), the 'Thunderbird' emblem on 1959s, and a toolbox lid with diagonal ribs and five catches that changed around 1960, about when the cradle front engine mount was dropped.

Importing & legality

Toyota sold a small number of FJ25s new in the US: one in 1958, then 61 Land Cruisers in 1959 and 162 in 1960, by the IH8MUD registry's count. Most FJ25s sold in the US since then have come in from Latin America, often after restoration in Colombia or Costa Rica.

Every J20 is far past the 25-year mark, so NHTSA's exemption from federal safety standards applies, counted from the date of manufacture. State rules still vary on titling, VIN inspection and emissions. California is the hardest, so check before you buy, and make sure the import paperwork matches the frame number rather than a loose VIN plate.

SYMPTOMSPulling, a long pedal or brakes that need pumping, on a truck with four drums and no booster.
THE FIXFollowing an IH8MUD FJ25 Owners Group walkthrough: strip each backing plate and set all adjusters to minimum before fitting new shoes. The upper adjuster turns 270 degrees from its flat side and the lower one 180 degrees, with a notch visible from the back. Both shoes are identical, with no leading or trailing shoe. Lube the shoe contact points and the felt washer under the retainer, then fit the horseshoe clip. Hone the wheel cylinders and fit 1-inch cups. Refit the drums and pump the pedal. Then bring each adjuster out until the shoe just drags and back it off slightly: lower adjusters first, then upper.

SYMPTOMSTicking from the top end, oil weeping down the block, or poor running from tight valves.
THE FIXThe F engine has adjustable rockers, and the procedure is the same as on an early FJ40. Warm the engine, find tight and loose valves with go/no-go feeler gauges, then shut it off and adjust cylinder by cylinder with the intake just closed. Use the clearances in your manual. Fit a new cork gasket: the IH8MUD FJ25 part list gives Toyota 11253-60010 for the original cover gasket.
TOOLS & PARTSFeeler gauges · 17 mm wrench · screwdriver · new gasket

Asked on every forum

Land Cruiser J20 / J25 (FJ25) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

There's no forum consensus for the FJ25, and the bigger problem is wheels. Restored trucks on Bring a Trailer have worn 6.00-16 military-style tires, and one 1959 ran 265/75R16 all-terrains on its 16-inch steel wheels. IH8MUD members warn that 15-inch disc-brake wheels hit the FJ25's tie-rod ends and 15-inch drum wheels won't clear the drum rivets. Stay with 16-inch wheels and a period-sized tire unless you want to start modifying the truck.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER · IH8MUD
Does it have low range?

No. Toyota describes the four-wheel-drive J20 as having a one-speed transfer case. The four-speed manual's low first gear has to do the crawling. Toyota's two-speed transfer case arrived with the 40 Series in 1960.

SOURCE · TOYOTA
What engine does it have?

Usually the 3.9-litre F inline-six, which Toyota rates at 105 PS (net) in the FJ25. Early J20s could also have the 3.4-litre B six carried over from the BJ, but Toyota says the range was cut to the F alone in mid-1956.

SOURCE · TOYOTA
What's the difference between FJ25, FJ28 and FJ35?

The FJ25 is the 2,285 mm short wheelbase. The FJ28 is the longer 2,430 mm version, used for pickups and vans. The FJ35V was a four-door van on the 2,650 mm chassis added in 1959. Wikipedia says the FJ35V was built for only nine months in 1960 and that about 60 were made.

SOURCE · TOYOTA · FJ COMPANY · WIKIPEDIA
How many came to the US?

Very few. The first US Land Cruiser was a single 1958 FJ25 sold through Rose Toyota. It's now at the Land Cruiser Heritage Museum. IH8MUD's FJ2X registry lists import figures of 61 Land Cruisers in 1959 and 162 in 1960.

SOURCE · AUTOBLOG · IH8MUD
Is it really a FJ25 or a rebodied FJ40?

Check the frame, not the plate. IH8MUD members point to the under-seat fuel tank, round rear wheel cut-outs, a flat floor without an offset transfer-case hump, a centered rear differential and FJ25-specific spring hangers and steering center link. A VIN plate on a later tub, or a 2F head on an F block, is a warning sign.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
Can I still get parts?

Engine and many service parts are shared with early F-engined FJ40s and still sold by specialists such as Cruiser Corps and Specter Off-Road. FJ25-specific chassis, body and hydraulic parts are another matter: Specter lists its FJ25 master-cylinder, clutch and distributor-cap items as discontinued, so owners trade among themselves through the IH8MUD FJ25 Owners Group.

SOURCE · SPECTER OFF-ROAD · CRUISER CORPS · IH8MUD
How do the brakes work, and are they safe?

They're four-wheel drums with no booster. Each drum has two identical shoes, set with upper and lower cam adjusters. Kept adjusted, they stop like a 1950s truck should, which isn't well by modern standards. Some restored FJ25s sold on Bring a Trailer have front disc conversions.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · BRING A TRAILER
What's one worth?

Bring a Trailer FJ25 sales ran from $14,001 (2024) to $39,000 (2021), with a 2F-swapped 1959 at $14,250 in 2025 and a refurbished F-powered 1959 at $37,500 in 2026. CLASSIC.com shows 1957 FJ25s selling at Mecum and Barrett-Jackson for $33,000 in 2026. RM Sotheby's sold a restored 1960 soft-top for $70,000 in 2019.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER · CLASSIC.COM · RM SOTHEBY'S
Can I import one into the US?

Yes. Every J20 is far older than the 25-year threshold for NHTSA's import exemption, and most FJ25s sold in the US recently came from Latin America. Allow for state titling and emissions rules (California is the hard one) and make sure the paperwork matches the frame number.

SOURCE · NHTSA · BRING A TRAILER
