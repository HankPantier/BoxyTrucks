Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

200 Series Land Cruiser problems to check before you buy

  1. Look for pink crust at the water pumpDried pink coolant running down the front of the engine from the water-pump weep hole is the classic 3UR sign. One owner's build thread notes the engine is known for water-pump weeps around 100,000 miles, and that the pump usually gives plenty of warning.
  2. Scan for air-injection codesA check-engine light with VSC and traction-control lights on together often turns out to be the secondary air-injection system, such as code P2438 for the pressure sensor. It's emissions-related and the truck drives normally, but a seller who has cleared codes is hiding a repair.
  3. If the suspension has been touched, check the KDSS valvesAfter a lift or shock swap, the two KDSS valves must be closed again. One IH8MUD owner found a shop had left them fully open and the truck rolled alarmingly on uneven roads. Check them with a 5 mm hex key, and test-drive on a winding road.
  4. Listen for a driveline clunkA thunk when stopping and pulling away usually isn't the transmission. Unsealed 4X4 traces it to dry universal joints, especially on trucks that cross water. Ask when the driveshafts were last greased.
  5. Check the oil filter capThe 3UR has a plastic oil filter housing cap that wrenches round off and that can warp or crack with age. Look for leaks and chewed edges.
  6. Know which phase you're buying2008–2011: six-speed, 3.90 gears. 2013–2015: facelift with Multi-Terrain Select, Turn Assist and cameras. 2016–2021: eight-speed, 3.30 gears, bigger brakes, Toyota Safety Sense. There's no 2012 in the US.
  7. Ask about towingKBB lists 8,500 lb for 2008 and 8,100 lb for 2016–2021. A truck that pulled a heavy trailer for years deserves a closer look at the transmission fluid, the brakes and the rear springs.
  8. Diesel (non-US) 200s: injectors, DPF and airboxOn the 1VD-FTV, Unsealed's big three are injector problems (rough idle, smoke, poor economy), DPF and EGR trouble on 2015-on trucks, and dust getting past the airbox seal.

200 Series Land Cruiser recalls and service bulletins

  • Seat-belt tension sensor harness can disable passenger airbags ↗ 2008–2019 · NHTSA 18V887000The harness can break and switch off the front passenger, knee and seat-mounted side airbags.
  • Incorrect driver or passenger airbag fitted ↗ 2008–2016 · NHTSA 17V548000Some trucks may have the wrong airbag assembly installed (FMVSS 208); dealers replace it.
  • Accelerator pedal floor-mat entrapment ↗ 2008–2011 · NHTSA 12V305000An unsecured or incompatible floor mat can trap the pedal wide open; dealers modify or replace the pedal and replace wrong mats.
  • TPMS non-compliance ↗ 2008–2011 · NHTSA 11V148000Tire-pressure monitoring system failed FMVSS 138 requirements.
  • Denso low-pressure fuel pump ↗ 2018–2019 · NHTSA 20V012000 / 20V682000The Denso low-pressure fuel pump can fail and stall the engine while driving. 20V012000 was the original campaign and 20V682000 expanded it; NHTSA's lookup also lists 20V012000 against some 2014–2015 trucks, so run your VIN.
  • Load-capacity label and dealer-installed accessory campaigns ↗ 2008–2011, 2014–2016, 2019 · NHTSA 10V035000 / 10V036000 / 13V123000 / 14V828000 / 16V236000 / 19V244000Mostly labeling campaigns (FMVSS 110 load-capacity labels) plus a Southeast Toyota campaign for incorrectly attached distributor-installed accessories such as running boards (14V828000). Run your VIN at NHTSA to see which apply.

Owner questions about 200 Series Land Cruiser reliability

What breaks?

On US 3UR trucks: water pumps around 100,000 miles, secondary air-injection faults, plastic oil filter caps and dry U-joints. On diesels: injectors, the DPF and EGR on 2015-on trucks, and dust past the airbox. Unsealed also notes some 200s have a vibration that owners argue about.

What to bring when you look at a 200 Series Land Cruiser

Six inexpensive tools that catch most of the problems above before you hand over the money. Or book a specialist: see how to get a pre-purchase inspection.

  • Bluetooth OBD-II scannerReads stored and pending codes, and shows the readiness monitors. If several say "not ready", someone may have cleared the codes just before you arrived.

  • Paint thickness gaugeThick readings on one panel mean body filler or a respray, which usually means an accident or rust repair the seller hasn’t mentioned.
    Amazon ↗
  • Inspection borescope cameraFits through drain holes into frame rails, rockers and sills, where these trucks rust from the inside out.
    Amazon ↗
  • Bright LED flashlightFor the underside: leaks at the axle seals, transfer case and rear main, and the frame behind the rear wheels.
    Amazon ↗
  • Telescoping inspection mirrorShows the back of the engine, the top of the fuel tank and the inner fenders without crawling under.
    Amazon ↗
  • Compression testerOn a high-mileage or long-parked engine, uneven compression is the cheapest way to spot a tired engine before you pay for it. Diesels need a diesel-rated tester.
