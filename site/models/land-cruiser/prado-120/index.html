PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The 120 is the Prado that America did get, just not under that name. Toyota launched the third-generation Land Cruiser Prado in Japan on 7 October 2002 on a new, stiffer frame, and the next month Lexus started selling the same truck in the US as the GX 470, with a V8. Everyone else got fours, V6s and diesels, a Torsen center differential they could lock, and up to 180 liters of fuel in Australian five-doors. It's softer, heavier and cleverer than the Prado 90, and the diesel has a flaw you need to know about before you buy.

DESIGNED IN FRANCE, BUILT IN JAPAN

Development started in 1997. The design came from Lance Scott at Toyota's ED² studio in France, whose proposal was picked in late 1999. Toyota built the 120 from September 2002 to August 2009 at its Honsha and Tahara plants, adding Toyota Auto Body from May 2006, and FAW Toyota assembled it in Chengdu, China. Toyota says the new high-rigidity frame improved handling, stability and quietness. The front suspension was revised for reliability, and the 120 shares suspension parts with the 4th-gen 4Runner and the FJ Cruiser.

The clever bits were in the drivetrain and electronics. A newly developed Torsen limited-slip center differential, lockable for the rough stuff, replaced the J90's center diff. Active traction control could manage climbs and descents, and top grades added Toyota's H∞-TEMS adaptive dampers and electronically controlled rear air suspension. Australians got Hill-start and Downhill Assist Control, and a 4-star ANCAP rating in 2003. A short three-door version (J125) had only an 87-liter tank, with no sub tank.

1GR, 1KD AND THE REST

At launch in Japan, the engines were the 3.4-liter 5VZ-FE V6 and 2.7-liter 3RZ-FE four from the J90, plus a 3.0-liter intercooled turbo-diesel. In July 2004 the new 4.0-liter 1GR-FE V6 replaced the 5VZ-FE there, and in August the 2.7-liter 2TR-FE replaced the 3RZ-FE. Australia had the 1GR-FE from its 2003 launch, at 179 kW and 376 Nm. It's a timing-chain engine, and CarsGuide owners reported 18 L/100 km against Toyota's 13.6 claim. A five-speed automatic arrived for the V6 in late 2005, and 4X4 Australia suggests buying that or the six-speed manual rather than the early four-speed.

Diesels varied by market. Toyota's model list includes KDJ120s from 2002, so Japan had the 1KD-FTV common-rail engine from the start. Australia kept the older 1KZ-TE at 96 kW until late 2006, when the Euro IV version of the 1KD-FTV arrived with 127 kW, 410 Nm, a five-speed automatic and a six-speed manual. In 2006, diesels passed half of Australian Prado sales. Some markets also had the naturally aspirated 5L-E.

THE INJECTOR-SEAT PROBLEM

The 1KD-FTV's Achilles heel is the copper seat under each injector. When a seat leaks, blow-by gas gets into the rocker cover, mixes with the oil and cooks into carbon. The carbon blocks the oil pickup in the sump, starves the engine and can wipe out the bottom-end bearings. Symptoms are white smoke and a rattle on a cold start. AustralianCar.Reviews says aluminum seats replaced the copper ones from August 2007, and that the injectors themselves often fail at 120,000–140,000 km, sometimes as early as 75,000 km.

CarsGuide's 2012 used-car review warned that a seized engine could mean 'a total write-off', with a replacement engine costing about A$15,000, and reported that New Zealand owners were having the seals replaced every 45,000 km. Unsealed 4X4 now suggests seals every 40,000 km. The cheap pre-purchase check is a flashlight through the sump drain-plug hole: if the pickup screen is coated in anything but oil, walk away or budget for a sump-off clean.

WHERE IT LEFT THE PRADO

Toyota replaced the 120 with the J150 in September 2009. That truck kept the suspension layout and the 2.7 four and 4.0 V6, finally merged the Prado and Hilux Surf into one line, and added crawl control and Multi-Terrain Select. The Prado line finally reached American showrooms as the 250 Series, badged simply Land Cruiser. In the US, the 120 is still off-limits: the first trucks turn 25 from September 2027. Until then, the closest thing on an American title is its Lexus twin, which is why GX 470 forum threads read like Prado 120 forum threads.

SIDEBAR · AMERICA'S PRADO

The Lexus GX 470

North America never got the Prado name, but it got the truck: Toyota built the 120 as the Lexus GX 470, with the 4.7-liter 2UZ-FE V8 and full-time four-wheel drive. Lexus showed it at the 2002 North American International Auto Show and started sales in November 2002, a month after the Prado's Japanese launch.

That shared hardware is why the same parts and advice serve both. CruiserTeq lists its ball joints, CV axles, hubs and steering-rack boots as fitting the 'GX470/LC120', Coastal Offroad sells one rear bumper for the GX470 and Prado 120, and IH8MUD runs the two in a single GX470/Prado120 forum.

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The 120 rides on a new high-rigidity ladder frame with double-wishbone independent front suspension and a coil-sprung, four-link live rear axle with a lateral rod. Top grades swap the rear coils for electronically controlled air springs. Every 120 has full-time four-wheel drive through a Torsen limited-slip center differential that can be locked, plus brake-based Active TRC. Wheels are 6 on 139.7 mm.

PART 03

Buying one

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    1KD-FTV: injector seats and oil pickupAsk for injector-seat (washer) receipts. Listen for a rattle and look for white smoke on a cold start, then shine a light through the sump drain-plug hole. CarsGuide and Unsealed 4X4 both say carbon on the pickup screen means a suspect engine. Trucks built from August 2007 got aluminum seats.
  2. 02
    1KD-FTV: injector ageAustralianCar.Reviews says the injectors typically fail at 120,000–140,000 km. Signs are a loud knock when cold, rough idle, poor running under load and worse fuel economy. Both it and 4X4 Australia suggest testing them at every service and replacing them at about 100,000 km, so ask whether they've been done.
  3. 03
    Timing belt or chain?The 1GR-FE V6 and the 2.7 fours use chains. The 1KD-FTV, 1KZ-TE and 5VZ-FE use belts, and HD Automotive quotes Toyota's 1KD interval as 150,000 km or five years. No receipt means budget for the belt and water pump.
  4. 04
    Cracked dash and split rear doorCarsGuide reports dashboards cracking near the passenger airbag on early 120s, and rear-door skins splitting along the hinge side under the weight of the spare. Check both, and look closely at the door if a heavier spare tire has been fitted.
  5. 05
    Rear air suspension (Grande and top grades)If it has air springs, check that it levels and holds height overnight. 4X4 Australia warns that self-leveling rear air makes aftermarket suspension 'a much bigger (and more expensive) task' than on coil-sprung trucks.
  6. 06
    V6 transmission4X4 Australia suggests skipping the early four-speed automatic V6 in favor of the five-speed auto (late 2005 on) or the rarer six-speed manual.
  7. 07
    Test the center diff lockEngage the center lock off pavement and make sure the light comes on and goes off. IH8MUD's GX 470 pre-buy advice applies to the Prado's shared system: a seized actuator is an expensive surprise on a truck that's never left the road.
  8. 08
    Import eligibilityA J120 can't be imported into the US until it's 25 years old by date of manufacture. The first ones qualify from September 2027. A '2003 Prado' advertised as US-legal today should set off alarms.

Spotting the real thing

The Prado 120 looks a lot like a Lexus GX 470 and shares its platform with the 4Runner and FJ Cruiser. Read the model code and the engine bay, not the badges.

  1. ID
    Model code = engine + bodyGRJ = 1GR-FE 4.0 V6, KDJ = 1KD-FTV diesel, KZJ = 1KZ-TE diesel, VZJ = 5VZ-FE 3.4 V6, RZJ = 3RZ-FE and TRJ = 2TR-FE 2.7 fours. Toyota's own list shows 120/121 as five-door codes and 125 as the three-door.
  2. ID
    Copper or aluminum injector seatsOn a 1KD-FTV, trucks built from August 2007 have aluminum injector seats; earlier trucks have copper ones. Ask for receipts if the copper seats have been replaced.
  3. ID
    Air or coils at the rearElectronically controlled rear air suspension came on the Australian Grande and top Japanese grades (TZ G Selection). Look for the air springs and compressor before you price a lift.
  4. ID
    Rear diffExplorOz owners say Australian GX/GXL trucks have a rear LSD, while VX and Grande have an open rear diff with traction control. Some markets list a rear diff lock, so check for the switch and confirm it works.
  5. ID
    Prado or rebadged GX?IH8MUD has threads on rebadging GX 470s as Prados. A 2UZ-FE V8 and a US VIN mean it's a Lexus GX 470, whatever the tailgate says.

Importing & legality

Toyota never sold the Prado 120 in the United States. North America got the same truck as the Lexus GX 470, with a V8. NHTSA allows vehicles at least 25 years old to be imported without meeting US safety standards, and counts the 25 years from the date of manufacture. Production began in September 2002 (Japanese sales on 7 October 2002), so the earliest J120s become eligible from September 2027. The last, built in August 2009, follow in 2034. EPA's separate exemption (Form 3520-1, code E) needs the vehicle to be at least 21 calendar years old and in original configuration, so from 2027 the NHTSA rule is the one that matters.

Canada is more relaxed. Transport Canada lets in vehicles from outside the US and Mexico once they're 15 years old by month and year of manufacture, so every J120 has qualified since August 2024. That doesn't help Americans: a Canadian-registered Prado 120 still has to reach 25 before it can be brought into the US. When the time comes, check your state's titling and emissions rules (California especially) before you buy.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Clean a carboned-up 1KD intake and EGR, then fit a catch can
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · Unsealed 4X4 calls the cleaning 'extremely laborious'SHOP COST · HD Automotive's 1KD intake-clean gasket kit lists at AUD $230
SYMPTOMSReduced performance and a choked, flat feel on a high-kilometer common-rail diesel.
THE FIXOily exhaust gas fed back through the EGR coats the intake in carbon on any common-rail diesel with EGR, the 1KD included. Remove the intake plumbing and EGR parts, scrape and clean them (or use a chemical clean), and refit with new gaskets. Then fit an oil separator (catch can) so oil mist is trapped before it gets back into the intake. Unsealed points out that blanking the EGR is illegal for road use.
TOOLS & PARTSMetric sockets · scrapers and solvent · new intake gaskets · catch-can kit

Front lower ball joints
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day for both sidesSHOP COST · Parts plus alignment
SYMPTOMSTorn boots weeping grease, clunks over bumps, vague steering.
THE FIXIt's the same front end as the GX 470. Support the truck, remove the wheel and separate the lower ball joint from the knuckle and arm. Then either press in a new joint (owners cite MOOG and Sankei 555 replacements) or fit a complete lower arm with joint and bushings. Make sure the grease fitting faces somewhere you can reach, grease the new joint if it came dry, and get an alignment. On KDSS-equipped GX 470s, buy KDSS-specific parts.

Replace a front wheel bearing hub
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 2 hours per sideSHOP COST · A complete hub assembly bolts in, so no press is needed
SYMPTOMSA growl or vibration from one front corner that changes with speed or load.
THE FIXThis is the same hub as the GX 470, FJ Cruiser and 4Runner. Pull the dust cap and break the axle nut with a 35 mm socket. Remove the caliper and rotor, then the four 17 mm bolts holding the hub to the knuckle. Once they're cracked loose, switch to an open-end wrench, because the bolts can't come fully out with the hub in place. Knock the hub free, keep or replace the O-ring behind it, and bolt in the new assembly with a new axle nut.
TOOLS & PARTS35 mm socket · breaker bar · 17 mm open-end wrench · hammer · torque wrench · new axle nut

Rear air springs: repair or convert to coils
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2 hours for a simple coil swapSHOP COST · Usually cheaper to convert than to buy new air parts, according to GX 470 owners
SYMPTOMSThe rear sags overnight or sits on the bump stops, the compressor runs constantly, and the height-control light blinks.
THE FIXConfirm the leak with soapy water where each bag folds over its perch. To stay on air, replace both springs, plus the compressor if it has worn out chasing leaks. To convert, support the axle, remove the shocks, cut the air lines, pop the upper clip on each bag and fit a coil-conversion kit with springs, retainers and isolators. Most kits are sold for the GX 470's identical rear layout, so confirm Prado fitment with the vendor. Recheck bolt torque after 100 miles.
TOOLS & PARTSFloor jack · jack stands · flat screwdriver · line cutters · torque wrench · coil-conversion kit

Front CV axle or boots
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–3 hours per sideSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSGrease flung around the inside of the wheel, split boots, clicking on tight turns.
THE FIXLoosen the axle nut with the wheel on the ground, then lift the truck. Separate the lower ball joint or tie rod so the knuckle can swing, pull the axle out of the hub and pop the inner joint out of the differential. Fit the new axle (or rebooted original), torque the axle nut, then stake or pin it. Expedition Portal's GX 470 project used factory Toyota axles.

Asked on every forum

Land Cruiser Prado 120 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

The stock size is 265/65R17 on a 7.5Jx17 +30 wheel. ExplorOz owners run 265/70R17 'with no rubbing issues', which matches the GX 470 consensus on the same chassis. 285/70R17 (about 33 in) needs at least a 50 mm (2 in) lift and +50 offset wheels, or some trimming of the body mount behind the mud flap. On 16-inch wheels, a 265/70R16 is a common upsize, but one owner says 16s may need caliper grinding on a 120.

SOURCE · EXPLOROZ
Can I import one into the US?

Not yet. NHTSA's 25-year exemption runs from the date of manufacture, and 120 production started in September 2002, so the first trucks qualify from September 2027 and the last (August 2009) in 2034. Canada's 15-year rule already covers every J120, but a Canadian-registered Prado still has to be 25 to come south.

SOURCE · NHTSA · TRANSPORT CANADA
Is a Prado 120 just a Lexus GX 470?

Same platform, different truck. The GX 470 got the 4.7 V8, plusher trim and US-market equipment; the Prado got fours, V6s and diesels, and Australian versions got a 93-liter sub tank. Suspension, hubs, ball joints, CV axles and much of the armor are shared, which is why IH8MUD runs them in one forum.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · IH8MUD
What's the 1KD-FTV injector problem?

The original injector seats were copper and leak with age. Blow-by then mixes with the oil, forms carbon and can block the sump pickup, starving the engine. Aluminum seats replaced copper from August 2007. Separately, injectors often fail at 120,000–140,000 km. Check the seat history, look for white smoke or a rattle on a cold start, and inspect the pickup through the drain-plug hole.

SOURCE · AUSTRALIANCAR.REVIEWS · UNSEALED 4X4 · CARSGUIDE
Which engine should I buy?

The 1GR-FE 4.0 V6 (179 kW) is the durable, chain-driven choice. 4X4 Australia says V6s have passed 300,000 km, but it's thirsty: 13.6 L/100 km officially and up to 18 in owners' hands. The 1KD-FTV diesel (127 kW and 410 Nm from 2006) is the tow rig, but only with injector-seat records. The 1KZ-TE has 96 kW and indirect injection, so it's simpler but slower.

SOURCE · 4X4 AUSTRALIA · CARSGUIDE
Does it have lockers?

Every 120 has a Torsen limited-slip center differential with a lock, plus Active TRC. According to ExplorOz owners, Australian GX and GXL grades had a rear LSD, while VX and Grande had an open rear diff and relied on traction control. A rear diff lock shows up in some markets' specs, but ARB Air Lockers are the usual retrofit.

SOURCE · TOYOTA · EXPLOROZ · ROUGHTRAX
How much can it tow?

4X4 Australia calls the 2006–2008 trucks' 2,500 kg (5,512 lb) braked rating 'low'. If you tow, the diesel's torque is the reason to buy one.

SOURCE · 4X4 AUSTRALIA
What fluids and capacities does a KDJ120 take?

Per Roughtrax's 1KD 120 sheet: engine oil 7.4 L with filter (5W-30); coolant about 11 L of Toyota pink Super Long Life or blue Long Life; automatic 3.0 L per drain-and-fill (10.6 L dry), T-IV if it has a dipstick and WS if not; manual 2.2 L and transfer case 1.4 L of 75W-90; front diff 1.4 L; rear 3.05 L on a five-door (2.95 L with a diff lock), with LSD oil in an LSD; power steering 1.0 L of Dexron II/III. Confirm against your manual.

SOURCE · ROUGHTRAX
How big are the fuel tanks?

Australian five-doors carry 180 L in total: an 87-liter main tank plus a 93-liter sub tank. Three-doors have only the 87-liter tank, and Roughtrax lists a 95-liter tank for UK five-doors.

SOURCE · 4X4 AUSTRALIA · WIKIPEDIA · ROUGHTRAX
Should I keep the rear air suspension?

It rides well while it's healthy, but the bags and compressor are wear items, and 4X4 Australia warns that air makes aftermarket suspension much harder and more expensive. Most owners who lift convert to coils with kits built for the GX 470's identical rear end.

SOURCE · 4X4 AUSTRALIA · IH8MUD
