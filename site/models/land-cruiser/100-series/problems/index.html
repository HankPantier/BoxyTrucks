Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

100 Series Land Cruiser problems to check before you buy

  1. Ask for the timing-belt receiptThe 2UZ-FE uses a belt, and owners treat the 90,000-mile service (belt, water pump, thermostat, often idlers and seals) as essential. With no paperwork, assume it's due and price it in.
  2. Know which front diff you're buyingModel-year 1998 and 1999 trucks have the two-pinion front differential that Wikipedia says fails most often under shock loads. The 2000 model year switched to a stronger four-pinion unit, but 1998–99 are also the only years with the optional rear locker.
  3. Have the heater-hose tees been done?The plastic tees in the heater hoses get brittle and can crack, dumping coolant. IH8MUD calls them part of the 90k service that most shops, Toyota dealers included, skip.
  4. Listen at cold startA tick or pop that sounds like an exhaust leak can be a spark plug working loose. IH8MUD's warning thread says to check the plugs before anything else, because a plug that blows out can take the cylinder-head threads with it.
  5. Inspect the IFSLook for cracks in the lower wishbones on trucks that have been driven hard enough to hit the bump stops, and check the lower ball joints and CV boots for play or splits.
  6. Try a cold start more than onceSlee lists worn starter contacts as a known issue. The symptom is a click with no crank on the first try or two.
  7. AHC trucks: check the ride heightOn an LX 470, or one of the late Land Cruisers with AHC, make sure it levels itself and holds height. Ask when the AHC fluid was last flushed; owners on IH8MUD cite a 60,000-mile interval.
  8. Do the body and wiring checksSlee's inspection list applies here too: overspray, filler, frame-straightening marks, a rat's nest of accessory wiring under the dash, and fluids that look neglected. Cracked leather seats are common and costly to re-skin.

100 Series Land Cruiser recalls and service bulletins

  • Side curtain airbag inadvertent deployment ↗ 2003–2006 · NHTSA 16V065000The airbag control module may be programmed wrong, so the side curtain airbags can deploy unexpectedly; shared with the LX 470, 4Runner, GX 470, Tundra and Sequoia.
  • Missing load-capacity modification label ↗ 2005–2007 · NHTSA 09V223000 / 10V036000Paperwork-only recalls under FMVSS 110 (tire selection and rims): some trucks were sold without the label showing reduced load capacity after accessories were added. 10V036000 covers Southeast Toyota distributor vehicles; 09V223000 covers certain 2005-on Toyota vehicles.
  • Exhaust booming at idle ↗ · TSB 00304 (Feb 2004)Booming after warm-up on 1998–2006 LC/LX; the fixes include a frame-mounted crossmember damper weight.
  • Transfer case buzz ↗ · TSB NV00101 (Jan 2001)Buzz under light or moderate acceleration; Toyota revised the high-speed output gear, shift linkage and centre diff case.
  • Front brake vibration ↗ · TSB BR0101 (Nov 2001)Bulletin for pulsing or vibrating front brakes on 1998-on trucks.
  • Continuous alarm noise from the combination meter ↗ · TSB EL00202 (Feb 2002)Loud constant buzzer from the gauge cluster.
  • A/C expansion valve noise ↗ · TSB AC00199 (Feb 1999)Groaning from the A/C expansion valve on early 1999 builds within a stated VIN range.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about 100 Series Land Cruiser reliability

What's the deal with the 1998–99 front differential?

Those years have a two-pinion front diff, and Wikipedia says the pinion could flex away from the ring gear under shock loads. Most reported failures are on 1998–99 trucks. Toyota fitted a four-pinion diff from the 2000 model year, and failures dropped.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · SLEE
When is the timing belt due?

At the 90,000-mile service. Owners replace the belt, water pump, thermostat and often the idlers, tensioner and cam and crank seals at the same time. IH8MUD has a full parts list and several step-by-step write-ups.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
Should I worry about AHC?

The LX 470 had Active Height Control and adaptive damping as standard from 1998, and owners report it was also optional on some Land Cruisers. It works well when maintained. Keep up with fluid flushes (owners cite 60,000 miles), and to lift an AHC truck you can adjust the height-sensor links, as IH8MUD's FAQ covers.

SOURCE · SLEE · IH8MUD
