PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

In January 1998 Toyota replaced the eight-year-old 80 Series with a wagon that broke two Land Cruiser traditions at once. The 100 was the first Land Cruiser with a V8, which Toyota added specifically to sell more trucks in North America, where it was the only engine offered. It was also the first with independent front suspension, chosen along with rack-and-pinion steering to improve on-road handling. Off-roaders grumbled, and they had a point. They also kept buying them.

FROM 404T TO 'GRAND CRUISER'

Development started in 1991 under the code name 404T, and the design was frozen in mid-1994. Toyota previewed the truck as the 'Grand Cruiser' at the 32nd Tokyo Motor Show in October 1997. Takeo Kondo is credited as designer, Araco built it in Toyota City, and from 2003 to 2008 it was also assembled in China.

Under one body there were really two trucks. The 100 had a slightly wider chassis, IFS and the new engines. The 105 carried over most of the 80's chassis and drivetrain: coil-sprung solid axles at both ends and straight-six petrol and diesel engines, sold only in Africa, Australia, Russia and South America. You can tell them apart at a glance. The IFS 100 often sits lower at the front, and its wheels are almost flat where the 105's are dished, which lets both keep similar track widths.

THE AMERICAN 100

US trucks were all UZJ100s with the 4.7-liter 2UZ-FE V8, rated at 230 hp and 320 lb-ft, driving through an A343F four-speed automatic and an HF2A full-time transfer case. Slee Off-Road notes that, unlike the 80, it has no viscous coupling, just a center differential you can lock. The rear axle is a semi-floating, 32-spline unit with a 9.5-inch ring gear. A rear electric locker was optional only for 1998 and 1999. For 2000 Toyota dropped it, added VSC and A-TRAC traction control, and swapped the weak two-pinion front differential for a four-pinion unit.

The 2003 update was the big one: an A750F five-speed automatic, drive-by-wire throttle, 235 hp, stronger U-joints and fatter driveshafts, 4.10 gears and 17-inch wheels as standard. Late trucks gained VVT-i, and Kelley Blue Book lists 275 hp and 332 lb-ft from 2005. Active Height Control was standard on the LX 470 from launch; owners report it also turns up as an option on some Land Cruisers, so check which suspension a given truck has.

THE LUXURY TWINS

The 100 was also the basis for the Lexus LX 470, sold in Japan as the Toyota Land Cruiser Cygnus. Slee's guide says the Lexus shares the drivetrain but adds body cladding, projector headlights, backlit gauges and standard AHC with adaptive damping from 1998. In 2002 the Cygnus and LX 470 got Night View, which projected an infrared camera image onto the windshield, and they were the first Toyota vehicles with a rollover sensor. In Britain and Ireland the 100 was called the Land Cruiser Amazon. When Toyota marked the Land Cruiser's 50th anniversary in 2000 with commemorative models, global production had reached 3.72 million.

HOW IT ENDED

The 100 got a series of small facelifts (lights, grille, spoiler and equipment) and stayed in production until August 2007. The 200 Series then kept the formula of V8 power, independent front suspension and a luxury twin in the LX 570. Wikipedia rates the 100 as durable and reliable, with two known weak spots on hard-worked trucks: cracked IFS lower wishbones on trucks that often hit the bump stops, and broken front differentials, mostly on 1998–99 two-pinion units.

Meanwhile, the solid-axle 70 Series kept going for buyers who wanted nothing to do with IFS. Today the 100 is a well-documented truck: IH8MUD's 100-series FAQ links to write-ups on everything from the 90,000-mile timing belt to lower ball joints, heater-hose tees and AHC fluid flushes.

SIDEBAR · THE SOLID-AXLE TWIN

The 105, and the diesel Australia couldn't forgive

The FZJ105 and HZJ105 looked like 100s but kept the 80's solid axles and straight sixes: the 4.5-liter 1FZ-FE petrol and the 4.2-liter 1HZ diesel. In Australia, the V8 100 was at first sold only in the top GXV trim, while entry and mid-range buyers got the 105.

The trouble was the 1HD-FTE turbo-diesel. It arrived in Australia in October 2000, after being sold in Europe and the UK since 1998, but only with IFS. Australian 4WD Monthly said it plainly: 'We will never forgive Toyota for going independent at the front with the mighty 4.2 turbo-diesel.'

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The 100 has a ladder frame, slightly wider than the 80's, with independent front suspension on torsion bars and rack-and-pinion steering, plus a coil-sprung live rear axle. US trucks use the 4.7-liter 2UZ-FE V8 and a full-time transfer case with a lockable center differential. The wheel pattern changed to 5 on 150 mm with M14 x 1.5 studs.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Ask for the timing-belt receiptThe 2UZ-FE uses a belt, and owners treat the 90,000-mile service (belt, water pump, thermostat, often idlers and seals) as essential. With no paperwork, assume it's due and price it in.
  2. 02
    Know which front diff you're buyingModel-year 1998 and 1999 trucks have the two-pinion front differential that Wikipedia says fails most often under shock loads. The 2000 model year switched to a stronger four-pinion unit, but 1998–99 are also the only years with the optional rear locker.
  3. 03
    Have the heater-hose tees been done?The plastic tees in the heater hoses get brittle and can crack, dumping coolant. IH8MUD calls them part of the 90k service that most shops, Toyota dealers included, skip.
  4. 04
    Listen at cold startA tick or pop that sounds like an exhaust leak can be a spark plug working loose. IH8MUD's warning thread says to check the plugs before anything else, because a plug that blows out can take the cylinder-head threads with it.
  5. 05
    Inspect the IFSLook for cracks in the lower wishbones on trucks that have been driven hard enough to hit the bump stops, and check the lower ball joints and CV boots for play or splits.
  6. 06
    Try a cold start more than onceSlee lists worn starter contacts as a known issue. The symptom is a click with no crank on the first try or two.
  7. 07
    AHC trucks: check the ride heightOn an LX 470, or one of the late Land Cruisers with AHC, make sure it levels itself and holds height. Ask when the AHC fluid was last flushed; owners on IH8MUD cite a 60,000-mile interval.
  8. 08
    Do the body and wiring checksSlee's inspection list applies here too: overspray, filler, frame-straightening marks, a rat's nest of accessory wiring under the dash, and fluids that look neglected. Cracked leather seats are common and costly to re-skin.

Spotting the real thing

In the US, every 100 is a V8 UZJ100 or its Lexus LX 470 twin. The solid-axle 105 and the diesels were never sold here, but they're now arriving as 25-year imports.

  1. ID
    100 vs 105The 105 kept the 80 Series' solid front axle and straight-six engines and was sold in Africa, Australia, Russia and South America. Tells: the IFS 100 often sits nose-low, and 100s wear almost flat-faced wheels where 105s have dished ones.
  2. ID
    Model codesUZJ100 = 2UZ-FE 4.7 V8 (US). FZJ100/105 = 1FZ-FE 4.5 straight-six. HZJ105 = 1HZ diesel. HDJ100 = 1HD-T or 1HD-FTE turbo-diesel. Anything other than a UZJ100 in the US is an import.
  3. ID
    Factory rear lockerThe selectable rear locker was an option on 1998–1999 trucks only. A later truck with a rear locker has an aftermarket one.
  4. ID
    Front diff1998–1999 trucks (built mid-1997 to mid-1999) came with the weaker 2-pinion front diff; model year 2000 onward got the 4-pinion unit.
  5. ID
    VINToyota's own bulletins quote US UZJ100 VIN ranges beginning JT3HT05J. As on any US VIN, the 10th character is the model year (X = 1999, Y = 2000).

Importing & legality

The solid-axle 105 (FZJ105, HZJ105) and diesel 100s (HDJ100) were never sold in the US. Production started in January 1998, so these trucks are reaching the 25-year NHTSA exemption one build year at a time, and Australian-market 105s are already appearing at US auctions.

Federal eligibility doesn't settle registration everywhere. California's smog rules make registering an overseas classic difficult, so check your state's titling and emissions rules before you buy a 105 or a diesel.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Press in new lower ball joints
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A daySHOP COST · Ball joints plus a 20-ton press if you don't own one
SYMPTOMSClunks over bumps, play at the bottom of the knuckle, or simply a high-mileage truck with original joints.
THE FIXSupport the truck on frame stands, pull the wheels and skid plates, and back the torsion-bar adjusters all the way off. Mark and slide back the torque arms, then remove the lower shock bolt, sway-bar link, cotter pin and ball-joint nut. Break the taper, pull the lower arm, remove the C-clip and press out the old joint (a 2-inch conduit coupler makes a good receiver). Press in the new joint with a 2-inch pipe nipple, refit the clip and reassemble in reverse order, following the factory manual for bolt torques. Reset the torsion bars and get an alignment.
TOOLS & PARTS20-ton shop press · 22, 19, 14 and 24 mm sockets · snap-ring pliers · sledge or pickle fork · lower ball joint (e.g. Cruiser Outfitters SUS43340) · factory manual section SA-73.
WHERE TO BUY PARTSLower ball joint, 1998–2007 100 Series/LX470 (SUS43340) · CruiserTeq ↗Amazon ↗Ball joint kit, 1998–2007 IFS 100/LX470 (SUS100BJKIT) · CruiserTeq ↗Amazon ↗Lower ball joint boot kit, 1998–2007 UZJ100/LX470 (SUS100BJ15) · CruiserTeq ↗Amazon ↗
Replace the starter contacts
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 3 hours with experience (one owner's first try took 6.5)SHOP COST · About $25 in contacts
SYMPTOMSThe solenoid clicks but the starter doesn't turn, usually on a cold start, then it fires after two or three tries.
THE FIXDisconnect the battery. The starter sits under the intake manifold, so remove the V-bank cover, the intake tube and airbox lid, the accelerator cable and the throttle body, then every hose and the ground strap on the manifold, and lift the manifold off. Photograph everything first. Pull the starter, open the solenoid cover and fit new contacts and a plunger. Moving the wiring-harness bracket out of the way makes the manifold easier to refit. Some owners, like the author of IH8MUD's '90K and starter replacement job' thread, do it during the timing-belt service.
TOOLS & PARTS10, 12 and 14 mm wrenches and sockets · camera for reference photos · starter contact and plunger kit · new intake gaskets.
WHERE TO BUY PARTSStarter and solenoid parts catalog, 2002 Land Cruiser 4.7 V8 · RockAuto ↗Valve cover gasket kit, 1998–7/2002 100/LX470 2UZ (VCC2UZKITE) — if the covers come off with the manifold · CruiserTeq ↗Amazon ↗
Flush the AHC fluid
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hoursSHOP COST · Toyota AHC fluid; one member priced it at about $30–35 a gallon
SYMPTOMSOld, dark fluid, a truck that's slow to level, or a lift via the height-sensor links that has left the reservoir low.
THE FIXPark on level ground with the suspension in N, engine off. Suck the reservoir empty and refill with fresh AHC fluid. Bleed one corner at a time (front driver, front passenger, rear driver, rear passenger): open the bleeder until that corner bottoms out, close it, start the engine and let the truck re-level, then switch off. Repeat until clean fluid comes out of each bleeder, and never let the reservoir run low.
TOOLS & PARTS10 mm wrench · clear tube and catch bottle · fluid extractor or turkey baster · Toyota AHC fluid.
WHERE TO BUY PARTSToyota/Lexus AHC fluid, 2.5 L (08886-01805) · LexusPartsNow (OEM) ↗Amazon ↗Toyota AHC fluid, 2.5 L (08886-01805) · ToyotaPartsDeal (OEM) ↗Amazon ↗
Check spark plugs for walking out
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2 hoursSHOP COST · Eight plugs (90080-91180 per the IH8MUD 90k list)
SYMPTOMSA tick-tick or pop-pop at cold start that sounds like an exhaust leak and, as the plug loosens, doesn't go away when warm. Premature coil-boot and coil failures.
THE FIXPull the coils and check every plug's torque. If one is loose, replace the set and torque them correctly; the thread quotes the factory spec as 13 ft-lb. If the tick is still there afterward, then look for an exhaust leak. A plug that has blown out usually needs a thread insert in the head.
TOOLS & PARTSSpark-plug socket and extension · torque wrench (inch-pound range) · dielectric grease for the coil boots.

Set the front ride height at the torsion bars
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1 hour plus an alignmentSHOP COST · Free to adjust; new torsion bars cost more
SYMPTOMSA front end that has settled or sits lower than the rear, or new tires that need more clearance.
THE FIXEach torsion bar has an adjuster bolt under the truck, one per side. Turn them evenly and measure both sides, keeping some rake, since IH8MUD members point out the 100 handles best with the rear a little higher. Clean and anti-seize the adjuster threads. If you need real lift or more load capacity, fit uprated torsion bars and matching rear coils, then align.
TOOLS & PARTSSocket and long extension · tape measure · anti-seize · Old Man Emu torsion bars (303001) and rear coils (2865) if upgrading.

Asked on every forum

Land Cruiser 100 Series questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

The 100-series FAQ answer on IH8MUD is 285/75R16 on 16-inch wheels or 285/65R18 on 18s, both about 33 inches. 295/75R16s do fit, with half an inch to an inch of clearance at full turn, but owners call it tight. Even 285/75s can be snug at the rear: one owner measured 3/4 inch to the plastic wheel-well liner.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
Does it have lockers?

Every 100 has a center differential you can lock. A rear electric locker was a factory option only for 1998 and 1999. From 2000 Toyota relied on A-TRAC traction control instead, so ARB Air Lockers are the usual retrofit.

SOURCE · SLEE
What's the deal with the 1998–99 front differential?

Those years have a two-pinion front diff, and Wikipedia says the pinion could flex away from the ring gear under shock loads. Most reported failures are on 1998–99 trucks. Toyota fitted a four-pinion diff from the 2000 model year, and failures dropped.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · SLEE
When is the timing belt due?

At the 90,000-mile service. Owners replace the belt, water pump, thermostat and often the idlers, tensioner and cam and crank seals at the same time. IH8MUD has a full parts list and several step-by-step write-ups.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
Which years should I buy?

It depends what you want. 2003 and later trucks have the five-speed A750F, stronger driveshafts and 4.10 gears, and late trucks have the 275-hp VVT-i engine. 1998–99 trucks are the only ones with the optional rear locker, but they also have the weaker front diff and, Slee notes, throttle-sensor faults that can stop the truck responding to the pedal.

SOURCE · SLEE · KBB
How much can it tow?

Kelley Blue Book lists 6,500 lb for 2002–2006 models (5,500 lb for a 1999). From 2003 a bolt-on factory hitch was offered, and the rear crossmember was changed to take it.

SOURCE · KBB · SLEE
What fuel economy should I expect?

KBB lists 12 mpg city and 15–16 highway, with a 25.4-gallon tank on a 2005. Lifts, 33s and a roof rack make that worse.

SOURCE · KBB
Should I worry about AHC?

The LX 470 had Active Height Control and adaptive damping as standard from 1998, and owners report it was also optional on some Land Cruisers. It works well when maintained. Keep up with fluid flushes (owners cite 60,000 miles), and to lift an AHC truck you can adjust the height-sensor links, as IH8MUD's FAQ covers.

SOURCE · SLEE · IH8MUD
Was there a solid-axle 100?

Yes, the 105 (FZJ105 and HZJ105), with coil-sprung solid axles at both ends and straight sixes carried over from the 80. It was never sold in the US; it went to Africa, Australia, Russia and South America.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Is the Lexus LX 470 the same truck?

Mechanically, yes: same chassis and drivetrain. The LX 470 adds body cladding, a plusher cabin and standard AHC with adaptive damping. Slee notes that it often got features before the Land Cruiser did.

SOURCE · SLEE
What are the lift options?

The common setup is Old Man Emu: uprated torsion bars up front, matching coils at the rear and new shocks, sold as a kit of about 1.5 inches. The torsion-bar adjusters handle front height. IH8MUD's FAQ also links a thread on Slee's front diff drop, and any height change needs an alignment.

SOURCE · CRUISERTEQ · IH8MUD
