PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Every Land Cruiser wagon you've ever seen traces back to two awkward long-wheelbase trucks. The FJ45 stretched the FJ40 into pickups, cab-chassis, hardtops and a short-lived four-door wagon. The FJ55, launched in August 1967, was something new: Hagerty calls it Toyota's first Land Cruiser designed from the start as a wagon, and Wikipedia calls it the first 'real' station wagon in the line. Americans nicknamed it the Iron Pig: Hagerty says the two-tone paint made the front end look like a pig's snout. Wikipedia records 'Moose' as another nickname.

THE LONG-WHEELBASE WORKHORSE

The FJ45 grew out of the 40 Series from the beginning. Wikipedia lists a four-door FJ45V wagon on a 2,650 mm wheelbase and, from 1963, a 2,950 mm long-wheelbase pickup and cab-chassis. Those long trucks became export staples. Truck Encyclopedia describes the super-long version as a major military troop carrier, often with the windshield folded flat, and notes the Irish Army took 77 FJ45s in 1977. They were praised for speed and reliability, and they rusted badly in the Irish wet.

The four-door FJ45V was the shortest-lived variant. Wikipedia says it was replaced by the FJ55 in 1967, and Truck Encyclopedia says only about 60 of the J30-style FJ45-V/LV wagon bodies were built. The pickups carried on through the 1970s, picking up the H diesel in the HJ45 from 1972 or 1973 (sources differ) and the 2H six in the HJ47 from 1980, before the 70 Series replaced the lot in 1984.

THE IRON PIG

The FJ55 was aimed squarely at North America and Australia. It used the FJ40's drivetrain under a four-door body on a 2,700 mm wheelbase, about 16 inches longer than the FJ40's according to Toyota. Under it was the first Land Cruiser frame with fully boxed members. In the US, Hagerty says, it was sold against the Chevrolet Suburban and Kaiser/Jeep Wagoneer, and it brought a rare luxury: a bottom-hinged tailgate with a power window, worked from a dash switch or the key.

Mechanical changes tracked the FJ40. From 1967 to 1974 it used the 3.9-liter F six at 125 hp with a three-speed manual whose first gear lacked synchromesh. In 1975 the 4.2-liter 2F (135 hp), a four-speed and front disc brakes arrived, along with a domed hood to clear the new induction system. Toyota UK says the design brief was a wagon that could cruise comfortably at 80 mph without losing the 40's off-road ability, and that it made up about 11 percent of Land Cruiser sales by the time production ended in July 1980.

HOW IT ENDED, AND WHY THEY'RE SO RARE

The FJ60 replaced the FJ55 in 1980, taking the wagon idea upmarket with air conditioning and a proper interior. That left the Iron Pig as a working truck that was driven until it dissolved. Hagerty says rust is the top concern and complete replacement panels generally don't exist (specialists sell only repair sections such as rockers and lower-fender patches), and trim and weatherstripping are hard to find. Low production and high attrition, it notes elsewhere, are why good ones are rare.

Rarity has started to show up in prices. In Hagerty's FJ55 buyer's guide (first published in 2021 and updated in 2023), the median FJ55 value for all conditions was $34,500 and for #2 condition $46,300, with the median quoted value up 65.8 percent since 2020. Gen X buyers accounted for 58.5 percent of the quotes. The FJ45 is rarer still in the US and turns up mostly as imported pickups and troop carriers. Bring a Trailer lists both as separate model pages.

SIDEBAR · NAMING THE PIG

Van, wagon, Moose or Pig

Toyota's codes are confusing. Japan registered the FJ55 as a van, hence the 'V' in FJ55V, and Wikipedia says the Japanese model became the FJ56 when the 2F arrived in 1975.

In English it went by two nicknames. Hagerty traces 'Iron Pig' to the front end, whose two-tone paint made it look like a pig's snout. Wikipedia records 'Moose' as well, and Australia's LandCruiserClub forum lists both on its 50 Series board.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

Both trucks share the 40 Series mechanicals: leaf-sprung live axles, closed-knuckle front axle with Birfield joints, a two-speed part-time transfer case and Toyota's long-stroke F or 2F straight-six. The FJ55 adds a longer, fully boxed frame and a wagon body, and the long FJ45 runs single rear wheels, which Truck Encyclopedia notes limited payloads in military service.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust is the dealOn an FJ55, Hagerty's checklist starts at the roof near the drip rails, then the cowl, fenders, doors, rocker panels and wheel arches. Under it, check the frame rails, body mounts and anywhere mud collects.
  2. 02
    Judge the old repairsReplacement panels generally don't exist for the FJ55, so past rust repairs were fabricated. Look closely at how well they were done, since redoing them is expensive.
  3. 03
    Trim and sealsWeatherstripping and trim are hard to find. A truck with complete trim is worth more than its paint suggests.
  4. 04
    Interior conditionVinyl seats tear and original dash tops and rubber mats go brittle. Original, undamaged interiors are the most desirable.
  5. 05
    Test the tailgate windowThe FJ55's power tailgate window works from a dash switch or the key. Make sure it does, with the tailgate both up and down. A dead window is often a stuck safety switch or a corroded power strip, but it can also be a stripped regulator (see the repair below).
  6. 06
    BrakesStock drums are adequate if they work well and the powertrain is stock. Disc upgrades are common and parts are easy to find; check what's been fitted and how.
  7. 07
    Engine healthThe F and 2F are durable with basic maintenance and periodic valve adjustments. Compression matters more than mileage, and mechanical repairs are cheap next to rust repair.
  8. 08
    Know which truck it isCheck the wheelbase and engine code against the chassis plate: FJ45 wagons, pickups and diesel HJ/BJ versions differ, and export-spec trucks are sometimes misidentified.

Spotting the real thing

Long-wheelbase 40s came in a confusing spread of body and engine codes, and Brazilian Bandeirantes sometimes get sold as FJ45s or FJ55s.

  1. ID
    FJ55 frame numbersOwners date FJ55s from the frame number using Toyota production microfiche, for example frames 76441–77572 for July 1977. Some California titles carry an extra leading zero that isn't on the frame, so check that the numbers match.
  2. ID
    FJ55 year tellsA center-domed hood means a 1975-or-later 2F truck; lower rear lamps with integrated turn signals mean 1978 or later. Two-tone paint and grille-set headlights are original.
  3. ID
    FJ45 body codesIH8MUD's J45 chart lists codes by body: FJ45V/FJ45L wagons (1961–67), FJ45P-B long-bed pickups (8/64–1/75), FJ45RP and FJ45LP pickups from 1975, and FJ45V/LV Troopies from 6/77. HJ45 and HJ47 are diesel sixes; BJ45 is the four-cylinder diesel.
  4. ID
    Bandeirante isn't a 55Brazilian Toyota Bandeirantes (OJ50/OJ55, later BJ50/BJ55) are 40-series derivatives with Mercedes-Benz or Toyota 14B diesels. An 'OJ55' is a Brazilian pickup or wagon, not an FJ55.

Importing & legality

The US got gasoline FJ45s and FJ55s. HJ45, HJ47 and BJ45 diesels, right-hand-drive Troopies and Brazilian Bandeirantes are gray-market. A vehicle at least 25 years old, measured from its date of manufacture, can be imported into the US without meeting FMVSS, so every J40-family truck now qualifies. JDMBUYSELL notes that EPA still expects the original-design engine or an EPA-certified replacement. Corsetti Cruisers warns that California may refuse to register an import without a 50-state-compliant emissions system, so plan for that before buying a BJ or HJ.

Clutch master and slave cylinders →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day including bleedingSHOP COST · Aisin master about $115 and slave about $35 (Cruiser Corps); OEM master rebuild kit about $51
SYMPTOMSA clutch that slowly loses pressure, has to be double-pumped to shift without grinding, or a pedal that sinks. Look for fluid under the dash or around the slave on the bellhousing.
THE FIXOff-Road.com's FJ40 project, which shares the early clutch hydraulics with the FJ45, replaced the master, the slave and the hose. Pump the old fluid out. Unbolt the slave (two bolts), fit the new one with thread-locker and set the fork pushrod with its locknut. Free the old line with penetrating oil and flare-nut wrenches. Under the dash, pull the cotter pin and spring at the pedal, then remove the three 12 mm master bolts. Fit the new master with a new cotter pin, connect the line and don't overtighten the banjo bolt. Fill with DOT 3, bleed at the slave with a helper, then adjust the pedal rod and slave pushrod. Bench-bleed the new master, and keep the pedal down until the bleeder is closed. Check fitment by build date: Cruiser Corps lists this master for 1970–75 trucks and the slave for 1974–80.
TOOLS & PARTS12 mm socket and wrench · 12 mm and 16 mm flare-nut wrenches · needle-nose pliers · bleeder hose and bottle · thread-locker · DOT 3 fluid

FJ55 power tailgate window won't move →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · An afternoon to a weekendSHOP COST · Often only cleaning, wiring and a power strip; a used motor or regulator if the gear is stripped
SYMPTOMSThe rear glass won't go up or down from the dash switch or the key. It may work only with the tailgate down. Or you hear the motor clunk while the glass stays put.
THE FIXIH8MUD members work from the simple stuff inwards. The tailgate has a safety switch: the motor won't run unless its plunger is pushed in. Slop in the gate or a broken plastic actuator can leave the switch open. Next, check the power strip and the harness where it flexes at the hinge; one owner found his strip 'rotten' and replaced it. A meter will tell you whether power reaches the motor when you press the dash switch. If the motor runs on direct battery power but the glass doesn't move, suspect a stripped regulator gear. Members say the glass tray has to be separated from the regulator by driving out two clips, and that the mechanism is often packed with old grime. Be gentle with the glass: one owner broke a donor truck's window getting it down.
TOOLS & PARTSScrewdrivers · multimeter · jumper leads and a 12 V battery · drift and hammer for the tray clips · grease

Asked on every forum

Land Cruiser FJ45 / FJ55 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

We couldn't find a solid FJ55- or FJ45-specific consensus. They use 40 Series axles, and for the stock FJ40, IH8MUD's 'tried and true' size is 31x10.50R15, with 33x9.50R15 fitting but rubbing at full flex. Try any size on your own truck before committing.

SOURCE · IH8MUD (FJ40)
What's the difference between an FJ45 and an FJ55?

The FJ45 is the long-wheelbase version of the 40 Series: pickups and cab-chassis on 2,950 mm and an early four-door wagon on 2,650 mm. The FJ55 (1967–80) is a purpose-built four-door wagon on a 2,700 mm wheelbase with its own fully boxed frame.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · HAGERTY
Why is it called the Iron Pig?

Hagerty traces the name to the FJ55's front end, where the two-tone paint made it resemble a pig's snout. Wikipedia also records 'Moose.'

SOURCE · HAGERTY · WIKIPEDIA
Which years are the best to buy?

There's no settled answer. Later trucks have the 4.2 L 2F, a four-speed and front disc brakes (the domed hood is the easy tell), and factory power steering came at the very end. Even IH8MUD members disagree on exactly when those arrived, with some 1974–75 trucks carrying odd mixes of parts. Earlier trucks use the 3.9 L F and a three-speed whose first gear has no synchro, which some collectors prefer for originality. Owners agree on one thing: rust is your biggest enemy, so buy the straightest body you can find.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · HAGERTY · WIKIPEDIA
Can I get body panels?

Not complete ones. Hagerty says replacement panels generally don't exist and missing metal usually has to be fabricated, and trim and weatherstripping are hard to find. Cruiser Corps does sell FJ55 repair sections, including outer rockers and lower-fender patch panels with the inner brace. Buy the most solid truck you can afford.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · CRUISER CORPS
Are the drum brakes good enough?

Hagerty says stock drums are adequate if they're in good order and the powertrain is stock. Disc conversions are common and parts are easy to find.

SOURCE · HAGERTY
What are the lift options?

Old Man Emu makes 2-inch kits for the FJ45 (1963–80 and 1980–84), sold through specialists such as Cruiser Corps, with greaseable shackles and a steering damper. Confirm fitment for an FJ55 before ordering.

SOURCE · CRUISER CORPS
Is the engine reliable?

Yes, with basic care. Hagerty calls the F and 2F durable with basic maintenance and periodic valve adjustments, and says mechanical repairs are easy and cheap compared with rust repair.

SOURCE · HAGERTY
What's an FJ55 worth?

In Hagerty's FJ55 buyer's guide (first published in 2021 and updated in 2023), the median value for all conditions was $34,500 and for #2 condition $46,300, with quoted values up 65.8 percent since 2020. Its earlier wagon-values piece mentioned private sales approaching six figures.

SOURCE · HAGERTY
Were FJ45s really used by armies?

Yes. Truck Encyclopedia describes the super-long-wheelbase FJ45 as a major military export used as a troop carrier, and the Irish Army took 77 FJ45s in 1977.

SOURCE · TRUCK ENCYCLOPEDIA · WIKIPEDIA
