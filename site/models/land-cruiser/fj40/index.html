PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Toyota launched the 40 Series in 1960 as a ground-up replacement for the 20 Series, and then barely changed it for nearly a quarter of a century. Toyota's own UK history notes the 1979 facelift was the first visual change in 19 years. Underneath sat solid axles on leaf springs at both ends and, for the first time in a Land Cruiser, a two-speed transfer case. The short-wheelbase FJ40 is the one on posters, but the J40 family also included the medium-wheelbase FJ43 and the long-wheelbase FJ45 pickups and wagons.

A JEEP RIVAL THAT OUTSOLD EVERYTHING

In America the FJ40 was pitched against the Jeep and it worked. Toyota's pressroom says the Land Cruiser was the company's best-selling vehicle in the US until 1965, when the Corona passenger car took over; Wikipedia records global output passing 50,000 that year. Toyota's US pressroom says the FJ40 came to America in 1960, two years after the first Land Cruiser was sold here; Hagerty dates FJ40 imports from 1963, so the exact start depends on whom you ask. Either way, a little Japanese 4x4 was carrying the brand in its biggest export market before most Americans had heard of Toyota.

The engine did a lot of the persuading. The 3.9-liter F straight-six made 125 hp in Toyota's US history, and Hagerty calls it 'virtually indestructible' and essentially a reverse-engineered Chevrolet 235 'Stovebolt' six (Wikipedia says the bottom end is closer to GMC's 236 six, so treat the family tree as folklore). It drove a three-speed column-shift manual at launch. Diesels came later for other markets, the H six in the long-wheelbase HJ45 from 1972 or 1973 (sources differ) and the four-cylinder B in 1974, but Wikipedia notes diesel 40s were never sold to the general US public; a few arrived as mine trucks.

QUIET EVOLUTION

Changes came mechanically rather than visually. US trucks got the 4.2-liter 2F and a four-speed in 1975, along with rear barn doors and, a year earlier, a standard roll bar. Front disc brakes arrived in 1976. In 1979 came the square headlight bezels, optional power steering and air conditioning on petrol trucks, and a switch from 4.11 to 3.70 axle gears. IH8MUD members still steer first-time buyers to post-1976 trucks for the discs and the 2F, and warn that 1980-and-later bodies seem to rust more readily.

The truck stayed crude to the end. Retro Rides sums up the driving experience as vague steering, wayward handling, a harsh ride on heavy leaf springs and a cabin that gets very hot in summer. That's part of the appeal: almost everything on it can be reached with a 17 mm wrench and a bit of patience. Free-wheeling front hubs became standard in Australia from 1982, and a five-speed arrived later in the run, but US buyers never saw much luxury.

THE END, AND THE AFTERLIFE

The 1983 model year was the last for US FJ40s; Wikipedia estimates only about 300 were imported that final year. Production ended in most markets in 1984, when the 70 Series took over the utility role, though Canada got a run of BJ42 diesels with a five-speed overdrive. Brazil kept going much longer: the Toyota Bandeirante used the 40's body with Mercedes-Benz and later Toyota diesels until 2001, giving the basic shape more than four decades of production.

Values climbed sharply in the 2010s, then settled. In 2019 Hagerty put the average #2-condition FJ40 at $47,375, down 7.6 percent since 2016, while Retro Rides tracks an Australian example rising from $4,400 in 1995 to $33,000 in 2024. Toyota noticed too: in 2021 it announced GR Heritage Parts reproductions for the 40 Series, starting with engine, driveline and exhaust components. The FJ40's legacy even includes the 2007 FJ Cruiser, which borrowed its white roof and round headlights.

SIDEBAR · THE BRAZILIAN COUSIN

The Bandeirante: a 40 that refused to die

Toyota do Brasil began building Land Cruisers in 1962 and, from 1968, switched its Bandeirante to the FJ40 body. Instead of Toyota petrol sixes, it used Mercedes-Benz diesels: the OM324, then the OM314 and OM364, before Toyota's own 14B four-cylinder diesel arrived in 1994.

Brazil also got a five-speed in 1993, square headlights from 1989 and pickup and wagon versions on the long wheelbase. Production finally ended in November 2001, decades after Japan had moved on to the 70 Series.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The FJ40 is about as simple as a 4x4 gets: a ladder frame, live axles on leaf springs, drum brakes until 1976 and a big, slow-revving straight-six. The front axle uses closed knuckles with Birfield CV joints and tapered knuckle bearings set up with shims, the same basic design Toyota carried into the FJ60 and FJ62.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust beats everythingIH8MUD veterans rank rust above every mechanical issue combined. Check door bottoms, the rear sill, the body behind the under-seat fuel tank, wheel wells, the outer front fender brace, inside sills and windscreen pillars. Get underneath and look at all of the metal.
  2. 02
    Body mounts and floorsOn 1979-and-later trucks, look hard at the body mounts. If they're heavily rusted, expect the floor pans and door pillars to be rotten too. Reproduction floor pans exist, but a badly rusted frame may not be worth saving.
  3. 03
    Frame endsCheck the rear frame horns and the body around them, a spot IH8MUD members flag as the biggest worry on many listings.
  4. 04
    Signs of hard wheelingHeavily dented skid plates, gouged differential drain plugs and leaf springs with a slight S-shape all suggest a hard life. None is fatal, but they mean work ahead.
  5. 05
    Driveline slop and leaksCheck both driveshafts for play ('any slop is too much'), look for cracks or welds on the transfer case, and watch for leaks at the transmission, transfer case, axles and wheels. Oil between engine and gearbox often means a rear main seal.
  6. 06
    Compression, not the odometerMileage tells you little on a truck whose engine may have been rebuilt. A compression test does. Members put a full 2F rebuild at $2,000–3,000 in the mid-2000s, and expect $1,000–2,000 of catch-up work after purchase.
  7. 07
    Brakes and fluidsFour-wheel drums need constant adjustment. Black brake or clutch fluid suggests the previous owner skipped bleeding, and probably other maintenance too.
  8. 08
    Smog and wiringIn smog-check states, make sure the emissions equipment is all present and the truck passes. Messy wiring is a sign of future electrical gremlins.

Spotting the real thing

Few FJ40s are still original: engines are swapped, bodies re-skinned and trucks titled by build date or sale date. Match the numbers before you pay for originality.

  1. ID
    Frame number and plateCheck that the frame number on the frame matches the VIN plate. On 1960s US trucks the plate is on the firewall, later on the passenger-side fender well; an A-pillar plate appears from about 3/1969, and from about 1978 the plate is pop-riveted back on the firewall. Specter Off-Road's frame-number chart dates a truck to its build month.
  2. ID
    Build date vs model yearThe driver's door pillar is stamped with the build month. A truck built late in a year is often titled as the next model year, so quote the production date at the parts counter.
  3. ID
    Engine numbersThe firewall plate lists the engine number. A mismatch means a replacement block. Small-block Chevy V8 swaps are very common, and on BaT swapped trucks sell for less than stock ones.
  4. ID
    Year tellsUS trucks: barn doors from 1975, front discs from 1976, vent-window changes for 1977, square grille bezels and optional power steering and A/C from 1979.
  5. ID
    Diesels and BandeirantesDiesel 40s (BJ, HJ) were never sold to US retail buyers, so a US diesel is an import or an ex-mine truck. Brazilian Bandeirantes (OJ and BJ codes) look like FJ40s but used Mercedes-Benz or later Toyota 14B diesels.

Importing & legality

US FJ40s were all gasoline sixes. Diesel BJ40s, BJ42s and H-engine 40s, and Brazilian Bandeirantes (built to 2001), are gray-market. A vehicle at least 25 years old, measured from its date of manufacture, can be imported into the US without meeting FMVSS, so every J40-family truck now qualifies. JDMBUYSELL notes that EPA still expects the original-design engine or an EPA-certified replacement. Corsetti Cruisers warns that California may refuse to register an import without a 50-state-compliant emissions system, so plan for that before buying a BJ or HJ.

SYMPTOMSLoud ticking from the top end (loose valves), or poor running and burned valves (tight valves, which are the ones to worry about).
THE FIXWarm the engine. With it idling, use go/no-go feeler gauges to find the tight and loose valves. THE FIXBolt-on kits replace the front drums with bracketed calipers and rotors: remove the hub cover, snap ring, drum and hub, fit the bracket, test-fit the rotor (Off-Road.com's install had to grind the hub slightly, only to the casting mark), refit the hub, mount the caliper, fit new lines and bleed. THE FIXOld Man Emu's 2-inch FJ40 kits come with springs, Nitrocharger shocks, greaseable shackles and pins, bushings, U-bolts and a steering damper. Swap one axle at a time with the frame supported. Asked on every forum

Land Cruiser FJ40 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

The 'old tried and true' answer on IH8MUD is 31x10.50R15 on stock wheels and suspension. 33x9.50R15 is a popular size on stock 15-inch rims too, but owners report rubbing at the rear over bumps or at full flex. One fixed it with 1-inch spacers under the bump stops, and another had to adjust the front steering stops. One member said 33x10.50s looked 'pretty stuffed.'

SOURCE · IH8MUD
Which years should I look for?

Most buyers prefer 1976-and-later trucks for front disc brakes and the 4.2 L 2F. 1979 added optional power steering and A/C and taller 3.70 gears in the US. IH8MUD veterans like the '78 and would consider 1979–83 at the right price, but note that 1980-and-later bodies seem to rust more readily.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · WIKIPEDIA
Three-speed or four-speed?

The four-speed arrived with the 2F in 1975 in the US. Most owners prefer it, especially on 33-inch tires. If you want overdrive, the H55F five-speed swap is well documented, though it takes floor, crossmember, linkage and driveshaft work.

SOURCE · TOYOTA · IH8MUD
Did the US get diesel FJ40s?

Not for the general public. Diesel 40s were never sold to US retail buyers, though some arrived as mine trucks. Canada was the exception in 1984, when it got the BJ42 diesel with an overdrive five-speed for about CA$14,000.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Does it have power steering?

Only as an option on petrol FJ40s from 1979. Earlier trucks have manual steering, and many owners fit a GM Saginaw power-steering conversion. It isn't a casual bolt-on, and Cruiser Corps recommends professional installation.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · CRUISER CORPS
Are the drum brakes OK?

They work, but they need constant adjustment. Front discs came on US trucks in 1976. Bolt-on conversion kits exist for 1970–75 trucks, and IH8MUD has a write-up using mini-truck knuckles with later Land Cruiser calipers and rotors.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · OFF-ROAD.COM
What are the lift options?

The common choice is an Old Man Emu 2-inch kit with greaseable anti-inversion shackles. For bigger lifts, some owners go spring-over-axle, which IH8MUD members say gives roughly 6 inches and is usually paired with power steering.

SOURCE · CRUISER CORPS · IH8MUD
Should I swap in a V8?

Plenty of owners have. Hagerty notes many FJ40s got small-block Chevy V8s on the secondary market. The stock six is durable, though, and a healthy 2F with good compression will run a long time.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · IH8MUD
Can I still get parts?

Yes. Toyota announced GR Heritage Parts reproductions for the 40 Series in 2021, starting with engine, driveline and exhaust parts. Specialists such as Cruiser Corps, CruiserTeq, Cool Cruisers of Texas and JT Outfitters stock everything from knuckle kits to floor pans.

SOURCE · GOAUTO · CRUISER CORPS
What's one worth?

It depends heavily on condition and originality. In 2019 Hagerty put the average #2-condition FJ40 at $47,375, after a 7.6 percent drop since 2016, with a 1968 at $49,100 and a 1975 at $44,800. Retro Rides says tidy Australian examples run roughly AU$30,000–50,000 and that US prices are far higher.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · RETRO RIDES
