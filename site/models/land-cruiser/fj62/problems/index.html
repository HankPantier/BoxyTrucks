Land Cruiser FJ62 problems: 8 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the Toyota Land Cruiser FJ62, the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Land Cruiser FJ62 problems to check before you buy

  1. Rear frame railsCorsetti says this is the most important check on any 60: drop the spare if necessary and inspect the rear frame rails. Rusted through means walk away.
  2. The usual 60 rust spotsWindshield gasket corners, roof drip rails, tailgate hinge areas, inner and outer rear wheel wells, front fenders and rockers. Trucks from salted-road states rust fast; a magnet finds filler around the wheel wells and tailgate.
  3. Find the oil leakMost will have one. Before blaming the rear main seal, IH8MUD members suggest checking that the oil pan bolts are torqued to spec and that the valve cover gasket isn't the real culprit.
  4. Rough idleUsually a tune-up item or a vacuum leak rather than anything serious, but it's a useful bargaining point.
  5. Test the A/C and the 4WDCheck that the A/C compressor engages; it may just need a recharge. Engage four-wheel drive and low range on a loose surface and make sure the push-button system works.
  6. Knuckles and steeringLook for sludge at the front knuckles, and drive it to check for wobble or wander, especially on a lifted truck.
  7. Power-steering leaksA wet, shiny driver's-side axle and spring usually means the power-steering box or pump is leaking.
  8. Ask about the transfer caseIH8MUD members treated a transfer-case rebuild at 74,000 miles as a red flag on one truck. Ask why any major driveline work was done.

Land Cruiser FJ62 recalls and service bulletins

  • Rear lap belt may not retract ↗ 1982–1988 · NHTSA 89V155000The rear lap belt can stay partly extended and lock; the fix was a belt guide or sheath. 1988 is the only FJ62 year covered.
  • Fuel tank welds can crack ↗ 1980–1990 · NHTSA 91V110000Heat and load can pressurize the tank and crack its welds, leaking fuel that can catch fire near an ignition source; the remedy was an improved tank (38,500 vehicles, 1980–90 Land Cruisers).

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about Land Cruiser FJ62 reliability

Is a little oil leak normal?

On a truck this age, mostly yes. As one IH8MUD member put it, 'any '88 Cruiser is going to leak some oil, from somewhere.' Check the pan bolts and valve cover gasket before assuming it's the rear main seal.
