PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The FJ62 is the last and most civilized of the 60 Series, and in America it marked the point where the Land Cruiser started to become a luxury vehicle. Hagerty says it brought two Land Cruiser firsts to the US: electronic fuel injection, on the 4.0-liter 3F-E six, and an automatic transmission. Toyota's own history picks out the new dash, the upgraded interior and the rectangular headlamps that make a 1988 easy to spot. Underneath, it's still a ladder-frame, leaf-sprung, solid-axle truck, much like the FJ60 it replaced.

BUILT FOR JAPAN, SOLD TO AMERICA

The FJ62 started at home. Wikipedia dates the 3F-E injected engine to 1987, when the FJ62G VX allowed the Land Cruiser to be registered in Japan as a passenger vehicle rather than a commercial one. That meant comfort features: Flexmotor lists power windows and locks among the new options. The US got the FJ62 for the 1988 model year, and for American buyers it was a noticeably more comfortable, easier-driving truck than the FJ60.

The 3F-E made 155 hp at 4,000 rpm and 220 lb-ft at 3,000 rpm, against the FJ60's carbureted 135 hp and 210 lb-ft. It drove through Toyota's A440F four-speed automatic, the only US transmission, an automatic Toyota UK says first went into the 60 Series in 1984 as the first of its kind in a Japanese 4x4. Corsetti Cruisers lists the curb weight at 4,480 lb, 234 lb more than an FJ60, and fuel economy of 12 mpg city and 19 highway, one better than the carbureted truck.

STILL A TRUCK UNDERNEATH

For all the velour and power windows, the FJ62 kept the 60 Series hardware: leaf springs, a closed-knuckle Birfield front axle and a two-speed, part-time transfer case. That makes it as capable in the dirt as an FJ60 and as simple to work on, with many parts shared across the 40, 55 and 60 Series. Toyota UK says push-button electro-pneumatic four-wheel-drive engagement replaced manual levers on 60s from August 1986; which markets and models got it isn't clear from that history, so whatever your truck has, check that four-wheel drive and low range engage cleanly.

The 3F-E has its own following. IH8MUD members note that in US trucks it was used only in the FJ62 and the 1991–92 FJ80, and consider a well-kept example with 159,000 miles to have plenty of life left. The common gripes are the ones you'd expect on a 35-year-old truck: oil leaks, vacuum leaks that cause a rough idle, tired A/C and, above all, rust. As one member put it, 'any '88 Cruiser is going to leak some oil, from somewhere.'

THE END OF THE LEAF-SPRING WAGON

Japanese production of the 60 Series ended around the turn of 1990; Toyota UK puts the total at 406,700. Venezuela kept building 60s for its own market until 1992, and Wikipedia says the last carbureted 3F FJ62 rolled out of the Cumaná plant that year. In the US the FJ62 handed over to the 80 Series for 1991, which kept the 3F-E and automatic for two years but switched to coil springs and full-time four-wheel drive.

That makes the FJ62 the last leaf-sprung Land Cruiser wagon sold in America, and the most comfortable vintage Cruiser you can drive every day. Hagerty calls the round-headlight FJ60 the bigger collector's prize and its wagon-values piece (around 2020) put the median quote for FJ60s and FJ62s at $18,500, but the FJ62's fuel injection and automatic make it the easier truck to live with. Restomod shops such as Corsetti Cruisers build both.

SIDEBAR · WANT THREE PEDALS?

Converting an FJ62 to a manual

US FJ62s only came with the A440F automatic, so some owners swap in the H55F five-speed used in other markets. IH8MUD's swap guide lists what it takes: a manual bellhousing, flywheel and clutch kit, crossmember and floor-cover modifications, transfer-case shift linkage, driveshaft changes, a clutch pedal, master and slave cylinders with hard line and hose, and minor wiring changes.

It's a big job, but the H55F's 0.845:1 fifth and deeper first suit both highway driving and trail work.

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The FJ62 is a 60 Series chassis with a modernized powertrain: the fuel-injected 3F-E four-liter six and an A440F four-speed automatic feeding a two-speed, part-time transfer case. Suspension is still leaf springs on solid axles, with the closed-knuckle Birfield front axle shared with the FJ60 and late FJ40.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rear frame railsCorsetti says this is the most important check on any 60: drop the spare if necessary and inspect the rear frame rails. Rusted through means walk away.
  2. 02
    The usual 60 rust spotsWindshield gasket corners, roof drip rails, tailgate hinge areas, inner and outer rear wheel wells, front fenders and rockers. Trucks from salted-road states rust fast; a magnet finds filler around the wheel wells and tailgate.
  3. 03
    Find the oil leakMost will have one. Before blaming the rear main seal, IH8MUD members suggest checking that the oil pan bolts are torqued to spec and that the valve cover gasket isn't the real culprit.
  4. 04
    Rough idleUsually a tune-up item or a vacuum leak rather than anything serious, but it's a useful bargaining point.
  5. 05
    Test the A/C and the 4WDCheck that the A/C compressor engages; it may just need a recharge. Engage four-wheel drive and low range on a loose surface and make sure the push-button system works.
  6. 06
    Knuckles and steeringLook for sludge at the front knuckles, and drive it to check for wobble or wander, especially on a lifted truck.
  7. 07
    Power-steering leaksA wet, shiny driver's-side axle and spring usually means the power-steering box or pump is leaking.
  8. 08
    Ask about the transfer caseIH8MUD members treated a transfer-case rebuild at 74,000 miles as a red flag on one truck. Ask why any major driveline work was done.

Spotting the real thing

The FJ62 is the easy 60 to live with, but carbureted Venezuelan trucks and imported HJ61s share its body.

  1. ID
    EngineUS FJ62s have the fuel-injected 3F-E (155 hp), which in the US was also used in the 1991–92 FJ80. Venezuelan FJ62s built to 1992 used a carbureted 3F.
  2. ID
    TransmissionUS trucks came only with the A440F four-speed automatic. A manual FJ62 has been converted, usually to an H55F.
  3. ID
    LooksSquare headlights and the facelifted front end set it apart from the round-headlight FJ60.
  4. ID
    JDM VXThe Japanese-market FJ62G VX launched in 1987. Right-hand-drive VX imports are gray-market, not US trucks.

Recalls & service bulletins

Open recalls follow the VIN, not the model. Run yours through NHTSA before you buy, and ask the seller for dealer records showing recall work.

Check a VIN at NHTSA ↗
Notable recalls
Rear lap belt may not retract ↗1982–1988 · NHTSA 89V155000The rear lap belt can stay partly extended and lock; the fix was a belt guide or sheath. 1988 is the only FJ62 year covered.
Fuel tank welds can crack ↗1980–1990 · NHTSA 91V110000Heat and load can pressurize the tank and crack its welds, leaking fuel that can catch fire near an ignition source; the remedy was an improved tank (38,500 vehicles, 1980–90 Land Cruisers).

Importing & legality

Every US FJ62 is a 1988–90 gasoline automatic. HJ61 turbodiesels, right-hand-drive FJ62G VXs and Venezuelan carbureted FJ62s (built to 1992) are gray-market but now more than 25 years old, measured from the date of manufacture. Corsetti Cruisers warns that California may refuse to register an import without a 50-state-compliant emissions system.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Push-button 4WD stuck on: vacuum solenoid and lines
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hoursSHOP COST · Vacuum hose, or a replacement solenoid valve; IH8MUD owners report paying anywhere from a $17.77 donor part to about $400 for a new Toyota one
SYMPTOMSThe green 4WD light stays on after you switch 4WD off, the front driveshaft won't turn by hand with the switch off, or the truck won't shift in or out of 4WD.
THE FIXOn the FJ62's push-button system, vacuum solenoid valves (VSVs) on the passenger-side firewall send vacuum to the transfer-case actuator. With the switch off and the truck in neutral on the parking brake, check for vacuum at each side of the solenoid. IH8MUD owners trace most cases to a burnt-out solenoid, but others found a clogged vacuum line or one corroded at a bracket, so check the lines and the actuator diaphragm before buying parts. One owner's interim fix is to pull a vacuum line and apply vacuum to the correct port by hand to get it out of 4WD. Don't swap the two lines: one engages 4WD and the other releases it. Replace the solenoid, or follow the IH8MUD write-ups that adapt a junkyard Toyota VSV or a MAC valve.

Fuel tank leak and the 91V-110 recall
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day to drop and replace the tankSHOP COST · Free if the recall is still open and parts are available; otherwise a tank, filler hose and lines
SYMPTOMSFuel smell, or fuel dripping near the rear axle, especially when filling. NHTSA owner complaints describe a tank split at a spot weld and a corroded fuel line in the left rear wheel well.
THE FIXNHTSA campaign 91V-110 covers 1980–1990 Land Cruisers, including every FJ62 year: heat and load can pressurize the tank and crack the welds that hold its internal separator, and Toyota's remedy was a new, improved tank. Run the VIN through NHTSA's recall lookup and ask a Toyota dealer whether the work was ever done; several owners told NHTSA the parts weren't available when they tried. If you're replacing it yourself, drain the tank, support it on a jack, disconnect the filler hose, lines and sending unit, and drop it. While it's down, replace the rubber filler hose and grommets and inspect the steel fuel lines, which can corrode through.

Asked on every forum

Land Cruiser FJ62 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

On a stock FJ62, one IH8MUD member runs 33x9.50s with no problems. 33x10.50 might fit, but 33x12.50s won't without a lift. Many owners choose 31x10.50R15, which looks right at stock height or on an OME lift. Corsetti says not to go wider than 10.5 in or taller than 33 in on a factory rim, and Cruiser Corps advises against tires wider than 10.5 in on the 15x5.5 OEM steel wheel.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · CORSETTI · CRUISER CORPS
FJ62 or FJ60?

The FJ62 has fuel injection, 155 hp, an automatic and a plusher cabin, so it's easier to drive every day. The FJ60 has the carbureted 2F, a four-speed manual and round headlights. Hagerty calls the FJ60 the more collectible of the two.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · CORSETTI
Is the 3F-E the same engine as an early 80 Series?

Yes. IH8MUD members note that in US trucks the 3F-E was used only in the FJ62 and the 1991–92 FJ80, so parts and knowledge carry across.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
Can I convert it to a manual?

Yes, with an H55F five-speed, but it's a big job. You'll need a manual bellhousing, flywheel, clutch, pedal and hydraulics, crossmember and floor modifications, shift linkage, driveshaft changes and some wiring.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
How much can it tow?

Corsetti lists 3,500 lb, the same as the FJ60.

SOURCE · CORSETTI
What fuel economy should I expect?

Corsetti lists 12 mpg city and 19 highway with a 23.8-gallon tank. Real-world numbers on bigger tires are lower.

SOURCE · CORSETTI
Is a little oil leak normal?

On a truck this age, mostly yes. As one IH8MUD member put it, 'any '88 Cruiser is going to leak some oil, from somewhere.' Check the pan bolts and valve cover gasket before assuming it's the rear main seal.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
What lift should I run?

The common setup is an Old Man Emu 2-inch kit for 4/1985–1/1990 trucks, in light, medium or heavy load ratings, with greaseable shackles and a steering damper.

SOURCE · CRUISER CORPS
What was the FJ62G VX?

The Japanese-market version introduced in 1987 with the 3F-E. Wikipedia says it let the Land Cruiser be sold in Japan as a passenger vehicle rather than a commercial one.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What's one worth?

Hagerty's wagon-values piece (around 2020) put the median quote for FJ60s and FJ62s at $18,500, and called the FJ60 the pick for collectibility. Restomod FJ62s from specialist shops sell for far more.

SOURCE · HAGERTY
What oil and fluids does it take?

Toyota's 3F-E repair manual supplement lists 7.8 liters (8.2 quarts) of oil with a filter change, API SC–SF or better, with the viscosity chosen from a temperature chart that runs from 5W-30 to 20W-50. Coolant capacity is 17.5 liters, or 19.5 with the rear heater. An IH8MUD compilation of Toyota data lists Dexron II for the A440F (5 liters on a drain-and-refill), GL-4 or GL-5 SAE 90 for the transfer case, GL-5 80W-90 for both differentials, Dexron or Dexron II for the power steering and DOT 3 brake fluid. Fill the engine to the dipstick, though, because the oil cooler holds some oil.

SOURCE · TOYOTA 3F-E MANUAL · IH8MUD
