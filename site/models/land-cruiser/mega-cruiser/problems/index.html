Toyota Mega Cruiser problems: 7 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the Toyota Mega Cruiser, the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

Toyota Mega Cruiser problems to check before you buy

  1. Civilian or military?A BXD20V civilian wagon and an ex-JGSDF BXD10 soft-top are different trucks with different values. Ex-military trucks have arrived via third countries (one BaT truck was repainted in Dubai and titled as a 1990), so check the import paperwork and the chassis plate.
  2. Do the 4WS and tire deflation work?Several BaT trucks were sold with inoperative four-wheel steering or central tire inflation, or with parts such as the deflator hose missing. Check them, because these systems are part of what you're paying for.
  3. Underbody rustBaT sellers have noted rust on the underside, painted-over underbodies and rust repair before a repaint. Get under it with a light and a probe, especially around the hub-reduction housings and frame.
  4. 24-volt electricsThe truck runs a 24-volt electrical system. Budget for batteries in pairs, and make sure any added accessories are wired for 24 V.
  5. Parts supplyIbaraki Prefecture sold its Mega Cruiser partly because parts were hard to get. The 15B engine family was also used in Dyna light trucks, which helps with engine service items, but body and driveline parts are specialist. Find your source before you buy.
  6. Size and accessIt's 2,170 mm wide, 5,090 mm long and 2,075 mm tall, so measure your garage, check parking and trail widths, and remember the high sills that need the grab handles.
  7. Service records and import papersRecent BaT trucks with records listed axle-shaft, wheel-bearing, motor-mount, gasket and differential-fluid work. Receipts like these, plus clean import documents and a title, add real value.

What to bring when you look at a Toyota Mega Cruiser

Six inexpensive tools that catch most of the problems above before you hand over the money. Or book a specialist: see how to get a pre-purchase inspection.

  • Bluetooth OBD-II scannerReads stored and pending codes, and shows the readiness monitors. If several say "not ready", someone may have cleared the codes just before you arrived.
    Amazon ↗
  • Paint thickness gaugeThick readings on one panel mean body filler or a respray, which usually means an accident or rust repair the seller hasn’t mentioned.
    Amazon ↗
  • Inspection borescope cameraFits through drain holes into frame rails, rockers and sills, where these trucks rust from the inside out.
    Amazon ↗
  • Bright LED flashlightFor the underside: leaks at the axle seals, transfer case and rear main, and the frame behind the rear wheels.
    Amazon ↗
  • Telescoping inspection mirrorShows the back of the engine, the top of the fuel tank and the inner fenders without crawling under.
    Amazon ↗
  • Compression testerOn a high-mileage or long-parked engine, uneven compression is the cheapest way to spot a tired engine before you pay for it. Diesels need a diesel-rated tester.
    Amazon ↗
