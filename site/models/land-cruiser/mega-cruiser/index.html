PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The Mega Cruiser is the largest 4WD Toyota has ever built, and it exists because the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force wanted its own Humvee. The military version is the Kōkidōsha, or High Mobility Vehicle, model code BXD10. In January 1996 Toyota put a civilian version on sale in Japan, the BXD20V, aimed at disaster rescue rather than the school run. It shares nothing with any 80 Series or 70 Series except the badge and the attitude, and it looks enough like an H1 that Japan nicknamed it the 'Japanese Hummer'.

THE KŌKIDŌSHA

Toyota developed and supplied the High Mobility Vehicle, which Japanese sources say Hino builds. From 1993 the JGSDF put test batches with its trials unit, the Infantry School Regiment and the 1st Airborne Brigade, then trial-deployed it to 2nd Division units in 1994. Regular deliveries began with the 1st Infantry Regiment in spring 1995. It carries a ten-soldier squad, tows mortars and trailers, and became the base for missile launchers and other specialist versions. Troops call it the 'Kōki'.

The early trucks used the 15B-FT, a 4.1-litre direct-injection turbo-diesel four from Toyota's Dyna light trucks. Vehicles procured from fiscal 1999 to 2003 got the electronically injected 15B-FTE. From fiscal 2004 the HMV switched to Hino's N04C engine, which changed the model code to XCD10. Only the 15B-engined trucks are BXD10s. By the count in Japanese Wikipedia, more than 3,000 HMVs and derivatives have been built, and the JGSDF is still buying them.

A RESCUE TRUCK FOR CIVILIANS

A prototype appeared at the 30th Tokyo Motor Show in October 1993. Toyota's launch release on 9 January 1996 pitched the Mega Cruiser at rescue and reconstruction work after natural disasters, and at research and exploration in harsh conditions. Final assembly was at Gifu Auto Body in Kakamigahara. It cost ¥9.62 million (¥9.8 million after the 1999 engine change), about the same as a Toyota Century or Honda NSX of the day, and there was no tachometer.

The buyers were mostly institutions: the Japan Automobile Federation (JAF), fire and rescue services and local governments. Gifu Prefectural Police run one as a disaster vehicle, the only Mega Cruiser police car in Japan. The Maritime Self-Defense Force chose the civilian Mega Cruiser rather than the HMV, and the Air Self-Defense Force uses it as an off-base rescue vehicle. Production ended in August 2001 and sales that December. According to Japanese sources, new registrations totalled 133, including some high-roof trucks that never appeared in the catalogue.

HOW IT WORKS

Hub-reduction gears at each wheel let Toyota mount the drive shafts high and run 37-inch tires. Toyota quotes a practical ground clearance of 420 mm. To keep the space under the frame clear, the transmission, transfer case, prop shafts and differentials are all mounted as high as possible, which leaves a huge tunnel between the front seats. The brakes are inboard ventilated discs next to the differentials, because there's no room for them inside the hubs. Suspension is double wishbones on longitudinal torsion bars at all four corners.

Drive is full-time 4WD through a four-speed lockup automatic and a two-speed transfer case with a centre diff lock. Front and rear axles have Torsen limited-slip diffs, and each can be locked. Reverse-phase four-wheel steering turns the rear wheels up to 12 degrees, which gives a 5.6-metre turning radius on a 3,395 mm wheelbase. An optional system could deflate the rear tires on the move for mud and snow. One Japanese line sums it up: if you get this stuck, the only thing left is something with tracks.

COMING OF AGE ABROAD

Mega Cruisers leaked out of Japan as gray imports to places like Australia and Russia. The first ones turned 25 in 2020–21, which made them legal to import into the US under NHTSA's 25-year rule. Jalopnik reported that the Land Cruiser Heritage Museum in Utah already had one. Since then Bring a Trailer has listed both civilian trucks and ex-military BXD10s. In 2026 Japan donated 30 HMVs to Ukraine.

In Japan the civilian truck has become hard to keep on the road. Ibaraki Prefecture sold the Mega Cruiser a local bank had donated to it for disaster duty, citing difficulty getting parts, and the 3,780 kg gross vehicle weight means a holder of a standard licence issued after March 2017 can't legally drive one there. Rare, enormous and short of spares, it's a truck for collectors with a plan.

SIDEBAR · BXD10 VS BXD20

Military, civilian and the in-between

BXD10 is the JGSDF's 15B-engined High Mobility Vehicle: a canvas or soft-top troop carrier with inward-facing rear benches, folding windscreen, run-flat inserts in its tires and a pintle hitch. BXD20V is the civilian five-door wagon with a hard roof, six seats, air conditioning, a side-hinged tailgate with a fold-out step and a spare tire lifted by a chain hoist.

Bring a Trailer listings show both. In 2024 an ex-military BXD10, repainted in Dubai and titled in Maryland as a 1990, was bid to $43,999 without selling. Civilian trucks have sold for $84,000 and $167,500. Most are right-hand drive. One BaT seller claimed only seven left-hand-drive examples were built, and Carscoops put the 2020 count at around twelve.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The Mega Cruiser rides on a straight ladder frame with four-wheel independent suspension (double wishbones and longitudinal torsion bars) and hub-reduction gearing at each wheel. That gearing raises the half-shafts for 420 mm of clearance under 37-inch tires and adds a final reduction, so the truck can creep up steep grades on torque-converter creep alone. Full-time 4WD, a locking centre diff, Torsen front and rear diffs with their own locks, and hydraulic reverse-phase rear steer complete the package.

FRAMELadder frame, 3,395 mm (133.7 in) wheelbaseThe HMV chassis was also used for longer-wheelbase Super Dyna and Coaster 4WD variants.
ENGINE15B-FT 4.1 L I4 turbo-dieselDirect injection, intercooled. 155 PS at 3,200 rpm and 39.0 kgm (282 lb-ft) at 1,800 rpm. Became the 15B-FTE (170 PS, 43.0 kgm) from May 1999.
DRIVELINE4-speed lockup auto, 2-speed transferFull-time 4WD with a centre diff lock. Torsen LSDs front and rear, each with a lock.
SUSPENSIONIndependent, torsion barsDouble wishbones at all four corners. The long torsion bars and the weight give a good ride.
HUBS & BRAKESHub reduction · inboard vented discsBrakes sit next to the diffs to cut unsprung weight. Backlash in the reduction gears makes the truck pitch under hard braking.
STEERINGReverse-phase 4WS, 12° rear5.6 m turning radius. The rear steer centres itself on a spring when the hydraulics are off.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Civilian or military?A BXD20V civilian wagon and an ex-JGSDF BXD10 soft-top are different trucks with different values. Ex-military trucks have arrived via third countries (one BaT truck was repainted in Dubai and titled as a 1990), so check the import paperwork and the chassis plate.
  2. 02
    Do the 4WS and tire deflation work?Several BaT trucks were sold with inoperative four-wheel steering or central tire inflation, or with parts such as the deflator hose missing. Check them, because these systems are part of what you're paying for.
  3. 03
    Underbody rustBaT sellers have noted rust on the underside, painted-over underbodies and rust repair before a repaint. Get under it with a light and a probe, especially around the hub-reduction housings and frame.
  4. 04
    24-volt electricsThe truck runs a 24-volt electrical system. Budget for batteries in pairs, and make sure any added accessories are wired for 24 V.
  5. 05
    Parts supplyIbaraki Prefecture sold its Mega Cruiser partly because parts were hard to get. The 15B engine family was also used in Dyna light trucks, which helps with engine service items, but body and driveline parts are specialist. Find your source before you buy.
  6. 06
    Size and accessIt's 2,170 mm wide, 5,090 mm long and 2,075 mm tall, so measure your garage, check parking and trail widths, and remember the high sills that need the grab handles.
  7. 07
    Service records and import papersRecent BaT trucks with records listed axle-shaft, wheel-bearing, motor-mount, gasket and differential-fluid work. Receipts like these, plus clean import documents and a title, add real value.

Spotting the real thing

With 133 civilian trucks and thousands of military ones, knowing exactly which Mega Cruiser you're looking at decides what it's worth.

  1. ID
    Model codesBXD20V (full type KC-BXD20V-RRPEW) is the civilian five-door wagon. BXD10 is the 15B-engined JGSDF High Mobility Vehicle. XCD10 is the later HMV with a Hino N04C engine, built from FY2004.
  2. ID
    Civilian vs militaryCivilian trucks have a hard roof, six seats, air conditioning, a right-hinged side-opening tailgate with a retractable step, and a chain hoist for the high-mounted spare. Military BXD10s typically have canvas tops, inward-facing rear benches, a folding windscreen and run-flat inserts in the tires.
  3. ID
    High roofSome civilian trucks had a high roof that wasn't in the catalogue. BaT's 2025 high-roof truck had a fiberglass roof with hatches and safari windows.
  4. ID
    Engine15B-FT (mechanical injection) on 1996 to April 1999 civilian trucks, and 15B-FTE (electronic injection) from May 1999.
  5. ID
    Drive sideToyota built the Mega Cruiser in right-hand drive. Carscoops put the left-hand-drive count at around twelve in 2020, and one BaT seller claimed seven.

Importing & legality

Toyota sold the Mega Cruiser new only in Japan. Some reached Australia, Russia, Kenya and the Philippines as gray imports. NHTSA's 25-year exemption made the first trucks legal to import into the US from 2020–21, and BaT trucks have carried Florida, New Hampshire, Georgia and Maryland titles.

Ex-military BXD10s come out through third countries, so check the paperwork carefully: one BaT truck was repainted in Dubai and titled in Maryland as a 1990 model. Check your state's emissions and titling rules before you buy. In Japan, the truck's 3,780 kg gross weight puts it beyond the standard car licence for drivers licensed after March 2017.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Asked on every forum

Toyota Mega Cruiser questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

It already wears 37s. Japanese sources give the factory tire as 37x12.50R17.5 LT, and BaT trucks run 37x12.50 tires on 17-inch steel or aftermarket wheels. We found no owner reports of going bigger. With hub reduction and 420 mm of clearance, it doesn't need more.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA (JA) · BAT
Is it a Land Cruiser?

No. It shares the Cruiser name and Toyota's off-road ethos, but it's a separate design built from the JGSDF High Mobility Vehicle, with hub reduction, independent suspension at all four corners and four-wheel steering.

SOURCE · TOYOTA
How many were made?

Japanese sources put civilian new-car registrations at 133. The Drive cites the same figure from Toyota. The military HMV is a different story: more than 3,000 have been built including derivatives, and it's still in production with Hino engines.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA (JA) · THE DRIVE
Can I import one to the US?

Yes. The first trucks became eligible under NHTSA's 25-year rule from 2020–21, and several are now titled in the US. Check your state's emissions and titling rules, and get the import paperwork with the truck.

SOURCE · THE DRIVE · BAT
What engine does it have?

The 15B-FT, a 4,104 cc direct-injection turbo-diesel four with an intercooler: 155 PS at 3,200 rpm and 39.0 kgm (282 lb-ft) at 1,800 rpm. From May 1999 it became the electronically injected 15B-FTE, with 170 PS and 43.0 kgm.

SOURCE · TOYOTA · WIKIPEDIA (JA)
Does it have lockers?

Three. The centre diff locks through the two-speed transfer case, and the front and rear Torsen limited-slip diffs each have a lock as standard.

SOURCE · TOYOTA
How tight does it turn?

Reverse-phase rear steering turns the rear wheels up to 12 degrees, for a 5.6-metre turning radius despite a 3,395 mm wheelbase.

SOURCE · TOYOTA
What tire pressures does it run?

Japanese sources give unequal pressures: 140 kPa front and 240 kPa rear, chosen for rough-terrain performance. The optional system could lower the rear pressures on the move.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA (JA) · TOYOTA
How much does one cost?

On Bring a Trailer, civilian trucks have sold for $167,500 (January 2024) and $84,000 (June 2025), and other civilian trucks were bid to $82,000–144,000 without selling. Ex-military BXD10s were bid to $32,025–43,999 in 2024 without selling.

SOURCE · BAT
What fuel economy and range does it get?

The Drive reports that Toyota quoted about 22 mpg at a constant 37 mph, with a 28.5-gallon tank. Real-world highway economy isn't documented.

SOURCE · THE DRIVE
