TOYOTA LAND CRUISER · J60 · 1988–1990 (US)

Land Cruiser FJ62

FJ623F-E 4.0 I6 (EFI)A440F 4-SPEED AUTOSQUARE HEADLIGHTSPART-TIME 4WD

“The FJ62 is where the Land Cruiser put on a collared shirt: fuel injection, an automatic, square headlights and power everything, all on the same leaf-sprung frame that crossed the Outback. It is the easiest old Cruiser to drive every day, and one of the hardest to find without rust.”

The story

The FJ62 is the last and most civilized of the 60 Series, and in America it marked the point where the Land Cruiser started to become a luxury vehicle. Hagerty says it brought two Land Cruiser firsts to the US: electronic fuel injection, on the 4.0-liter 3F-E six, and an automatic transmission. Toyota's own history picks out the new dash, the upgraded interior and the rectangular headlamps that make a 1988 easy to spot. Underneath, it's still a ladder-frame, leaf-sprung, solid-axle truck, much like the FJ60 it replaced.

BUILT FOR JAPAN, SOLD TO AMERICA

The FJ62 started at home. Wikipedia dates the 3F-E injected engine to 1987, when the FJ62G VX allowed the Land Cruiser to be registered in Japan as a passenger vehicle rather than a commercial one. That meant comfort features: Flexmotor lists power windows and locks among the new options. The US got the FJ62 for the 1988 model year, and for American buyers it was a noticeably more comfortable, easier-driving truck than the FJ60.

The 3F-E made 155 hp at 4,000 rpm and 220 lb-ft at 3,000 rpm, against the FJ60's carbureted 135 hp and 210 lb-ft. It drove through Toyota's A440F four-speed automatic, the only US transmission, which Wikipedia describes as the first automatic in a Japanese four-wheel drive. Corsetti Cruisers lists the curb weight at 4,480 lb, 234 lb more than an FJ60, and fuel economy of 12 mpg city and 19 highway, one better than the carbureted truck.

STILL A TRUCK UNDERNEATH

For all the velour and power windows, the FJ62 kept the 60 Series hardware: leaf springs, a closed-knuckle Birfield front axle and a two-speed, part-time transfer case. That makes it as capable in the dirt as an FJ60 and as simple to work on, with many parts shared across the 40, 55 and 60 Series. Toyota UK says push-button electro-pneumatic four-wheel-drive engagement replaced manual levers on the 60 from August 1986, so FJ62 owners should check that the system engages cleanly.

The 3F-E has its own following. IH8MUD members note it was used only in the FJ62 and the 1991–92 FJ80, and consider a well-kept example with 159,000 miles to have plenty of life left. The common gripes are the ones you'd expect on a 35-year-old truck: oil leaks, vacuum leaks that cause a rough idle, tired A/C and, above all, rust. As one member put it, 'any '88 Cruiser is going to leak some oil, from somewhere.'

THE END OF THE LEAF-SPRING WAGON

Japanese production of the 60 Series ended around the turn of 1990; Toyota UK puts the total at 406,700. Venezuela kept building 60s for its own market until 1992, and Wikipedia says the last carbureted 3F FJ62 rolled out of the Cumaná plant that year. In the US the FJ62 handed over to the 80 Series for 1991, which kept the 3F-E and automatic for two years but switched to coil springs and full-time four-wheel drive.

That makes the FJ62 the last leaf-sprung Land Cruiser wagon sold in America, and the most comfortable vintage Cruiser you can drive every day. Hagerty calls the round-headlight FJ60 the bigger collector's prize and puts 60 Series median quotes around $18,500, but the FJ62's fuel injection and automatic make it the easier truck to live with. Restomod shops such as Corsetti Cruisers build both.

Converting an FJ62 to a manual

US FJ62s only came with the A440F automatic, so some owners swap in the H55F five-speed used in other markets. IH8MUD's swap guide lists what it takes: a manual bellhousing, flywheel and clutch kit, crossmember and floor-cover modifications, transfer-case shift linkage, driveshaft changes, a clutch pedal, master and slave cylinders with hard line and hose, and minor wiring changes.

It's a big job, but the H55F's 0.845:1 fifth and deeper first suit both highway driving and trail work.

How it’s built

The FJ62 is a 60 Series chassis with a modernized powertrain: the fuel-injected 3F-E four-liter six and an A440F four-speed automatic feeding a two-speed, part-time transfer case. Suspension is still leaf springs on solid axles, with the closed-knuckle Birfield front axle shared with the FJ60 and late FJ40.

Buying one: what to check

  1. 01
    Rear frame railsCorsetti says this is the most important check on any 60: drop the spare if necessary and inspect the rear frame rails. Rusted through means walk away.
  2. 02
    The usual 60 rust spotsWindshield gasket corners, roof drip rails, tailgate hinge areas, inner and outer rear wheel wells, front fenders and rockers. Trucks from salted-road states rust fast; a magnet finds filler around the wheel wells and tailgate.
  3. 03
    Find the oil leakMost will have one. Before blaming the rear main seal, IH8MUD members suggest checking that the oil pan bolts are torqued to spec and that the valve cover gasket isn't the real culprit.
  4. 04
    Rough idleUsually a tune-up item or a vacuum leak rather than anything serious, but it's a useful bargaining point.
  5. 05
    Test the A/C and the 4WDCheck that the A/C compressor engages; it may just need a recharge. Engage four-wheel drive and low range on a loose surface and make sure the push-button system works.
  6. 06
    Knuckles and steeringLook for sludge at the front knuckles, and drive it to check for wobble or wander, especially on a lifted truck.
  7. 07
    Power-steering leaksA wet, shiny driver's-side axle and spring usually means the power-steering box or pump is leaking.
  8. 08
    Ask about the transfer caseIH8MUD members treated a transfer-case rebuild at 74,000 miles as a red flag on one truck. Ask why any major driveline work was done.

After you buy it

An FJ62 is now 35+ years old, so treat every fluid and rubber part as suspect. The good news is that it's simple: a pushrod six with no timing belt, one automatic, a gear-driven part-time transfer case and two greased-knuckle solid axles. Common repairs

The jobs owners actually end up doing, rated for a competent home mechanic with a decent tool kit. Always confirm torque figures and procedures against the factory service manual for your VIN.

DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–3 hoursSHOP COST · Hoses and tune-up parts
SYMPTOMSRough or hunting idle, hesitation, or a hiss under the hood.
THE FIXStart with a tune-up: plugs, cap, rotor and wires. Then check every vacuum hose, since IH8MUD members point to vacuum leaks as the most likely cause on the 3F-E. Replace hard or cracked hoses rather than patching them, and recheck the idle once warm. Trucks over 200,000 miles will probably need a full set of vacuum hoses.

Diagnose oil leaks before pulling the transmission
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hours to diagnoseSHOP COST · Gaskets; a rear main seal job is far more
SYMPTOMSOil drips at the bellhousing or under the engine.
THE FIXClean the engine, then drive and look again. DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A weekend per sideSHOP COST · Parts plus labor

Asked on every forum

Land Cruiser FJ62 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

On a stock FJ62, one IH8MUD member runs 33x9.50s with no problems. 33x10.50 might fit, but 33x12.50s won't without a lift. Many owners choose 31x10.50R15, which looks right at stock height or with about 2.5 inches of lift, though some shops won't mount it on the stock 15x6 wheel.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
FJ62 or FJ60?

The FJ62 has fuel injection, 155 hp, an automatic and a plusher cabin, so it's easier to drive every day. The FJ60 has the carbureted 2F, a four-speed manual and round headlights. Hagerty calls the FJ60 the more collectible of the two.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · CORSETTI
Is the 3F-E the same engine as an early 80 Series?

Yes. IH8MUD members note the 3F-E was used only in the FJ62 and the 1991–92 FJ80, so parts and knowledge carry across.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
Can I convert it to a manual?

Yes, with an H55F five-speed, but it's a big job. You'll need a manual bellhousing, flywheel, clutch, pedal and hydraulics, crossmember and floor modifications, shift linkage, driveshaft changes and some wiring.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
How much can it tow?

Corsetti lists 3,500 lb, the same as the FJ60.

SOURCE · CORSETTI
What fuel economy should I expect?

Corsetti lists 12 mpg city and 19 highway with a 23.8-gallon tank. Real-world numbers on bigger tires are lower.

SOURCE · CORSETTI
Is a little oil leak normal?

On a truck this age, mostly yes. As one IH8MUD member put it, 'any '88 Cruiser is going to leak some oil, from somewhere.' Check the pan bolts and valve cover gasket before assuming it's the rear main seal.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
What lift should I run?

The common setup is an Old Man Emu 2-inch kit for 4/1985–1/1990 trucks, in light, medium or heavy load ratings, with greaseable shackles and a steering damper.

SOURCE · CRUISER CORPS
What was the FJ62G VX?

The Japanese-market version introduced in 1987 with the 3F-E. Wikipedia says it let the Land Cruiser be sold in Japan as a passenger vehicle rather than a commercial one.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What's one worth?

Hagerty's wagon-values piece put the median quote for 60 Series trucks at $18,500, with the FJ60 the more collectible. Restomod FJ62s from specialist shops sell for far more.

SOURCE · HAGERTY
