Buying guide · 1955–present

Jeep years to avoid, and the best years to buy

17 generations of the Jeep, and for each one the model years owners and specialists recommend, the ones with documented problems, and the recalls to check. Seven of them have a clear sweet spot; the rest are best bought on condition.

Sweet spot years owners and specialists recommend · Beware years with documented problems · Recall years with a recall to check by VIN. Each one is sourced on the model's own guide.

Jeep CJ-5

1955–1983

Condition beats year on a truck this old, but there are two sensible targets. Hagerty would aim for an AMC-era 232 or 258 six from 1972 on, for smooth power and cheaper running than the V8. Jeep-CJ.com and The Drive both rate the Buick Dauntless V6 (optional 1965–71) as strong. The one to skip is a 1980–83 truck with the GM Iron Duke four, which Jeep-CJ.com calls particularly weak; the optional 258 those years is fine. The Drive adds that pre-1976 open frames tend to crack and later boxed frames tend to rust, so check either kind.

  1. 1965Buick-designed 225 cu in Dauntless V6 (155 hp) becomes optional; by 1968 about 75% of CJ-5s have it. Jeep-CJ.com calls the 1966–71 Dauntless desirable, strong and reliable. 'Jeep CJ-5' replaces 'Willys' after 1964Sweet spot
  2. 1972AMC 232/258 sixes and optional 304 V8; wheelbase stretched 2.5 in to 83.5 in, nose 5 in longer, open-knuckle Dana 30 front, fuel tank behind the rear axle. Hagerty's pick is an AMC-era 232 or 258 six from 1972 onSweet spot

Jeep CJ-7

1976–1986

There's no single answer, so we don't flag one. Owners on Off-Road.com split between 1976–79 trucks, for the T-18 four-speed and the 304 V8 option, and 1982-on trucks with the wider-track axles; a 1986 with the factory Dana 44 rear is a good find. Jeep-CJ.com calls the T-18 the best CJ manual and says to avoid the GM Iron Duke four used from 1980 to 1983. The Drive says to watch out for Quadra-Trac. Buy the best frame you can find, then pick the drivetrain.

Jeep Grand Wagoneer (SJ)

1984–1991

1989–91, if you can. Those years got better rust-proofing (electrocoat primer), base-clear paint, an improved A/C compressor, an overhead console, keyless entry and a rear wiper. The 1991 Final Edition carries a premium. Hagerty's specialist adds that low mileage, under about 100,000, is the safest buy.

  1. 1989Start of the 'best of the breed' run: more reliable A/C compressor, rear wiper/washer, minivan-sourced overhead console with compass and thermometer, keyless entry, electrocoat primer and base/clear paint, gunmetal wheel inlaysSweet spot
  2. 1991Final year of the 1989–91 'best of the breed' run; buyers after the February 27 discontinuation announcement could add a gold 'Final Edition' dash badge, and Hagerty says low-mileage examples are desirable and expensive. Last truck built June 21, 1991Sweet spot

Jeep Cherokee XJ

1984–2001

Buy a 4.0. OffroadXtreme recommends the 1991-and-later HO, says 1987–90 Renix trucks suit owners who like diagnosing sensors, and says to avoid 1984–86 (no 4.0). NAXJA members single out non-ABS 1998–99 trucks with the 4.0, AW4, NP231 and 29-spline 8.25 as about the most solid combination. The 2000–01 trucks are good if the head has been checked or replaced.

  1. 1984Launch: unibody 'Uniframe', Quadra-Link coil front and leaf rear on live axles; 2.5 L AMC four or 2.8 L GM V6; NP207 Command-Trac or NP228/229 Selec-Trac. 1,200 lb lighter and 31 in shorter than the SJ Cherokee. No 4.0 until 1987: Off Road Xtreme recommends staying away from 1984–86 trucksBeware
  2. 19914.0 L 'High Output' with Chrysler multi-point injection, 190 hp; Chrysler 8.25 in rear axle available on non-ABS trucks. The HO is the XJ Off Road Xtreme recommends: easier to diagnose than the Renix 4.0, and more powerSweet spot
  3. 2000Coil-on-plug ignition on the 4.0 (193 hp); NV3550 replaces the AX15; front Dana 30 goes low pinion. New '0331' cylinder head: early castings can crack between cylinders 3 and 4 and put coolant in the oil (Wikipedia). A revised 'TUPY' casting arrived late in 2001, so check the head on any 2000–01Beware

Jeep Comanche MJ

1986–1992

Buy a 4.0, ideally a 1991–92 HO: 190 hp and Chrysler's easier-to-diagnose engine management, the same reason Off Road Xtreme recommends the HO in the XJ. 1987–90 Renix trucks are good too, but they're diagnosed with a multimeter. 1986 trucks have no 4.0. If you do buy a 2.5, Comanche Club members say look for 4.10 or 4.56 gears.

  1. 1991Chrysler engine management replaces Renix; 4.0 'High Output' 190 hp; Chrysler 8.25 rear appears; no automatic with the 2.5. The 1991–92 HO is the one to buy: easier to diagnose than the Renix 4.0, which is why Off Road Xtreme recommends the HO in the XJ that shares the engineSweet spot

Jeep Wrangler YJ

1987–1995

Sources disagree. 4WD Mechanix says 1987–92 YJs used a slave cylinder built into the release bearing and that 1993 brought the external slave and pivot arm used in the TJ. Wikipedia dates the move outside the bellhousing to 1994, and Advance Adapters lists its internal-bearing hose kit for 1987–93. Treat 1993 as either, and look for an external slave on the bellhousing. The internal type means pulling the transmission to replace it.

  1. 1991Fuel-injected 4.0 L six replaces the carbureted 4.2; Hagerty says the 4.0 sixes are often the most desirable YJs. Renegade Decor Group arrives (built from October 1990)Sweet spot
  2. 1994Automatic offered with the four; center high-mounted stop lamp. Manual trucks get an external clutch slave cylinder (Wikipedia; 4WD Mechanix says 1993), which is why SlashGear calls 1994–95 the best YJ years: no pulling the gearbox to replace the slaveSweet spot

Jeep Grand Cherokee ZJ

1993–1998

There's no real consensus, so we don't flag one. SlashGear picks 1993, 1994 and 1998 and says to avoid 1995–96 for transmission complaints, but NHTSA's complaint database holds a similar 1,200 to 1,560 complaints for every ZJ model year, with 1998 logging the most powertrain reports, and CarComplaints.com shows no ZJ year standing out. Buy on condition instead: frame rust, leaks, a transfer case that works in every mode and the 13V-252 hitch recall done. If you want the collectible, it's the 1998 5.9 Limited.

Jeep Wrangler TJ

1997–2006

There's no consensus year, which is why we only flag the early trucks. SlashGear rates mid-to-late TJs among the most desirable Wranglers and points manual buyers to 2005–06 for the NSG370 six-speed. RealTruck, on the other hand, lists 2005 and 2006 among its worst Wrangler years, and Quadratec says 2005–06 4.0s need the oil pump drive assembly checked or replaced. Driving Line doesn't pick a year at all: it says get the six-cylinder. Every TJ frame can rust, so the frame matters more than the model year. Check the rear lower control-arm mounts first.

  1. 1997–99Early-TJ teething: SlashGear notes seven recalls for 1997 and early engine-computer, fuel-system and brake bugs; Driving Line says cracked 4.0 exhaust manifolds are most common on 1997–2000 trucks; Off Road Xtreme says the early cowl vent leaks water onto the floors, a design Jeep revised on later TJsBeware

Jeep Wrangler Unlimited (LJ)

2004–2006

2005–06 brought the NSG370 six-speed manual. Those years also need the OPDA checked. The 2005–06 Rubicon Unlimited is the one with front and rear Dana 44s, air lockers and the 4:1 Rock-Trac. A 2004 or non-Rubicon LJ still has the Dana 44 rear and is a better starting point for a build than a Dana 35 TJ.

Jeep Wrangler JK (2-door)

2007–2018

A 2012-on Pentastar. Quadratec calls it a generally strong engine, while Driving Line says the 2007–11 3.8 burns oil, leaks intake gaskets and cracks exhaust manifolds, and has seen some spin bearings by 100,000 miles. Driving Line's daily-driver pick is 2013 or newer; SlashGear rates 2012–13 as the standout years. The year to be wary of is 2007: several first-year recalls, and CarComplaints.com logs more complaints for 2007 and 2008 than for any other Wrangler year. Not everyone agrees on the Pentastar's first years, either: RealTruck lists 2012–14 among its worst, citing engine complaints. So on a 2012–13 ask about the left cylinder head, and on any Pentastar check the oil filter housing.

  1. 2007JK launch (production from August 2006): 3.8 EGH V6 (202 hp) with a 6-speed manual or 4-speed 42RLE auto; Rubicon has Dana 44s with lockers, 4:1 Rock-Trac and a sway-bar disconnect; the four-door Unlimited is new. First-year trucks drew several recalls (SlashGear), 2007–08 log the most complaints on CarComplaints.com (CarGurus), and Driving Line warns the 3.8 burns oil, leaks intake gaskets and cracks exhaust manifoldsBeware
  2. 20123.6 Pentastar V6 (285 hp, 260 lb-ft) and 5-speed W5A580 automatic replace the 3.8 and the 4-speed. The Pentastar is the engine to have (Quadratec, Driving Line); Driving Line's daily-driver pick is a 2013 or newerSweet spot

Wrangler JL

2018–present

2018, mainly. SlashGear counted 11 recalls and more than 1,000 owner complaints for the 2018 Wrangler, most about wandering at highway speed, and says steering complaints carried on, less often, for two more years. NHTSA's 2018 list includes track-bar bracket welds (18V-675, also 2019), the intermediate steering shaft (18V-343) and rear frame-rail corrosion (18V-231), and manual trucks from 2018 on are covered by the clutch recalls (20V-124, 21V-028, 23V-116). Quadratec says Jeep later moved from aluminum to cast-iron steering boxes. No later year is a write-off: buy any JL with its recalls and steering fixes documented.

  1. 2018JL launch (four-door production from November 2017, two-door from early 2018): 3.6 Pentastar with a 6-speed manual or 8-speed auto, plus a 2.0 turbo with eTorque (auto only). The year to be wary of: NHTSA recalls cover track-bar bracket welds (18V-675), the intermediate steering shaft weld (18V-343) and rear frame-rail corrosion (18V-231), manual trucks are in the clutch recalls, and SlashGear counts more than 1,000 owner complaints, mostly highway wander. Buy one only with every fix documentedBeware

Wrangler JL Unlimited

2018–present

No year is the consensus pick, so we don't flag one. The one to be wary of is 2018: SlashGear counted 11 recalls and more than 1,000 complaints, mostly about highway wander, and NHTSA's list includes track-bar bracket welds (18V-675, 2018–19), the steering shaft (18V-343) and rear frame-rail corrosion (18V-231). SlashGear calls 2020-on trucks the more enticing buy for their engine choices, but each brings its own recalls: EcoDiesel fuel pumps (22V-767, 23V-263), 4xe high-voltage batteries (23V-787, 25V-741) and, on 2021–25 trucks, the power-steering pump wiring (26V-363). Buy the truck whose recall list you can see closed.

  1. 2018JL launch (four-door production from November 2017, two-door from early 2018): 3.6 Pentastar with a 6-speed manual or 8-speed auto, plus a 2.0 turbo with eTorque (auto only). Jeep Gladiator JT

2020–present

There's no consensus year yet, so we don't flag one. The recall history is the better guide. Early 2020 trucks had the rear driveshaft recall (19V-636). Manual trucks from 2020 to 2023 are covered by the clutch pressure-plate recalls (20V-124, 21V-028, 23V-116), and some 2021s needed a second fix (24V-572). Diesels have fuel-pump recalls 22V-767 (2021–22) and 23V-263 (2021–23), and 2021–25 trucks are in the power-steering pump wiring recall (26V-363). The 2024 refresh added curtain airbags and the big screen, if those matter to you. Buy whichever year you can prove every recall on.
