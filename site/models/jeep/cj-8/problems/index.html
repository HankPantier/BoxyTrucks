Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

Jeep CJ-8 Scrambler problems to check before you buy

  1. Rust in the long tubCheck the rockers, the rear corners of the bed, the wheel wells, under the roll bar and the floor. Jeep-CJ.com warns that patches welded over rust become rust. Knock on panels to find filler. Replacement Scrambler tubs exist, but they are a full strip-down job.
  2. Frame and shock towersInspect the whole frame, especially the shackle mounts, the area behind the rear wheels and the body mounts. On 1982–86 wide-track trucks, Jeep-CJ.com says the shock towers can trap water. Mud blocks the drain holes in the boxed rails.
  3. Steering box mountBoxed frames often crack around the steering box, especially on lifted trucks with big tires. Look for cracks, fresh welds and movement when someone turns the wheel.
  4. Engine choiceSlashGear calls the 258 six the most desirable engine. Jeep-CJ.com says to avoid the Iron Duke four. Look for start-up smoke, rattles, and oil weeping from the rear main seal.
  5. Five-speed healthOn 1985–86 trucks, check that the T-5 doesn't jump out of gear or grind. Jeep-CJ.com members call it weak. A T-176 swap is the common fix, and the driveshafts must be modified for length.
  6. Tailgate, tops and sealsCheck the tailgate hardware and weatherstripping and the cowl seals, since failed seals trap water. Tops and half cabs are Scrambler-specific, so price in a replacement if they're missing.
  7. Originality and paperworkJeep-CJ.com recommends mostly stock trucks over projects or heavy trail builds. 4WDrive's featured truck came with a binder documenting its restoration, and paperwork like that matters on a truck this valuable.
  8. Test driveStart it cold. Test every gear, 4WD high and low on gravel, the brakes (gently at first), the gauges and the heater. Check for leaks at the transfer case and both axles.
