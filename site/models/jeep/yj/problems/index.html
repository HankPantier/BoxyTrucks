Jeep Wrangler YJ problems: 8 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the Jeep Wrangler YJ, the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Jeep Wrangler YJ problems to check before you buy

  1. Frame, spring hangers and floors firstQuadratec's inspection checklist names the rear leaf-spring mounts, frame rails, skid-plate mounts, rear crossmember, driver-side floor and roll-bar mounts. Lift the carpet: it warns that moisture trapped under old padding rots YJ floor pans from the top down.
  2. Does 4WD actually engage?The front axle is engaged by a vacuum motor. If the 4WD light stays off, or 4WD feels inconsistent, suspect dry-rotted, cracked or disconnected vacuum lines. Many owners convert to a cable-operated disconnect.
  3. Clutch slave cylinder (early YJs)Early manual YJs use an internal slave cylinder built into the release bearing. 4WD Mechanix dates it to 1987–92 with an external slave from 1993; Wikipedia says the slave moved outside the bellhousing for 1994, and Advance Adapters sells its internal-bearing hose kit for 1987–93. When the internal slave leaks, fluid can ruin the clutch disc, and replacing it means pulling the transmission. Look for fluid weeping from the bellhousing and a sinking pedal, and check whether the slave is visible on the outside of the bellhousing.
  4. Know the gearbox and transfer caseSix-cylinder trucks from 1987–89 may have the Peugeot BA-10/5, which ExtremeTerrain calls historically unreliable. A 1987 may also have the NP207 transfer case. An AX-15 and NP231 combination is what most buyers want.
  5. Carbureted 4.2 or injected 4.0?The 1987–90 4.2 uses a computer-controlled Carter BBD with a maze of vacuum lines that are prone to leaks. It can be made reliable, but the 1991–95 4.0 is the engine Hagerty says commands the premium.
  6. Leaf springs, shackles and bushingsCheck for sagging or broken leaves, cracked bushings, rusted shackles and uneven ride height. Most YJs are more than 30 years old, so tired springs are normal and fresh ones change the ride.
  7. Electrical groundsFlickering lights, jumping gauges and intermittent starting often come from bad grounds. Quadratec suggests cleaning the firewall ground straps and battery connections before chasing anything bigger.
  8. The rear axleMost YJs have the Dana 35 rear. Drivingline calls the same axle in the TJ 'junk' because of its C-clip design, so if you plan bigger tires, price in a Dana 44 swap or upgraded shafts. A factory Dana 44 is worth looking for.

Jeep Wrangler YJ recalls and service bulletins

  • Lug nuts and steering-shaft clamp bolts not tightened ↗ 1987 · NHTSA 86V058000Wheel lug nuts and steering-shaft clamp bolts may not have been properly tightened at the factory; dealers tightened both.
  • Windshield frame cracks at wiper pivots ↗ 1987–1988 · NHTSA 88V155000The frame can crack at the wiper-arm pivots; the fix was a reinforcement and redesigned wiper linkage. A follow-up campaign (93V150000) covered 1989 trucks.
  • Front brake hoses rub on splash shields ↗ 1987–1992 · NHTSA 92V148000Hoses can wear through on the wheel-opening splash shields; the fix was new shields or fasteners and new hoses where needed.
  • Fuel tank skid plate misbuilt ↗ 1989 · NHTSA 90V168000In a rear impact the tank could leak more fuel than FMVSS 301 allows because of a misbuilt skid plate.
  • Starter can engage without the key ↗ 1991–1993 · NHTSA 93V083000Salt corrosion in the bulkhead connector can short the starter wire to battery feed; the fix relocates the wire in the connector.
  • Fuel sender gasket cracks ↗ 1987–1994 · NHTSA 94V106000On trucks with plastic tanks the sending-unit gasket can crack and leak fuel and vapor; the fix is a new gasket.
  • Parking brake can self-release (manual trans) ↗ 1990–1995 · NHTSA 02V041000Dealers fitted a parking-brake assembly with a stronger pawl return spring.
  • Front rotor corrosion (salt-belt states) ↗ 1990–1991 · NHTSA 98V005000For trucks sold or registered in listed salt-belt states, the stamped-steel rotor hub can rust through; replaced with coated rotors.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about Jeep Wrangler YJ reliability

Should I avoid the Peugeot gearbox?

Six-cylinder YJs from 1987–89 could have the Peugeot BA-10/5 five-speed, which ExtremeTerrain describes as historically unreliable. Wikipedia lists the Aisin AX-15 behind the 4.2 from 1989, and the AX-15 stayed with the 4.0 through 1995. Four-cylinder trucks use the AX-5.

SOURCE · EXTREMETERRAIN · WIKIPEDIA
Can the 4.2's carburetor be made reliable?

Yes. Moses Ludel's 4WD Mechanix guide says the Carter BBD can be reliable when rebuilt to factory specs, and that a clogged fuel filter, a weak mechanical pump or a vacuum leak can mimic carburetor faults. He calls Mopar's multi-point injection conversion the bigger upgrade.

SOURCE · 4WD MECHANIX
