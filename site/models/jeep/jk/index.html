PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

In January 2006, Chrysler group CEO Tom LaSorda drove a new Wrangler up a flight of steps and through a plate-glass window at the Detroit auto show. The JK was a clean-sheet redesign from the DaimlerChrysler years. It was wider than the TJ by 3.4 inches of track and had 2 inches more wheelbase, yet it was 2.5 inches shorter overall. It also ran almost everything except the steering through computers, a first for a Wrangler. Purists grumbled. Then it went on to set new Wrangler sales records anyway.

BIGGER, WIDER, WIRED

Mark Moushegian's design won an internal competition by 2003 and was frozen in January 2004. The JK kept the things that make a Wrangler a Wrangler: a separate body and frame, live axles front and rear on coils, a fold-flat windshield, removable doors and part-time four-wheel drive. Around them came stability control, off-road-tuned ABS and traction control acting as an electronic limited slip. It also got power windows and locks, available navigation and, on the Rubicon, an electronic front sway-bar disconnect. With the factory 32-inch tires, the two-door Rubicon is quoted at a 44.3-degree approach and a 40.4-degree departure.

The four-door JK Unlimited arrived at the same time and stole the show. By mid-2017, Autotrader counted three-quarters of new Wranglers for sale as four-doors. The two-door kept the 95.4-inch wheelbase that Quadratec calls 'still nimble, just slightly longer' than a TJ, which is why trail regulars still seek it out.

THE MINIVAN ENGINE, THEN THE PENTASTAR

From 2007 to 2011 the JK used the 3.8-liter EGH V6, the same pushrod engine as Chrysler's minivans and the Pacifica. It made 202 hp and 237 lb-ft by Jeep's 2008 figures, and it came with the six-speed NSG370 manual or the 42RLE four-speed automatic. It was adequate in the way a tent is adequate housing. The 2011 model year brought a fully redesigned interior, with a new dash, steering-wheel controls, available heated leather and Bluetooth.

For 2012 the 3.6-liter Pentastar replaced it, with 285 hp and 260 lb-ft, and Mercedes' five-speed W5A580 automatic replaced the 42RLE. Quadratec says the change meant the Wrangler 'is no longer the rolling brick it was'. The early Pentastar had a teething problem: Chrysler replaced faulty left cylinder heads (excessive cylinder leakage, showing up as misfires) on some 2012–13 engines and extended their warranty to 10 years or 150,000 miles. Diesel JKs, using VM Motori's 2.8, were sold outside the US only.

RUBICONS, MOABS AND WILLYS WHEELERS

Every Rubicon has Dana 44 axles at both ends with electric lockers, the Rock-Trac NV241 transfer case with a 4:1 low range, 4.10 gears, rock rails and the sway-bar disconnect. Sport and Sahara models used a Dana 30 front, a Dana 44 rear (Quadratec says fewer than a thousand early 2007s got a Dana 35) and the 2.72:1 Command-Trac. A rear locker on a non-Rubicon was a rare option.

The special editions came thick and fast. The 2012 Call of Duty: MW3 edition wore AEV bumpers and a sculpted hood. The 2013 Moab brought AEV-style steel bumpers and sliders but kept the 2.72:1 transfer case. The 2013 10th Anniversary Rubicon was the edition chosen for the one-millionth JK, built on May 17, 2013. The 2014 Rubicon X added steel bumpers and a Power Dome hood. For 2015 the lineup included the Willys Wheeler and the Rubicon Hard Rock.

HOW IT ENDED

For 2018 the JK was built alongside the new JL at Toledo, with a 'Wrangler JK' fender decal to tell them apart, and a final Golden Eagle edition. The last one rolled off the line on April 27, 2018, and its plant was retooled to build the Gladiator pickup. The JK is the Wrangler that made the aftermarket explode: Mopar alone offered more than 250 accessories for the 2012 model. That's why a used JK today is rarely stock, and why you should inspect what the last owner bolted on as carefully as what Jeep did.

SIDEBAR · DEATH WOBBLE GOES TO COURT

The shake that launched a lawsuit

Any solid-front-axle Jeep can get 'death wobble': a violent oscillation of the front axle and steering, usually triggered by a bump at 45–65 mph. Heavier tires, lifts and worn parts made the JK notorious for it. Quadratec reports that a New Jersey owner sued FCA seeking class-action status for 2015–2018 Wranglers, saying the dealer had replaced her steering stabilizer three times in six months without curing it.

FCA's position was that any vehicle with a solid axle can experience steering vibration, and that it is routinely corrected. NHTSA said it had never issued a recall for death wobble, and Chrysler's 2012 service bulletin only reminded owners to keep their steering systems in good condition. The real fix is mechanical: find the play in the track bar, tie rods, ball joints or bearings, and remove it.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The JK has a ladder frame with coil-sprung live axles at both ends, each located by four links and a track bar, and recirculating-ball steering with a damper. The drivetrain is a part-time two-speed transfer case with no center differential. Standard models get a Dana 30 front and Dana 44 rear. Rubicons get Dana 44s at both ends with electric lockers, a 4:1 Rock-Trac and an electronic front sway-bar disconnect. Nearly every system is electronically controlled.

Buying one

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

JKs are firmly in used-truck territory. Low miles, Rubicon hardware and a manual gearbox earn the premiums, and tidy late Pentastar trucks outsell early 3.8s.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Which engine? Check the VIN year2007–11 trucks have the 3.8 and the four-speed automatic; 2012-on trucks have the Pentastar and the five-speed. The Pentastar is the one most buyers want. On a 2012–13, ask whether the left cylinder head was ever replaced: Chrysler extended coverage on affected heads to 10 years or 150,000 miles, which has run out on most of them by now.
  2. 02
    Look into the engine valley (2012–18)Quadratec says the Pentastar's plastic oil filter housing 'has a bad habit of cracking'. Look for oil pooled under the intake plenum, a burning-oil smell or slow oil loss. Because the housing contains the oil cooler, coolant can leak there too. Many owners fit an aluminum housing.
  3. 03
    Jack up the front axleJK ball joints often wear surprisingly quickly, especially with 35s, bumpers and winches. Check for vertical wheel play, steering looseness, uneven tire wear and highway wander. Then drive it at 45–65 mph over bumps to rule out death wobble.
  4. 04
    Listen at cold startA tick-tick-tick that fades as the engine warms is usually snapped rear exhaust manifold bolts, which Quadratec calls notorious on both the 3.8 and the 3.6. It isn't urgent, but it's a negotiating point.
  5. 05
    Electrical gremlins and waterJK electrical faults often trace to the TIPM (Totally Integrated Power Module) or a failing clockspring. Symptoms include airbag lights, dead steering-wheel controls, fuel-pump problems and wipers with a mind of their own. Water in the cabin makes them worse, so lift the carpets and look for damp.
  6. 06
    Inspect the front axle C's on modified trucksOn 35-inch tires or bigger, hard use can bend the Dana 30's C-shaped knuckle ends where the ball joints sit. Look for odd camber, strange tire wear and whether gusset plates or a truss are already fitted.
  7. 07
    Prove the Rubicon hardware worksEngage both lockers and the sway-bar disconnect, and confirm 4-Low is the 4:1 Rock-Trac. A Moab or Willys Wheeler looks the part but has the 2.72:1 transfer case, and lockers were optional at best.
  8. 08
    Read the lift like a résuméMost JKs are modified. A quality lift should include a correct track bar, geometry correction and an alignment. Cheap spacer lifts with 35s are where worn joints and death wobble come from.

Twelve years of near-identical bodies, heaps of sticker-pack special editions and plenty of Hemi swaps mean the badge on the hood is only a starting point.

Replace the Pentastar oil filter housing (2012–18)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · About 6 hours in one owner's write-up, including prepSHOP COST · Housing plus gaskets; under $30 in special tools for the Curbside Classic author
SYMPTOMSOil (and sometimes coolant) pooling in the valley between the cylinder banks, a burning smell, slow oil loss. You often won't see drips on the ground until the leak is bad.
THE FIXRemove the engine cover and intake duct. Unplug the intake connectors (the MAP sensor's red tab first), the vacuum hoses and the throttle-body brackets, then remove the seven captive 8 mm bolts and lift off the upper intake. Unplug the injectors and right-bank coils, vacuum away debris, plug the ports, and remove the eight lower-intake bolts, supporting the manifold out of the way. Unplug the oil and coolant sensors and the coolant hose, then remove the five E8 Torx bolts and lift out the housing. On the bench, move the cooler and sensors (with thread sealant) to the new housing. Fit new gaskets and O-rings and bolt it down in the specified sequence. The filter cap is 18 lb-ft. Reassemble, change the oil (6 qt of 5W-20) and check for leaks. 2014-on housings differ from 2012–13, so buy for your year.
TOOLS & PARTS27 mm and 19 mm deep sockets · E8 external Torx · 24 mm cap socket · inch-pound torque wrench · 8, 10 and 13 mm sockets · magnet and pick · shop vac.

Cure death wobble: track bar, tie rods and steering
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoon to diagnoseSHOP COST · Often a track-bar bushing or end, tie-rod ends or a drag link, plus an alignment
SYMPTOMSViolent side-to-side shaking of the front end after a bump at 45–65 mph that won't stop until you slow right down.
THE FIXStart with the front track bar. With the Jeep on the ground, have a helper turn the wheel about a quarter turn each way while you watch the track-bar ends and bracket holes; any movement is a fault. Then check the tie-rod ends, drag link, ball joints, control-arm bushings, wheel bearings, and tire balance and pressure. Quadratec says a stabilizer 'rarely fixes the root cause'. Replace the worn parts, torque all front-end hardware to spec (Northridge4x4's chart gives 125 ft-lb for both front track-bar bolts and 63 ft-lb for the tie-rod end nuts; confirm against the factory manual), and finish with a proper alignment. On lifted trucks, a correctly sized adjustable track bar re-centers the axle.
TOOLS & PARTSHelper · pry bar · torque wrench · sockets · tape measure.

Press in new ball joints
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · A day for both sidesSHOP COST · Ball joint set plus a rented ball-joint press
SYMPTOMSVertical play at the top and bottom of the tire, wandering, uneven tire wear, and wobble after bumps. Quadratec says factory JK ball joints 'often wear surprisingly quickly', especially on heavy, lifted trucks.
THE FIXSupport the truck on stands and remove the wheel, caliper, rotor and unit bearing to free the axle shaft. Separate the tie rod and drag link, remove the knuckle, then press the old upper and lower joints out of the axle C. Inspect the C for bends and cracks at the same time. Press in the new joints with the correct adapters, refit the knuckle and torque the castle nuts to spec (Northridge4x4 lists 75 ft-lb for the upper and lower ball-joint nuts). Finish with an alignment. Adjustable or heavy-duty kits are worth it on 35s.
TOOLS & PARTSBall-joint press and JK adapter set · pickle fork or tie-rod puller · large sockets · torque wrench · new cotter pins.

Replace a front unit bearing
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · A couple of hours per sideSHOP COST · One hub and bearing assembly
SYMPTOMSPlay when you rock the tire with the truck in the air, a growl that changes with steering, and, as part of the death-wobble checklist, looseness in the front end.
THE FIXSupport the axle and remove the wheel, then the caliper (hang it, don't let it hang from the hose) and rotor. Remove the axle-shaft nut, then the bolts holding the unit bearing to the knuckle from behind, and tap the hub free. Clean the knuckle bore, anti-seize it, fit the new hub, and torque the bolts and axle nut to the factory spec (Northridge4x4 lists 75 ft-lb for the hub bolts and 100 ft-lb for the 35 mm axle nut). Mopar lists different hubs for 2007–10 and 2011–18 trucks, so buy by year.
TOOLS & PARTSLarge axle-nut socket · breaker bar · torque wrench · anti-seize · hammer.

Fix a ticking exhaust manifold (broken bolts)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · Half a day to a day, depending on how the bolts come outSHOP COST · Gaskets and hardware; more if a broken bolt has to be drilled out
SYMPTOMSA tick-tick-tick on cold start that quiets as the engine warms, from a leak at the rear of a manifold. Quadratec says the rear manifold bolts on both the 3.8 and 3.6 are 'notorious for snapping'.
THE FIXFind the leak with the engine cold (a manifold that's black at the flange or a snapped bolt head is the giveaway). Penetrating oil the studs for a day or two. Unbolt the downpipe and remove the manifold. Extract broken bolts by drilling and using an extractor, or by welding a nut onto the stub. Chase the threads, fit new hardware and gaskets, torque to spec and reconnect the exhaust.
TOOLS & PARTSPenetrating oil · sockets and extensions · left-hand drill bits and extractors (or a welder) · thread chaser · torque wrench.

Replace the clockspring
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An hour or twoSHOP COST · One clockspring
SYMPTOMSAirbag warning light, horn or steering-wheel buttons that don't work, and cruise or audio controls dropping out. Quadratec lists it with the TIPM as the usual JK electrical culprits. On 2007–10 trucks, check recall 16V-290 first: Chrysler recalled the clockspring because dust contamination could stop the driver's airbag from deploying.
THE FIXPoint the wheels straight ahead. Disconnect the battery and wait for the airbag system to discharge, as the factory manual specifies. Remove the airbag module and steering wheel, then the column shrouds. Unplug and remove the old clockspring and fit the new one centered, following its instructions (don't spin it). Reassemble, reconnect the battery and clear any codes. Quadratec Academy has a step-by-step JK video.

Change the spark plugs (and don't over-torque the Pentastar)
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An hour or twoSHOP COST · Six plugs
SYMPTOMSMisfires, rough idle and lost economy, or simply a truck past the interval. ExtremeTerrain quotes Chrysler's interval as 102,000 miles [CONFIRM IN MANUAL]. ExtremeTerrain suggests pulling one plug for inspection at 60,000 on modified, trail-used trucks.
THE FIXOn the 3.8, pull the wires by the boots. On the 3.6, unclip each coil-on-plug connector and remove the coil (typically one 10 mm bolt). Gap the new plugs at 0.050 in for the 3.8 (Champion RE14PLP5) or 0.043 in for the 3.6 (Champion RER8ZWYCB4). Torque them to 13 lb-ft (±2) on the 3.6. ExtremeTerrain gives 12 lb-ft for the 3.8 [CONFIRM IN MANUAL]. The Pentastar's 12 mm plugs in aluminum heads don't tolerate over-torquing. Add dielectric grease to the boots.
TOOLS & PARTS5/8 in spark plug socket · extensions · 3/8 in torque wrench · gap gauge · dielectric grease.
WHERE TO BUY PARTSMopar SP149125AF spark plug, 2012-on 3.6 JK · Quadratec ↗Amazon ↗Crown Automotive S2RE14PLP5 double-platinum plug, 2007–11 JK 3.8 · Quadratec ↗Amazon ↗
Gusset or truss a bent front axle
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · A day (welding required)SHOP COST · Gusset/truss kit plus welding
SYMPTOMSCamber you can't align out, odd tire wear or changed steering on a JK running 35s or bigger. Quadratec warns that heavy tires and hard use can bend the axle C's where the ball joints sit.
THE FIXHave the axle checked first: a bent C needs straightening or a replacement housing before any gusset goes on. On a straight axle, strip the paint and coatings where the gussets and truss go and tack them in place. Weld in short alternating beads to limit heat distortion, then let it cool and coat it. Many owners do it with the axle out of the Jeep, along with the ball joints.
TOOLS & PARTSWelder · grinder · clamps · axle stands · rust-proof coating.

Asked on every forum

Jeep Wrangler JK (2-door) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

33 inches is the usual answer, with caveats. ExtremeTerrain says many JK owners fit 33s on a stock suspension, but fender rubbing will probably show up off-road at full flex, and width matters as much as height. On JK-Forum, owners of stock two-door Rubicons report 285/75R17s clearing on pavement, with rubbing on the control arms at full lock that adjusting the steering stops can cure. Not every wheel works: one owner couldn't fit 285/75R17s on AEV Pintlers without a lift. 255-width 33s are the safe bet; 35s need a lift.

SOURCE · EXTREMETERRAIN · JK-FORUM
3.8 or 3.6, which should I buy?

The 3.6 Pentastar (2012–18) makes 285 hp against the 3.8's 202, and comes with a five-speed automatic instead of a four-speed. Quadratec says the 2012 switch ended the JK's 'rolling brick' era. The 3.8 is simpler and cheaper to buy. On a Pentastar, budget for the oil filter housing.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · QUADRATEC
What's the Pentastar cylinder head problem?

Some early 2012–13 Wrangler Pentastars had faulty left cylinder heads. Chrysler's bulletin describes excessive cylinder leakage showing up as misfire codes (P0300, P0302, P0304 or P0306); owners also reported a tick from the left bank. Chrysler replaced heads under warranty, fitted redesigned heads to new engines from 2012 (AutoGuide), and in 2014 extended coverage on affected heads to 10 years or 150,000 miles (Warranty Bulletin D-14-12, code X56).

SOURCE · AUTOGUIDE · CHRYSLER TSB 09-002-14
What causes death wobble and how do I fix it?

Looseness or wear in the front end, most often the track bar, tie-rod ends, ball joints, control-arm bushings or wheel bearings, usually set off by a tire imbalance or a bump at 45–65 mph. A stabilizer only masks it. Find and replace the worn part, torque the hardware and align the truck.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC
Is the Rubicon worth it over a Sport?

If you'll wheel it, yes. You get Dana 44s at both ends instead of a Dana 30 front, front and rear electric lockers, a 4:1 Rock-Trac instead of 2.72:1, 4.10 gears and the electronic sway-bar disconnect. Retrofitting all of that to a Sport is a big, expensive job. A Moab or Willys Wheeler looks the part but keeps the 2.72:1 transfer case.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · QUADRATEC
How much can a 2-door JK tow?

2,000 lb, against 3,500 lb for the four-door Unlimited, per Quadratec's 2012 spec summary. Maximum payload is about 1,000 lb.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC
What fuel economy should I expect?

The EPA rated a 2012 Pentastar two-door at 17 mpg city and 21 highway. The tank holds 18.6 gallons. Lifts, 33–35 inch tires and steel bumpers cost several mpg.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA (EPA) · QUADRATEC
What fluids does it take?

Per ExtremeTerrain's JK chart: 3.8 and 3.6 engines take 6 qt of 5W-20. Coolant is HOAT for the 3.8 and 2012 3.6 (about 13 qt), and OAT for 2013-on 3.6s (10.5 qt). The 42RLE takes ATF+4 (4 qt service fill), the W5A580/NAG1 ATF+4 (5.3 qt service fill), and the NSG370 1.6 qt of Mopar Manual Transmission Lubricant. The NV241 takes 2.1 qt of ATF+4. The Dana 30 front takes 2.1 pints, the Dana 44 front 2.7 pints and the Dana 44 rear 4.75 pints of 80W-90, with 75W-140 recommended for towing. Trac-Lok rears need 4 oz of limited-slip additive.

SOURCE · EXTREMETERRAIN
How often should I change the oil?

Quadratec cites Jeep's 8,000 miles or six months for the Pentastar, and never more than 10,000. Severe use (towing, dust, off-road) shortens it. Mopar specifies oil meeting MS-6395. Note that the oil filter changed in 2014, so buy the right one.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC
Why does it tick at cold start?

Usually broken rear exhaust manifold bolts letting the manifold leak until it heats up and seals. It's very common on both the 3.8 and the 3.6.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC
What are the TIPM and clockspring problems?

The TIPM is the JK's central power-distribution module. With a failing clockspring, it's the usual source of airbag lights, dead steering-wheel controls, fuel-pump problems and wipers with a mind of their own. Water in the cabin makes it worse.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC
