Jeep Wrangler JK problems: 8 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the Jeep Wrangler JK (2-door), the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Jeep Wrangler JK problems to check before you buy

  1. Which engine? Check the VIN year2007–11 trucks have the 3.8 and the four-speed automatic; 2012-on trucks have the Pentastar and the five-speed. The Pentastar is the one most buyers want. On a 2012–13, ask whether the left cylinder head was ever replaced: Chrysler extended coverage on affected heads to 10 years or 150,000 miles, which has run out on most of them by now.
  2. Look into the engine valley (2012–18)Quadratec says the Pentastar's plastic oil filter housing 'has a bad habit of cracking'. Look for oil pooled under the intake plenum, a burning-oil smell or slow oil loss. Because the housing contains the oil cooler, coolant can leak there too. Many owners fit an aluminum housing.
  3. Jack up the front axleJK ball joints often wear surprisingly quickly, especially with 35s, bumpers and winches. Check for vertical wheel play, steering looseness, uneven tire wear and highway wander. Then drive it at 45–65 mph over bumps to rule out death wobble.
  4. Listen at cold startA tick-tick-tick that fades as the engine warms is usually snapped rear exhaust manifold bolts, which Quadratec calls notorious on both the 3.8 and the 3.6. It isn't urgent, but it's a negotiating point.
  5. Electrical gremlins and waterJK electrical faults often trace to the TIPM (Totally Integrated Power Module) or a failing clockspring. Symptoms include airbag lights, dead steering-wheel controls, fuel-pump problems and wipers with a mind of their own. Water in the cabin makes them worse, so lift the carpets and look for damp.
  6. Inspect the front axle C's on modified trucksOn 35-inch tires or bigger, hard use can bend the Dana 30's C-shaped knuckle ends where the ball joints sit. Look for odd camber, strange tire wear and whether gusset plates or a truss are already fitted.
  7. Prove the Rubicon hardware worksEngage both lockers and the sway-bar disconnect, and confirm 4-Low is the 4:1 Rock-Trac. A Moab or Willys Wheeler looks the part but has the 2.72:1 transfer case, and lockers were optional at best.
  8. Read the lift like a résuméMost JKs are modified. A quality lift should include a correct track bar, geometry correction and an alignment. Cheap spacer lifts with 35s are where worn joints and death wobble come from.

Jeep Wrangler JK recalls and service bulletins

  • Driver airbag clockspring ↗ 2007–2010 · NHTSA 16V-290Dust can contaminate the clockspring so the driver's airbag may not deploy; Chrysler recall S33, 388,532 vehicles.
  • Takata passenger airbag inflator ↗ 2007–2012 · NHTSA 16V-352Passenger airbag inflator may rupture as the propellant degrades; this campaign covered trucks sold or registered in hot, humid states.
  • Takata passenger inflator (Zone A states) ↗ 2009–2013 · NHTSA 18V-021Passenger inflator replacement for trucks sold or registered in hot, humid states (recall U03).
  • Driver airbag inflator ↗ 2010–2016 · NHTSA 19V-018Driver inflator may over-pressurise and explode on deployment (recall V01).
  • Automatic transmission overheat warning ↗ 2007–2008 · NHTSA 09V-436Autos lacked a fluid-temperature warning; fluid could boil over onto hot parts. Dealers add a 'HOT OIL' message and chime (recall J30).
  • Front brake lines vs inner fender liner ↗ 2007–2010 · NHTSA 10V-236Inner fender liners can rub through front brake hoses and cause a leak (recall K13).
  • Axle-to-chassis fastener torque ↗ 2010–2011 · NHTSA 11V-145Front and rear axle attachment fasteners could lose torque; dealers re-torque all 19 (recall L09).
  • Power-steering line rubs transmission cooler tube ↗ 2012–2013 · NHTSA 13V-2343.6 automatics: the power-steering return line can wear a hole in the transmission cooler tube (recall N28, 180,131 vehicles).
  • Heated mirror connector short ↗ 2011–2013 · NHTSA 14V-631Corrosion in the heated power-mirror connector can short.
  • Front impact sensor wiring ↗ 2016–2017 · NHTSA 16V-734Sensor wiring can pull loose in some crashes before it signals the airbags.
  • Right-hand-drive airbag clockspring ↗ 2011–2016 (RHD) · NHTSA 16V-288Right-hand-drive (postal) trucks built February 2010 to May 2016: dust can contaminate the clockspring and disable the driver's airbag. See also 11V-528 and 13V-176 (2008–2012 RHD) and 24V-196 (2016 RHD).
  • Death wobble: Chrysler steering-vibration bulletin ↗ In August 2012 Chrysler sent dealers a bulletin acknowledging the solid-axle shimmy, naming aftermarket parts, wrong tire pressures and worn steering and suspension components as causes. NHTSA had earlier concluded the condition doesn't cause loss of control.
  • Shimmy with the 4-inch Mopar lift ↗ · TSB 02-001-162012–2015 JKs built before June 15, 2015 with the Mopar 4 in lift: replace the front axle track bar bracket and recheck alignment (supersedes 02-008-15).
  • 3.6 Pentastar left cylinder head (X56) ↗ · TSB 09-002-14 Rev. A2012–2013 JKs with misfire codes P0300/P0302/P0304/P0306: check cylinder leakage and replace the left head. Warranty Bulletin D-14-12 extended head coverage to 10 years/150,000 miles.
  • Water leaks ↗ Bulletins cover cowl trim panel fastener leaks (water on the front floor, 2012–2014), door and windshield-frame gaps, and Freedom Top centre-seal leaks.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about Jeep Wrangler JK reliability

What's the Pentastar cylinder head problem?

Some early 2012–13 Wrangler Pentastars had faulty left cylinder heads. Chrysler's bulletin describes excessive cylinder leakage showing up as misfire codes (P0300, P0302, P0304 or P0306); owners also reported a tick from the left bank. Chrysler replaced heads under warranty, fitted redesigned heads to new engines from 2012 (AutoGuide), and in 2014 extended coverage on affected heads to 10 years or 150,000 miles (Warranty Bulletin D-14-12, code X56).

SOURCE · AUTOGUIDE · CHRYSLER TSB 09-002-14
What are the TIPM and clockspring problems?

The TIPM is the JK's central power-distribution module. With a failing clockspring, it's the usual source of airbag lights, dead steering-wheel controls, fuel-pump problems and wipers with a mind of their own. Water in the cabin makes it worse.
