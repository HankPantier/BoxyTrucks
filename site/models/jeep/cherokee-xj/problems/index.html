Jeep Cherokee XJ problems to check before you buy

  1. The unibody rails come firstAvoid trucks with rust on the unibody frame rails, especially near suspension mounts. If rust reaches the suspension bolts, you may have to cut and repair the rails before a lift. Check the steering-box and track-bar mounts for cracks as well.
  2. Rockers, floors and lower quartersRocker panels under the doors and the lower rear quarters are the usual rust spots. Floors are relatively easy to replace; patch panels are cheap. Rust is much less common on Southwest trucks.
  3. 2000–01? Check the headAll 2000–01 XJs have the 0331 head. With the oil cap off, shine a light between the cylinder 3 and 4 exhaust rockers: a 'TUPY' mark means a revised head. Unexplained coolant loss, brown coolant or milky oil means a likely crack.
  4. Idle it until the fan comes onXJs are known for running hot. Idle for 5–10 minutes, make sure the electric fan kicks in, and watch the gauge on a hill. Bent pulleys suggest an overtightened belt and tired bearings.
  5. Find the leak before you blame the rear mainCommon leaks are the rear main seal, oil pan gasket, valve cover gasket and oil-filter-adapter O-rings. Oil runs down to the bellhousing, so check the top of the engine first.
  6. Test drive for wobble and wanderDeath wobble is usually the track bar. Wandering at speed means worn suspension or steering parts. A transfer case that pops out of 4WD may be worn.
  7. Know the electronics era1987–90 Renix trucks store no fault codes and are diagnosed with a multimeter, so buy one only if you're comfortable chasing sensors. The 1991 HO trucks and later are easier. Avoid 1984–86 unless you plan an engine swap.
  8. Read the axle tagsABS trucks have the Dana 35 rear, which is fine stock but weak with big tires. The Chrysler 8.25 is the common, decent rear axle, and a tow-package Dana 44 is worth extra.

Jeep Cherokee XJ recalls and service bulletins

  • Rear axle assembly ↗ 1984 · NHTSA 84V-024Improper assembly could cause rear axle bearing failure and separation of an axle shaft and wheel.
  • Rear differential pinion shaft ↗ 1984 · NHTSA 84V-045A defective pinion shaft could contact the drive pinion gear and lock the rear wheels.
  • Steering linkage couplers ↗ 1986–1987 · NHTSA 86V-159Couplers left too long in an acid-etching bath during manufacture.
  • ABS hydraulic system ↗ 1989–1990 · NHTSA 91V-003Condensation could plug the ABS booster-pump filter, a leaking low-pressure hose could corrode the master-cylinder snap ring, and front hoses could contact the tires.
  • ABS hydraulic control unit wear ↗ 1989–1991 · NHTSA 96V-260Worn actuator piston seals could kill the ABS pump motor, leaving reduced power assist.
  • Salt-belt front rotor corrosion ↗ 1990–1991 · NHTSA 98V-005In salt-belt states the front rotor's cast-iron braking surface could corrode away from the hub.
  • Salt-belt front rotor corrosion ↗ 1992–1995 · NHTSA 99V-340Same rotor-separation risk for trucks sold or registered in salt-belt states.
  • Steering-gear frame weld omitted ↗ 1993–1996 · NHTSA 97V-069A fillet weld joining the steering-gear reinforcement to the frame rail was left off; gear bolts could break or the rail could crack. About 2,500 trucks.
  • Driver airbag missing arming lever ↗ 1995 · NHTSA 95V-057Some driver airbag modules were assembled without an arming lever and would not deploy; about 70,000 covered.
  • Salt-belt front rotor corrosion ↗ 1996–1999 · NHTSA 00V-136Same rotor-separation risk for later trucks in salt-belt states.
  • Fuel gauge sender accuracy ↗ 1997 · NHTSA 97V-194The tank sender could drift and show far more fuel than was left.
  • Airbag control module corrosion ↗ 1997–1999 · NHTSA 00V-105Water or road salt near the module could corrode it, lighting the airbag lamp or causing inadvertent deployment.
  • 4.0 L manifold debris fire risk ↗ 2000–2001 · NHTSA 02V-104Intake and exhaust manifold design could let debris collect at the No. 3 cylinder and start a fire.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about Jeep Cherokee XJ reliability

What's the 0331 head problem?

The 2000–01 4.0 got a head with casting number 0331 that can crack between cylinders 3 and 4, letting coolant into the oil. Look through the oil filler between the cylinder 3 and 4 exhaust rockers: a 'TUPY' mark means a revised head, though Wikipedia says even those occasionally crack. Most failures follow an overheat, so keep the cooling system healthy, and never replace a cracked one with another 0331.
