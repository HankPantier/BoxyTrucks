PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The 1984 Cherokee XJ was Jeep's first all-new design since the 1963 Wagoneer, and the first American off-roader with a unibody, which AMC called 'Uniframe'. It was 1,200 lb lighter, 31 in shorter, 6 in narrower and 4 in lower than the full-size SJ Cherokee it replaced, yet kept 90% of the interior space. It also had better articulation, ground clearance and approach, departure and breakover angles. AMC sold it as a 'Sportwagon'. About 3 million were built through 2001, and it is still the default answer to 'what's a cheap, capable 4x4?'

AMC, RENAULT AND A $250 MILLION BET

Work began in the late 1970s, when AMC needed something smaller than the SJ and Renault had just bought a 22% stake. Renault pushed for less size and weight and better economy. Styling came from AMC's team under design vice-president Dick Teague. Engineering VP Roy Lunn designed the 'Quadra-Link' coil-sprung front suspension, which locates a solid axle with four control arms and a track bar. Renault's François Castaing planned a light drivetrain around a much smaller engine than 4x4s usually carried. AMC spent $250 million and offered four doors as well as two, which GM's new S-10 Blazer didn't.

Early engines were weak: AMC's 2.5-liter four, a GM 2.8 V6 and a Renault 2.1 turbodiesel. Everything changed for 1987, when AMC introduced its 4.0-liter straight-six with Renix multipoint injection, rated at 173 hp and 220 lb-ft. Wikipedia calls it one of the best 4x4 and off-road engines. It brought the Aisin AW4 four-speed automatic and the NP231 Command-Trac and NP242 Selec-Trac transfer cases. The 1991 'High Output' version made 190 hp with Chrysler's fuel injection and became the engine every XJ shopper looks for.

CHRYSLER KEEPS THE BOX

Chrysler inherited the XJ when it bought AMC in 1987. The plan was to replace it with the bigger ZJ Grand Cherokee, but the XJ sold too well to kill, so both were built from 1993. The 1997 facelift brought a steel liftgate, a new dash with dual airbags, and a stiffer unibody for less noise and vibration. The 2000–01 trucks got coil-on-plug ignition and 193 hp, and also a cylinder head casting, '0331', that is known to crack between cylinders 3 and 4.

Underneath, XJs differ by year and option. The front axle is always a Dana 30: high-pinion until 1999, low-pinion for 2000–01. At the rear you'll find a Dana 35, a Chrysler 8.25 or, on some 1987–91 tow-package trucks, a Dana 44. The Up Country package added an inch of lift and, per Wikipedia, deleted the rear anti-roll bar. The XJ also spawned the Comanche MJ pickup (1986–92) and a woodgrain Wagoneer Limited that shared its name with the full-size Grand Wagoneer.

WHY THE CULT EXISTS

Production in Toledo ended in June 2001, and the Liberty took over. Its reputation has grown since. Automobile's Robert Cumberford named it one of the 20 greatest cars of all time and called it 'possibly the best SUV shape of all time'. Kiplinger listed it among 'cars that refuse to die', and NAXJA members routinely report 250,000–300,000 miles on factory drivetrains.

The appeal on the trail is simple. You get a solid axle at both ends, a torquey six, a body roughly the size of a Wrangler YJ, and an aftermarket that sells everything from a 1.5-inch Budget Boost to 6.5-inch long-arm kits. It's the budget alternative to a CJ-7 or an FJ60 for people who want doors, a heater and room for a dog. Just remember that the unibody is the frame: a rusted rail is not a bodywork problem.

SIDEBAR · THE XJ THAT NEVER DIED

Beijing Jeep and two decades of Chinese XJs

In 1984 AMC set up Beijing Jeep, the first automotive joint venture in the People's Republic of China, to assemble the four-door Cherokee. Chinese BJ2021 (4WD) and BJ7250 (RWD) versions had a raised rear roof for chauffeured passengers. A facelifted 'Jeep 2500' ran until 2005.

After 2009, Beijing Auto Works kept building it as the BAW Qishi ('Knight') with old Nissan-designed four-cylinders, until 2014. That's 30 years for AMC's original design. XJs were also assembled in Argentina, Egypt and Venezuela.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The XJ's body and frame are one welded structure with box-section rails built in. A finished body has about 3,200 welds. Both axles are solid: coil springs and four control arms with a track bar at the front, four-leaf springs and compression shackles at the rear. The wheelbase is 101.4 in on both two- and four-door bodies. All 4WD transfer cases are chain-driven two-speeds in aluminum housings.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    The unibody rails come firstAvoid trucks with rust on the unibody frame rails, especially near suspension mounts. If rust reaches the suspension bolts, you may have to cut and repair the rails before a lift. Check the steering-box and track-bar mounts for cracks as well.
  2. 02
    Rockers, floors and lower quartersRocker panels under the doors and the lower rear quarters are the usual rust spots. Floors are relatively easy to replace; patch panels are cheap. Rust is much less common on Southwest trucks.
  3. 03
    2000–01? Check the headAll 2000–01 XJs have the 0331 head. With the oil cap off, shine a light between the cylinder 3 and 4 exhaust rockers: a 'TUPY' mark means a revised head. Unexplained coolant loss, brown coolant or milky oil means a likely crack.
  4. 04
    Idle it until the fan comes onXJs are known for running hot. Idle for 5–10 minutes, make sure the electric fan kicks in, and watch the gauge on a hill. Bent pulleys suggest an overtightened belt and tired bearings.
  5. 05
    Find the leak before you blame the rear mainCommon leaks are the rear main seal, oil pan gasket, valve cover gasket and oil-filter-adapter O-rings. Oil runs down to the bellhousing, so check the top of the engine first.
  6. 06
    Test drive for wobble and wanderDeath wobble is usually the track bar. Wandering at speed means worn suspension or steering parts. A transfer case that pops out of 4WD may be worn.
  7. 07
    Know the electronics era1987–90 Renix trucks store no fault codes and are diagnosed with a multimeter, so buy one only if you're comfortable chasing sensors. The 1991 HO trucks and later are easier. Avoid 1984–86 unless you plan an engine swap.
  8. 08
    Read the axle tagsABS trucks have the Dana 35 rear, which is fine stock but weak with big tires. The Chrysler 8.25 is the common, decent rear axle, and a tow-package Dana 44 is worth extra.

Spotting the real thing

Every XJ shares a body and bolt pattern, so what you're really identifying is engine, axles and transfer case, plus whether it's a right-hand-drive import.

  1. ID
    VIN (1987–1990)The 4th character is the engine (M = 4.0 L six, E/H = 2.5 L four, B = 2.1 L turbodiesel); characters 6–7 are the body: 73/74 = 2WD two-/four-door, 77/78 = 4WD two-/four-door, 75 = Wagoneer. The 10th character is the year (H = 1987, J = 1988).
  2. ID
    Rear axleDana 35 was standard throughout. A Chrysler 8.25 in came on non-ABS trucks from 1991 (27-spline, 29-spline from late 1996), and a 30-spline Dana 44 only on 1987–91 tow-package trucks. Read the axle tag or ring-gear size before you pay for 'the good axle'.
  3. ID
    Transfer caseNP231 Command-Trac is part-time only (2HI, 4HI, N, 4LO). NP242 Selec-Trac adds a full-time 4WD position, so a lever with both 'Part Time' and 'Full Time' marks means Selec-Trac.
  4. ID
    Wagoneer badgeThe 1984–90 XJ Wagoneer and Wagoneer Limited are woodgrained Cherokees, unrelated to the full-size SJ Grand Wagoneer.
  5. ID
    Right-hand-drive XJsJeep built RHD Cherokees for the US Postal Service and for Japan and the UK, and many Japanese trucks have come back for rural mail routes. US Drive Right recalled imported 1994–98 RHD Cherokees for failing several federal safety standards, so check an imported truck's recall status.

Importing & legality

XJs were US-built, so the 25-year rule rarely matters, but right-hand-drive trucks are a special case. Jeep built RHD Cherokees for the US Postal Service and for export to the UK, Ireland and Japan, and Japan's strict shaken inspections pushed many used Japanese-market trucks back to the US for rural mail carriers.

Importer US Drive Right recalled 107 imported 1994–98 RHD 4WD Cherokees (NHTSA 08V-056) for failing nine federal standards, from controls and lighting to fuel-system integrity, and 1,126 1994–96 trucks imported between 2007 and 2011 (NHTSA 11V-279) for lacking the required third brake light. If you're buying one, run the VIN through NHTSA's lookup and check your state's titling rules for RHD vehicles.

Replace a cracked 0331 cylinder head (2000–01) →
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A weekendSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSUnexplained coolant loss, brown or dirty coolant, milky residue under the oil cap, and often a recent overheat. Wikipedia says the crack sits between cylinders 3 and 4 and often shows as a tan line with the valve cover off.
THE FIXConfirm with a cooling-system pressure test and by looking through the oil filler (no 'TUPY' mark means an original 0331). Drain the coolant and remove the intake and exhaust manifolds, the coil rail, the valve cover and the rocker assemblies, keeping pushrods in order. Remove the head bolts and lift the head off. Change the oil and filter, then flush the oil again after the first short run to get coolant out. Fit a revised or aftermarket head; never another 0331, and confirm the casting with the seller before you buy (Wikipedia notes even 'TUPY' heads occasionally crack). Use a new gasket and head bolts, torque in sequence to the service-manual spec, then refill and bleed the cooling system. Fix the cooling problem that probably caused the crack.

Replace the rear main seal (4.0 two-piece) →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ A daySHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSOil dripping from the joint between engine and transmission. Rule out the valve cover gasket, oil-pressure sender, oil-filter-adapter O-rings and (on distributor engines) the distributor gasket first.
THE FIXThe 4.0 uses a two-piece seal, so it can be done with the engine in the truck and the transmission in place. Raise the Jeep high and support it, drain the oil and drop the pan. Remove the rear main bearing cap, pull the lower seal half, then work the upper half out by pushing one end around the crank. Clean everything, oil the new seal lips, install the upper half and then the lower half in the cap, fit the cap and torque it to spec. One NAXJA member quotes 80 ft-lb for a 1999 confirm in manual. Refit the pan with a new gasket, which is where most comebacks happen.

Fix the overheating: radiator, fan clutch, pump and mounts →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a daySHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSThe gauge climbs on hills or in traffic, the electric fan doesn't come on, or the radiator's plastic tanks seep. OffroadXtreme found factory radiators cracking from frame twist on the trail.
THE FIXStart with the cheap checks: the electric fan and its relay and sensor, the fan clutch (it should resist turning when hot), the thermostat and the cap. Check the engine mounts; a worn mount let one author's fan clutch push into the radiator and close up its fins. Then replace the radiator (a thicker all-aluminum core is the usual upgrade; on 1990–95 trucks the core-support tabs may need grinding), the water pump and the hoses. Refill and bleed thoroughly.

Replace the crankshaft position sensor (hot stalls) →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1 hourSHOP COST · Sensor plus your time
SYMPTOMSThe engine dies when hot and restarts after cooling. No spark, and fuel pressure checks out but fuel doesn't reach the injectors. Sometimes the gauges go dead or 'no bus' shows on the odometer. NAXJA members call thermal failure 'pretty common'.
THE FIXTest it hot, right after it fails, because a bad sensor often tests fine cold. On 1991–2001 4.0 HO engines, unplug the sensor pigtail near the rear of the intake manifold and measure across terminals B and C on the 1K–10K ohm scale. It should read open (infinite). Low resistance means replace it. Fit the sensor for your exact year: the part changed several times.

Cure death wobble: track bar, steering linkage and box mount →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a daySHOP COST · Parts plus alignment
SYMPTOMSViolent front-end shake after a bump at speed, wandering, clunks. OffroadXtreme says the track bar is the usual cause.
THE FIXWith the engine running and a helper turning the wheel back and forth, watch the track bar ends, the frame-side bracket, the tie-rod and drag-link ends and the steering box for movement. Replace worn bushings or the whole track bar (an adjustable one recenters a lifted axle), tighten the bracket bolts, and replace worn linkage. DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A weekend per sideSHOP COST · Parts plus welding

THE FIXStrip the interior, cut out the rot back to solid metal, and treat rust you can't cut out. Trial-fit the patch panels, then weld them in with plug or stitch welds. Asked on every forum

Jeep Cherokee XJ questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

In a NAXJA thread, members agree a 235/75R15 (about 29 in) on the stock 15x7 wheels with 5.25 in backspacing fits fine, and treat a 30x9.50R15 as the practical limit. One owner's '88 on 30x9.50s rubbed only the front control arms at full lock, which a steering-stop adjustment fixes at the cost of turning circle. A 255/70R16 (about 30 in) rubbed the rear fenders on a loaded 2000. Less backspacing pushes the tire out toward the flares.

SOURCE · NAXJA
Which years and engines should I buy?

Buy a 4.0. OffroadXtreme recommends the 1991-and-later HO, says 1987–90 Renix trucks suit owners who like diagnosing sensors, and says to avoid 1984–86 (no 4.0). NAXJA members single out non-ABS 1998–99 trucks with the 4.0, AW4, NP231 and 29-spline 8.25 as about the most solid combination. The 2000–01 trucks are good if the head has been checked or replaced.

SOURCE · OFFROADXTREME · NAXJA
What's the 0331 head problem?

The 2000–01 4.0 got a head with casting number 0331 that can crack between cylinders 3 and 4, letting coolant into the oil. Look through the oil filler between the cylinder 3 and 4 exhaust rockers: a 'TUPY' mark means a revised head, though Wikipedia says even those occasionally crack. Most failures follow an overheat, so keep the cooling system healthy, and never replace a cracked one with another 0331.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · NAXJA
Which rear axle do I have, and is it any good?

There are three possibilities. The Dana 35 was used on early trucks and on ABS-equipped trucks throughout; it's fine stock but weak with bigger tires. The Chrysler 8.25 is common on non-ABS 1991–2001 trucks and went from 27 to 29 splines in late 1996. The Dana 44 came on some 1987–91 tow-package trucks. The axle tag or the cover shape will tell you.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · OFFROADXTREME
Command-Trac or Selec-Trac?

From 1987 both have 2.72:1 low range. The NP231 Command-Trac is part-time (2H, 4H, N, 4L) and NAXJA calls it better for heavy off-road use. The NP242 Selec-Trac adds a full-time 4WD mode for slippery pavement.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · NAXJA
What fluids and capacities does it take?

Per a NAXJA member's capacity summary (year not stated): 4.0 oil 6 qt with filter, coolant 10.5 qt, AW4 4 qt drain-and-fill (Dexron III/Mercon), NP231 2.2 pt, Dana 30 2.5 pt 75W-90, Dana 35 3.5 pt, 8.25 4.4 pt, plus friction modifier for Trac-Lok. ExtremeTerrain lists ATF+4 for the AW4, 12 qt of coolant, 1.6 qt for the NP231 and GL-5 oil for the AX-15, so the sources conflict: check your manual confirm in manual. See 'After you buy it' for the full list.

SOURCE · NAXJA · EXTREMETERRAIN
Why does my XJ overheat?

The usual suspects are a clogged or cracked radiator (the factory plastic tanks can split under frame twist), a weak fan clutch, a dead electric fan or relay, an old water pump, and worn motor mounts that let the fan get too close to the radiator. On Renix trucks, check whether someone has converted the cooling system.

SOURCE · OFFROADXTREME
What causes death wobble, and how do I fix it?

Usually the track bar: worn bushings, a loose bracket or a bent bar. Worn tie-rod ends, the drag link or ball joints can also cause it. Find the loose part with a helper turning the wheel, replace it, and check the unibody around the track-bar and steering-box mounts for cracks. A new steering stabilizer only hides the problem.

SOURCE · OFFROADXTREME · NAXJA
How long do they last?

NAXJA members report XJs commonly running 250,000–300,000 miles or more on factory drivetrains if they're maintained. The original poster in NAXJA's beginner thread called anything under 100,000 miles hard to find and probably overpriced.

SOURCE · NAXJA
Why is my Renix XJ so hard to diagnose?

The 1987–90 Renix computer doesn't store fault codes, so you diagnose sensors with a multimeter. It's well documented, but it's more work than the 1991-and-later Chrysler system, which became OBD-II with the JTEC computer in 1996.

SOURCE · NAXJA · WIKIPEDIA
How much lift do I need?

It depends on the tires and the trail. The factory Up Country package added an inch. Kits run from TeraFlex's 1.5-inch Budget Boost, through 3-inch leaf-and-coil systems from Zone and Rough Country, to 4.5–6.5-inch long-arm kits. Bigger lifts usually need an adjustable track bar and driveline work.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · QUADRATEC
