Jeep Grand Wagoneer problems: 8 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the Jeep Grand Wagoneer (SJ), the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Jeep Grand Wagoneer problems to check before you buy

  1. Rust first, everything else secondHagerty's buyer's guide calls rust the biggest concern. Lift the front carpet to see the floor pans, then check where the fuel tank meets the body, the underbody and frame, and the front fenders near the parking lamps. New fenders are no longer available. YourMechanic adds the frame near the fuel-tank skid plate and the rockers, and says the driver's floor is the most common hole.
  2. Bubbles under the woodgrainThe wood is vinyl. Bubbles under it usually mean rust working from the inside. Replacement Cherry Oak and Marine Teak (1987–91) vinyl kits exist, but the metal underneath has to be fixed first.
  3. Drive it cold and hotKerr estimates the Motorcraft carburetor causes about 40% of owner headaches. Stumbling or stalling cold or hot is common. Also listen for ignition faults and watch the oil-pressure gauge. A rear main seal leak costs $500–1,000 to fix.
  4. Test 4WD on the moveSelec-Trac should engage on the fly. Check that 4WD engages and disengages properly and shift through every range; low range can grind. Look for leaking transfer-case seals.
  5. Work every switchWindow flex tracks snap, and window and power-lock switches fail often. The rear-window switch frequently dies, and because the glass must come down to reach the inside tailgate handle, a dead switch can lock you out of the tailgate.
  6. Check the A/C and the cabinA/C repairs are expensive. The 1989–91 trucks got a better compressor than the earlier leak-prone one. Sagging headliners and water-damaged door panels are common.
  7. Know the yearWikipedia points to 1989–91 for better rustproofing, paint and equipment, and a 1991 Final Edition commands a premium. Hagerty says tan or sand interiors and blue, green or white paint sell best.
  8. Ask where it livedKerr won't buy trucks from road-salt states and estimates about 20% of needed parts have to come from salvage yards. A dry-state truck with a known history is worth paying for.

Jeep Grand Wagoneer recalls and service bulletins

  • Front wheel bearing lubrication ↗ 1984 · NHTSA 84V-009Front wheel bearings might not have enough grease; covered 10,504 1984 Grand Wagoneers and J-trucks.
  • Contaminated brake fluid ↗ 1986 · NHTSA 85V-140Some 1986 Grand Wagoneers and J-trucks (230 vehicles) were filled with contaminated brake fluid, which can affect the whole braking system.
  • Cruise-control module ↗ 1987 · NHTSA 87V-022Cruise-control module could operate inconsistently.
  • Key-operated tailgate window ↗ 1984–1988 · NHTSA 87V-178The power tailgate glass could keep closing if the spring-loaded key switch failed to return to off; part of a 180,000-vehicle campaign back to 1970 Wagoneers.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.
