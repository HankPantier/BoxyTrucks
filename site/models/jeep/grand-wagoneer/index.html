PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Jeep introduced the Wagoneer in November 1962 for the 1963 model year, and the same SJ body was still in production 29 model years later. By 1991 it was the longest-running domestically produced vehicle on one platform. For its final eight years it was called Grand Wagoneer: a body-on-frame, solid-axle, carbureted-V8 station wagon with simulated woodgrain on its flanks and leather inside. It was sold as the gold standard of the SUV market at a time when nobody had yet called it an SUV.

THE KAISER ORIGINAL (1963–1969)

Industrial designer Brooks Stevens shaped the Wagoneer for Kaiser-owned Willys, which renamed itself Kaiser Jeep Corporation shortly after launch. It shared its chassis with the Gladiator pickup and was more car-like than any other 4x4 on sale. It offered an overhead-cam 'Tornado' six of 140 hp, independent front suspension, power steering, an automatic gearbox and optional air conditioning. Development took three years and cost about $20 million. When it arrived it had no direct rival: the two-door Range Rover was still eight years away.

The independent front end did not catch on and was dropped from four-wheel-drive models in 1965. An AMC 327 V8 arrived the same year. The 1966 Super Wagoneer added a 270 hp four-barrel V8, a tilt wheel, a console-shifted TH400 and a power tailgate window. Only about 3,989 were built, and Wikipedia calls it the precursor of today's luxury SUVs. From 1968 to 1971 a Buick 350 'Dauntless' V8 did the work.

AMC TURNS IT INTO A LIMOUSINE

AMC bought Kaiser Jeep in 1970 and refined the truck rather than replacing it. The AMC 360 and 401 V8s arrived after 1971. In 1973 came Quadra-Trac, a full-time 4WD system that saved buyers from locking hubs and a stiff transfer-case lever, and woodgrain side trim appeared from 1971. The 1978 Wagoneer Limited added leather, power seats, cruise control and forged aluminum wheels. At $10,500 it was priced against Cadillacs and Lincolns, and AMC built 28,871 Wagoneers that year.

When the unibody Cherokee XJ arrived for 1984, AMC planned for the compact XJ Wagoneer to replace the big wagon. Demand kept the old truck alive. The Limited became the Grand Wagoneer, which freed the 'Wagoneer Limited' name for the XJ. A cheaper, wood-free Wagoneer Custom lasted only through 1984, and from 1985 there was a single fully loaded version. The 1986 update brought a new grille and hood ornament, a redesigned dash with square gauges, new seats and a Trac-Lok limited-slip for the Selec-Trac driveline.

CHRYSLER LEAVES IT ALONE

Chrysler agreed to buy AMC on March 9, 1987, and largely left the Grand Wagoneer alone. It still made $5,000 to $6,000 per truck, and Chrysler kept building it with the carbureted AMC 360. The 360 became one of the last carbureted engines in an American-built vehicle. The 1989–91 trucks are considered the best: they got a better A/C compressor to replace the earlier leak-prone unit, a rear wiper, an overhead console with compass and thermometer, keyless entry, electrocoat primer and base-clear paint.

Then fuel prices rose with the 1990 oil shock. Sales fell from more than 10,000 in 1989 to 6,449 in 1990, and only 4,253 were built for 1991. The platform would also have needed major re-engineering for upcoming federal light-truck safety rules. Chrysler announced the end on February 27, 1991, offered a gold 'Final Edition' dash badge, and built the last Grand Wagoneer in Toledo on June 21, 1991. The name came back for one year on the 1993 ZJ Grand Cherokee and then returned in 2021 on the WS-platform Wagoneer and Grand Wagoneer.

THE AFTERLIFE: RESTOMODS AND RISING PRICES

Specialist shops were refurbishing Grand Wagoneers before Chrysler built the last one. Wagonmaster, founded by Leon Miller in Kerrville, Texas, keeps the original look but replaces the carburetor with throttle-body fuel injection. Classic Gentleman, G.K. Kerr's shop in Bemus Point, New York, looks for low-mileage trucks and offers full rebuilds and color changes. In 2019 DrivingLine reported $40,000 to $80,000 for trucks from either shop. Kerr told Hagerty he won't buy trucks from road-salt states.

Hagerty said its Grand Wagoneer values rose an average of 2–6% with each bi-monthly update, with a 1991 in #2 condition at $36,900 and a #1 at $62,200 when that piece ran. By 2021, Wikipedia notes, Hagerty listed the 1984–91 trucks among the most sought-after vintage cars of the COVID-19 pandemic. The 1984–91 Grand Wagoneer belongs to the same era as the FJ60 Land Cruiser and the four-door Range Rover Classic. Of the three, it is the one most likely to be restored with an EFI V8 and sold as a lifestyle object.

SIDEBAR · THE MEXICAN AMBASSADOR

VAM's 4.6-liter six and the 'Ambassador' finale

Mexico had its own full-size Jeeps. Vehículos Automotores Mexicanos built Wagoneers with a 282 cu in (4.6 L) six from its Lerma plant, an engine designed for low-octane fuel and high altitude, and offered Alpina and base trims that never reached the US.

Its last product was the 1987 VAM Wagoneer Ambassador: a four-seater with a second pair of front seats or captain's chairs in place of the rear bench, landau-style rear windows and 'Ambassador' badges. Most sources put production at 10 to 30, in black or white only.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The Grand Wagoneer is a 1963 design updated over the years. It has a separate ladder frame (boxed and strengthened from 1976), leaf springs and solid axles at both ends, a carbureted AMC 360 V8, a Chrysler TorqueFlite 727 three-speed automatic and a chain-drive New Process transfer case. Jeep's 1989 spec sheet lists a 108.7 in wheelbase, 186.4 in length, 10.1 in minimum running clearance, a 20.3-gallon tank behind a skid plate, and P235/75R15 tires on 15x7 six-lug wheels.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust first, everything else secondHagerty's buyer's guide calls rust the biggest concern. Lift the front carpet to see the floor pans, then check where the fuel tank meets the body, the underbody and frame, and the front fenders near the parking lamps. New fenders are no longer available. YourMechanic adds the frame near the fuel-tank skid plate and the rockers, and says the driver's floor is the most common hole.
  2. 02
    Bubbles under the woodgrainThe wood is vinyl. Bubbles under it usually mean rust working from the inside. Replacement Cherry Oak and Marine Teak (1987–91) vinyl kits exist, but the metal underneath has to be fixed first.
  3. 03
    Drive it cold and hotKerr estimates the Motorcraft carburetor causes about 40% of owner headaches. Stumbling or stalling cold or hot is common. Also listen for ignition faults and watch the oil-pressure gauge. A rear main seal leak costs $500–1,000 to fix.
  4. 04
    Test 4WD on the moveSelec-Trac should engage on the fly. Check that 4WD engages and disengages properly and shift through every range; low range can grind. Look for leaking transfer-case seals.
  5. 05
    Work every switchWindow flex tracks snap, and window and power-lock switches fail often. The rear-window switch frequently dies, and because the glass must come down to reach the inside tailgate handle, a dead switch can lock you out of the tailgate.
  6. 06
    Check the A/C and the cabinA/C repairs are expensive. The 1989–91 trucks got a better compressor than the earlier leak-prone one. Sagging headliners and water-damaged door panels are common.
  7. 07
    Know the yearWikipedia points to 1989–91 for better rustproofing, paint and equipment, and a 1991 Final Edition commands a premium. Hagerty says tan or sand interiors and blue, green or white paint sell best.
  8. 08
    Ask where it livedKerr won't buy trucks from road-salt states and estimates about 20% of needed parts have to come from salvage yards. A dry-state truck with a known history is worth paying for.

Spotting the real thing

Three different Jeeps wore 'Wagoneer' in the 1980s, woodgrain is easy to stick on, and restomod shops trade on 'Final Edition' badges, so the VIN and the year-specific details matter.

  1. ID
    SJ Grand Wagoneer vs XJ WagoneerThe 1984–90 XJ Wagoneer and Wagoneer Limited are compact unibody Cherokees with woodgrain; the Grand Wagoneer is the body-on-frame SJ on a 108.7 in wheelbase with a 6-lug 15x7 wheel. The 1993 Grand Wagoneer is a ZJ Grand Cherokee.
  2. ID
    VINCharacters 6–7 read 15 on a Grand Wagoneer. On 1984–86 VINs the 4th character is the engine (C = 258 six, N = 360 V8); on 1987–90 VINs N = 5.9 V8, the only engine offered. The 10th character is the model year.
  3. ID
    1989–91 tellsRear wiper/washer, overhead console with compass and outside temperature, infrared keyless entry, and alloy wheels with gunmetal-grey inlays instead of the earlier gold.
  4. ID
    1986-on interiorSquare-gauge dash, two-spoke steering wheel, plastic woodgrain glovebox door and adjustable front head restraints mark a 1986 or later truck; earlier trucks have the old dash and metal glovebox door.
  5. ID
    Final Edition badgeThe gold 'Final Edition' dash badge was a 1991-only option for trucks sold after February 27, 1991, so one on any earlier year was added later.

Replace the rear main seal (AMC 360)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ A long daySHOP COST · $500–1,000 at a shop (Hagerty)
SYMPTOMSOil dripping from where the engine meets the transmission, with no obvious leak above it. Hagerty lists rear main leaks as a common 360 fault.
THE FIXRule out the valve covers, intake and oil-pressure sender first, because oil runs downhill to the bellhousing. Then drain the oil, support the engine, and drop the oil pan; on a full-size Jeep, front axle and crossmember clearance make this the hard part. Remove the rear main cap, pull both seal halves, clean the grooves, install the new seal, refit the cap to the factory torque spec, and refit the pan with a new gasket. Fill and check for leaks after a heat cycle.

SYMPTOMSThe rear glass won't go down, so the interior tailgate handle can't be reached. YourMechanic says the tailgate window is 'notorious for failing'.
THE FIXTest the dash switch and the key switch in the tailgate first; Hagerty says the rear switch often dies. If the motor hums but the glass doesn't move, open the tailgate inner panel (lower the glass by hand with the manual crank if you need to) and check the lifter cushions and glass channels for binding. Patch floors, rockers and lower quarters
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ Several weekendsSHOP COST · Parts plus bodywork labor
SYMPTOMSWet or crunchy carpet, holes under the driver's floor mat, bubbling under the woodgrain, rotten rockers that can let exhaust into the cabin.
THE FIXPull the seats and carpet and wire-brush everything so you can see the full extent. Cut out rot back to clean metal, then trial-fit and weld in repair sections: front driver's and passenger's floor, rear sections, rockers, dog legs and lower quarters. Seam-seal and undercoat. Drop the fuel tank and replace the skid plate and straps
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ A daySHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSRust where the tank meets the body, a sagging or perforated skid plate, fuel smell, a sending unit that reads wrong. Both Hagerty and YourMechanic name this area.
THE FIXRun the tank nearly empty and disconnect the battery. Support the skid plate and tank with a jack, remove the skid-plate bolts and straps, disconnect the filler and vent hoses and the sender wiring, and lower the assembly. Inspect the frame and body above it. Treat the rust, then refit with a new skid plate (or the master kit), insulator mat, straps and fresh hardware.

Selec-Trac won't engage: vacuum system and transfer-case service
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–3 hoursSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSThe dash 2WD/4WD control does nothing, the truck stays in one mode, or 4WD drops out. Hagerty says to check that 4WD engages on the fly; seals leak with age.
THE FIXSelec-Trac uses vacuum to shift between 2WD and 4WD. Check the vacuum lines from the engine to the reservoir, the dash switch and the transfer-case actuator for cracks or disconnected hoses, and test with a hand vacuum pump. On 1984–86 trucks, the transfer-case vacuum switch is a known replacement part. Fix any seal leaks at the yokes, then drain and refill the transfer case (Team Grand Wagoneer recommends a synthetic ATF-type fluid).

Asked on every forum

Jeep Grand Wagoneer (SJ) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

We couldn't find a forum consensus we trust for the SJ, so we won't guess. The factory size is P235/75R15 on 15x7 six-lug alloys (1989 spec). Anything larger should be test-fitted at full lock and with the suspension compressed. Team Grand Wagoneer sells a 3-inch lift kit with springs and shocks for owners who want more. Running bigger tires on yours? Tell us through Show Us Your Rig.

SOURCE · WAGONEER WORLD
How much power does the 360 V8 make?

It depends on the source. Hagerty quotes 140 hp and 280 lb-ft, and Offroaders.com and Wikipedia's AMC V8 table both give 144 hp (280 lb-ft) for the 1991 two-barrel 360. Either way, the torque is what moves it. The 360 is a tall-deck AMC engine. Chrysler kept building it until 1991, changing only the casting from '360' to '5.9L'.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · WIKIPEDIA
What fuel economy should I expect?

Not much. Wikipedia cites a combined 11 mpg, and Hagerty says single digits in town and about a dozen on the highway with the V8. Fuel prices after the 1990 oil shock helped kill the truck. TBI conversions are said to help, but don't expect miracles from a carbureted-era V8 in a 4,000-plus-pound wagon.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · HAGERTY
How much can it tow?

Jeep's 1989 spec lists 5,000 lb maximum trailer weight (750 lb tongue) with the Class III Package B, and 3,500 lb (350 lb tongue) with the Class I and II Package A.

SOURCE · WAGONEER WORLD
Is it full-time 4WD?

Most are Selec-Trac: an NP228 or NP229 (the 229 adds a viscous coupling) with a dash control that shifts between 2WD and 4WD on the move using vacuum. Offroaders.com says some 1984–88 Grand Wagoneers used the part-time NP208 instead. Going between 4-High and 4-Low takes neutral and a crawl of under 5 mph.

SOURCE · OFFROADERS.COM · WIKIPEDIA
Which years should I buy?

1989–91, if you can. Those years got better rust-proofing (electrocoat primer), base-clear paint, an improved A/C compressor, an overhead console, keyless entry and a rear wiper. The 1991 Final Edition carries a premium. Hagerty's specialist adds that low mileage, under about 100,000, is the safest buy.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · HAGERTY
Carburetor or fuel injection?

Every Grand Wagoneer left the factory with a carburetor, a two-barrel Motorcraft on the 360. Throttle-body injection kits are the most common upgrade. Hagerty puts the conversion at about $2,000 in parts and up to $1,000 in labor, and Wagonmaster builds its trucks around a Howell TBI system.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · DRIVINGLINE
What are they worth?

Values rose for years. In one report, Hagerty listed a 1991 at $36,900 in #2 condition and $62,200 in #1, and said a nice #3 driver shouldn't cost much more than $20,000. Restomods from Wagonmaster or Classic Gentleman were $40,000–80,000 in 2019. These figures are dated, so check current Hagerty valuations and recent BaT results.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · DRIVINGLINE
Is this the same as the Wagoneer Limited (XJ)?

No. From 1984 to 1990 Jeep also sold a compact, unibody Wagoneer based on the Cherokee XJ, with woodgrain decals. The Grand Wagoneer is the full-size, body-on-frame SJ.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Can I still get parts?

Mostly. Specialists such as Team Grand Wagoneer and BJ's Off Road stock everything from floor pans to woodgrain vinyl. But Hagerty's specialist estimates about 20% of the parts a restoration needs come from salvage yards, and front fenders are no longer available.

SOURCE · HAGERTY
Can I replace the woodgrain?

Yes. It's vinyl, not wood. The 1987–91 trucks use 'marine teak' and earlier trucks the cherry-oak style, and replacement kits are sold for both. Fix any rust under the old vinyl first, because bubbles usually mean the metal is rotting.

SOURCE · TEAM GRAND WAGONEER · YOURMECHANIC
