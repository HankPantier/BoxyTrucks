Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

Jeep Wrangler JK Unlimited problems to check before you buy

  1. Pick your engine first2007–2011 trucks have the 3.8 and four-speed automatic; 2012–2018 have the Pentastar and five-speed. Driving Line suggests 2013 or later for a daily driver and says any year is fine for a weekend trail rig.
  2. Drive it over a pothole at 50 mphDeath wobble usually shows up at 45–65 mph after a bump. Have someone turn the wheel a quarter turn each way with the engine running while you watch both ends of the front track bar. Any movement at the bushing or bracket is a red flag.
  3. Look at the oil filter housingOn a 3.6, pull the engine cover and look for oil in the valley at the back of the engine, and for oil on the bellhousing. The plastic housing is a known leaker. Curbside Classic's 2015 JK started leaking at about 55,000 miles.
  4. On a 3.8, check for oil burning and manifold ticksAsk how much oil it uses between changes, and listen for a cold-start exhaust tick, which can mean a cracked manifold. Driving Line also lists intake-gasket failures and, on hard-used engines, ring and main-bearing failures from about 100,000 miles.
  5. 2012–2013: ask about the left cylinder headChrysler extended the warranty on the left cylinder head of affected 2012–2013 Wrangler Pentastars (2011–2013 across its range) to 10 years or 150,000 miles; its bulletin covers excessive cylinder leakage showing up as misfire codes. Ask whether the head has been replaced and get the dealer record.
  6. Sight down the front axleOn lifted trucks with big tires, look for bent axle tubes or C-ends and uneven tire wear. Check for a truss. A bent Dana 30 housing usually means replacing it, often with a Dynatrac or Currie unit.
  7. Weigh the build against the door stickerBumpers, a winch, a rack and a tent can put a JKU over its payload before anyone gets in. Check the sticker, and look at the rear coils and the bump-stop gap for sag.
  8. Modified Jeeps: price the parts, then get a shop inspectionDriving Line's advice is to research every listed aftermarket part, have a 4x4 shop inspect the truck, and assume a heavily built Jeep has had a hard life.

Jeep Wrangler JK Unlimited recalls and service bulletins

  • Driver airbag clockspring ↗ 2007–2010 · NHTSA 16V-290Dust can contaminate the clockspring so the driver's airbag may not deploy; Chrysler recall S33, 388,532 vehicles.
  • Takata passenger airbag inflator ↗ 2007–2012 · NHTSA 16V-352Passenger airbag inflator may rupture as the propellant degrades; this campaign covered trucks sold or registered in hot, humid states.
  • Takata passenger inflator (Zone A states) ↗ 2009–2013 · NHTSA 18V-021Passenger inflator replacement for trucks sold or registered in hot, humid states (recall U03).
  • Driver airbag inflator ↗ 2010–2016 · NHTSA 19V-018Driver inflator may over-pressurise and explode on deployment (recall V01).
  • Automatic transmission overheat warning ↗ 2007–2008 · NHTSA 09V-436Autos lacked a fluid-temperature warning; fluid could boil over onto hot parts. Dealers add a 'HOT OIL' message and chime (recall J30).
  • Front brake lines vs inner fender liner ↗ 2007–2010 · NHTSA 10V-236Inner fender liners can rub through front brake hoses and cause a leak (recall K13).
  • Axle-to-chassis fastener torque ↗ 2010–2011 · NHTSA 11V-145Front and rear axle attachment fasteners could lose torque; dealers re-torque all 19 (recall L09).
  • Power-steering line rubs transmission cooler tube ↗ 2012–2013 · NHTSA 13V-2343.6 automatics: the power-steering return line can wear a hole in the transmission cooler tube (recall N28, 180,131 vehicles).
  • Heated mirror connector short ↗ 2011–2013 · NHTSA 14V-631Corrosion in the heated power-mirror connector can short.
  • Front impact sensor wiring ↗ 2016–2017 · NHTSA 16V-734Sensor wiring can pull loose in some crashes before it signals the airbags.
  • Right-hand-drive (postal) Unlimited airbag clockspring ↗ 2011–2016 (RHD) · NHTSA 16V-288Right-hand-drive (postal) trucks built February 2010 to May 2016: dust can contaminate the clockspring and disable the driver's airbag. Earlier RHD trucks were covered by 11V-528 and 13V-176 (2008–2012).
  • Right-hand-drive driver seat belt buckle strap ↗ 2011–2018 (RHD) · NHTSA 19V-680The buckle mounting strap may fracture and separate from the seat frame (recall VA6).
  • Death wobble: Chrysler steering-vibration bulletin ↗ In August 2012 Chrysler sent dealers a bulletin acknowledging the solid-axle shimmy, naming aftermarket parts, wrong tire pressures and worn steering and suspension components as causes. NHTSA had earlier concluded the condition doesn't cause loss of control.
  • Shimmy with the 4-inch Mopar lift ↗ · TSB 02-001-162012–2015 JKs built before June 15, 2015 with the Mopar 4 in lift: replace the front axle track bar bracket and recheck alignment (supersedes 02-008-15).
  • 3.6 Pentastar left cylinder head (X56) ↗ · TSB 09-002-14 Rev. A2012–2013 JKs with misfire codes P0300/P0302/P0304/P0306: check cylinder leakage and replace the left head. Warranty Bulletin D-14-12 extended head coverage to 10 years/150,000 miles.
  • Water leaks ↗ Bulletins cover cowl trim panel fastener leaks (water on the front floor, 2012–2014), door and windshield-frame gaps, and Freedom Top centre-seal leaks.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about Jeep Wrangler JK Unlimited reliability

What's the Pentastar cylinder-head issue?

In June 2014 Chrysler extended the warranty on the left cylinder head of some 2011–2013 3.6 V6s (2012–2013 on the Wrangler) to 10 years or 150,000 miles after cylinder-leakage misfires. Dealers replaced the head where testing showed excessive leakage, and AutoGuide reported that redesigned heads went into production in 2012. The extension has now run out, so a 2012–2013 truck with a misfire on that bank deserves a careful look.
