PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

On April 12, 2006, at the New York Auto Show, Jeep did something it had resisted for 65 years: it put four doors on a Wrangler. The JK Unlimited kept everything that made a Wrangler a Wrangler (a separate frame, solid axles at both ends, a windshield that folds and doors that come off) and added 20.6 inches of wheelbase so adults could sit in the back. It was priced from $21,000. Purists predicted disaster. By mid-2017, three-quarters of the new Wranglers listed for sale were four-doors.

LONGER THAN EVERYTHING BEFORE IT

Jeep had stretched its short-wheelbase rig before, with the CJ-8 Scrambler and the TJ-based Wrangler Unlimited (LJ), but both added about 10 inches and kept two doors. The JK Unlimited added 20, for a 116-inch wheelbase against the two-door JK's 95.4, and yet it was only 14 inches longer overall than the 2006 LJ. It went on the line in August 2006 alongside the two-door at Toledo, Ohio, and it was the only four-door convertible on sale in America from the death of the Hummer H1 until the Ford Bronco arrived in July 2021.

Mechanically it is the same truck as the two-door. Driving Line's buyer's guide calls the two nearly identical and admits a soft spot for the Unlimited. X (later Sport), Sahara and Rubicon trims were offered from day one. Base trucks got a Dana 30 front axle, a Dana 44 rear and the Command-Trac transfer case with a 2.72:1 low range. The Rubicon added Dana 44s at both ends with electric Tru-Lok lockers, the 4:1 Rock-Trac transfer case and an electronically disconnecting front sway bar. Two-wheel-drive Unlimiteds were sold until 2010; from 2011, every one was a 4x4.

3.8 VERSUS 3.6

The JK's original engine was the minivan-derived 3.8-liter EGH V6, rated at 202 hp and 237 lb-ft, with a six-speed NSG370 manual or a four-speed 42RLE automatic. In a heavy four-door it was hard work, and Driving Line lists its habits: burning oil, intake-gasket failures, cracked exhaust manifolds, and a heat-stressed automatic once owners fitted bigger tires. The 2011 update gave the cabin a proper redesign, but the engine had to wait.

For 2012 the Pentastar 3.6 arrived with 285 hp and 260 lb-ft, plus a five-speed W5A580 automatic. Quadratec's spec history says it stopped being a 'rolling brick'. The Pentastar isn't flawless: some 2012–2013 Wrangler engines (2011–2013 across Chrysler's range) got a 10-year/150,000-mile warranty on the left cylinder head, and the plastic oil filter housing in the valley is a known leaker. It's still the JK engine to buy, and Driving Line recommends a 2013 or later truck for daily use.

THE OVERLAND JEEP

The long wheelbase changed what a Wrangler was for. The four-door rode better, had a usable cargo area behind the rear seat and was rated to tow 3,500 lb against the two-door's 2,000. An industry grew up around it: roof racks, drawer systems, bumpers, and full conversions such as AEV's Brute, a 139-inch-wheelbase double-cab pickup inspired by the Defender 130. Mopar's own JK-8 Independence kit turned an Unlimited into a two-seat pickup in 2011.

The catch is weight. Payload is roughly 900 to 1,300 lb depending on year, trim and source, and a JKU with steel bumpers, a winch, a rooftop tent and two adults goes through that quickly. Expedition Portal's long-running payload thread is full of owners over their door-sticker rating. Their advice: spend on springs and shocks, and cut weight before you add more armor.

HOW IT ENDED

Toledo built its millionth JK on May 17, 2013. The JK's last model year, 2018, overlapped the new JL Unlimited, and fender decals told the two apart. Production ended on April 27, 2018, and the plant was retooled to build the JL-based Gladiator pickup. The JL kept the formula and fixed much of what JK owners complained about, but it didn't make the JKU obsolete. A well-sorted 2012–2018 Unlimited is still one of the cheapest ways into a solid-axle overland rig that will happily take 35s, and it is far easier to find parts for than a Land Cruiser 80.

SIDEBAR · THE UNLIMITED GOES TO WORK

Mail carriers, soldiers and the J8

Jeep built right-hand-drive Wranglers for US rural mail carriers, and for 2009 that job moved from the two-door to the right-hand-drive Unlimited.

In September 2007 Chrysler showed the J8, a military Unlimited with larger brakes, axles and suspension, heavy-duty rear leaf springs and a 2,952-lb (1,339 kg) payload. Power came from a 2.8-liter VM turbodiesel, and a hood-mounted snorkel allowed 30 inches of wading. It was to be built in Egypt and was never sold in the US, because it couldn't meet US emissions rules.

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The JK Unlimited is a body-on-frame four-door convertible with coil springs and solid axles front and rear, part-time four-wheel drive with a two-speed transfer case, and a 116-inch wheelbase. Unlike the TJ it replaced, it runs nearly everything except the steering through computer control, and it brought stability control to the Wrangler for the first time.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Pick your engine first2007–2011 trucks have the 3.8 and four-speed automatic; 2012–2018 have the Pentastar and five-speed. Driving Line suggests 2013 or later for a daily driver and says any year is fine for a weekend trail rig.
  2. 02
    Drive it over a pothole at 50 mphDeath wobble usually shows up at 45–65 mph after a bump. Have someone turn the wheel a quarter turn each way with the engine running while you watch both ends of the front track bar. Any movement at the bushing or bracket is a red flag.
  3. 03
    Look at the oil filter housingOn a 3.6, pull the engine cover and look for oil in the valley at the back of the engine, and for oil on the bellhousing. The plastic housing is a known leaker. Curbside Classic's 2015 JK started leaking at about 55,000 miles.
  4. 04
    On a 3.8, check for oil burning and manifold ticksAsk how much oil it uses between changes, and listen for a cold-start exhaust tick, which can mean a cracked manifold. Driving Line also lists intake-gasket failures and, on hard-used engines, ring and main-bearing failures from about 100,000 miles.
  5. 05
    2012–2013: ask about the left cylinder headChrysler extended the warranty on the left cylinder head of affected 2012–2013 Wrangler Pentastars (2011–2013 across its range) to 10 years or 150,000 miles; its bulletin covers excessive cylinder leakage showing up as misfire codes. Ask whether the head has been replaced and get the dealer record.
  6. 06
    Sight down the front axleOn lifted trucks with big tires, look for bent axle tubes or C-ends and uneven tire wear. Check for a truss. A bent Dana 30 housing usually means replacing it, often with a Dynatrac or Currie unit.
  7. 07
    Weigh the build against the door stickerBumpers, a winch, a rack and a tent can put a JKU over its payload before anyone gets in. Check the sticker, and look at the rear coils and the bump-stop gap for sag.
  8. 08
    Modified Jeeps: price the parts, then get a shop inspectionDriving Line's advice is to research every listed aftermarket part, have a 4x4 shop inspect the truck, and assume a heavily built Jeep has had a hard life.

Spotting the real thing

The Unlimited is the JK most people actually bought, so condition, hardware and the swap history decide the price more than the badge does.

  1. ID
    Real RubiconDana 44 axles front and rear with Tru-Lok electronic lockers, Rock-Trac transfer case with 4:1 low range, 4.10 gears and an electronic sway-bar disconnect switch. Sport and Sahara trucks have Command-Trac with 2.72:1 low range.
  2. ID
    Axles by trimSport (X) and Sahara have a Dana 30 front and a Dana 44 rear; Quadratec says fewer than a thousand early 2007s got a Dana 35 rear instead. Rubicon has Dana 44s at both ends. A rear locker was optional on X and Sahara for 2007, then on select X models for 2008–2009.
  3. ID
    Engine and year2007–2011 trucks have the 3.8 EGH V6 (4-speed 42RLE auto or 6-speed manual). 2012–2018 trucks have the 3.6 Pentastar (5-speed W5A580 auto or 6-speed manual). 2011 got the new interior a year before the new engine.
  4. ID
    Four-door vs TJ UnlimitedThe JK Unlimited's wheelbase grew by 20.6 in to 116 in, a true four-door rather than the stretched two-door TJ Unlimited. Two-wheel-drive Unlimiteds (X and Sahara) were sold only through 2010.
  5. ID
    Hemi swaps and AEV buildsPlenty of Unlimiteds carry 5.7 or 6.4 Hemi swaps or AEV conversions (AEV's 'Brute' double-cab pickup ran about four years). Ask for the build paperwork and an emissions-compliance record for your state.
  6. ID
    Postal right-hand driveFrom 2009 the factory right-hand-drive Wrangler was an Unlimited. These have their own RHD-only recalls.

Post-purchase baseline

After you buy it

With no receipts, assume nothing has been done. Change every fluid, and know which engine you have: the 3.8 (2007–2011) and the Pentastar (2012–2018) use different filters, plugs and automatic transmissions. Check the Pentastar's oil filter housing for leaks at the first oil change. Then do the gear oil, which is the service owners skip most. Rubicons and trucks with Trac-Lok need the right axle oil, and a limited-slip axle needs friction modifier.

For the hands-on owner

Common repairs

The jobs owners actually end up doing, rated for a competent home mechanic with a decent tool kit. Always confirm torque figures, fluids and procedures against the factory service manual for your VIN, and work safely — support the truck on stands, never just a jack. You do this work at your own risk; see the disclaimer. Some parts links are affiliate links.

Chase down death wobble (track bar first)
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–3 hours to diagnose; longer if parts need replacingSHOP COST · From free (re-torquing) to about $250–$400 for a JK track bar, per ExtremeTerrain's price guide
SYMPTOMSViolent side-to-side shaking of the steering wheel and front axle, usually at 45–65 mph after a bump or bridge seam, which stops only when you slow right down.
THE FIXCheck the front track bar first: with the engine running, have a helper turn the wheel a quarter turn each way while you watch both ends. If the bar moves without the bolt, or one end moves and the other doesn't, the bushing or bracket is worn. ExtremeTerrain lists the frame-side bolt at 125 ft-lb. Then work through the ball joints, tie-rod and drag-link ends, control-arm bushings and wheel bearings, and check tire balance and pressure. Replace whatever is loose, re-torque everything with the Jeep on the ground, and get an alignment. Quadratec and ExtremeTerrain both warn that a new steering stabilizer only hides the cause.

Replace the Pentastar oil filter housing / cooler (2012–2018)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 6 hours the first time (one owner's log)SHOP COST · Housing plus gaskets and an oil change; tools under $30 for one owner
SYMPTOMSOil pooling in the valley under the intake, running over the back of the engine and dripping from the bellhousing. Sometimes coolant leaks too.
THE FIXRemove the engine cover and intake duct, then disconnect the injectors, coils, vacuum lines and brackets. Take off the upper intake (seven 8 mm bolts) and the lower manifold (eight bolts). Unplug the two sensors on the cooler and remove its five E8 external-Torx bolts. Mop the oil out of the valley, move the sensors and fittings to the new aluminum housing, fit new gaskets and O-rings, and torque in sequence. Reassemble, then refill the oil and coolant. Plug the intake ports with towels while you work. Don't overtighten the filter cap, which is specified at 18 lb-ft. Curbside Classic suspects overtightening contributes to the cracking.

Replace a cracked 3.8 exhaust manifold (2007–2011)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day per side, more if studs breakSHOP COST · Manifold plus gaskets and studs; studs that snap add drilling time
SYMPTOMSA ticking or puffing noise at cold start that quietens as the engine warms, a smell of exhaust in the engine bay, and soot marks at the manifold-to-head joint.
THE FIXDriving Line names cracked exhaust manifolds as a 3.8 weak point. Soak the studs in penetrating oil the night before. Unbolt the heat shield and the downpipe flange, then remove the manifold nuts. Inspect the studs and replace any that are stretched or corroded. Clean the head flange, fit new gaskets and a new manifold, and torque the nuts evenly from the center out. Check the oxygen sensor connectors and the downpipe gasket while you're in there.

Press in new front ball joints
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A day for both sidesSHOP COST · About $40–$60 a pair for stock joints, $200–$250 for a heavy-duty set of four (ExtremeTerrain)
SYMPTOMSClunks over bumps, wandering, uneven tire wear, or play when you rock a lifted wheel top to bottom. Worn joints can contribute to death wobble.
THE FIXLift and support the axle, then remove the wheel, caliper, rotor, ABS sensor and the hub/bearing. Pull the axle shaft, then separate and remove the knuckle. Press out the upper and lower joints with a ball-joint press and the correct adapters, and press in the new ones square. Refit everything, torque to the factory figures (Northridge4x4 lists 75 ft-lb for the ball-joint nuts, 75 ft-lb for the hub bolts and 100 ft-lb for the axle nut), and get an alignment. Many owners replace the hub bearings and axle-shaft U-joints at the same time.

Truss a front axle before it bends (or after)
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A weekend with weldingSHOP COST · Truss kit plus welding; a bent housing needs replacing, not trussing
SYMPTOMSToe that won't stay in alignment, inside-edge tire wear, or a visibly bent tube after a hard hit. It's common on lifted trucks running 35s and up on the factory axle.
THE FIXDriving Line says the small tubes and C-ends on both the Dana 30 and the Rubicon's Dana 44 can bend with oversized tires, and points to EVO Manufacturing trusses as a preventive fix. Check the housing is straight first, because a truss locks in whatever shape it has. Strip the axle (or at least remove the brackets that are in the way), grind the tubes clean, tack the truss in place, then weld in short alternating stitches to limit heat distortion. Paint it and re-check the alignment. If the housing is already bent, Dynatrac and Currie sell replacement housings.

THE FIXWeigh the loaded truck first. Expedition Portal owners say the rear axle and suspension are the weak points under long-term heavy loads, and recommend springs and shocks rather than frame or axle changes. Asked on every forum

Wrangler JK Unlimited questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

33s. JK-Forum owners agree that most 285/70R17s (about 33 inches) fit on stock wheels, at stock height, with the stock bump stops. Expect light rubbing at full lock and at full flex, where they touch the flares, liners and sometimes the front sway bar. A steering-stop washer and the right backspacing help. ExtremeTerrain says 35s need at least a 2–3-inch lift, the right wheel offset and possibly new gears.

SOURCE · JK-FORUM · EXTREMETERRAIN
3.8 or 3.6?

The 3.6 Pentastar (2012–2018) by a mile: 285 hp against 202, plus a five-speed automatic instead of the four-speed. The 3.8 is known for burning oil, intake-gasket leaks and cracked exhaust manifolds. Driving Line suggests a 2013 or later truck for daily use. A cheap 3.8 can still make a good trail rig if you accept its limits.

SOURCE · DRIVING LINE · QUADRATEC
How much can a JK Unlimited tow?

3,500 lb, with a 350-lb tongue weight for the 2017 Rubicon. That's against 2,000 lb for the two-door. For heavy towing the factory manual calls for synthetic 75W-140 in the rear axle.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC · CARS.COM
What's the payload?

Less than you think. Quadratec lists 1,067–1,301 lb for 2012 four-doors depending on trim. For 2017, KBB lists 880–1,000 lb and Cars.com gives the Rubicon 1,178 lb against a 5,700-lb GVWR. Your door-jamb sticker is the number that counts, and a fully built overland JKU often exceeds it.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC · KBB · CARS.COM
What fuel economy should I expect?

The EPA rated the 2017 Unlimited 3.6 at 16 mpg city and 20 highway with the automatic (16/21 manual), and KBB lists a 22.5-gallon tank. Bigger tires, a lift and a rack all bring that down.

SOURCE · EPA (FUELECONOMY.GOV) · KBB
What oil does it take?

3.6 Pentastar: six quarts of 5W-20 meeting Chrysler MS-6395, or 5W-30 in hot climates. The filter is a cartridge under a cap on top of the engine, and Quadratec notes the filter changed for 2014, so buy for your year. The 3.8 also takes about six quarts with the filter, in 5W-20 or 5W-30; check the oil filler cap.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC · JK-FORUM
Which Unlimiteds have lockers?

Every Rubicon has electric Tru-Lok lockers front and rear. Per Wikipedia, a rear locker was optional on X and Sahara for 2007, then only on select X models for 2008–2009; later the only non-Rubicon options were the 2015 Wrangler X and the 2016 75th Anniversary Edition. A Trac-Lok limited slip was the usual non-Rubicon option.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Will the Dana 30 front axle handle 35s?

Driving Line rates the non-Rubicon Dana 30 at about 35-inch tires. The tubes and C-ends can bend with big tires or hard use, and that's true of the Rubicon's Dana 44 too. Trusses help prevent it; replacement housings from Dynatrac or Currie are the cure.

SOURCE · DRIVING LINE
What do I do if it gets death wobble?

Grip the wheel, don't brake hard, and slow down smoothly until it stops. Then fix the cause: start with the track bar, then the tie-rod ends, drag link, ball joints, control-arm bushings and wheel bearings, and check tire balance. A new steering stabilizer only masks it, and an alignment comes last.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC · EXTREMETERRAIN
What's the Pentastar cylinder-head issue?

In June 2014 Chrysler extended the warranty on the left cylinder head of some 2011–2013 3.6 V6s (2012–2013 on the Wrangler) to 10 years or 150,000 miles after cylinder-leakage misfires. Dealers replaced the head where testing showed excessive leakage, and AutoGuide reported that redesigned heads went into production in 2012. The extension has now run out, so a 2012–2013 truck with a misfire on that bank deserves a careful look.

SOURCE · AUTOGUIDE · CHRYSLER TSB 09-002-14
Is the four-door worse off-road than the two-door?

On tight, technical trails the 116-inch wheelbase gives up breakover angle and turning circle compared with the 95.4-inch two-door. Everywhere else it rides better and carries more. Driving Line says the two are mechanically nearly identical and recommends driving both before you choose.

SOURCE · DRIVING LINE · WIKIPEDIA
Which years are the best to buy?

A 2012-on Pentastar. Quadratec calls it a generally strong engine, while Driving Line says the 2007–11 3.8 burns oil, leaks intake gaskets and cracks exhaust manifolds, and has seen some spin bearings by 100,000 miles. Driving Line's daily-driver pick is 2013 or newer; SlashGear rates 2012–13 as the standout years. The year to be wary of is 2007: several first-year recalls, and CarComplaints.com logs more complaints for 2007 and 2008 than for any other Wrangler year. Not everyone agrees on the Pentastar's first years, either: RealTruck lists 2012–14 among its worst, citing engine complaints. So on a 2012–13 ask about the left cylinder head, and on any Pentastar check the oil filter housing.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC · DRIVING LINE · SLASHGEAR · CARGURUS · REALTRUCK
