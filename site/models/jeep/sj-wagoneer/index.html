PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Jeep introduced the Wagoneer in November 1962 for the 1963 model year. It replaced the Willys station wagon that had been built since 1946, and it was the first Jeep line that owed nothing to the CJ. Brooks Stevens shaped it, it shared its SJ chassis with the Gladiator pickup, and it offered features unheard of on other mainstream 4x4s: independent front suspension, power steering, an automatic gearbox and optional air conditioning, all behind an overhead-cam six. For 1974 AMC cut two doors off it and called the result the Cherokee. This page covers both, from 1963 to 1983, before the big wagon carried on alone as the Grand Wagoneer.

THE KAISER WAGONEER (1963–1969)

The first Wagoneers came as two- and four-door wagons and a two-door panel delivery with barn doors. Power came from the 230 cu in 'Tornado' overhead-cam six, rated at 140 hp. Independent front suspension was standard on two-wheel-drive trucks and optional on 4x4s. It was not popular and was dropped from 4WD models in 1965, the same year AMC's 327 V8 (250 hp) and 232 six arrived. Development had taken three years and about $20 million, and Wikipedia notes that the Wagoneer had no direct competitor when it launched; the two-door Range Rover was still eight years away.

The 1966 Super Wagoneer pushed the idea further: a 270 hp four-barrel 327, a tilt wheel, a console-shifted TH400 automatic, air conditioning and a power tailgate window. About 3,989 were built before it ended in 1969, and it is widely regarded as the precursor of today's luxury SUVs. From 1968 to 1971 a Buick 350 'Dauntless' V8 replaced the 327. By the end of 1968 the slow-selling two-door had gone, and every Wagoneer had solid axles and leaf springs at both ends.

AMC, QUADRA-TRAC AND A TWO-DOOR CALLED CHEROKEE

AMC bought Kaiser Jeep in 1970 and replaced the outsourced Buick engine with its own 360 and, later, the 401 V8. In 1973 it added Quadra-Trac, a Borg-Warner full-time 4WD system that saved owners from locking hubs and a stiff transfer-case lever. In 1974 AMC took the dead two-door Wagoneer, gave it wide D-pillars and long single rear side windows, and sold it as the Cherokee. The 1974 Cherokee brochure is where the term 'Sport Utility Vehicle' first appears, and that February the Cherokee became the first winner of Four Wheeler magazine's Achievement Award.

The Cherokee came as a base model or the sportier S. The Chief package arrived in 1976 with wider axles and bigger fender flares so it could run larger tires, and a four-door Cherokee followed in 1977 (never with the wide-track axles). The Golden Eagle and Golden Hawk were graphics packages, the Laredo from 1980 was mostly an upholstery upgrade, and a Pioneer package arrived for 1983. With the 401's forged crank and rods and 3.07:1 gearing, Wikipedia says a Cherokee could top 100 mph; early ones had 120 mph speedometers.

THE WAGONEER GOES UPMARKET (1978–1983)

With the Cherokee taking the sporty buyers, AMC moved the Wagoneer upmarket. The Limited arrived mid-1978 with leather, power seats, cruise control, a CB radio and forged aluminum wheels. At $10,500 it was priced against Cadillacs and Lincolns, and Jeep still built 28,871 Wagoneers that year and 27,437 in 1979. The 1979 restyle brought square headlights, aluminum bumpers and the 'pig nose' grille. The 401 had gone after 1978, and after 1979 Chrysler's TorqueFlite 727 replaced GM's TH400.

The 1979 fuel crisis hit hard. Wagoneer output fell to 10,481 in 1980, the 258 six came back as an option, and from 1981 the range ran Custom, Brougham and Limited. The original Borg-Warner Quadra-Trac was gone after 1979, and an NP219 carried the name to early 1982. For 1983 the Selec-Trac system, with a dash switch for 2WD and 4WD, became standard on the Wagoneer. Wikipedia reports EPA figures of 18 mpg city and 25 highway for the six with the automatic and Selec-Trac, remarkable for a full-size SUV.

1984: THE SPLIT

For 1984 the unibody Cherokee XJ replaced the SJ Cherokee outright, and AMC meant the compact XJ Wagoneer to replace the big one too. Demand kept the old wagon alive. The Brougham was dropped, the Limited became the Grand Wagoneer, and that truck ran until June 1991. Australia's tariff rules treated separate-frame 4x4s more kindly than unibody vehicles, so SJ Cherokees were assembled in Brisbane until about 1985.

Today the 1963–1983 trucks sit in the shadow of the woodgrain Grand Wagoneer, which makes them the better buy for someone who wants to use one. The two-door Cherokee is the rarer and more off-road-minded of the pair, the era rival to the Scout II and the early Bronco with a much bigger cabin. Restomod builders know it too: Bring a Trailer has sold Hemi-, Vortec- and Viper-swapped examples, one of them for $315,000.

SIDEBAR · WHERE 'SUV' CAME FROM

The 1974 Cherokee brochure and the first Four Wheeler award

Wikipedia, citing Jim Allen's Jeep: Collector's Library, says the phrase 'Sport Utility Vehicle' appears for the first time in the 1974 Jeep Cherokee sales brochure. Jeep sold the Cherokee as the 'sporty' two-door version of its station wagon.

In February 1974, Four Wheeler magazine gave the Cherokee its first Achievement Award, the prize that later became the annual Four Wheeler of the Year.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The SJ is a separate ladder frame with semi-elliptic leaf springs and solid axles at both ends from 1969. The frame got boxed side rails and stronger crossmembers from 1976. Both bodies share a 108.7 in wheelbase and 186.4 in length; the wide-track Chief is up to 78.7 in wide against 74.8 in for the standard track. Engines are AMC sixes and V8s fed by carburetors, and the drivetrain changed with the year. Read the VIN and look under the truck before you buy parts.

Buying one

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

SJ Wagoneers and Cherokees sell for less than the woodgrain Grand Wagoneer. Low-mileage originals and V8 restomods bring the big money, and the two-door Cherokee carries a premium over the Wagoneer.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust firstIt's the same body as the Grand Wagoneer, and Hagerty's buyer's guide for that truck calls rust the biggest concern: floor pans under the front carpet, the area where the fuel tank meets the body, the frame and underbody, and the front fenders. Original front fenders are no longer made. Floor, rocker and lower-quarter repair panels are still sold.
  2. 02
    Find out which 4WD system it hasThis matters for parts, fluids and how it drives. A one-lever floor shifter on a 1974–79 automatic is the Borg-Warner Quadra-Trac; a manual from those years has a Dana 20. The 1980–82 'Quadra-Trac' is really an NP219, and 1980–83 trucks may have a part-time NP208 or, from mid-1982, Selec-Trac with a dash switch.
  3. 03
    Listen in tight turnsThe factory manual describes Quadra-Trac 'stick-slip': a low grunting or rasping noise when turning slowly or parking. It's usually the wrong fluid, mismatched tires or uneven pressures. A much louder noise when driving straight points to a stretched chain. Team Grand Wagoneer's chain kit is aimed at trucks with more than about 120,000 miles or skipped fluid changes.
  4. 04
    Check the Chief is a ChiefThe real Chief from 1976 has wider axles and flared fenders and was only built as a two-door. On 1981–83 VINs, characters 6–7 read 16 or 17 for a two-door Cherokee and 18 for a four-door; 15 is a Wagoneer. Golden Eagles are Chiefs with a big hood decal, gold-painted steel wheels and denim seats, so look for more than a sticker.
  5. 05
    Drive it cold and hotHagerty's Grand Wagoneer specialist blames the Motorcraft carburetor for about 40% of owner headaches. Expect stumbling, hard hot starts and ignition faults on an untouched truck. NHTSA recalled 1975–76 trucks for ignition wiring connectors that could not carry enough current (78V-065).
  6. 06
    Work the tailgate windowThe power rear glass has to come down before the tailgate opens. Recall 87V-178 covers 1970–83 Wagoneers and Cherokees whose key-operated window kept closing if the spring-loaded key failed to return. Test the dash switch, the key switch and the glass travel.
  7. 07
    Look at the brakesEarly Cherokees had four-wheel drums as standard, with front discs optional; Wagoneers got front discs as standard from 1974. Check what's fitted and how old the steel lines are. Pre-bent stainless kits exist for 1977-on trucks.
  8. 08
    Budget for scarce partsSpecialists such as Team Grand Wagoneer and BJ's Off Road stock most mechanical and body parts. But on the later Grand Wagoneer, Hagerty's specialist estimates about 20% of restoration parts still come from salvage yards. Two-door-specific trim and glass are harder to find than Wagoneer parts.

Spotting the real thing

Three Jeeps called themselves Cherokee or Wagoneer in the 1980s, the Chief package is easy to fake with stickers, and the drivetrain changed almost every year, so check the VIN, the axles and the transfer case.

  1. ID
    SJ vs XJThe SJ is body-on-frame on a 108.7 in wheelbase with six-lug wheels (from 1974). The 1984-on Cherokee and the 1984–90 Wagoneer Limited are compact unibody XJs.
  2. ID
    Real wide-track ChiefChiefs from 1976 have wider axles and larger fender flares, and were never built as four-doors. Golden Eagles add denim seats, gold-painted steel wheels and a big gold hood eagle.
  3. ID
    VIN, 1981–83Character 4 is the engine (C = 258 six, N = 360 V8); 5 is the transmission and transfer case; 6–7 are the body (15 = Wagoneer, 16/17 = two-door Cherokee, 18 = four-door Cherokee); 10 is the year (B = 1981, C = 1982, D = 1983); 11 is the plant (T = Toledo, B = Brampton). The VIN is also stamped on a tag on the upper-left firewall.
  4. ID
    Early VINsPre-1981 VINs use an older, shorter format. Wikipedia lists the first-year 1974 Cherokees as Model 16 (base) and Model 17 (S).
  5. ID
    Transfer case1974–79 automatics with a single floor lever have the Borg-Warner Quadra-Trac; 1974–79 manuals have a Dana 20. From 1980 look for a tag reading NP208, NP219 or NP229; Selec-Trac trucks have a 2WD/4WD switch.

Cure Quadra-Trac stick-slip and chain slap (1974–79) →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5 (fluid) · 4 / 5 (chain)TIME · ≈ 1 hour (fluid) · a weekend (chain)SHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSA pulsing grunt or rasp when turning slowly or parking, worst after the truck has sat for a week; or a slapping noise against the case, even at idle, which means a loose chain.
THE FIXSet all four tires to the same pressure and make sure they match in size and type. Drain the Quadra-Trac and the low-range unit if fitted, and refill only with Quadra-Trac lubricant, never multigrade detergent oil. The factory manual then says to drive in circles both ways for about 15 minutes without hitting the steering stops. If it still slaps, split the case and fit a new chain and seals; Team Grand Wagoneer says to use about 3 quarts of its Quadra-Trac fluid after a rebuild.

SYMPTOMSStumbling or stalling cold or hot, hard hot starts, rich running, fuel smell.
THE FIXRule out vacuum leaks and ignition faults first. Rebuild with the kit for your year and engine: the V8 two-barrel kit is listed for 1981–91 and the 4.2 kit for 1982–86. Fit a new base gasket and insulator, set the float and choke pull-off to the kit spec, and check timing with a light. Throttle-body injection replaces the carburetor and needs its own pump, return line and wiring; it's the most common long-term fix.

THE FIXJack the axle, rock each wheel at 12 and 6 o'clock and at 3 and 9. DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ A long daySHOP COST · $500–1,000 at a shop (Hagerty, 360 V8)
SYMPTOMSOil dripping where the engine meets the transmission, with nothing wet above it.
THE FIXRule out the valve covers, intake and oil-pressure sender first, because oil runs back to the bellhousing. Drain the oil and drop the pan; on a full-size Jeep the front axle and crossmember make this the hard part. Remove the rear main cap, roll out both seal halves, fit the new seal, refit the cap to factory torque, and refit the pan with a new gasket.

Asked on every forum

Jeep Wagoneer & Cherokee (SJ) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

We couldn't find a forum consensus we trust for unlifted SJs, so we won't guess. Tire databases list 225/75R15 for 1976–83 Wagoneers (235/70R15 is also listed for 1976). The Cherokee Chief's wider axles and bigger fender flares were added so it could run larger tires. For anything bigger, test-fit at full lock and with the suspension compressed, or add a modest leaf-spring lift. Running bigger tires on yours? Tell us through Show Us Your Rig.

SOURCE · TIRESIZE.COM · WIKIPEDIA
What's the difference between a Wagoneer, a Cherokee and a Grand Wagoneer?

They're all SJs on the same 108.7 in wheelbase. The Wagoneer is the four-door wagon from 1963. The Cherokee is the 1974–83 version with two wide-pillar doors (a four-door Cherokee was added in 1977). From 1984 the Wagoneer Limited became the Grand Wagoneer, while 'Cherokee' moved to the compact XJ.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What makes a Cherokee Chief special?

The Chief package arrived in 1976 with wider axles and larger fenders so the truck could run bigger tires. It was only offered on two-doors. The 1978–79 Golden Eagle was a Chief with denim upholstery, gold-painted steel wheels and a large gold eagle on the hood.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Which 4WD system do I have?

It depends on the year and gearbox. 1974–79 manuals have a gear-driven Dana 20 (2.03:1 low). 1973–79 automatics have the Borg-Warner Quadra-Trac, a chain-drive full-time case with a vacuum-locked center differential. For 1980–82 the 'Quadra-Trac' name went on an NP219. Part-time NP208s appear from 1980, and Selec-Trac (NP229) arrived in mid-1982 and became standard on the 1983 Wagoneer.

SOURCE · OFFROADERS.COM · WIKIPEDIA
Why does my Quadra-Trac groan in parking lots?

The factory manual calls it stick-slip: the cone brakes in the center differential stick and release when you turn slowly. It's usually the wrong lubricant, mismatched tires or uneven tire pressures. Drain and refill with Quadra-Trac lubricant and drive in slow circles for about 15 minutes. A loud noise in a straight line is a different problem, usually a stretched chain.

SOURCE · WAGONEERS.COM (FSM EXTRACT)
Which engine should I look for?

The 360 V8 is the common one and parts are easy. The 401 was offered from 1974 to 1978, made 215 net hp and had a forged crank and rods, and Wikipedia says the SJs still hold the record for the largest engine Jeep ever offered. The 258 six is the economy choice and returned for 1981–83 after the fuel crisis.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · WAGONEERS.COM
What fuel economy should I expect?

Not much from a V8. Wikipedia cites a combined 11 mpg for the later 5.9 Grand Wagoneer. The best case is the 1983 Wagoneer with the six, an automatic and Selec-Trac, which Wikipedia says was rated at 18 mpg city and 25 highway by the EPA.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Is it really the first SUV?

It's the first vehicle sold under that name. Wikipedia, citing Jim Allen, says 'Sport Utility Vehicle' appears for the first time in the 1974 Cherokee brochure. The 1966 Super Wagoneer is widely called the first luxury SUV.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Can I still get parts?

Mostly. Many Grand Wagoneer parts listed for 1974–91 fit these trucks, and specialists such as Team Grand Wagoneer and BJ's Off Road stock Quadra-Trac chains and seals, floor pans, tailgate parts and steering parts. Original front fenders and some two-door trim are hard to find.

SOURCE · TEAM GRAND WAGONEER · HAGERTY
What are they worth?

Bring a Trailer sold 31 SJ Wagoneers and Cherokees between October 2025 and October 2026, with a median of about $19,000. Projects went for a few thousand dollars. A 29,000-mile 1983 Wagoneer Limited made $51,000, an original 1975 Cherokee S $80,000, and a Viper-powered 1979 Cherokee Chief restomod $315,000. Hagerty puts a good-condition 1976 Cherokee Chief at about $14,100.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER · HAGERTY
Are there open recalls I should know about?

The big one is NHTSA 87V-178: on 1970–83 Wagoneers and Cherokees the key-operated tailgate window could keep closing if the key didn't spring back to off. NHTSA also lists ignition-connector (1975–76) and rear-wheel-bearing (1979–80) campaigns. Ask a Jeep dealer to check the VIN.

SOURCE · NHTSA
