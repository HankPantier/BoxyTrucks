Jeep Comanche MJ years to avoid, and the best years to buy

Every Jeep Comanche MJ model year: which ones owners and specialists recommend, which have documented problems, which carry recalls, and what they sell for now.

Read the full Jeep Comanche MJ guideCommon problemsBiggest tireSpecs & fluids
The short answer

Buy a 4.0, ideally a 1991–92 HO: 190 hp and Chrysler's easier-to-diagnose engine management, the same reason Off Road Xtreme recommends the HO in the XJ. 1987–90 Renix trucks are good too, but they're diagnosed with a multimeter. 1986 trucks have no 4.0. If you do buy a 2.5, Comanche Club members say look for 4.10 or 4.56 gears.

Jeep Comanche MJ year by year

YEARVERDICTWHAT CHANGED
1985—Unveiled in August at the MGM Grand, Las Vegas, for MY1986: XJ-based, long bed only, 2.5 I4, 2.8 GM V6 or 2.1 Renault turbodiesel; $7,049 base 2WD
1987—Short bed (113 in wheelbase) added; 4.0 I6 (173 hp) replaces the V6; AW4 auto; NP231/NP242; Chrysler buys AMC in March; diesel dropped
1988—Eight-slot grille; 4.0 rated 177 hp; Eliminator trim and Olympic Edition
1989—Aisin AX-15 five-speed replaces the Peugeot BA-10/5 mid-year
1991Sweet spotChrysler engine management replaces Renix; 4.0 'High Output' 190 hp; Chrysler 8.25 rear appears; no automatic with the 2.5. The 1991–92 HO is the one to buy: easier to diagnose than the Renix 4.0, which is why Off Road Xtreme recommends the HO in the XJ that shares the engine
1992—Final year; production ends June 12, 1992 after 952 trucks built that calendar year

“Sweet spot” means owners and specialists recommend those years; “Beware” means documented problems; “Recall” means check the recall was done rather than walk away.

What to bring when you look at a Jeep Comanche MJ

Five inexpensive tools that catch most of the problems above before you hand over the money. Or book a specialist: see how to get a pre-purchase inspection.

  • Paint thickness gaugeThick readings on one panel mean body filler or a respray, which usually means an accident or rust repair the seller hasn’t mentioned.
    Amazon ↗
  • Inspection borescope cameraFits through drain holes into frame rails, rockers and sills, where these trucks rust from the inside out.
    Amazon ↗
  • Bright LED flashlightFor the underside: leaks at the axle seals, transfer case and rear main, and the frame behind the rear wheels.
    Amazon ↗
  • Telescoping inspection mirrorShows the back of the engine, the top of the fuel tank and the inner fenders without crawling under.
    Amazon ↗
  • Compression testerOn a high-mileage or long-parked engine, uneven compression is the cheapest way to spot a tired engine before you pay for it. Diesels need a diesel-rated tester.
