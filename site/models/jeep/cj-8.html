PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Jeep launched the CJ-8 in March 1981, according to 4WDrive, and called it the Scrambler. It was a long-wheelbase CJ-7: 103.5 inches between the axles, 10 more than the CJ-7, with a removable half cab and a small pickup-style box built into the body tub instead of a separate bed. 4WDrive magazine calls it North America's first factory convertible 4x4 pickup. It kept the CJ formula of a ladder frame, leaf springs, live axles, a gear-driven two-speed transfer case and manual locking hubs. It also kept the CJ's open top, the part a pickup normally gives up.

A PICKUP THAT ISN'T QUITE

4WDrive says the stretch added two feet to the bed and quotes a 636 kg (about 1,400 lb) payload. SlashGear says a hard half cab was standard, with full soft and hard tops available; 4WDrive describes a removable soft or hard cab. Jeep sold the truck on fun as much as utility. eBay Motors notes that the Scrambler name came from an appearance package, and many trucks wore loud decal kits. The SR Sport and SL Sport packages added bigger tires, under-hood insulation and lighting, a three-spoke wheel, chrome trim, carpet and decals. A denim edition topped the range at $8,392.

Under the skin it's a CJ-7 with a longer middle. Like the CJ-7, it moved to wider-track Dana 30 and AMC 20 axles for 1982. The Dana 300 transfer case is a strong, gear-driven unit, according to Jeep-CJ.com. Prices fell quickly. 4WDrive lists a base price of $7,288 at launch and a low of $6,765 in 1982. By 1985 Jeep was throwing in a free soft top to keep the trucks moving.

WHAT'S UNDER THE HOOD

Engines followed the CJ-7. Early trucks came with GM's 151 Iron Duke four as standard, which SlashGear calls 'relatively wimpy' and Jeep-CJ.com's buyer's guide says to avoid. AMC's own 2.5-litre four replaced it later in the run, rated at 105 hp. Offroaders.com and Wikipedia date the GM 151 in CJs to 1980–83, and the AMC 150 arrived for 1984. The engine to have is the 258 straight-six, rated at 115 hp. No Scrambler had a factory V8.

Manual Scramblers used the BorgWarner SR-4 behind the four and the Tremec T-176 behind the six. Automatics were Chrysler TorqueFlites: the 904 with the four and the 999 with the six. The BorgWarner T-4 four-speed and T-5 five-speed were in the CJ line from 1982, per Offroaders.com (SlashGear dates the Scrambler's T-5 to 1985–86). The T-5 has a 4.03:1 first gear and an overdrive fifth that SlashGear gives as 0.76:1 and Offroaders.com as 0.8. On Jeep-CJ.com, members call it weak and suggest the T-176 as the easiest swap. Most Scramblers have the AMC 20 rear axle, but some 1986 trucks got a Dana 44.

SLOW SALES, LONG AFTERLIFE

Sales started at 8,355 in 1981 and slid every year to 128 in 1986, and there's debate over whether those last ones were leftovers. That adds up to about 27,800 Scramblers, against more than 379,000 CJ-7s built from 1976 to 1986. The Cherokee-based Comanche effectively replaced it. The rarest variants went abroad or north. Alaska's postal service ordered 230 right-hand-drive automatics with full steel 'World Cab' tops and barn doors. Venezuela and Australia got a similar truck called the CJ8 Overlander, with full-length rear windows.

The low production numbers are why prices have climbed. SlashGear says a well-preserved Scrambler now costs at least $30,000, and eBay Motors featured one showing just under 2,100 miles on its five-digit odometer, with a $39,900 asking price. Long-wheelbase Jeeps came back with the 2004 Wrangler Unlimited (LJ) and the four-door JK Unlimited. Neither has a bed, though, and that is why Scrambler owners keep theirs.

SIDEBAR · THE GIPPER'S SCRAMBLER

A blue Scrambler at Rancho del Cielo

Ronald Reagan drove his own vehicles at Rancho del Cielo, his ranch near Santa Barbara. His favorite was a red 1962 Willys CJ-6. KEYT reports that Nancy Reagan bought a new blue Jeep Scrambler for the ranch after Barbara Walters called the old Willys a 'scroungy jeep.' RealTruck identifies it as a sky-blue 1983 CJ-8 Scrambler Renegade, given to the President by Nancy. A White House photograph dated 2 April 1983 shows him with it at the ranch.

The truck is now the centerpiece of the Reagan Ranch Center exhibit in Santa Barbara, complete with a 'GIPPER' license plate. RealTruck says it shows a little over 18,000 miles.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The CJ-8 is a CJ-7 with the frame and tub stretched to 103.5 inches. It keeps a boxed ladder frame, leaf springs on live axles, a gear-driven Dana 300 and manual locking hubs. The back of the tub becomes a short bed behind a removable half cab. Everything mechanical is shared with the 1981–86 CJ-7, which makes parts easy. Body panels behind the front seats are Scrambler-specific.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust in the long tubCheck the rockers, the rear corners of the bed, the wheel wells, under the roll bar and the floor. Jeep-CJ.com warns that patches welded over rust become rust. Knock on panels to find filler. Replacement Scrambler tubs exist, but they are a full strip-down job.
  2. 02
    Frame and shock towersInspect the whole frame, especially the shackle mounts, the area behind the rear wheels and the body mounts. On 1982–86 wide-track trucks, Jeep-CJ.com says the shock towers can trap water. Mud blocks the drain holes in the boxed rails.
  3. 03
    Steering box mountBoxed frames often crack around the steering box, especially on lifted trucks with big tires. Look for cracks, fresh welds and movement when someone turns the wheel.
  4. 04
    Engine choiceSlashGear calls the 258 six the most desirable engine. Jeep-CJ.com says to avoid the Iron Duke four. Look for start-up smoke, rattles, and oil weeping from the rear main seal.
  5. 05
    Five-speed healthOn 1985–86 trucks, check that the T-5 doesn't jump out of gear or grind. Jeep-CJ.com members call it weak. A T-176 swap is the common fix, and the driveshafts must be modified for length.
  6. 06
    Tailgate, tops and sealsCheck the tailgate hardware and weatherstripping and the cowl seals, since failed seals trap water. Tops and half cabs are Scrambler-specific, so price in a replacement if they're missing.
  7. 07
    Originality and paperworkJeep-CJ.com recommends mostly stock trucks over projects or heavy trail builds. 4WDrive's featured truck came with a binder documenting its restoration, and paperwork like that matters on a truck this valuable.
  8. 08
    Test driveStart it cold. Test every gear, 4WD high and low on gravel, the brakes (gently at first), the gauges and the heater. Check for leaks at the transfer case and both axles.

Spotting the real thing

Stretched CJ-7s and cut-down CJ-7 conversions exist, and every Scrambler's value depends on proving it left Toledo as one.

  1. ID
    Wheelbase and body103.5 in wheelbase versus the CJ-7's 93.5 in, same 55 in windshield and front end, with a longer rear overhang and a pickup-style box formed by the tub rather than a separate bed.
  2. ID
    VINOn the 17-character VIN, characters 6–7 read 88 for a CJ-8 (87 is a CJ-7). The 4th character is the engine (B = 151 Iron Duke, C = 258 six, U = AMC 150 four; per Offroaders.com and ExtremeTerrain) and the 10th the model year (B = 1981 … G = 1986).
  3. ID
    VIN plate vs frameCheck the riveted firewall tag against the partial VIN on the passenger-side frame rail above the rear wheel; a CJ-7 tag on a Scrambler frame, or the reverse, means a rebuild.
  4. ID
    Postal and export Scramblers230 right-hand-drive automatics with a full-length steel 'World Cab' hardtop and barn-door rear went to the Alaska Postal Service; the export CJ8 Overlander (Venezuela, Australia) also used full-length rear windows.

DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day to a daySHOP COST · Crown J8133885-1 driver-side kit about $215 at Quadratec (October 2026); the passenger side is a separate kit
SYMPTOMSClunks on take-off, a rear hub loosening on its shaft, or spun splines. Quadratec explains that the factory hardened hubs cut into softer axle-shaft splines, leaving weak spots in the driveline.
THE FIXOne-piece shafts fix the hub flange to the shaft, which stops the splines spinning. Remove the wheels and drums, pull the pressed-on hubs (a hub puller is the usual tool), remove the bearing retainers and pull the shafts. Fit the new shafts with bearings and seals per the kit instructions, replace the inner seals while you're there, and refill with gear oil. Add friction modifier if the axle has a Trac-Lok. Trucks with the 1986 Dana 44 don't need this.

Carter BBD rough idle (258)
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hoursSHOP COST · A can of carb cleaner
SYMPTOMSStumbling or stalling at idle and poor economy, but fine at higher rpm. Offroaders.com says the cause is almost always clogged idle tubes. It also says post-1981 feedback carbs with a stepper motor are 'a bit more troublesome.'
THE FIXWith the engine running and choke open, look for fuel dripping from the idle nozzles. Tag the hoses, then remove the air cleaner, choke plate and rod, and the venturi cluster (two screws, two small gaskets). Check that the idle pickup tubes are seated, clear the tubes and nozzles with fine wire and carb cleaner without losing the check ball, and reassemble. Tighten the carb body screws. Drilling the idle tubes to 0.032 in reduces clogging. If it still idles badly, check for vacuum and manifold leaks.

Ignition refresh or HEI swap (258)
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a daySHOP COST · The BustedJeep author paid $15 for a junkyard HEI
SYMPTOMSHard starting, rough idle, hesitation and poor fuel economy, which Jeep-CJ.com's write-up blames on the factory Duraspark.
THE FIXStart with a cap, rotor, plugs and wires. For the HEI swap, use a 1976–82 GM inline-six HEI with an AMC V8 drive gear. Mark No. 1, swap the distributors and feed the HEI from the constant-12 V firewall wire, not the resistor coil feed. Gap the plugs to .045 in (firing order 1-5-3-6-2-4) and set the timing with a light; many owners run about 8° BTDC. Trucks with the feedback ECM need it bypassed first.

Rear main seal (258)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day to a daySHOP COST · Seal and pan gaskets plus labor
SYMPTOMSOil dripping from the joint between the engine and bellhousing.
THE FIXFollowing Jeep's 1973 service-manual procedure (via IFSJA): drain the oil and drop the pan, then remove the rear main cap and lower seal half. Loosen the other caps and tap out the upper half with a brass drift. Oil the crank and fit the new upper half lip-forward. Put sealant only on the lower half's end tabs, seat it in the cap, seal the cap's chamfers and torque to spec. Refit the pan with new gaskets. Always replace both halves together.

Asked on every forum

Jeep CJ-8 Scrambler questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

Jeep-CJ.com members put 31-inch tires as the safe maximum on a stock CJ. They say 32s rub the rear wheel wells without trimming, and doubt 33s will fit. The thread covers CJs generally rather than the Scrambler specifically, but the CJ-8 shares the CJ-7's front end and springs. Saggy old springs and wheel backspacing can make even 31s tight.

SOURCE · JEEP-CJ.COM
How many Scramblers were built?

Wikipedia lists 8,355 in 1981, 7,759 in 1982, 5,405 in 1983, 4,130 in 1984, 2,015 in 1985 and 128 in 1986, about 27,800 in total. Some argue the 1986 trucks were leftovers. SlashGear compares that with more than 379,000 CJ-7s.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · SLASHGEAR
Which engine should I buy?

The 258 straight-six, rated at 115 hp. SlashGear calls it the most desirable option, and Jeep-CJ.com praises it for torque and reliability. The Iron Duke four in early trucks is the one Jeep-CJ.com says to avoid. The later AMC four, at 105 hp, is better. No Scrambler came with a V8.

SOURCE · SLASHGEAR · JEEP-CJ.COM
Which transmission is best?

Jeep-CJ.com recommends the T-176 four-speed among 1980s CJ gearboxes, and members call the T-5 five-speed weak. The automatics were Chrysler TorqueFlites: the 904 behind the four and the 999 behind the six.

SOURCE · JEEP-CJ.COM · SLASHGEAR
Is it really a pickup?

Sort of. The bed is part of the body tub behind a removable half cab, not a separate box. 4WDrive quotes a 636 kg (about 1,400 lb) payload and a bed two feet longer than the CJ-7's rear tub. SlashGear says full soft and hard tops were available, and with the top off it's a long open CJ.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · 4WDRIVE · SLASHGEAR
Did Ronald Reagan really own one?

Yes. A blue Scrambler was bought for Rancho del Cielo, and a White House photograph in the National Archives shows Reagan with it there on 2 April 1983. RealTruck identifies it as a 1983 CJ-8 Renegade with about 18,000 miles. It's displayed at the Reagan Ranch Center in Santa Barbara with a 'GIPPER' plate.

SOURCE · REALTRUCK · KEYT · NARA
What's wrong with the AMC 20 rear axle?

Its two-piece shafts let the hardened hub cut into the softer shaft splines over time. One-piece kits for 1982–86 CJ-7s and CJ-8s, such as Crown's, fix the hub to the shaft. Some 1986 Scramblers have a Dana 44 instead, which doesn't have this issue.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC · SLASHGEAR
What fluids does it take?

ExtremeTerrain's CJ chart lists 5W-30 or 10W-30 oil (6 qt for the 4.2, 4 qt for the 2.5), 80W-90 in the Dana 300 (2 qt), Dana 30 (1.25 qt), AMC 20 (2.4 qt) and Dana 44 (2 qt), ATF+4 in the T-4/T-5 (2 qt) and TorqueFlites (3 qt service fill), and DOT 3 brake fluid. Trac-Lok axles need limited-slip additive.

SOURCE · EXTREMETERRAIN
What are the World Cab and Overlander?

Right-hand-drive Scramblers with a full-length steel top and rear barn doors in place of the tailgate. The Alaskan postal service bought 230, with automatic transmissions. Venezuela and Australia got a similar CJ8 Overlander with full-length rear windows.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · 4WDRIVE
What's one worth?

A lot more than it cost new. 4WDrive lists a $7,288 base price at launch. SlashGear says a well-preserved Scrambler now costs at least $30,000, and eBay Motors featured one showing about 2,100 miles on a five-digit odometer, asking $39,900. Rust and originality drive the price.

SOURCE · 4WDRIVE · SLASHGEAR · EBAY MOTORS
What replaced it?

Wikipedia says the Cherokee-based Comanche pickup effectively replaced the Scrambler in 1986. The open-top CJ itself gave way to the Wrangler YJ in 1987.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
