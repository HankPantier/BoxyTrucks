Jeep Grand Cherokee ZJ problems: 8 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the Jeep Grand Cherokee ZJ, the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Jeep Grand Cherokee ZJ problems to check before you buy

  1. Rust is the deal-breakerOffroadXtreme says nearly every ZJ outside the desert has some rust, usually on the lower doors, the rockers and the bottom of the hatch, which can stop it opening. Heavy rust in the unibody frame rails is a reason to walk away.
  2. Test for death wobble at 35–50 mphHit a bump in that speed range and feel for violent shaking from the front axle. OffroadXtreme says the lower track bar bolt is usually the cause, though worn tie-rod ends, ball joints and bushings also contribute. NHTSA recalled some 1993 trucks for front lower control arm cam bolts (93V-051).
  3. Work the transfer case through every modeFind out whether it has Command-Trac, Selec-Trac or Quadra-Trac, then shift through every position on a loose surface. OffroadXtreme says Selec-Trac linkages often stick, and many owners swap to the simpler part-time NP231. Quadra-Trac needs four matching tires; NHTSA recalled 1996 trucks because the front axle could overheat on the temporary spare.
  4. Find the leaksOn the 4.0, OffroadXtreme lists the rear main seal, oil pan gasket, valve cover gasket and oil filter adapter gasket. On the V8s it's the rear main, the valve covers and the oil pressure sending unit. Most are easy fixes, but a rear main can be a multi-day job.
  5. Watch the temperature gaugeOffroadXtreme lists an insufficient cooling system among the common ZJ faults. Let the truck idle hot, then drive it up a hill, with the A/C on if it has it. A tired radiator or fan clutch is cheaper to fix before you tow or wheel it.
  6. Test the small stuffOffroadXtreme's list: rear hatch stuck shut, failed alarm systems, broken auto-dimming mirrors and dead rear wiper motors. On a Limited, check the overhead trip computer and the EVIC warning display too.
  7. Check the recalls were doneTwo big ones: 13V-252, where Chrysler inspected the rear structure and fitted a tow hitch to protect the fuel tank in rear impacts, and 02V-053, a secondary detent for a shifter that could appear to be in Park when it wasn't. A Jeep dealer can check the VIN.
  8. Know what you're buying1993–95 trucks have the driver's airbag only and pre-OBD-II diagnostics; 1996–98 trucks have dual airbags and OBD-II. A real 5.9 Limited has a louvered hood, a mesh grille, 16 in five-spoke wheels and came only in Deep Slate, Stone White or Bright Platinum. OffroadXtreme warns that the factory axles can struggle behind the 5.9.

Jeep Grand Cherokee ZJ recalls and service bulletins

  • Fuel tank leak risk in rear impacts ↗ 1993–1998 · NHTSA 13V-252About 1.56 million ZJs and 2002–07 Libertys; dealers inspected the rear structure and fitted a Chrysler-designed tow hitch.
  • Shifter can rest out of Park ↗ 1993–1998 · NHTSA 02V-053If not fully in Park the vehicle could roll backward; dealers added a secondary detent to the floor shifter.
  • Front axle overheating on the temporary spare ↗ 1996 · NHTSA 98V-006Quadra-Trac trucks on 225/70R16, 225/75R16 or 245/70R15 tires; dealers replaced the space-saver with a full-size spare.
  • Front lower control arm cam bolts ↗ 1993 · NHTSA 93V-051Eccentric cam adjuster bolts at the front lower arm-to-axle brackets could fail due to assembly misalignment.
  • Liftgate latch bumpers swell ↗ 1993 · NHTSA 93V-055Assembly grease swelled the latch bumpers, so the liftgate might not latch or might be hard to open.
  • Steering column shaft pin ↗ 1993 · NHTSA 93V-210A sheared plastic pin could let the upper and lower steering shafts separate.
  • Inadvertent airbag deployment ↗ 1997 · NHTSA 97V-080The circuit design could allow an airbag to deploy when the ignition was switched off.
  • Brake booster vacuum reservoir diaphragm ↗ 1998 · NHTSA 98V-023The vacuum reservoir diaphragm could split or tear.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Six inexpensive tools that catch most of the problems above before you hand over the money. Or book a specialist: see how to get a pre-purchase inspection.

  • Bluetooth OBD-II scannerReads stored and pending codes, and shows the readiness monitors. If several say "not ready", someone may have cleared the codes just before you arrived.

  • Inspection borescope cameraFits through drain holes into frame rails, rockers and sills, where these trucks rust from the inside out.
    Amazon ↗
  • Bright LED flashlightFor the underside: leaks at the axle seals, transfer case and rear main, and the frame behind the rear wheels.

  • Telescoping inspection mirrorShows the back of the engine, the top of the fuel tank and the inner fenders without crawling under.
