PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The ZJ Grand Cherokee went on sale in April 1992 as a 1993 model. It was a bigger, plusher Cherokee XJ that AMC had started developing in the 1980s to replace both the XJ and the full-size Grand Wagoneer. It kept solid axles front and rear on coil springs, offered the 4.0 straight six or a Magnum V8, and introduced a fully automatic full-time 4WD system called Quadra-Trac. Thirty years on, it's the forgotten middle child of the Jeep lineup, and that is exactly why it makes such a good budget wheeler.

AN AMC IDEA, A CHRYSLER LAUNCH

AMC hired outside designers to sketch a larger follow-up to the XJ, and Larry Shinoda styled a prototype in 1985. Shinoda had to sue to be paid and credited, and after Chrysler bought AMC in 1987 it settled with his estate for more than $200,000. A running pre-production version appeared in 1989 as the 'Jeep Concept 1'. Dodge also built a ZJ-based Ramcharger replacement with its own grille, but cancelled it before launch.

Chrysler built the truck at the new Jefferson North Assembly Plant in Detroit and introduced it at the January 1992 Detroit auto show in style. Chrysler's Bob Lutz drove a red Laredo from the factory to Cobo Hall, then he and Mayor Coleman Young drove it up the front steps and through a plate-glass window into the lobby. Export trucks built by Magna Steyr in Graz, Austria, carry the code ZG.

WHAT MADE IT DIFFERENT

At launch every ZJ had the 190 hp 4.0 six. Early in 1993 the 5.2 Magnum V8 arrived, making it the only SUV in its class with a V8 option, and with it a woodgrain Grand Wagoneer version that lasted one year and 6,378 trucks. There were three 4WD systems: part-time Command-Trac (NP231), Selec-Trac (NP242) with a full-time mode, and the new Quadra-Trac (NV249). Quadra-Trac was a permanent system with a viscous coupling that sent drive forward when the rear slipped, with no driver input needed. A driver's airbag and four-wheel ABS were standard.

Underneath, both axles sit on coil springs with four control arms and a track bar each, which Jeep called Quadra-Link. It's a close cousin of the setup the Wrangler TJ got for 1997, and much of the TJ and XJ aftermarket bolts on. The ZJ was Motor Trend's 1993 Truck of the Year, the V8 made Car and Driver's Ten Best list, and Petersen's named it 4x4 of the Year in 1993, 1996 and 1998.

1996 FACELIFT AND THE 5.9 LIMITED

The 1996 update brought new bumpers and cladding, a bigger grille, a new interior with a passenger airbag, and an OBD-II port. The manual gearbox had already gone after 1994, and Command-Trac and the base SE were dropped. The 4.0 was rerated at 185 hp. The NV249 was revised: from 1996 low range gave a true 50/50 lock instead of still running through the viscous coupling.

For 1998 Jeep fitted the 5.9 Magnum V8 from the Ram pickup to create the 5.9 Limited. It made 245 hp and 345 lb-ft, came with louvered hood, mesh grille, 16 in wheels, 3.73 gears and a rear Trac-Lok, and needed premium fuel. Motor Trend timed it at 6.8 seconds to 60 mph, and Jeep advertised it as the world's fastest SUV. Kevin's Offroad puts 1998 production at 14,286 out of 247,372 Grand Cherokees. Then the WJ arrived and the ZJ was gone.

THE BUDGET WHEELER

Today the ZJ is cheap. Bring a Trailer's ZJ sales over the past year ran from $4,250 to $18,500, and ordinary trucks change hands for less. OffroadXtreme calls the 4.0 one of Jeep's most reliable engines, if a little underpowered for the ZJ's weight, and the 5.2 'extremely reliable'. The catches are rust, leaks, worn steering and the age of the Quadra-Trac and Selec-Trac cases.

Its contemporaries were the third-generation 4Runner, the Discovery and, further up the price list, the 80 Series and P38 Range Rover. Of that group, the ZJ is usually the cheapest way in. Its successor, the WJ, moved to gerotor-based Quadra-Trac II and Quadra-Drive, so the ZJ remains the last Grand Cherokee with the simpler chain-and-viscous Quadra-Trac.

SIDEBAR · THE COBO HALL CRASH

How Chrysler launched the Grand Cherokee

At the 1992 North American International Auto Show, Chrysler's Bob Lutz drove a Poppy Red 1993 Grand Cherokee Laredo from Jefferson North Assembly to Cobo Hall. Then he and Detroit mayor Coleman Young drove it up the front steps and through a plate-glass window, rigged to shatter just before the truck reached it.

Jeep revisited the launch for the Grand Cherokee's 25th anniversary. The 2017 'Grand One' Easter Jeep Safari concept was a stock 1993 Laredo 5.2, bought off Craigslist, repainted robin's-egg blue over a woodgrain-style finish and given a stretched wheelbase, lift and big tires.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The ZJ is a unibody SUV with solid axles at both ends, each located by four control arms and a track bar and sprung on coils. The wheelbase is 105.9 in, and Wikipedia gives curb weights of 3,574 lb with the six and 3,901 lb with the V8. Choose your drivetrain carefully: three transfer cases, five automatic gearboxes and several rear axles were fitted over six years.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust is the deal-breakerOffroadXtreme says nearly every ZJ outside the desert has some rust, usually on the lower doors, the rockers and the bottom of the hatch, which can stop it opening. Heavy rust in the unibody frame rails is a reason to walk away.
  2. 02
    Test for death wobble at 35–50 mphHit a bump in that speed range and feel for violent shaking from the front axle. OffroadXtreme says the lower track bar bolt is usually the cause, though worn tie-rod ends, ball joints and bushings also contribute. NHTSA recalled some 1993 trucks for front lower control arm cam bolts (93V-051).
  3. 03
    Work the transfer case through every modeFind out whether it has Command-Trac, Selec-Trac or Quadra-Trac, then shift through every position on a loose surface. OffroadXtreme says Selec-Trac linkages often stick, and many owners swap to the simpler part-time NP231. Quadra-Trac needs four matching tires; NHTSA recalled 1996 trucks because the front axle could overheat on the temporary spare.
  4. 04
    Find the leaksOn the 4.0, OffroadXtreme lists the rear main seal, oil pan gasket, valve cover gasket and oil filter adapter gasket. On the V8s it's the rear main, the valve covers and the oil pressure sending unit. Most are easy fixes, but a rear main can be a multi-day job.
  5. 05
    Watch the temperature gaugeOffroadXtreme lists an insufficient cooling system among the common ZJ faults. Let the truck idle hot, then drive it up a hill, with the A/C on if it has it. A tired radiator or fan clutch is cheaper to fix before you tow or wheel it.
  6. 06
    Test the small stuffOffroadXtreme's list: rear hatch stuck shut, failed alarm systems, broken auto-dimming mirrors and dead rear wiper motors. On a Limited, check the overhead trip computer and the EVIC warning display too.
  7. 07
    Check the recalls were doneTwo big ones: 13V-252, where Chrysler inspected the rear structure and fitted a tow hitch to protect the fuel tank in rear impacts, and 02V-053, a secondary detent for a shifter that could appear to be in Park when it wasn't. A Jeep dealer can check the VIN.
  8. 08
    Know what you're buying1993–95 trucks have the driver's airbag only and pre-OBD-II diagnostics; 1996–98 trucks have dual airbags and OBD-II. A real 5.9 Limited has a louvered hood, a mesh grille, 16 in five-spoke wheels and came only in Deep Slate, Stone White or Bright Platinum. OffroadXtreme warns that the factory axles can struggle behind the 5.9.

Spotting the real thing

The ZJ came with three transfer cases, three rear axles and several special editions, so identify what's actually under the truck before you pay a premium.

  1. ID
    5.9 Limited1998 only. Look for working hood louvers, a mesh grille, unique five-spoke 16 in wheels, unique rocker moldings, a chrome exhaust tip and a 46RE with 3.73 gears and a rear Trac-Lok. US colors were Deep Slate, Stone White and Bright Platinum only.
  2. ID
    1993–95 vs 1996–981996-on trucks have new bumpers and side cladding, a larger grille, a passenger airbag, an OBD-II port under the dash, a three-spoke wheel with cruise buttons, and raised metal 4x4 badges instead of decals.
  3. ID
    Transfer caseCommand-Trac (NP231, part-time, 1993–95), Selec-Trac (NP242, part-time or full-time) or Quadra-Trac (NV249, full-time). All three use a lever beside the gear selector; read the tag on the case to be sure.
  4. ID
    Rear axleDana 35, Dana 44 or Chrysler 8.25 depending on year, engine and package. Read the tag on the cover or housing before buying gears, lockers or shafts.
  5. ID
    Grand Wagoneer (1993)A Limited with standard 5.2 V8 and Quadra-Trac, woodgrain vinyl on the fenders, doors and tailgate, chrome Grand Wagoneer badges, no lower cladding and a beige interior; 6,378 built.
  6. ID
    Orvis and Up CountryOrvis Limiteds (1995–97) are Moss Green (or Light Driftwood in 1997) with roan red accents and green-and-tan leather. The Up Country package (1993–97) has taller springs, longer bump stops and heavier German gas shocks mounted upside down, for about 1 in more height.

Fix the Magnum V8's oil leaks
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5 (sender) · 3 / 5 (valve covers)TIME · ≈ 1 hour (sender) · half a day (valve covers)SHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSOil running down the back of the block, a flickering oil-pressure gauge, oil on the exhaust manifolds.
THE FIXOffroadXtreme names the rear main seal, valve cover gaskets and oil pressure sending unit as the V8's usual leaks. Check the sender first: it's cheap, and a leaking one also makes the gauge unreliable. Replace it, then reseal the valve covers with new gaskets, torquing the bolts evenly to the manual spec. A rear main leak on the V8 is a pan-off job like the six's.

THE FIXCheck the shift linkage first: OffroadXtreme says Selec-Trac linkages often stick, so clean, lube and adjust the linkage and bushings. THE FIXOffroadXtreme lists a stuck rear hatch as a common ZJ fault, and rust at the bottom of the hatch can stop it opening. Get it open from inside: remove the interior trim panel and work the latch by hand. Clean and lube the latch and linkage, replace broken rods or clips, and check the latch bumpers. NHTSA recalled 1993 trucks because grease made the latch bumpers swell and jam the latch pawl (93V-055).

Stalling at speed: check the crankshaft position sensor
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · About an hour, once diagnosedSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSThe engine cuts out without warning, often warm and sometimes at highway speed. It may restart after sitting, or crank but not start. NHTSA's complaint file holds dozens of ZJ stalling reports in which the crankshaft position sensor was replaced, sometimes more than once (e.g. ODI 878399, 10460757).
THE FIXRead any stored codes first (1996–98 trucks are OBD-II). Then inspect the sensor's harness for chafing and heat damage. One 1995 owner traced his stalling to a broken front motor mount that let the engine tug on the crank-sensor leads until they chafed through and shorted (NHTSA ODI 784679), so check the mounts too. If the wiring and mounts are good, replace the sensor. Stalling has other causes, including a failing fuel pump (see below), so confirm the diagnosis before you buy parts.

Fuel pump failure and a flaking tank
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · Half a day (tank drop)SHOP COST · Parts plus labor; one 1995 owner reported $600 for the pump plus $300 to flush the tank (NHTSA ODI 761423)
SYMPTOMSHesitation, low fuel pressure, stalling or a crank-no-start, and replacement pumps that keep failing. Fuel-pump complaints to NHTSA cluster on 1995–96 trucks. Several owners were told debris from inside the plastic tank had clogged the pump (ODI 756730, 8001105, 758045).
THE FIXConfirm low pressure with a gauge before you condemn the pump. Run the tank low, support it, disconnect the lines and straps, lower it and pull the pump module. Look inside the tank: if the lining is flaking or there's sediment, clean or replace the tank, or the new pump will clog too. Several owners went through multiple pumps before the tank was dealt with. Fit a new strainer with the pump and replace rusty straps. While the tank is down, check that recall 13V-252 (rear-impact fuel tank protection) was done.

Asked on every forum

Jeep Grand Cherokee ZJ questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

About 30 in. Factory sizes ran from 215/75R15 to 245/70R15 and 225/70R16, all roughly 28 in tall. A 30x9.50R15 is the practical limit on stock springs: one owner ran 30x9.50 BFGoodrich all-terrains on a stock '93 and said they came close to rubbing but didn't. Rough Country and Zone rate their 1.5–2 in spacer lifts for 30x9.50s, and Rough Country's 3.5 in kit for 31x10.50s.

SOURCE · TIRESIZE.COM · OFF-ROAD.COM · QUADRATEC
Six or V8?

OffroadXtreme calls the 4.0 one of the most reliable engines Jeep ever built, but a little underpowered for a heavier truck. The 5.2 has more power and torque, is very reliable, and is in most ZJs. The 1998-only 5.9 is rare, and the factory axles can struggle behind it. EPA figures are 16 mpg combined for the 4.0, 14 for the 5.2 and 13 for the 5.9.

SOURCE · OFFROADXTREME · FUELECONOMY.GOV
Which 4WD system is best?

It depends what you want. Command-Trac (NP231, 1993–95) is part-time and the simplest. Selec-Trac (NP242) adds a full-time mode, but OffroadXtreme says its linkage often sticks and many owners swap in an NP231. Quadra-Trac (NV249) is full-time with a viscous coupling and needs no driver input. Before 1996 its low range still ran through the coupling; from 1996 low range locks 50/50.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · OFFROADXTREME
What causes death wobble on a ZJ?

It's violent front-axle oscillation after a bump, usually between 35 and 50 mph. OffroadXtreme says the lower track bar bolt is the usual culprit, but worn tie rods, ball joints and bushings all add to it. Fix every loose joint, not just the one you find first.

SOURCE · OFFROADXTREME · JEEPPROBLEMS.COM
How much can it tow?

Wikipedia says the 5.2 V8 was rated for up to 5,000 lb at launch, and the 4.0 was rated for 5,000 lb by 1995. Check the trailer-tow package and your owner's manual. The fuel-tank recall (13V-252) remedy was a Chrysler-designed hitch, so many trucks already have one.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · NHTSA
What fluids and capacities does it take?

ExtremeTerrain's ZJ guide: 5W-30 or 10W-30 oil, 50/50 IAT coolant (12 qt six, 14.9 qt V8), ATF+4 in the automatics and transfer cases (1.1–1.5 qt), 80W-90 in the axles with 4 oz of friction modifier for a Trac-Lok, and a 23-gallon tank of 87 octane. The 5.9 needs premium. Oil capacity is disputed: ExtremeTerrain gives 5 qt for the 4.0, while AMSOIL's 1998 lookup gives 6 qt (and 5 qt for both V8s), so fill to the dipstick.

SOURCE · EXTREMETERRAIN · AMSOIL · WIKIPEDIA
Which rear axle do I have?

Check the tag or count the cover bolts; don't guess. Offroaders.com lists the Dana 35 and Chrysler 8.25 in ZJs. Dana 44 rear-axle parts are catalogued for 1996–98 ZJs, and ExtremeTerrain's capacity guide covers both the Dana 35 and 44. The front is a Dana 30.

SOURCE · OFFROADERS.COM · QUADRATEC · EXTREMETERRAIN
Is the 5.9 Limited worth the premium?

As a collectible it's the one to have: one year only, 14,286 built, 245 hp, 6.8 seconds to 60, and Petersen's 4-Wheel & Off-Road named it 4x4 of the Year. On Bring a Trailer, though, 5.9s have sold for about $6,500–25,500 since 2019, roughly the same as nice 5.2 Limiteds. Remember the premium fuel and the axles.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · KEVIN'S OFFROAD · BRING A TRAILER
Are there recalls I should check?

Yes. 13V-252 covers 1993–98 trucks, whose fuel tanks could leak in some rear impacts; the remedy was a rear-structure inspection and a free tow hitch. 02V-053 added a secondary detent so the shifter can't sit just out of Park. There are smaller campaigns too, such as the 1996 Quadra-Trac temporary-spare recall (98V-006).

SOURCE · NHTSA
What are the differences between 1993–95 and 1996–98?

The 1996 facelift brought new bumpers, cladding and a bigger grille, an all-new interior with a passenger airbag, OBD-II diagnostics and a revised NV249. Command-Trac and the manual gearbox were gone, and the 4.0 was rerated from 190 to 185 hp. In IIHS testing, 1996–98 trucks scored Marginal in the moderate-overlap frontal test.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What are they worth?

Not much. Nineteen ZJs sold on Bring a Trailer between October 2025 and October 2026 for between $4,250 and $18,500, with a median of about $9,700. The top result was an 11,000-mile 1998 Limited. In 2017 OffroadXtreme put rough ones at about $1,000 and nice ones at $3,000 and up.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER · OFFROADXTREME
Which years are the best to buy?

There's no real consensus, so we don't flag one. SlashGear picks 1993, 1994 and 1998 and says to avoid 1995–96 for transmission complaints, but NHTSA's complaint database holds a similar 1,200 to 1,560 complaints for every ZJ model year, with 1998 logging the most powertrain reports, and CarComplaints.com shows no ZJ year standing out. Buy on condition instead: frame rust, leaks, a transfer case that works in every mode and the 13V-252 hitch recall done. If you want the collectible, it's the 1998 5.9 Limited.

SOURCE · SLASHGEAR · NHTSA · CARCOMPLAINTS.COM
