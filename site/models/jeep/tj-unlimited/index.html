PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

In April 2004, eighteen years after the CJ-8 Scrambler went away, Jeep brought back a long-wheelbase Wrangler. It stretched the TJ by 10 inches between the axles and about 15 inches overall, and called it the Wrangler Unlimited. Owners call it the LJ. That gave a two-door Wrangler 2 more inches of rear legroom, 13 more inches of cargo space and a tow rating of 3,500 lb instead of 2,000. It sold for $24,995 at launch and lasted barely two and a half model years. Then it became one of the most chased Wranglers ever built.

THE SCRAMBLER'S WHEELBASE, RETURNED

The LJ's 103.4-inch wheelbase is almost exactly the 103.5 inches of the CJ-8 Scrambler it was reviving. The longer frame gets one extra crossmember, and the body grows behind the rear wheels. That stretch is why the rear overhang is the LJ's one real off-road compromise: Jalopnik's David Tracy lists a 27.7-degree departure angle, and the low transfer-case skid makes the belly the next thing to touch.

Mechanically it is a late TJ. The engine is the 4.0-liter AMC-derived straight six, rated at 190 hp and 235 lb-ft. Standard LJs got a Dana 44 rear axle with a 3.73 limited-slip and the Command-Trac NV231 transfer case with a 2.72:1 low range. The standard short-wheelbase TJ made do with a Dana 35 unless you ticked the right option boxes. Automatics used the 42RLE four-speed, and Jeep's 2004½ spec sheet lists only that automatic for the first LJs. For 2005, manual Wranglers moved from the NV3550 five-speed to the six-speed NSG370.

THE RUBICON UNLIMITED

For 2005, Jeep gave the long-wheelbase body the Rubicon drivetrain. The Rubicon Unlimited had Dana 44 axles at both ends with air-actuated lockers, the Rock-Trac NV241 transfer case with a 4:1 low range, 4.10 gears (Jeep's spec sheet says 4.11), diamond-plate rocker guards and LT245/75R16 Goodyear MT/R tires. You could have it with the six-speed manual or the four-speed automatic.

Jeep also built 1,000 numbered 'Sahara Edition' Unlimited Rubicons in 2005 to cash in on the film Sahara. All were Light Khaki, with a chrome grille, khaki tops, graphite Moab wheels, two-tone seats and a numbered dash badge. In Jalopnik's breakdown, the Rubicon LJ is the one that puts the whole formula together: TJ coil springs, a fully boxed frame and a wheelbase in the trail 'sweet spot', plus a factory locker on each axle.

WHY COLLECTORS CHASE THEM

Jalopnik, citing forum and sales data, puts LJ production at about 47,000 over the run, roughly 20 percent of Wrangler sales in those years. That isn't rare by Jeep standards. What made them collectible is that the LJ has no direct replacement. In 2007 the JK Unlimited grew four doors, a 116-inch wheelbase and about 350 more pounds. The LJ is still the only factory Wrangler that combines two doors, a long wheelbase, a coil-sprung solid-axle chassis and the 4.0 straight six.

Hagerty's valuation team says later TJs, 'and especially the Unlimited Rubicon, have been the priciest TJ on the market.' Jalopnik called the LJ the 'holy grail' of Jeeps. Unrusted, unmodified examples now command a clear premium over short-wheelbase TJs, while a clapped-out one is still just a TJ with more frame to rot.

HOW IT ENDED

The TJ family finished with the 2006 model year. Its replacements debuted at the January 2006 Detroit show. The JK two-door kept a short wheelbase, and the JK Unlimited replaced the LJ with four doors and a wheelbase about 13 inches longer again. The long two-door Wrangler was never offered again, and the TJ and LJ were the last Wranglers with the AMC-derived 4.0 straight six.

SIDEBAR · THE FIVE-DOOR COUSIN

Israel built the LJ that Jeep wouldn't

From 2006 the Israeli firm Automotive Industries Ltd. built the M-242 Storm Mark II under licence from Chrysler for the Israel Defense Forces, based on the TJ. Unlike any civilian Wrangler of the time, it had rear passenger doors, which makes it the first five-door Wrangler derivative.

The Storm II stretched the wheelbase to 2,931 mm, almost exactly the 116 inches Jeep would later use for the four-door JK Unlimited. In other words, the military answer to 'what comes after the LJ' arrived before Jeep's own.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

Underneath, the LJ is a TJ with a longer ladder frame (fully boxed, according to the Jeep engineer Jalopnik interviewed) and one extra crossmember. It has coil springs and live axles at both ends, located by four links and a track bar at each end, and recirculating-ball steering. Every LJ got the stronger Dana 44 rear axle. Rubicon Unlimiteds add a Dana 44 front axle, air lockers and a 4:1 transfer case.

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Get under it with a flashlight and a hammerQuadratec calls frame rot near the rear lower control-arm brackets 'the TJ issue'. The other early-rot zones are the transfer-case crossmember area and the central body mounts. A clean outside means nothing. Probe the inside of the rails and the arm brackets, and walk away from anything that's flaking through.
  2. 02
    2005–06: ask about the OPDALate 4.0 engines can wear out the oil pump drive assembly. Owners describe the warning noise as a 'laughing monkey' squeal. Quadratec says that if there's no record of it being replaced or updated, deal with it proactively.
  3. 03
    Is it a real Rubicon Unlimited?Rubicons have a Dana 44 front axle, an NV241 Rock-Trac transfer case, air lockers and 4.10 gears. Check that both lockers engage, that 4-Low is the 4:1 Rock-Trac, and that a Sahara Edition has its numbered dash badge. Rubicon decals are cheap; Dana 44 fronts are not.
  4. 04
    Drive it at 30–50 mph over bumpsOffroadXtreme suggests testing for death wobble in that speed range. A loose lower track-bar bolt, bent steering linkage, worn ball joints or tie-rod ends can all start it. On lifted examples, also check the rear track-bar bracket on the axle for cracks.
  5. 05
    Look for leaks, and price themThe 4.0 commonly seeps from the rear main seal, oil pan, valve cover and oil-filter adapter gasket. Pinion and transfer-case output seals weep too. Mostly this is money rather than doom, but the rear main seal is a big job.
  6. 06
    Check the radiator tanksFactory TJ radiators have plastic end tanks crimped to an aluminum core. Quadratec says many start leaking at the upper seams around 100,000 miles. Look for green crust and a coolant smell.
  7. 07
    Shift into 4-Low, twiceThe factory 'Z-bar' transfer-case linkage pops out of worn plastic bushings, and the symptom is a Jeep that won't go into 4-Low. The bushings are cheap; a missed diagnosis is not.
  8. 08
    Judge the modificationsA lifted LJ needs a longer or adjustable track bar, alignment correction and often a slip-yoke eliminator above 2 inches of lift. Ask who did the work, and inspect welds on frame repairs and brackets.

Spotting the real thing

"LJ" is an unofficial nickname. Jeep sold it as the Wrangler Unlimited, so the VIN, the wheelbase and the axles are how you tell one apart, and how you spot a stretched TJ or a Sport wearing Rubicon parts.

  1. ID
    VINIn BaT-listed VINs we checked, an Unlimited reads 1J4FA44S… and an Unlimited Rubicon 1J4FA64S…, while short-wheelbase trucks have a 9 as the 7th character (1J4FA49S Sport, 1J4FA69S Rubicon). NHTSA's decoder doesn't reliably separate Unlimiteds from short-wheelbase trucks, so confirm with a tape measure: 103.4 in between the axle centers.
  2. ID
    Wheelbase and length103.4 in wheelbase against 93.4 in for a TJ, and about 167 in long to the bumper. The rear doors aren't there, but the long rear quarters and cargo area are hard to miss.
  3. ID
    Rubicon hardwareUnlimited Rubicons have front and rear Dana 44s with lockers, the NV241OR (4:1 low range), diamond-plate rocker guards and 245/75R16 tires. Base Unlimiteds have a Dana 30 front, Dana 44 rear and the NV231 (2.72:1 low).
  4. ID
    Sahara EditionThe 2005 Sahara Edition Unlimited Rubicon is Light Khaki with a numbered dash badge, chrome grille and Sahara spare-tire cover.

Replace the oil pump drive assembly (2005–06 4.0)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · An afternoonSHOP COST · Parts plus labor; ExtremeTerrain rates the install 'difficult' and suggests a professional
SYMPTOMSA squeak or 'laughing monkey' chirp from the back of the engine, sometimes with a check-engine light. Left alone, Quadratec warns, OPDA failure can damage the engine.
THE FIXThe OPDA sits in the block where a distributor would be. It drives the oil pump and carries the camshaft position sensor, so it must go in with the engine at top dead centre on cylinder 1 and be indexed so the cam sensor stays in sync with the crank. Follow the factory service manual's alignment procedure. Remove the cam sensor, unbolt the hold-down and lift the old drive out. Fit the replacement with a new gasket at the same orientation, then check cam/crank sync with a scan tool. ExtremeTerrain says the factory unit's two bronze bushings sit either side of a seal that starves the upper bushing of oil, and the redesigned part moves the seal above the top bushing.
TOOLS & PARTSSocket set · factory service manual for TDC and indexing · scan tool to verify cam/crank sync · new camshaft position sensor (5149034AA for 2005–06 4.0) if the old one is suspect.

Repair rotted rear control-arm frame mounts
DIFFICULTY · 5 / 5TIME · A weekend per sideSHOP COST · Frame sections plus welding; Quadratec calls shop cost 'prohibitive' for an old Jeep
SYMPTOMSFlaking, swollen or holed frame around the rear lower control-arm brackets, the transfer-case crossmember or the central body mounts. Quadratec's correspondent found all three on a 1998 TJ with 190,000 miles.
THE FIXOnly for someone who can cut and weld. Support the frame on stands, jack the axle, then unbolt the upper and lower control arms on one side, the sway-bar link and, if needed, the shock. Mark at least 1 inch of overlap and cut out the rotten section and the old arm mounts, without cutting the body mount and keeping clear of brake and fuel lines. Mock up the new section, grind to bare metal, measure, tack, measure again, then weld it fully. Prime and coat it, and refit the arms with thread locker and correct torque. Re-check after a day, or a mile, and then monthly. One side at a time.

Hunt down death wobble and set toe in the driveway
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoon to diagnose; minutes to set toeSHOP COST · Usually a track-bar bushing, tie-rod end or ball joints, plus an alignment
SYMPTOMSViolent front-end shake after a bump, usually at 45–65 mph, that stops only when you slow right down.
THE FIXStart at the front track bar. With the Jeep on the ground, have a helper rock the steering wheel a quarter turn each way while you watch both track-bar ends and the bracket holes; any side-to-side movement is a fault. Then check the tie-rod ends, drag link, ball joints, control-arm bushings, wheel bearings and tire balance. Quadratec says a steering stabilizer rarely fixes the cause. Replace the worn part and re-torque the front-end hardware. Set toe-in by loosening the tie-rod sleeves and turning the tie rod until the front of the tires is about 1/16 inch (no more than 1/8 inch) closer than the rear. Center the wheel with the drag-link turnbuckle, then get a proper alignment.

DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoonSHOP COST · Radiator plus coolant

Replace a throttle position or crank sensor
DIFFICULTY · 1 / 5TIME · Under an hour (Quadratec's TPS how-to)SHOP COST · One sensor
SYMPTOMSStalling, rough idle or random check-engine lights. Quadratec's example was a TJ throwing P0122 that stalled while driving; automatics rely on the TPS to run properly. Aging crankshaft-position, throttle-position and oxygen sensors are all common failures.
THE FIXFor the TPS: disconnect the negative battery terminal, then unplug the TPS, IAC motor and MAP sensor connectors. Remove the intake tube and unclip the throttle cable (plus the kickdown and cruise cables where fitted). Remove the four throttle-body bolts, take the TPS off with a T25 Torx, fit the new sensor with the supplied hardware, and refit the throttle body, replacing the gasket if it's tired. For a crank sensor, Mopar's 4727451AA is listed for 2003–06 4.0 TJs.
TOOLS & PARTS13 mm (or 1/2 in) socket for the battery · 5/16 in driver · 10 mm socket · T25 Torx · code reader.
WHERE TO BUY PARTSMopar 68404428AA throttle position sensor, 2002–06 TJ · Quadratec ↗Amazon ↗Mopar 4727451AA crankshaft position sensor, 2003–11 TJ 4.0 & JK 3.8 · Quadratec ↗Amazon ↗
Fix a transfer case that won't go into 4-Low
DIFFICULTY · 1 / 5TIME · Under an hourSHOP COST · A few dollars in bushings, or an adjustable linkage
SYMPTOMSThe lever moves but the transfer case won't fully engage 4-Low, or pops out of it. Quadratec says the factory 'Z-bar' linkage pops out of worn plastic bushings.
THE FIXPut the lever in neutral, chock the wheels and crawl under. Pry the linkage rod ends off the shifter and transfer-case arms, replace the worn plastic bushings, and snap the linkage back on. If the Z-bar is bent or the geometry has changed after a lift, fit an adjustable linkage and set it so the lever fully reaches every detent. Then test 4-Hi, N and 4-Lo with the engine running.
TOOLS & PARTSFlat screwdriver or trim tool · pliers · wheel chocks.
WHERE TO BUY PARTSLynx adjustable transfer-case shifter linkage, 1997–06 TJ · Quadratec ↗Amazon ↗TJ transfer-case shifter linkage and brackets · Quadratec ↗Amazon ↗
Replace a front axle U-joint and unit bearing
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · Half a day per sideSHOP COST · U-joint plus hub
SYMPTOMSA squeak that fades as speed rises, a vibration or bind on slow turns as if you were in 4WD, and visible play in the axle-shaft U-joint. Quadratec's correspondent found the hub was worn too once it was apart.
THE FIXJack up the axle and remove the wheel. Remove the two caliper bolts and hang the caliper so it isn't supported by the brake hose, then pull the rotor. Stop the hub from turning and break the 36 mm axle nut loose with a long breaker bar. Remove the three bolts behind the knuckle and tap the hub out. Slide the axle shaft out, remove the U-joint clips and press out the old joint. Press in the new joint, being careful not to disturb the needle bearings, then refit the clips, shaft, hub and brakes. Anti-seize the hub, torque everything to spec, and expect the other side to need the same soon.
TOOLS & PARTS36 mm socket · big breaker bar · 13 mm sockets · press (strongly preferred) · snap-ring pliers · anti-seize · torque wrench.
WHERE TO BUY PARTSCrown Automotive 8126638SP heavy-duty front axle U-joint, YJ/TJ/JK · Quadratec ↗Amazon ↗TJ axle seals and bearings · Quadratec ↗Amazon ↗
Stop the rear main seal leak
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · A long day or moreSHOP COST · Seal kit plus oil-pan gasket; labor-heavy if paid for
SYMPTOMSOil dripping from the joint between engine and transmission and a wet bottom of the bellhousing. Rule out the valve cover and oil-filter adapter gasket first, because oil runs downhill.
THE FIXClean the area and confirm the source. The seal is reached from below, which means dropping the oil pan. OffroadXtreme notes that on a TJ this can mean lowering the front axle to get the pan out. With the pan off, replace the seal following the kit's instructions and the factory manual's torque specs, then refit the pan with a new gasket and refill the oil. Do the oil-filter adapter O-rings while you're under there.
TOOLS & PARTSJack stands and floor jack (to lower the front axle if needed) · torque wrench · RTV per the factory manual · drain pan · factory service manual.

Asked on every forum

Jeep Wrangler Unlimited (LJ) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

The LJ uses the TJ's axles, flares and suspension, so the TJ answer applies: Four Wheel Trends puts the stock-suspension limit at about a 31x10.50, which may still rub at full flex until you add a washer or two to the steering stops. Jeep itself sent the Rubicon Unlimited out on LT245/75R16 (about 31-inch) tires at stock height. For 33s, plan on a lift; Quadratec's buyer's guide pairs 33s with a 3-inch-class suspension lift.

SOURCE · FOUR WHEEL TRENDS · QUADRATEC
What's the difference between an LJ and a TJ?

The LJ has 10 more inches of wheelbase (103.4 in against 93.4 in), is about 15 inches longer overall, and has 2 more inches of rear legroom and 13 more inches of cargo length. It tows 3,500 lb instead of 2,000. Every LJ also got a Dana 44 rear axle (limited-slip on standard models, locking on the Rubicon), which many short TJs lacked.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · JALOPNIK
How many LJs were built?

Jalopnik's tally from forum and sales data puts it at about 47,000 across 2004–2006, roughly 20 percent of Wrangler sales in that period. Driving Line says a little over 46,000, fewer than 13,000 of them Rubicons. The 2005 Sahara Edition Unlimited Rubicon was a numbered run of 1,000.

SOURCE · JALOPNIK · DRIVING LINE · WIKIPEDIA
Why are LJs so expensive now?

There's never been a replacement. It's the only factory Wrangler with two doors, a long wheelbase, coil springs and the 4.0 straight six, and the Rubicon version adds lockers at both ends. Hagerty says late TJs, 'especially the Unlimited Rubicon,' have been the priciest TJs on the market.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · JALOPNIK
Which years and drivetrains should I look for?

2005–06 brought the NSG370 six-speed manual. Those years also need the OPDA checked. The 2005–06 Rubicon Unlimited is the one with front and rear Dana 44s, air lockers and the 4:1 Rock-Trac. A 2004 or non-Rubicon LJ still has the Dana 44 rear and is a better starting point for a build than a Dana 35 TJ.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · QUADRATEC
How much can it tow?

Jeep rated the LJ at 3,500 lb, against 2,000 lb for the short-wheelbase TJ.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · JALOPNIK · ROCKCRAWLER (JEEP SPECS)
What fluids does it take?

Quadratec's factory-based chart gives the 4.0 6 qt of 10W-30 with a filter and 10.5 qt of coolant. The 42RLE takes ATF+4, 4 qt at a service fill. The NSG370 takes 3.17 pints. The NV231 takes 2.2 pints and the NV241 Rock-Trac 4.2 pints of ATF+4. The Dana 30 takes 2.5 pints and a Dana 44 4.0 pints of GL-5 80W-90 or 75W-140 synthetic. Rubicons specify 75W-140 synthetic, and a Trac-Lok rear needs 4 oz of limited-slip additive.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC · EXTREMETERRAIN
What's the OPDA problem?

The oil pump drive assembly on 2005–06 4.0 engines can wear. The warning sign owners describe is a 'laughing monkey' noise, and if it's ignored the engine can be damaged. Replacements with a relocated seal are cheap; the indexing to keep the cam sensor in sync is the fiddly part.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC · EXTREMETERRAIN
What fuel economy and range should I expect?

Jeep's 2005–06 TJ spec sheet originally listed 16/20 mpg (city/highway) for the 4.0 with either transmission, on the short-wheelbase truck. EPA's revised figures on fueleconomy.gov are lower: 13/16 for the automatic and 13/17 for the six-speed. The LJ carries a 19-gallon tank (Jeep's 2004½ spec sheet). Bigger tires, a lift and a roof load cost several mpg.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC SPECS · FUELECONOMY.GOV · ROCKCRAWLER
What about the low belly and the rear overhang?

Jalopnik flags both. The departure angle is 27.7 degrees, and the transfer-case skid hangs low, so a high-clearance or 'tummy tuck' skid is a common first mod. Rough Country's 1126 is listed for 2003–06 TJ and Unlimited.

SOURCE · JALOPNIK · QUADRATEC
Do I need a slip-yoke eliminator if I lift it?

Quadratec says lift-kit makers strongly recommend an SYE and a CV driveshaft above 2 inches of lift, and treat it as required at 4 inches or more, to cure vibration and stop the slip yoke pulling out of the transfer case.

SOURCE · QUADRATEC
