Maintenance schedule · 1976–1986

Jeep CJ-7 maintenance schedule

Treat every fluid and wear item as unknown on the day you buy a CJ, then keep to the factory schedule. The intervals below are from Jeep's 1981 Technical Service Manual (Chapter B), which covers the CJ range. A 1976–80 CJ-7 had its own year's schedule; follow its manual if you have it.

Read the full guide

Service schedule

From the 1981 Jeep Technical Service Manual, Chapter B maintenance schedules.

WHENWHAT TO DO
On the day you buy it
  • Engine oil and filter; air filter
  • Fuel filter; plugs, cap, rotor and wires
  • Coolant, hoses and thermostat
  • Gearbox, transfer case and both axle oils
  • Repack the front wheel bearings
  • Brake and clutch fluid; grease every fitting
Engine oil and filter (258 six)
  • First change at 5,000 mi, then every 7,500 mi or 7.5 months
Fuel filter
  • At 12,500 mi, then every 15,000 mi
Coolant
  • At 12,500 mi or 12.5 months, then at the start of each winter season, with a 50/50 mix
Every 25,000 mi
  • Automatic transmission fluid and filter (every 10,000 mi in heavy-duty use)
Every 30,000 mi
  • Spark plugs, cap, rotor and wires
  • Repack the front wheel bearings (every 15,000 mi in heavy-duty or off-road use)

Severe service: towing, dust, water, short trips

The 1981 schedule halves the front wheel bearing interval to 15,000 mi and cuts the automatic transmission service to 10,000 mi for heavy-duty or off-road use. Repack the bearings after any water crossing deep enough to cover the hubs. Many owners change the oil of an old carbureted six every 3,000–5,000 mi.

Age items the schedule doesn't cover

ITEMWHENWHY
Fuel sender groundWhenever the tank is droppedJeep-CJ.com members trace dead gauges to a missing or poor sender ground.
Clutch bellcrank bushingsAt any sloppy clutchThe plastic bushings wear, and bad motor mounts can break the pivots.
Frame at the steering boxEvery serviceCracks around the steering box mount are a known CJ weak spot.
