Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

Jeep CJ-7 problems to check before you buy

  1. Frame first, everything else secondJeepCJ.com's buyer's guide starts with the frame. Probe the front and rear spring-shackle mounts, where mud blocks the drain holes in the boxed rails, the area behind the rear wheels, and all six body mounts. The guide says to check them with a hammer and screwdriver.
  2. Look for cracks around the steering boxThe guide says the area around the steering box is one of the most common places for a CJ frame to crack, especially on lifted trucks or trucks on big tires. Look for fresh paint, weld beads or splice plates and ask what was done.
  3. Tub, floors and windshield frameCheck the rockers, rear corners, wheel wells, the area under the roll bar and the floor; lift any carpet. The bottom of the windshield frame and its hinges rust, often from a leaking defroster. On 1982–86 wide-track trucks, check the front-fender shock towers, which trap water.
  4. Know which gearbox you're buyingA T-18A (1977–79) or T-176 (1980–86) is what you want. The SR-4, T-4 and T-5 are light-duty Borg-Warner boxes that Novak says suffer from sheared splines and stretched cases behind the 258. Check the casting number on the driver's side: 13-32 or 13-40 for an SR-4, 13-51 for a T-4, 13-52 for a T-5.
  5. Inspect the AMC 20's two-piece shaftsOn a stock CJ rear axle the hub is keyed onto the shaft and held with a big nut. If the nut loosens, the key shears and that wheel stops driving. Look for one-piece axle shafts in the receipts, or budget for them. Read the gear-code stamp on the housing to the left of the cover.
  6. Quadra-Trac trucks need a testOn gravel, try high and (if fitted) low range and listen for chain noise. The JeepCJ.com guide says the chain stretches, possibly to the point of jumping teeth, if the oil level wasn't kept up. Quadra-Trac trucks have drive plates instead of locking hubs, and the vacuum 'Emergency Drive' control locks the center differential; check that it engages.
  7. Listen to the carburetorStumbling and stalling at idle usually means a Carter BBD with clogged idle tubes, and its tangle of emissions vacuum hoses is the other usual suspect. Not a deal-breaker, but price in a clean-up or a Weber or EFI swap.
  8. Electrics and emissions bitsDuct-tape splices, wire nuts, Scotch-locks and foil-wrapped fuses are warning signs, and most CJ electrical faults come down to rusty grounds. If your state inspects 1980s trucks, check that the TAC air cleaner, EGR, PCV and air-pump hardware are still there; the guide notes air-pump pulleys and brackets are hard to find.

Jeep CJ-7 recalls and service bulletins

  • Steering gear ↗ 1976 · NHTSA 76V-034Campaign on the steering gear box of 18,858 1976 CJ-5 and CJ-7s; NHTSA's online record names the component but carries no defect summary.
  • Electronic ignition wire connectors ↗ 1976 (campaign covers 1975–76 Jeeps) · NHTSA 78V-065Connectors could not carry enough current for the electronic ignition control.
  • Transmission adapter-plate seal ↗ 1976–1978 · NHTSA 78V-039A mis-seated seal could let gear oil migrate from the manual transmission into the transfer case; 96,933 CJs covered.
  • Intermediate steering-shaft coupling ↗ 1978 · NHTSA 78V-049Oversized serrations could stop the coupling clamping properly to the steering-gear shaft, causing free play.
  • Tie-rod adjuster clamp bolts ↗ 1979 · NHTSA 78V-243Loose clamp bolts on the connecting-rod adjuster tube could shift the steering-wheel position and risk loss of steering.
  • Transmission-to-transfer-case gasket ↗ 1979 · NHTSA 79V-070Wrong gasket could block lubricant flow and damage the manual transmission.
  • Rear seat-belt buckles missing ↗ 1981 · NHTSA 80V-158Some Jeeps got two tongue-end rear belt halves and no buckle, so the rear belts could not be fastened.
  • Automatic throttle-control rod spring ↗ 1981 · NHTSA 81V-015Improperly installed spring could make the throttle close slowly.
  • Manual transmission first-gear lubrication ↗ 1982 · NHTSA 82V-125Factory-fill gear oil might not protect first gear; covered 1982 CJ-5, CJ-7, Cherokee, Wagoneer and J-trucks.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.
