Jeep CJ-7

“The CJ-7 is the classic Jeep that still makes sense: a longer wheelbase, real doors, AMC's unkillable 258 six and a gear-driven transfer case. It's also a 40-year-old steel tub on a frame that cracks by the steering box, so bring a magnet and a hammer when you go to look at one.”

PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

American Motors bought Kaiser Jeep in 1970 (for $70 million by Wikipedia's account; Jeep's own history says about $75 million), and in 1976 it gave the little CJ-5 a bigger brother. The CJ-7 sat on a 93.5-inch wheelbase, ten inches longer than the CJ-5's, and Jeep says the extra length was there to make room for an automatic transmission. The side openings were squared off to take doors, and for the first time a Jeep CJ could be ordered with a molded plastic hardtop and steel doors. It ran for eleven model years, and Wikipedia counts 379,299 built.

THE AMC RECIPE

Under the tub, the CJ-7 has two parallel C-section frame rails, stepped outward at the rear so the springs and shocks sit closer to the body, with leaf springs at both ends. The front axle is an open-knuckle Dana 30. At the back is AMC's own Model 20, which has a big 8⅞-inch ring gear but thin 29-spline shafts, each made of two pieces: a hub keyed and nutted onto the shaft. Wheels use the old CJ bolt pattern of 5 on 5.5 inches.

The engines came from AMC. Early trucks had the 232 straight six as standard, with the 258 optional until it became the base six for 1979. The 258 is the one owners talk about: cast iron, seven main bearings, hydraulic lifters and enough low-end torque to idle up a hill. Offroaders.com lists it at 110 hp and 195 lb-ft in 1979–81, then 115 hp and 210 lb-ft from 1982 with a computer-controlled Carter BBD two-barrel. The 304 V8 was offered until 1981, choked by smog equipment to 125 hp by the end. From 1980 a GM 151 'Iron Duke' four was standard, rated at 82 hp. JeepCJ.com's buyer's guide tells you to skip it.

GEARBOXES, TRANSFER CASES AND QUADRA-TRAC

Early trucks (1976–79) got a three-speed T-150 or the Borg-Warner T-18. From 1977 the T-18A had a 6.32:1 granny first, and Offroaders.com calls it the best transmission the factory ever put in a CJ. Behind it was a Dana 20 with a feeble 2.03:1 low range. The alternative was Quadra-Trac, a Borg-Warner 13-39 chain-drive full-time system bolted to a TH400 automatic. It had a vacuum-operated 'Emergency Drive' lock and an optional bolt-on low-range unit (2.57:1). Jeep had introduced Quadra-Trac on its full-size trucks in the early 1970s (the 1973 model year, by Wikipedia's dating), years before the Grand Wagoneer name existed.

For 1980 everything changed. The Dana 300 arrived with a 2.62:1 low range, and Novak Conversions calls it the 'gold standard' of transfer cases. It came with lighter Borg-Warner gearboxes: the SR-4 in 1980–81, then the T-4 and five-speed T-5 from 1982. Novak's verdict on those is 'essentially a small car transmission in a truck'. The tougher choice was the Tremec T-176 four-speed (1980–86), and a Chrysler TF999 three-speed automatic replaced the TH400.

WIDE TRACK, GOLDEN EAGLES AND LAREDOS

In 1982 the axles grew. The narrow-track AMC 20 measured 50.5 inches wheel to wheel, and the wide-track version 54.5 inches, which made the CJ-7 noticeably more stable. AMC also understood showroom bait. The Renegade ran the whole production life. The Golden Eagle (on the CJ-7 from 1977 to 1980, tied to the 304 V8 per Wikipedia) added a tachometer, a clock and the big Golden Eagle decals; a white 1980 CJ-7 Golden Eagle named 'Dixie' became famous as Daisy Duke's Jeep on The Dukes of Hazzard. The Laredo (1980–86) brought high-back leather seats, a tilt wheel and a chrome package.

The collectible ones are the Limited, built in 1982–83 (2,500 of them), and the 1982 Jamboree. That was 630 numbered CJ-7s, each with the 4.2-liter six, built for the 30th anniversary of the Rubicon Trail.

LAST OF A GREAT BREED

The CJ's reputation took a hit in December 1980, when 60 Minutes aired an IIHS rollover demonstration of a CJ-5. AMC green-lit a replacement in 1982, and the Wrangler YJ was designed to be more comfortable on the road and harder to flip, with track bars and anti-roll bars. It was unveiled in February 1986 and went on sale on 13 May 1986. The last CJ-7s carried a dash plaque that read 'Last of a Great Breed', and by then AMC 20 supplies had run so low that some 1986 trucks left the factory with wide-track Dana 44 rear axles.

The CJ-7 has outlived its replacement's reputation. AMC was working with Renault by the end, and Chrysler took over Jeep in 1987. The CJ-8 Scrambler, the YJ and the TJ all owe it something. Against its 1970s rivals, the FJ40 and the Series Land Rover, the CJ-7's case in the US was cheap parts, easy modification and a huge aftermarket, and that case still holds: almost every part is still reproduced.

SIDEBAR · THE CJ-7 AMERICA NEVER GOT

Isuzu diesels and Canadian Army Jeeps

From 1980 to 1982 AMC built an export-only CJ-7 diesel in Ohio. It had Isuzu's 2.4-liter C240 four, a T-176 gearbox, a Dana 300 (Wikipedia says some reportedly got a Dana 20) and, typically, narrow-track axles with 4.10 gears.

In 1985 the Canadian Army took delivery of 195 militarized CJ-7s, codified as Equipment Configuration Code 121526. They were a stop-gap between the retirement of the M38A1 and the arrival of the Bombardier Iltis, so the CJ, born of a military Jeep, ended up back in uniform.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The CJ-7 is old-school body-on-frame: a steel tub on six body mounts, leaf springs at all four corners, a part-time gear-driven transfer case (or the full-time, chain-drive Quadra-Trac option until 1979), a Dana 30 front axle with locking hubs, and AMC's Model 20 rear axle. Everything bolts together with hand tools, and almost every part is still made.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Frame first, everything else secondJeepCJ.com's buyer's guide starts with the frame. Probe the front and rear spring-shackle mounts, where mud blocks the drain holes in the boxed rails, the area behind the rear wheels, and all six body mounts. The guide says to check them with a hammer and screwdriver.
  2. 02
    Look for cracks around the steering boxThe guide says the area around the steering box is one of the most common places for a CJ frame to crack, especially on lifted trucks or trucks on big tires. Look for fresh paint, weld beads or splice plates and ask what was done.
  3. 03
    Tub, floors and windshield frameCheck the rockers, rear corners, wheel wells, the area under the roll bar and the floor; lift any carpet. The bottom of the windshield frame and its hinges rust, often from a leaking defroster. On 1982–86 wide-track trucks, check the front-fender shock towers, which trap water.
  4. 04
    Know which gearbox you're buyingA T-18A (1977–79) or T-176 (1980–86) is what you want. The SR-4, T-4 and T-5 are light-duty Borg-Warner boxes that Novak says suffer from sheared splines and stretched cases behind the 258. Check the casting number on the driver's side: 13-32 or 13-40 for an SR-4, 13-51 for a T-4, 13-52 for a T-5.
  5. 05
    Inspect the AMC 20's two-piece shaftsOn a stock CJ rear axle the hub is keyed onto the shaft and held with a big nut. If the nut loosens, the key shears and that wheel stops driving. Look for one-piece axle shafts in the receipts, or budget for them. Read the gear-code stamp on the housing to the left of the cover.
  6. 06
    Quadra-Trac trucks need a testOn gravel, try high and (if fitted) low range and listen for chain noise. The JeepCJ.com guide says the chain stretches, possibly to the point of jumping teeth, if the oil level wasn't kept up. Quadra-Trac trucks have drive plates instead of locking hubs, and the vacuum 'Emergency Drive' control locks the center differential; check that it engages.
  7. 07
    Listen to the carburetorStumbling and stalling at idle usually means a Carter BBD with clogged idle tubes, and its tangle of emissions vacuum hoses is the other usual suspect. Not a deal-breaker, but price in a clean-up or a Weber or EFI swap.
  8. 08
    Electrics and emissions bitsDuct-tape splices, wire nuts, Scotch-locks and foil-wrapped fuses are warning signs, and most CJ electrical faults come down to rusty grounds. If your state inspects 1980s trucks, check that the TAC air cleaner, EGR, PCV and air-pump hardware are still there; the guide notes air-pump pulleys and brackets are hard to find.

Spotting the real thing

CJ-7s have been re-engined, re-axled and re-badged for 40 years, and plenty of 'Renegades' and 'Laredos' are sticker jobs, so trust the VIN, the axle tags and the body measurements over the decals.

  1. ID
    Body and wheelbase93.5 in wheelbase, 55 in windshield, about 81 in from the door hinge to the rear of the tub, and a 'U'-shaped door opening rather than the CJ-5's inverted 'S'. A CJ-8 looks the same up front but has a 103.5 in wheelbase and a longer rear.
  2. ID
    VIN format1976–80 trucks carry a 13-character Jeep VIN; 1981–86 use the 17-character federal format. On 1981–86 VINs the 4th character is the engine (B = 151 Iron Duke, C = 258 six, H = 304 V8, U = AMC 150 four, F = Isuzu diesel), the 5th the transmission/transfer case, characters 6–7 read 87 for a CJ-7, and the 10th is the model year (B = 1981 … G = 1986).
  3. ID
    VIN plate locationsMain riveted tag on the driver's side firewall near the master cylinder; a partial VIN is stamped on the passenger-side frame rail above the rear wheel. Make sure the tag and the frame stamping agree: a mismatch can mean a swapped tub or a salvage rebuild.
  4. ID
    Axles1976–81 narrow-track Dana 30 / AMC 20; 1982–86 wide-track versions; a factory Dana 44 rear only on some late-1986 trucks (and, per Offroaders.com, on some Quadra-Trac trucks as a narrow-track, offset Dana 44). Any other Dana 44 under a CJ-7 is probably an aftermarket swap.
  5. ID
    Special editionsThe 1982 Jamboree has a numbered dash plaque (630 built: 560 Topaz Gold Metallic, 70 Olympic White) and, per Wikipedia, is the only numbered AMC Jeep. The Laredo added highback leather buckets, tilt wheel and chrome bumpers, grille cover and mirrors; Golden Eagles were 304 V8 trucks.

Post-purchase baseline

After you buy it

Assume a 40-year-old CJ-7 has had nothing done unless there are receipts, and remember that three owners ago someone probably swapped the gearbox, carb or axle. Identify what you actually have first (Dana 20, Dana 300 or Quadra-Trac; which transmission; narrow or wide track), because the fluids differ. Then change every fluid, repack the front bearings and grease every zerk.

Repair a cracked frame at the steering box
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A weekend, or a day at a weld shopSHOP COST · Steel and welding wire; several members recommend paying a weld shop
SYMPTOMSSteering that wanders or clunks, a steering box that moves when someone turns the wheel with the engine running, or a visible crack in the driver's frame rail near the box. Lifted trucks and trucks on big tires crack more often.
THE FIXFind the end of the crack and drill a small hole there to stop it spreading. Remove the box, clean the rail to bare metal and weld the crack. Then add splice plates: one owner cut 1/8-inch plate long enough to extend a few inches past the crack on each side. One member's advice is to weld the top and bottom in intermittent beads (about 1 inch of weld, 2 inches of gap) and leave the plate ends unwelded, because welds concentrate stress. Several owners say their DIY MIG repairs failed next to the welds and a weld shop's held. Add a steering-box brace to tie the box to the opposite rail, and replace any rotted frame sections with weld-in repair pieces.

Clean the Carter BBD idle tubes
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hoursSHOP COST · A can of carb cleaner and a pair of venturi gaskets
SYMPTOMSStumbling and sputtering at idle, stalling at stop signs, poor fuel economy, or an idle that someone has turned up to hide the problem. Offroaders.com says the most common cause is clogged idle tubes letting fuel drip from the venturis, so it runs rich.
THE FIXWith the engine idling and the choke open, look down the carb: fuel dripping from the venturi nozzles at idle means the idle tubes are clogged. Engine off. Remove the air cleaner (tag the hoses), the choke-plate screws, the choke-rod clip and screw, then the two screws holding the venturi cluster, and lift it out with its two gaskets. Spray everything with carb cleaner without dislodging the center check ball, clear the idle tubes and nozzles with fine wire, and reassemble with every body screw tight. The article says Jeep once recommended drilling the idle tubes to 0.032 in to stop them clogging again. If it still idles badly, look for split vacuum hoses and intake leaks before blaming the carb.

Service the front wheel bearings and locking hubs
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–3 hours for both sidesSHOP COST · Bearings, races and seals [price not sourced]
SYMPTOMSPlay at the top and bottom of the tire, growling that changes when you turn, grease flung inside the rim, or hubs that won't engage. The stock five-bolt Warn hubs on 1981–86 CJs are weak, and Quadra-Trac trucks have drive plates rather than hubs.
THE FIXRemove the hub, the outer nut, lock washer and inner nut, then pull the rotor and catch the outer bearing. Clean everything, replace bearings and races as pairs, and press the new races in square. Seals come in two sizes (2 1/8 in and 2 5/16 in), so measure the old one first. Pack the bearings fully and tighten the inner nut to 50 ft-lb while turning the rotor to seat them, as the manuals quoted in the Off-Road.com thread say. Then back it off (one owner backs off about 1/6 turn), fit the lock washer and torque the outer locknut to 50 ft-lb and bend the lock-washer lip over it (1981 Jeep factory service manual; the inner nut is set the same way there, 50 ft-lb then back off 1/6 turn), and check end play: the manuals quoted there specify 0.001–0.005 in with a dial indicator. If the six spindle bolts come out, replace the needle bearing inside the spindle too.

Reseal a leaking Dana 300
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–4 hours in the truckSHOP COST · A seal kit and 2 quarts of 80W-90
SYMPTOMSGear oil on the skid plate, wet yokes or a drip from the rear output. The Dana 300 holds only 2 quarts, so a slow leak can starve it.

Asked on every forum

Jeep CJ-7 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

A 31x10.50R15 on the right wheel, on a healthy stock CJ-7. Performance Plus Tire's guide says it needs healthy springs and shallow backspacing (about 3.5 in on a 15x8 for narrow-track trucks), and JeepCJ.com owners agree 31s are the practical limit. One member reported 32x11.50s on a stock CJ with very little rubbing. 33x12.50R15s need a lift; most CJs running 33s have about 4 inches of spring lift, though 2.5 inches can work with the right backspacing and some fender trimming. Every CJ-7 used 15-inch wheels on a 5 x 5.5 in bolt pattern, with factory sizes from H78-15 bias-plies to metric radials up to 235/75R15 (Performance Plus).

SOURCE · PERFORMANCE PLUS · JEEPCJ.COM
Should I keep the AMC 20 or swap it?

The AMC 20 has a bigger ring gear (8⅞ in) than a Dana 44, but thinner 29-spline shafts, smaller tubes and, on CJs, keyed two-piece hub-and-shaft axles that shear. On a mild truck, one-piece shafts and stitch-welded tubes fix most of it. If you're running big tires and a locker, the usual swap is a Dana 44, and some late-1986 CJ-7s came with a wide-track Dana 44 from the factory.

SOURCE · OFFROADERS.COM · JEEPCJ.COM
Which transmission should I look for?

The T-18A (1977–79) with its 6.32:1 granny first is the toughest factory CJ gearbox. From 1980, the Tremec T-176 four-speed (3.52:1 first) is the strong option. The SR-4 (1980–81) and the T-4 and T-5 (1982–86) are light-duty Borg-Warner boxes that Novak calls 'essentially a small car transmission in a truck'.

SOURCE · OFFROADERS.COM · NOVAK
Dana 20 or Dana 300?

The Dana 300 (1980–86) has a 2.62:1 low range against the Dana 20's 2.03:1, and helical low-range gears that make it quieter. Novak says there's probably no real strength advantage, and the Dana 20 is shorter, which helps driveshaft angles. For more crawl, TeraFlex's TeraLow gearset gives a Dana 300 a 4:1 low range; one owner's crawl ratio went from 32.3:1 to 49.8:1.

SOURCE · NOVAK · OFFROADERS.COM
What is Quadra-Trac, and should I keep it?

Quadra-Trac was the optional full-time 4WD in 1976–79 CJ-7s: a chain-driven Borg-Warner 13-39 case paired only with a TH400 automatic. It has a vacuum-locked center differential (the 'Emergency Drive' switch) and an optional bolt-on low range (2.57:1). The chain is known for a short life. Mile Marker sold an overdrive gear-and-chain set that also converts it to part-time, and Offroaders.com names the Dana 18 as a good gear-driven replacement because its outputs line up (it needs a transmission swap too).

SOURCE · OFFROADERS.COM · JEEPCJ.COM
How do I find my axle ratio?

Look for the code stamped on the AMC 20 housing boss to the left of the cover, near the tube. Narrow-track codes differ from wide-track ones: on a wide-track axle, A is 3.54 open, AA is 3.54 Trac-Lok, H is 3.73 open, and C is 4.10 open. Offroaders.com advises confirming with a tape measure or the front axle's Dana tag, because codes conflict between versions. Offroaders.com gives the common Dana 30 ratios as 3.54 for 1976–79 and 2.73 for 1980–86, while Wikipedia pairs the 258 and four with 3.73 and the 304 with 3.55, so check yours rather than assume.

SOURCE · OFFROADERS.COM
What's the difference between narrow track and wide track?

1976–81 CJ-7s use narrow-track axles, 50.5 in wheel to wheel on the AMC 20. From 1982 the wide-track axles measure 54.5 in, which helps stability. Both use 5 x 5.5 in wheels. The 1982–86 Dana 30 also switched to pin-bolted calipers and crimped brake hoses.

SOURCE · OFFROADERS.COM
Which engine is the one to have?

The 258 straight six (1976–78 trucks may have the smaller 232 six, which was standard until 1979). JeepCJ.com's buyer's guide calls it 'a strong, torquey, reliable motor' and says to avoid the GM Iron Duke four (1980–83). The 304 V8 (to 1981) is quicker but was choked by smog controls by the end: 125 hp in 1980–81 against the 258's 110.

SOURCE · JEEPCJ.COM · OFFROADERS.COM
What fluids does it take?

ExtremeTerrain's CJ guide lists 80W-90 gear oil for the Dana 30 (1.25 qt), AMC 20 (2.4 qt; add 4 oz of limited-slip additive for Trac-Lok), Dana 300 (2 qt) and Dana 20 (1.6 qt). Quadra-Trac needs its own transfer case fluid (2.25 qt). The T-176, SR-4, T-150 and T-18 take 80W-90; ExtremeTerrain lists ATF+4 for the T-4 and T-5 and for the automatics, while the 1981 Jeep factory manual calls for Dexron or Dexron II in the 999 and 904 automatics and SAE 85W-90 GL-5 in the axles, the manual gearboxes and the Dana 300. The T-4/T-5 and the TH400 (a GM unit) aren't covered there, so check your manual before filling them [CONFIRM IN MANUAL]. Brakes take DOT 3. The 258 takes about 6 quarts of oil including the filter, per ExtremeTerrain and a 1984 owner's manual quoted on JeepCJ.com, and about 10.5 qt of coolant (ExtremeTerrain).

SOURCE · EXTREMETERRAIN · JEEPCJ.COM
Will Wrangler wheels and parts fit?

Not the wheels. CJ axles use 5 x 5.5 in, and later Jeep Dana 30s use 5 x 4.5 in. Lots of other parts carry over, though: the 258 lived on in the YJ until 1990, and outer Dana 30 parts (knuckle outward) swap between CJ versions, so a drum-brake CJ can be converted to discs.

SOURCE · OFFROADERS.COM
Was there a diesel CJ-7?

Only for export. From 1980 to 1982 AMC built CJ-7s in Ohio with Isuzu's 2.4-liter C240 diesel four, a T-176, a Dana 300 and 4.10 axles. None were sold new in the US.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Which years are the best to buy?

There's no single answer, so we don't flag one. Owners on Off-Road.com split between 1976–79 trucks, for the T-18 four-speed and the 304 V8 option, and 1982-on trucks with the wider-track axles; a 1986 with the factory Dana 44 rear is a good find. Jeep-CJ.com calls the T-18 the best CJ manual and says to avoid the GM Iron Duke four used from 1980 to 1983. The Drive says to watch out for Quadra-Trac. Buy the best frame you can find, then pick the drivetrain.

SOURCE · OFF-ROAD.COM FORUMS · JEEPCJ.COM · THE DRIVE
