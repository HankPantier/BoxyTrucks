PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

In September 1981 Isuzu announced a compact four-wheel-drive wagon called the Rodeo Bighorn. Underneath it was the company's own pickup, sold in America as the Isuzu P'UP and Chevy LUV. Car and Driver reckoned that almost everything under the squarish steel skin, from the chassis to the driveline, was identical or very similar to the four-wheel-drive pickup. On top sat a body drawn with a set square: flat glass, flat sides and a roof you could land a helicopter on. It would be sold under more names than almost any SUV in history, and every one of them was a box.

THE TROOPER II ARRIVES

America got it for 1984 as the Trooper II, a two-door imported as a truck. Isuzu chose to pay the 25 percent duty on light trucks rather than squeeze it into Japan's car import quotas, which is why the rear seat was a $300 option. The 'II' marked that truck version; a passenger-car 'Trooper I' was promised and never came. Car and Driver's May 1984 test car had the 1.95-liter four with 82 hp, a four-speed manual and a 13.2-gallon tank. The magazine called the power modest and the ride about as plush as a 4x4 got, and estimated the base price at well under $9,000.

Isuzu's own 1985 brochure shows how quickly the truck grew. That year brought a four-door body, a 2.3-liter gas four, a 2.2-liter turbo-diesel (not sold in California), a five-speed, auto-locking front hubs, three skid plates and a 21.9-gallon tank. The brochure also boasted that the Trooper II had been named 4-Wheel & Off-Road magazine's 1985 '4x4 of the Year', ahead of rivals including the Jeep Cherokee and Toyota 4Runner.

GROWING UP, 1987–1991

For 1987 the Trooper II got flush rectangular headlights, a bold new grille, a wider track and bigger P235/75R15 tires, and the four-door could now be had as a plusher LS. The brochure quoted 96 hp and 123 lb-ft from the 2.3-liter four, and the same brochure shows a Trooper II racing in the Baja 1000's Class 14 in November 1986. For 1988 the 2.6-liter 4ZE1 with fuel injection and 120 hp arrived, and for 1989 Isuzu added an optional 2.8-liter General Motors V6, the pushrod engine from the Chevrolet S-10, until its own V6 was ready.

Late trucks could also be had with a four-speed automatic: Wikipedia says four-cylinder trucks used an Aisin A340H and V6 trucks a GM 4L30E. The basic hardware never changed much. An independent front end on torsion bars, a live rear axle on leaf springs, part-time four-wheel drive and a two-speed transfer case made it more capable than its skinny tires and narrow track suggested. In 1989 only, Isuzu sent America the short-wheelbase, two-door Trooper RS (see the sidebar).

ONE BOX, MANY BADGES

Outside the US the truck was everywhere, under different names. Australians and New Zealanders bought it as the Holden Jackaroo, named after the word for a young man working on a sheep or cattle station. Indonesia and South America knew it as the Chevrolet Trooper, Venezuela built it locally as the Caribe 442, and in Japan it was the Isuzu Bighorn. From November 1988 Subaru sold a Bighorn of its own, only with Irmscher trim and the 2.8-liter turbo-diesel. Isuzu also offered a limited run of 100 Bighorn Irmscher R models, with Recaro seats and graphics but no extra power.

The strangest relative came from Korea. In November 1988 SsangYong began licensed production of the Trooper under its own bodywork as the Korando Family, first with the Trooper's 2.2-liter diesel and later with Peugeot and Mercedes-Benz diesels. Rival boxes of the era included the first Mitsubishi Pajero and Toyota's 4Runner, while Curbside Classic's owner story compares the Trooper's shape to the Range Rover at less than half the price.

HOW IT ENDED

Isuzu replaced it with a bigger, more luxurious second-generation Trooper in December 1991. The old shape went out on a high in Australia, where a short-wheelbase Holden Jackaroo won the Marathon class of the 1989 Australian Safari in Peter Lockhart's hands, and it lived on for years in Korea as the Korando Family.

Today the first Trooper is a cult truck with modest prices: Bring a Trailer sales from 2024 to 2026 run from $3,500 for a 2.6-powered 1986 project to $14,250 for a 1986 two-door LS five-speed, with a Japanese Bighorn Irmscher at $25,000. Owners love the space and the visibility. They also warn about rusted rear-door hinges, cracked cylinder heads and failed head gaskets on the fours, and wheel studs that strip at the first impact gun. Parts support has thinned, so many owners rely on the Planet Isuzoo community for help.

SIDEBAR · THE SHORT ONE

Trooper RS and the Safari-winning Jackaroo

For 1989 only, Isuzu imported a short-wheelbase two-door to the US as the Trooper RS. Wikipedia lists the wheelbase at about 90 inches, against 104 for the regular truck. Every RS had the fuel-injected 2.6-liter four, 4.77:1 axle gears and 15 x 7-inch 'snowflake' alloys, with a choice of manual or automatic.

Australia had the same short body as the Jackaroo SWB, and one of them gave the box its best sporting moment of the decade: Peter Lockhart won the Marathon class of the 1989 Australian Safari in one.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The first Trooper is a pickup-derived ladder frame with an independent, torsion-bar front end and a leaf-sprung live rear axle. Four-wheel drive is part-time, through a two-speed transfer case and either auto-locking or manual front hubs. Engines started as small Isuzu fours and finished with a GM V6, and the wheels bolt on with six studs on a 139.7 mm circle.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust first, everything else secondCheck the rear cargo doors, their hinges and pins, the floors and the sills. Isuzu recalled 1984–87 trucks (92V093) because salt-belt corrosion in the rear-door hinges could let the doors close in a false latch, and a Curbside Classic reader summed up the rest: once the rear doors started to rust, it was game over.
  2. 02
    Look at every wheel studStripped or snapped wheel studs and galled lug nuts are among the most common owner complaints logged with NHTSA for 1988–91 Troopers, and Curbside Classic's owner broke them too. Make sure all 24 studs are present and the nuts thread on by hand.
  3. 03
    Ask about the head and head gasketNHTSA's complaint files for 1988 and 1989 Troopers include several cracked cylinder heads and repeated head-gasket failures, and a Curbside Classic reader lost his truck to 'the head gasket issue'. Look for a history of overheating, coolant in the oil and a radiator that hasn't been neglected.
  4. 04
    Check that the recall work was done1990–91 trucks were recalled for an oil-cooler hose that could touch the exhaust manifold and melt (90V166), and for automatic transmissions that could spill overfilled fluid onto the exhaust (90V167). On 1987–88 trucks (89V001), a heater bolt could rub through a fuel pipe. NHTSA's 1990–91 complaint files also include several under-hood fires.
  5. 05
    Engage the hubs and the low rangePut it in 4WD high and low on loose ground. Auto-locking hubs should lock and unlock reliably, and the transfer case should shift without a fight. Many owners swap tired auto hubs for manual ones.
  6. 06
    Know which engine you're gettingEarly 1.95s and 2.3s are slow. The injected 2.6-liter four (1988–91) and the 2.8-liter GM V6 (1989–91) are the stronger engines, but NHTSA's 1988–89 complaint files include cracked heads, so the history matters more than the badge. US turbo-diesels from the mid-1980s are rare and were never sold in California.
  7. 07
    Drive the automatic hardA Curbside Classic reader's automatic failed at about 175,000 miles, and NHTSA's 1990–91 complaint files include several slipping, leaking or failed transmissions. A five-speed is the simpler buy.
  8. 08
    Ask for the timing-belt receiptThe Isuzu four-cylinder petrol engines use a timing belt (the 2.3 switched from a chain to a Kevlar belt), so a truck with no record needs one before it goes anywhere remote.

Spotting the real thing

First-generation Troopers were sold under many names and with engines the US never got, so check what you're looking at, especially with right-hand-drive Japanese or Australian imports.

  1. ID

Recalls & service bulletins

Open recalls follow the VIN, not the model. Run yours through NHTSA before you buy, and ask the seller for dealer records showing recall work.

Importing & legality

Every first-generation Bighorn, Jackaroo and export Trooper is now well past 25 years old, so it can be imported to the US under the 25-year exemption from federal safety and emissions standards. That's the route to the 2.8-liter 4JB1 diesels and the Japanese special editions the US never got. Car and Driver reported that the Irmscher R Bighorn sold in 2019 had arrived in the US in 2015.

Federal eligibility doesn't guarantee registration everywhere, and California is the hardest state for imported diesels, so check your state's titling and emissions rules before you buy.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Common repairs

DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoonSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSLug nuts that won't thread on, studs that snap when a tire shop uses an impact gun, or a wheel that loosens. Owners have reported all three to NHTSA.
THE FIXSupport the truck safely and pull the wheel. On the front, remove the caliper and rotor to reach the back of the hub; on the rear, pull the drum. Drive out the damaged stud, clean the hub, then draw the new stud in with a stack of washers and a lug nut, or press it in, until the head seats fully. Fit new lug nuts with any damaged studs, start every nut by hand and torque them to the factory spec in a star pattern. Tell your tire shop to do the same.

Cooling-system refresh to protect the head
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · A morningSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSCreeping temperature, steam, coolant loss or a sweet smell. NHTSA complaint files describe cracked heads and blown head gaskets on 1988–89 trucks.
THE FIXWith the engine cold, drain the radiator and replace the thermostat, the radiator hoses and the cap. Inspect the radiator for blocked or weeping cores and replace it if in doubt. Free up and protect the rear-door hinges
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoonSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSRear doors that sag, bind or don't latch properly. Isuzu's 1992 recall (92V093) covered 1984–87 trucks for exactly this in salt-belt states.
THE FIXSupport each door before you work on it. Soak the hinge pins with penetrating oil, work them free, clean the knuckles and grease them. Treat rust around the hinge mounts and the bottom of the door shells. Then adjust the latch strikers so each door closes positively, with no false latch.
TOOLS & PARTSPenetrating oil · brass drift · grease · rust converter and paint · screwdrivers for striker adjustment.

Asked on every forum

Isuzu Trooper (first generation) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

The factory size from 1987 was P235/75R15, about 29 inches, on 6.0- or 6.5-inch-wide 15-inch wheels. We couldn't verify a first-generation forum consensus for the largest no-lift size, so check the tire-fitment chart and build threads on Planet Isuzoo before you buy, and check the rear-door-mounted spare carrier too. Wheels are 6 x 139.7 mm with a 100 mm center bore and M12 x 1.5 studs.

SOURCE · ISUZU 1987 BROCHURE · THREEPIECE.US
Why is it called the Trooper II?

American Isuzu's product-development manager told Car and Driver in 1984 that 'II' marked the truck version, with the rear seat a $300 option, and 'I' would have been the car version with a standard rear seat. A Trooper I never came. Isuzu imported it as a truck to avoid Japan's car import quotas, and paid the 25 percent truck duty instead.

SOURCE · CAR AND DRIVER
Which engine should I look for?

The 1988–91 2.6-liter 4ZE1 is fuel-injected and makes 120 hp; the optional 1989–91 2.8-liter GM V6 makes 122 hp with more torque. The 1984 1.95-liter made 82 hp and the 1985–87 2.3-liter about 96 hp, so early trucks are slow.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · CAR AND DRIVER · ISUZU BROCHURE
Was there a diesel in the US?

Yes. 1984 trucks could have a 2.2-liter pushrod diesel with 62 hp, and from 1985 Isuzu offered a 2.2-liter turbo-diesel (80 hp in the 1987 brochure), which wasn't sold in California. They're rare now; Bring a Trailer sold a 1986 turbo-diesel five-speed for $8,200 in November 2025.

SOURCE · CAR AND DRIVER · ISUZU BROCHURES · BAT
How does the four-wheel drive work?

It's part-time with a two-speed transfer case on its own lever. From 1985 Isuzu fitted auto-locking front hubs, so once they're engaged you can shift between 2WD and 4WD without stopping. Some trucks had Aisin manual hubs instead. Don't drive in 4WD on dry pavement.

SOURCE · ISUZU BROCHURES · WIKIPEDIA
What fuel economy should I expect?

The 1984 1.95-liter was rated at 21 mpg city and 27 highway, and Car and Driver averaged 21 mpg. Isuzu's 1987 brochure lists 17 city and 20 highway for the 2.3-liter five-speed and claims up to 438 miles per tank of gas.

SOURCE · CAR AND DRIVER · ISUZU 1987 BROCHURE
How much can it carry?

Car and Driver's 1984 two-door had a 1,222 lb payload rating. Isuzu's 1987 brochure lists 1,374 lb (two-door) and 1,319 lb (four-door) for gas trucks, and 79 cubic feet of cargo space with the rear seat removed.

SOURCE · CAR AND DRIVER · ISUZU 1987 BROCHURE
How big is the fuel tank?

The 1984 truck Car and Driver tested had a 13.2-gallon tank. From 1985 Isuzu quoted 21.9 gallons.

SOURCE · CAR AND DRIVER · ISUZU BROCHURES
Is a Holden Jackaroo or Isuzu Bighorn the same truck?

Yes. The Jackaroo (Australia and New Zealand), Bighorn (Japan), Subaru Bighorn, Chevrolet Trooper and Venezuela's Caribe 442 are all the same UBS truck. SsangYong's Korando Family was built under licence with its own bodywork. Imports of Japanese and Australian versions can bring diesel engines the US never got.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What is one worth?

Bring a Trailer sales from late 2024 to mid-2026 ranged from $3,500 (a 2.6-powered 1986) to $14,250 (a 1986 Trooper II LS two-door five-speed), with most clean drivers between $7,000 and $9,500. A 1991 Bighorn Irmscher turbo-diesel sold for $25,000 in August 2025.

SOURCE · BAT
Can I still get parts?

Service parts such as filters, belts, hubs, CV axles and brake parts are still listed for 1988–91 Troopers at RockAuto. Trim and body parts are harder. Owners on Overland Bound say parts support for Isuzu light trucks has gone downhill, and point to Planet Isuzoo for help.
