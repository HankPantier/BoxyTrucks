PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

In December 1991 Isuzu replaced the Trooper with a completely new one, and Japan liked it enough to give the Bighorn a Special Award in the 1992/93 Japan Car of the Year. It was bigger, softer and far more powerful than the tall, narrow original, with a new aluminium V6 that Isuzu said it had built specifically for the truck. Underneath it was still a ladder frame with a live rear axle and a two-speed transfer case. It also stayed a box, just a wider one with rounded edges, and it went on to wear more badges than almost anything else on the road.

THE V6S: 6VD1 AND 6VE1

Isuzu's 1992 US brochure introduced the 3.2-liter V6 with an aluminium block and heads, four valves per cylinder, a variable intake system and distributorless ignition. The Trooper S got a single-cam version with 175 hp and 188 lb-ft, and the LS a twin-cam with 190 hp and 195 lb-ft. Both are 75-degree V6s with belt-driven cams. For 1996 a revised single-cam engine made 190 hp, and that's what the new Acura SLX used.

For 1998 the 6VE1 arrived: a longer-stroke 3.5-liter twin-cam rated at 215 hp and 230 lb-ft at 3,000 rpm, with cast-iron cylinder liners in an aluminium block. MotorTrend thought it smoother and much torquier than the old 3.2. Owners later found the catch. NHTSA's complaint files for 1998–2000 Troopers are full of 3.5s burning a quart every few hundred miles, and several ran dry and seized with no warning light. One long-time owner on Bob Is The Oil Guy blames too few oil-return drains in the piston skirts, which clog and let the rings stick.

AUTO HUBS, SHIFT-ON-THE-FLY, THEN TORQUE ON DEMAND

The chassis was a big step up. Front suspension is independent, with double wishbones and torsion bars, and the rear is a coil-sprung live axle on a multi-link location, with Acura quoting 7.5 inches of wheel travel at each end. Disc brakes were fitted at all four wheels. Buyers could have a five-speed manual or a GM 4L30-E four-speed automatic with 'power' and 'winter' modes, and in 1992 a limited-slip rear differential was standard on the LS. The trucks were rated to tow 5,000 lb, and Isuzu's brochure claimed they could climb a 39-degree slope.

Four-wheel drive stayed part-time at first, with automatic front hubs. Some 1996 trucks gained shift-on-the-fly, and the 1998 facelift added a Borg-Warner Torque On Demand system on S Performance and S Luxury trucks. In '4WD Auto' it uses sensors in the transfer case to send torque to the front axle when the rear slips, which suited snowy commutes. The 1998 update also brought a new grille, bumper, headlamps and front fenders and a body-coloured hard spare-tire cover. The grille changed again for 2000, and 2000–02 automatics got Grade Logic, which downshifts on steep descents.

'NOT ACCEPTABLE'

On August 20, 1996, Consumers Union, the publisher of Consumer Reports, labelled the Trooper and its Acura SLX twin 'Not Acceptable'. It said both had tipped up high on two wheels in emergency maneuvers during its road tests. The October 1996 issue described the 1995–96 Trooper as dangerously prone to tipping during sudden lane changes, while three other SUVs in the test were not. Consumers Union also petitioned NHTSA to open a defect investigation. The damage was immediate: Trooper sales fell by more than half in the first six months of 1997, to 4,313 from 9,290, and SLX sales fell 63.2 percent to 448.

NHTSA ran its own tests and in July 1997 denied the petition, saying it found no evidence that an investigation would lead to a defect finding or recall. Isuzu then sued Consumers Union for $242 million, arguing that the testers had steered harder than any real driver would. Judge Richard Paez ruled that Isuzu's public campaign made it a public figure, which raised the bar for proving defamation. In April 2000 a federal jury in Los Angeles found that seven of 15 disputed statements were false, and one was made with reckless disregard for the truth. It still awarded Isuzu nothing, and both sides claimed victory.

Isuzu later put the cost of the affair at $244 million, and the bad press is often blamed for the SLX's early death. For owners today the lesson is practical rather than legal. A tall, narrow-tracked truck on soft springs leans, and NHTSA's 1995–96 complaint files include owners worried about rollover; one 1996 complaint records the front ride height being lowered at the torsion bars to cure a top-heavy, tippy feel. Keep lifts modest, keep weight off the roof and keep the shocks fresh.

ONE BOX, MANY BADGES

General Motors, Isuzu and Honda turned the UBS into a badge-engineering showcase. Honda sold it in Japan as the Horizon from 1994 to 1999 and in America as the Acura SLX (see the sidebar). In Europe it was the Opel Monterey from 1992 to 1999, and in Britain the Vauxhall Monterey from spring 1994. Hagerty UK says that one was well reviewed, beating a Range Rover and a Mitsubishi Shogun in one 1994 group test, but found little more than 4,000 buyers. Australians bought it as the Holden Jackaroo and the more luxurious Holden Monterey, and Holden Special Vehicles built 79 HSV Jackaroos and 234 Jackaroo SEs. Subaru sold a Lotus-tuned Bighorn until 1994.

Outside North America the engine list was longer. A 2.6-liter four was offered in some markets, and diesels started with the 3.1-liter 4JG2 turbo-diesel (114 PS in export form). From 1998 they moved to the direct-injection 3.0-liter 4JX1 with about 159 PS. What Car? rated the 3.0 turbo-diesel the better buy for towing and off-road work in the UK, and called the Trooper one of the very best in the sticky stuff, though less good on the road.

HOW IT ENDED

Isuzu dropped the Trooper in the US after 2002, replacing it with the smaller Axiom and the GMC Envoy-based Ascender. The last Japanese Bighorn was built in September 2002, when Isuzu stopped making its own passenger vehicles, though the Philippines kept selling a 3.0 diesel Trooper until 2005. The UBS left a good competition record: a Marathon-class win and 12th overall in the 1994 Paris–Dakar, and Australian Safari wins for Bruce Garland's Holden Jackaroos in 1996 and every year from 1999 to 2002.

Today it's a cheap way into a solid-rear-axle, low-range four-door with a huge cargo bay. On Bring a Trailer, second-generation Troopers have sold for $6,000 to $14,250 since late 2024, and an Acura SLX for $11,250. An Overland Bound owner with 203,000 miles on his 1998 summed it up: it averages 16–17 mpg, gets a quart of oil every 700–800 miles, and has needed little else.

SIDEBAR · THE LUXURY TWIN

Acura SLX: Honda's first SUV was an Isuzu

Acura launched the SLX in November 1995 for the 1996 model year. Acura calls it the first SUV from a Japanese luxury brand; it was built by Isuzu and jointly developed with it. Early trucks had the 190 hp 3.2-liter V6 and an automatic only, and the 1998 update brought the 215 hp 3.5, Torque On Demand and the new nose. Caught up in the Consumer Reports rating, it sold just 6,590 units in the US over four model years before the in-house Acura MDX replaced it.

Acura hasn't forgotten it. In December 2019 it showed a 'Super Handling SLX' at the RADwood show in California: a 1997 SLX with an RDX 2.0-liter turbo, a 10-speed automatic and SH-AWD. To make it fit, Acura welded new subframes to the boxed ladder frame and replaced the solid rear axle with independent suspension.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The UBS is a body-on-frame wagon with independent front suspension on torsion bars and a coil-sprung, multi-link live rear axle. It has four-wheel ventilated disc brakes and an Isuzu 75-degree aluminium V6 in the US. Four-wheel drive was part-time with automatic hubs, then shift-on-the-fly, and from 1998 Borg-Warner's Torque On Demand on some trims. Wheels bolt on with six studs on a 139.7 mm circle.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Check the oil on any 3.5 (1998–2002)Pull the dipstick before anything else and ask how much oil it uses. NHTSA's complaint files describe 6VE1s using a quart every 450–1,000 miles and several that ran dry and seized without an oil-light warning. A truck with a documented engine rebuild or replacement can be the better buy.
  2. 02
    Inspect the 4L30-E automaticOne long-time owner on Bob Is The Oil Guy says most failures of these gearboxes come from running low on fluid, and that they're very sensitive to fill level. Look for leaks at the accumulator cover and drain bolt, check the fluid, and make sure every gear engages cleanly hot and cold.
  3. 03
    Test the Torque On Demand system (1998+)A flashing TOD light and shudder usually point to the transfer-case or driveshaft speed sensors, according to Edmunds forum owners. One was quoted about $700 for the pair. Test 4WD Auto, 4H and 4L on loose ground.
  4. 04
    Listen for stalling at stopsMany 1998–2000 owners told NHTSA about stalling when slowing or turning, and about upper and lower intake manifold gaskets replaced more than once. Edmunds owners call the 3.5's intake gasket a common fault.
  5. 05
    Ask for the timing-belt and water-pump receiptBoth V6s have belt-driven cams. Owners on Edmunds plan the belt, water pump and spark plugs around 75,000 miles, and one on Bob Is The Oil Guy says the belt is good for about 100,000 miles. With no records, do it now and confirm the interval in the manual.
  6. 06
    Make sure the 4WD actually engagesBob Is The Oil Guy owners say the front-axle 4WD actuator is known to seize in rust-prone areas. Lock it in, drive on gravel and listen for the front axle pulling.
  7. 07
    Check that the recalls were doneAccelerator cables that can stick on 1992–95 trucks (04V199), cam seal end plugs that can blow out and leak oil on 1992–94 trucks (94V132), loose transfer-case flange nuts on 1998s (98V180) and fuel-line separation in frontal crashes on 2000–01 TOD trucks (00V253, 00V376).
  8. 08
    Look underneath for rustBob Is The Oil Guy owners rate Trooper rust as similar to other Japanese trucks of the era, but rust-belt trucks do die of it. One 1996 owner's NHTSA complaint describes chassis corrosion. Check the frame rails, rear suspension mounts and the rear doors.

Spotting the real thing

The UBS was sold under at least nine badges, and the US got only the V6s, so it pays to know which truck and which drivetrain you're looking at.

  1. ID
    Chassis codesUBS25 = 3.2 L 6VD1 V6, UBS26 = 3.5 L 6VE1 V6, UBS69 = 3.1 L 4JG2 turbo-diesel, UBS73 = 3.0 L 4JX1 turbo-diesel. Japanese model codes add a prefix and suffix, for example E-UBS25GW or KD-UBS69GW.
  2. ID
    3.2 or 3.51992–97 US trucks have the 3.2 (SOHC on the S, DOHC on the 1992–95 LS); 1998–2002 trucks have the 3.5 DOHC. The 1998 facelift, with new lamps and fenders and a body-colored hard spare cover, is the easy visual tell.
  3. ID
    Torque On DemandTOD trucks (1998 on, S Performance and S Luxury at first) have a '4WD Auto' mode and a TOD indicator. Part-time trucks don't.
  4. ID
    Acura SLXSold for the 1996–99 model years, automatic only, with the 3.2 and then (from 1998) the 3.5 and TOD. Mechanically a Trooper with Acura trim.
  5. ID
    Re-badges and importsHolden Jackaroo and Monterey, Opel and Vauxhall Monterey, Honda Horizon, Subaru Bighorn, Chevrolet Trooper and HSV Jackaroo are all UBS. Diesels and right-hand drive mean an import.

Importing & legality

The UBS diesels (the 3.1-liter 4JG2 and, from 1998, the 3.0-liter 4JX1) were never sold in the US, nor were the Holden, Opel, Vauxhall, Honda and Subaru versions. Trucks built at least 25 years ago can be imported under the US 25-year exemption, so in 2026 that covers UBS production from 2001 and earlier, one build month at a time.

Check your state's titling and emissions rules before buying an imported diesel. California is the most difficult.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Fix a flashing TOD light

THE FIXStart simple. Edmunds owners suggest cycling into 4L and back, pulling and refitting the TOD fuse, or disconnecting the battery to reset the computer. If the light comes back, check the wiring and connectors at the front and rear driveshaft speed sensors on the transfer case, and replace a faulty sensor.

Set ride height at the torsion bars, or fit a mild lift
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An hour, plus an alignmentSHOP COST · Free to adjust; a Pedders kit costs more
SYMPTOMSSagging front end, uneven side-to-side height, or room needed for bigger tires.
THE FIXMeasure both sides from hub center to fender lip. Turn the torsion-bar adjuster bolts evenly, a little at a time, and settle the suspension between measurements. Edmunds owners note that adjusting one side changes the other, and that the bars may need re-indexing if you run out of adjustment. Don't crank the bars for lift: a higher, stiffer front end makes this tall truck tippier. For real height, Pedders sells a kit of about 50 mm with new torsion bars, coils and shocks; the Gen 2 has two bar lengths, so check yours. Get an alignment afterwards.

Asked on every forum

Isuzu Trooper (second generation) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

Owners put it at about 33 inches. An Overland Bound owner says you can fit 33s before you need a lift, citing a chart on Planet Isuzoo, and an Expedition Portal member says 285/75R16 fits with no lift. Stock was P245/70R16 on 16 x 7 wheels (Acura SLX spec). The bolt pattern is 6 x 139.7 mm with a 100 mm center bore; one owner runs wheels from a 2014 Toyota 4Runner.

SOURCE · OVERLAND BOUND · EXPEDITION PORTAL · ACURA
Which engine should I buy, the 3.2 or the 3.5?

The 1998–2002 3.5 (215 hp, 230 lb-ft) is much stronger, but its oil consumption is notorious, and owners describe engines running dry and seizing. One long-time owner calls the 3.2 adequate but not exciting, with poor mileage and no real vices. If you buy a 3.5, budget for monitoring it, or for a rebuild that fixes the oil control.

SOURCE · MOTORTREND · NHTSA · BITOG
Why does the 3.5 burn oil?

A Bob Is The Oil Guy owner blames too few oil-return drains in the piston skirts, which clog and let the rings stick. Isuzu told some owners that a quart per 1,000 miles was normal; NHTSA complaints describe far more. Check the level at every fill-up and replace the PCV valve. Planet Isuzoo has threads on rebuild fixes.

SOURCE · BITOG · NHTSA · PLANET ISUZOO
What was the Consumer Reports rollover controversy?

In August 1996 Consumers Union rated the 1995–96 Trooper and Acura SLX 'Not Acceptable', saying they tipped up on two wheels in its avoidance test. NHTSA ran its own tests and refused to open a defect investigation in July 1997. Isuzu sued for $242 million; in April 2000 a jury found some of the magazine's statements false but awarded no damages.

SOURCE · LA TIMES · CNN
How does the 4WD work?

Early trucks are part-time with automatic front hubs: stop briefly, select 4WD, and the hubs lock as you drive off. Some 1996 and later trucks shift on the fly. From 1998 Torque On Demand trucks have a 4WD Auto mode that sends torque to the front axle when the rear slips, plus 4H and 4L.

SOURCE · ISUZU BROCHURE · WIKIPEDIA · MOTORTREND
How much can it tow?

5,000 lb with a braked trailer. That figure appears in Isuzu's 1992 brochure and Acura's 1998 SLX specifications.

SOURCE · ISUZU · ACURA
What fuel economy should I expect?

Acura's factory estimates were 15 mpg city and 18 highway for the 1996 3.2, and 15 city and 19 highway for the 1998 3.5. An Overland Bound owner averages 16–17 mpg in his 1998 3.5.

SOURCE · ACURA · OVERLAND BOUND
Is the Acura SLX the same truck?

Yes: an Isuzu-built Trooper with Acura trim, sold for the 1996–99 model years. It came only with the automatic and the V6s (the 3.2, then the 3.5 from 1998). Acura sold 6,590 of them, so they're rarer than Troopers but mechanically the same.

SOURCE · ACURA · WIKIPEDIA
What about the Holden Jackaroo, Opel Monterey and Bighorn?

All UBS Troopers. They're worth knowing about for parts and for diesels: export trucks offered the 3.1-liter 4JG2 and later the 3.0-liter 4JX1 turbo-diesels, which were never sold in the US.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · HAGERTY UK
Can I lift it?

Yes, modestly. The torsion bars adjust ride height, and Pedders sells a kit of about 50 mm with new torsion bars, coils and shocks for the long-wheelbase Gen 2 (there are two torsion-bar lengths). Given the truck's rollover history, keep it modest and refresh the shocks and sway-bar bushings.

SOURCE · LVB OVERLAND · EDMUNDS
What is one worth?

Bring a Trailer sales since late 2024 run from $6,000 (a 2000 Limited) to $14,250 (a 50,000-mile 2001 LS). Low-mileage trucks top the results, and a 1996 Acura SLX sold for $11,250.

SOURCE · BAT
