PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

For 1981 Dodge restyled its pickups and renamed them Ram, and the Ramcharger came along. The formula stayed the same: take a half-ton 4x4 pickup, shorten it to a 106-inch wheelbase, and put seats where the bed used to be. What changed was the roof. The removable top of the first-generation truck was gone, replaced by a welded steel roof and a one-piece rear liftgate. Then Dodge left the truck alone. It ran for 13 model years, picking up fuel injection, overdrive, rear anti-lock brakes and, at the very end, Magnum V8s.

A RAM WITH THE BED ROOFED OVER

The new Ram trucks went on sale in October 1980 with squarer bodies, wraparound taillights and a Ram's-head hood ornament last used on Dodges in 1954. The Ramcharger shared most of its parts with the pickups, from the engines and axles to the front sheet metal. Plymouth's Trail Duster got the new body too, but only for 1981 before Plymouth dropped it. The rivals were the K5 Blazer and the full-size Bronco, and both still let you take the top off. Hagerty notes that many Mopar fans still hold the fixed roof against the second generation.

Dodge made a virtue of it. Its 1993 brochure said the all-steel roof was 'more resistant to squeaks and rattles than Ford Bronco's bolted-on piece of fiberglass', and claimed more cargo room than the Bronco or Blazer: 86.1 cubic feet behind the rear seat, or 105.7 with it removed. It also pointed out that the Ramcharger was the only full-size SUV you could still buy with two-wheel drive, which had an independent front suspension, or with four-wheel drive and a solid front axle.

SMALL BLOCKS THAT SLOWLY GOT SMARTER

There were no big-blocks in the second generation, only the 318 (5.2-liter) and 360 (5.9-liter) small-block V8s. Through 1987 they had carburetors. Throttle-body injection reached the 318 in 1988 and the 360 in 1989, and Dodge's 1990 brochure rated the TBI 318 at 170 hp. For 1992 the 5.2 became a Magnum, with multi-port injection, new heads and a 'beer keg' intake, and made 230 hp. The 5.9 Magnum followed for 1993 with 230 hp and 325 lb-ft. Dodge called the Ramcharger 'America's most powerful sport utility vehicle'. Hagerty's view is that the Magnum trucks are the ones to have.

Behind the engines, most automatic trucks had the three-speed TorqueFlite, and the most common manual was the NP435 four-speed with its very low first gear. Dodge's 1990 brochure introduced a four-speed overdrive automatic, standard on two-wheel-drive trucks and optional on 4x4s. By 1993 Dodge listed only that automatic for the Ramcharger. Owners say the overdrive units are the weak link if they run hot, and that the tall factory axle ratios, typically 3.21 or 3.55, make them work harder still.

THE AXLE THAT DISCONNECTS ITSELF

Every second-generation 4x4 had part-time four-wheel drive, a Dana 44 front axle and a Chrysler rear, usually the 9.25-inch. From 1981 to 1984 the front axle was a conventional open-knuckle Dana 44 with locking hubs, and the transfer case was the chain-drive NP208. For 1985 Dodge introduced Ram-Trac, which meant shift-on-the-fly with a Center Axle Disconnect (CAD). The hubs are always locked, and a vacuum motor on the axle tube slides a collar to join a two-piece axle shaft when you shift into 4WD. Dodge's brochures said you could shift between 2WD and 4WD High at up to 55 mph.

It works when it's new. After 30-odd years of cracked vacuum lines, sticky shift forks and tired switches, it's often the reason a truck won't go into 4WD. RamCharger Central members call it notorious for failing at the worst moment. They also point out that it's easy to fix, or to lock permanently and pair with manual hubs. The NP208 gave way to the NP241 for 1988. Rear-wheel anti-lock brakes became standard for 1990, the same year a new instrument cluster with an electronic speedometer arrived.

HOW IT ENDED

Trim names changed often. Hagerty lists a Macho 4x4 for 1981, there were Royal SE and Prospector packages in the early and mid-1980s, LE trucks from the late 1980s, and a cheap '150 S' in 1990. For 1993 the line ran S, S-Plus, Base, LE and Canyon Sport. American production ended with the 1993 models, and the 1994 Ram's new body never got a US-market Ramcharger. Mexico kept building the old Ramcharger until 1996. From 1999 to 2001 it built a two-wheel-drive Ramcharger on the newer Ram platform, sold only in Mexico. In the US, Dodge's next SUV was the Durango.

Today the second generation is the cheap way into a classic full-size American 4x4. Hagerty found Ramchargers harder to find than Broncos or Blazers, because they sold in smaller numbers when new. On Bring a Trailer in the year to October 2026, second-generation trucks sold for $6,250 to $28,000. The parts are shared with a decade of Dodge pickups, the axles are Dana and Chrysler units that every 4x4 shop recognizes, and the 9.25 rear and Dana 44 front take regearing and lockers like any other.

SIDEBAR · CANYON SPORT

The loaded last hurrah

The Canyon Sport was the Ramcharger's final dress-up package, and it's the one collectors look for. Dodge's 1993 brochure lists a Magnum V8, an automatic transmission, a heavy-duty transfer case, outlined-white-letter tires and a 600-amp battery as standard on the 4x4. It also had a body-color grille, Light Sand Metallic lower paint and bumpers, cast aluminum wheels and an upper tape stripe. Upper colors were Black, Bright White, Flame Red and Emerald Green.

Bring a Trailer's listing for a 1993 example calls it one of about 200 Canyon Sports built that year. That truck had the 5.9 Magnum, a four-speed automatic, a limited-slip rear, fuel-tank and transfer-case skid plates and an auxiliary transmission cooler. Canyon Sports were also sold for 1992, when one BaT truck had the 360 TBI and a four-speed automatic. The package is mostly paint, tape and trim, so check the VIN, the engine and the lower body color before you pay a premium for one.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

Underneath, the second-generation Ramcharger is a short-wheelbase Dodge W150 pickup. It has a ladder frame, leaf springs at both ends, a Dana 44 front axle, a Chrysler rear and a chain-drive part-time transfer case. Two-wheel-drive trucks got the pickup's independent front suspension. Almost every mechanical part is shared with 1981–93 Dodge half-tons, but the front axle, transfer case and fuel system changed over the years, so identify yours before you order parts.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust in the rockers, floors and body mountsOn RamCharger Central, owners list the rockers (especially just ahead of the rear wheels), the front floors, the wheel lips, the rear quarters, the drip rail and the body-mount crossmembers. One member says flaky or feathered metal there is always worse once you strip it, and another says that if the very rear body crossmember is good, everything else is fixable. Check the frame around brackets and crossmembers too.
  2. 02
    Make it go into 4WDOn 1985–93 trucks, shift into 4WD High on the test drive and make sure the front axle actually drives. The CAD system depends on engine vacuum, a switch on the transfer case and a vacuum motor on the axle, and trucks that haven't used 4WD for years often have a sticky collar. If someone has fitted manual hubs, ask whether the CAD was locked, because hubs alone won't fix it.
  3. 03
    Steering slopOwners say the coupler where the steering shaft meets the steering box is known for wearing loose. A rebuild only returns it to original condition, so most fit an aftermarket shaft with needle-bearing U-joints. Have someone rock the wheel while you watch the coupler, the drag link and the tie-rod ends. Loose ball joints and worn U-joints turn up regularly too.
  4. 04
    The 9.25 clunkMany second-generation trucks clunk when shifted into Drive or Reverse. One member's diagnosis is that the pinion has lost preload and worn its bearings. Pull on the pinion yoke with the driveshaft off: it shouldn't move in and out. Worn spider gears, U-joints, slip-yoke splines and slack ring-and-pinion backlash all add to it, so a clunk can be a cheap fix or a full rear rebuild.
  5. 05
    Liftgate cracks and hatch fitRamCharger Central members list cracks in the fiberglass liftgate around the hinges as a common problem, alongside sagging door hinges. Open and close the hatch, check the glass and seals, and look for water in the cargo area. On 1990 and later trucks with the electric rear defroster, check that it works.
  6. 06
    Overdrive automatic healthFour-speed overdrive automatics arrived around 1990, and owners say the overdrive is the weak point if it overheats under load or with big tires. Drive it hard enough to get a full set of shifts and a kickdown, check that the fluid isn't dark or burnt, and look for an auxiliary cooler. Trucks with the three-speed TorqueFlite or the NP435 four-speed have less to worry about.
  7. 07
    Engine and fuel systemOn carbureted mid-1980s trucks, find out whether the Lean Burn / Spark Control Computer (behind the battery in the inner fender) is still fitted. Owners say it will eventually give trouble and is often converted to plain electronic ignition. TBI trucks need working sensors and a service manual for diagnosis. Magnums are prone to vacuum leaks from blown valley-pan gaskets, and owners report cracked heads in some years.
  8. 08
    Vents, vacuum and wiringIf the vents are stuck on defrost or won't change direction, the HVAC vacuum harness is usually to blame. One owner warns that the air box under the dash can fill with leaves and become a fire risk. Check the wiring and the fusible links on the driver's inner fender, and make sure every gauge works. Power window motors and locks are frequent failures.

Spotting the real thing

After 30 to 45 years, plenty of Ramchargers have had engine, axle and transfer case swaps, and special-edition paint and badges are easy to copy. These checks help you work out what a truck started out as.

  1. ID
    VINThe 10th character is the model year: D for 1983, F for 1985, H for 1987, J for 1988, K for 1989, N for 1992 and P for 1993, as on the trucks sold on BaT. The first character shows where it was built. Warren Truck in Michigan built Ramchargers through 1985, and the 1983 and 1985 trucks we checked start 1B4, while the late-1980s and 1990s trucks start 3B4, the Mexican-assembly prefix.
  2. ID
    Year by the grilleEgg-crate grille through 1985, the crosshair grille with rectangular headlamps for 1986–90, and a revised grille for 1991–93. Panels interchange across many years, so check the grille against the VIN.
  3. ID
    Front axleA 1981–84 4x4 has a conventional open-knuckle Dana 44 with locking hubs. From 1985 the CAD axle has a hump on the long side of the axle tube (the driver's side on these trucks) with a vacuum motor, two vacuum lines and a 4WD indicator switch. If a 1985–93 truck has manual hubs, the CAD may have been locked or converted.
  4. ID
    Transfer caseTorque King lists the NP208 for 1980–87 Ramchargers and the NP241D for 1988–93. Both are aluminum chain-drive part-time cases. On CAD trucks, a vacuum switch threads into the top of the case above the shift lever.
  5. ID
    Engine1981–87 trucks have carburetors. 1988–91 TBI engines have a twin-injector throttle body on a cast-iron intake. Magnum engines (5.2 from 1992, 5.9 for 1993) have the tall 'beer keg' intake with a fuel rail and eight injectors. The 5.9 Magnum was only offered in a Ramcharger for 1993.
  6. ID
    Canyon SportDodge's 1993 brochure lists a body-color grille, Light Sand Metallic lower paint and bumpers, an upper tape stripe and cast aluminum wheels. The upper colors were Black, Bright White, Flame Red or Emerald Green. A Magnum V8, automatic transmission, heavy-duty transfer case and outlined-white-letter tires were standard on the 4x4. Paint and tape are easy to copy, so check the build details.

Check what you have, and buy the factory service manual for your year, because most capacities aren't published anywhere else.

Diagnose and fix CAD 4WD engagement (1985–93) →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–3 hoursSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSThe transfer case shifts into 4WD and the 4WD light may come on, but the front wheels don't drive. Or the axle grinds, won't disengage, or works only sometimes. Cold weather can make it worse when water freezes in the vacuum lines.
THE FIXRamCharger Central's KThaxton gives the quickest first test. With the engine running, pull both vacuum lines off the CAD motor on the axle and note which one has vacuum in 2WD. Then shift into 4WD: the vacuum should switch to the other line. If it doesn't, the vacuum switch on the transfer case is bad. If it does, look for leaks in the lines and the vacuum reservoir, then pull the motor and check that the fork and collar slide freely. Torque King's list of usual causes adds a leaking or bent vacuum motor, worn fork pads, worn shift collar splines and swollen rubber connectors at the switch.

Lock the CAD and fit manual locking hubs →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–4 hoursSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSYou're tired of chasing vacuum faults, or you want 4WD you can trust off-road. The factory hubs are always engaged, so the front axle can't be disconnected at the wheels.
THE FIXRamCharger Central owners describe this method. Remove the CAD vacuum motor and move the fork and shift collar to the engaged position, so the two-piece shaft is locked as if in 4WD. Refit the E-clips so the fork stays there, then put the motor back on to seal the housing. Replace the drive flanges at the wheels with manual locking hubs. In 2WD you leave the hubs unlocked. To use 4WD, lock the hubs and shift the transfer case. Without hubs, a locked CAD means both front shafts spin all the time.

Replace the steering shaft coupler →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hoursSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSPlay at the steering wheel that you can see at the coupler where the intermediate shaft meets the steering box. The truck wanders and needs constant correction on the highway.
THE FIXOwners say rebuilding the factory pot-joint coupler only returns it to original condition, which wasn't good to begin with. The usual fix is a Borgeson shaft with needle-bearing U-joints. Geno's Garage says the factory coupler on '79–'93 Dodge trucks can wear in as little as 4,500 miles with towing, plowing or big tires. Rebuild a clunking 9.25 rear axle →
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A daySHOP COST · Parts plus labor (gear setup is specialist work)
SYMPTOMSA heavy clunk when shifting into Drive or Reverse, or on and off the throttle. The pinion yoke moves in and out, or the gear oil is full of metal.
THE FIXFirst rule out the easy causes. Check the U-joints, and grease the rear driveshaft slip-yoke splines, which owners say are often dry. With the driveshaft off, pull and push the pinion yoke. RamCharger Central's Elwenil says in-and-out movement usually means the pinion has lost preload and worn its bearings. The proper fix is new pinion bearings, a new crush sleeve and pinion nut, then resetting preload and backlash. On Sure-Grip axles, inspect the clutch packs while you're in there.

Asked on every forum

Dodge Ramcharger (1981–1993) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

About a 32 or a narrow 33. RamCharger Central's Elwenil says 32s fit most Ramchargers with a little rubbing or minor trimming, but the front springs tend to sag, so measure your truck. 33x10.50s on 8-inch-wide wheels are the usual safe choice. Owners disagree about 33x12.50s: one says they rub at full lock, while another ran them on every unlifted Ramcharger he owned. A member survey found 8-inch wheels work and 10-inch wheels rub. 31x10.50s clear with room to flex, and expect 33s to feel slow on stock gears.

SOURCE · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL
Which years should I look for?

It depends on what you want. One long-time RamCharger Central member picks 1981–84 for the conventional, non-CAD Dana 44 front axle and 1992–93 for the Magnum engines. Others recommend 1988–93 for fuel injection, which also runs better at steep angles off-road. For the most power and equipment, owners point to a 1993 Canyon Sport with the 5.9 Magnum. Hagerty also rates the late Magnum trucks highest.

SOURCE · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL · HAGERTY
What is the CAD axle, and should I get rid of it?

CAD stands for Center Axle Disconnect, which Dodge sold as Ram-Trac from 1985. The hubs are always locked, and a vacuum motor on the axle tube slides a collar to connect a two-piece axle shaft when you shift into 4WD. When it fails, the cause is usually a vacuum leak, a bad switch on the transfer case or a worn fork. Fixing it keeps shift-on-the-fly. Owners who want certainty lock the CAD permanently and fit manual hubs.

SOURCE · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL · TORQUE KING
What fluid goes in the transfer case?

ATF. Both the NP208 (1981–87) and the NP241 (1988–93) are chain-drive cases, and owners say to use what the manual calls for: red ATF, Dexron or ATF+4. One owner quoting the shop manual gives 6 pints (3 L) for the NP208. Don't use 90-weight gear oil in an NP241, because it has an oil pump designed for ATF.

SOURCE · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL
How much can it tow?

It depends on the year, engine and axle ratio. Dodge's 1990 brochure says a 4x2 with the Trailer Towing Prep Package and the 5.9 could tow up to 7,600 lb. For 1993, Dodge rated the 4x4 at 6,700 lb with the 5.2 Magnum and 3.55 gears, 7,100 lb with the 5.9 and 3.55s, and 7,500 lb with 3.90 gears, with an 800-lb maximum tongue weight. Payload for the 1993 4x4 was 1,420–1,430 lb, or 1,785 lb with the 6,400-lb GVW snowplow package.

SOURCE · DODGE 1990 & 1993 BROCHURES
What fuel economy should I expect?

Not good. Owners on RamCharger Central mostly report 10–12 mpg, with one saying he gets 16.5 mpg at a steady 55 mph and 10–12 mpg driving hard. Big tires, tall stock gears and highway speeds all make it worse. The upside is a 34-gallon tank, according to Dodge's 1990 and 1993 brochures.

SOURCE · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL · DODGE BROCHURES
318 or 360?

The 360. Owners say it has more seat-of-the-pants pull, though on paper the TBI 360 only made about 20 hp more than the TBI 318. By 1993 the 5.9 Magnum was rated at 230 hp and 325 lb-ft. If you want to modify it, the Magnums have far more aftermarket support, and owners say the TBI system offers little beyond a few mild cams.

SOURCE · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL · DODGE 1993 BROCHURE
Do I need to regear for bigger tires?

Almost certainly. RamCharger Central members say most trucks came with tall gears, often 3.21:1, and Dodge's 1990 brochure lists 3.21 as standard on the 4x4 with 3.55 optional. For 33s, owners want 4.10s. For 35s, they suggest 4.10 or 4.56, and say 4.88 is too much. Overdrive automatics suffer most with big tires on stock gears.

SOURCE · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL · DODGE 1990 BROCHURE
Is a body lift a good idea?

It's possible, but it's more work than on some trucks. Owners list shift-linkage changes, a new steering shaft, fan shroud and radiator-hose work, filler-neck adjustments, unclipping wiring and watching the brake lines at the master cylinder. One owner warns that the rear body bolts over the axle are studs that may be too short and can break when you remove them. Many owners prefer a suspension lift.

SOURCE · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL
Can I take the roof off?

No. The 1981–93 trucks have a welded, non-removable steel roof and a one-piece liftgate. If you want a removable top, you need a 1974–80 truck. RamCharger Central members say 1980 was the only year with both a removable top and part-time 4WD.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL
Is the overdrive automatic reliable?

It's the weak point if it gets hot. Owners say the four-speed overdrive automatics are prone to overdrive failures, especially under load or with oversized tires. They recommend an auxiliary transmission cooler, regearing for bigger tires and a thorough road test before you buy. Dodge offered an auxiliary transmission cooler as an option on 1993 trucks, and the Canyon Sport sold on BaT had one.

SOURCE · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL · DODGE 1993 BROCHURE
