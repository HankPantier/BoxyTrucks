PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

By 1974 Ford had been selling the Bronco since 1966 and Chevrolet the K5 Blazer since 1969, and Dodge had nothing to sell against them. Its answer was the Ramcharger, a Dodge pickup with the bed cut off and the wheelbase shortened by 9 inches. It arrived for the 1974 model year, and Plymouth dealers got a twin called the Trail Duster. It was late, but it came with something rare in 1974: full-time four-wheel drive as standard. You could also order a 440 big-block, and the roof was optional.

THE RHINO

During development the truck was code-named 'Rhino', and the finished product lived up to the name. It used a 106-inch version of the Dodge pickup's AD platform and was assembled at Warren Truck in Michigan. Dodge sold it as a basic utility vehicle. Early trucks had only a driver's seat as standard, and Wikipedia says the passenger seat stayed on the options list until 1976. The roof was optional too. You could have no top, a dealer-fitted fabric top, or a removable steel hardtop with a flip-up rear window.

The very first trucks were stranger still. Until the June 10, 1974 build date, the door pillars were part of the removable roof, so the 'half doors' had no frame above the glass. Later trucks got normal pickup doors. The name had history too. AutoGuide notes that 'Ramchargers' had been a club of Chrysler employees who went drag racing in the 1950s, and the badge had stayed on factory-backed racing efforts ever since. A 1974 SUV with a roof that unbolted and a cooler built into the console was a fitting way to use it.

FULL-TIME, ALL THE TIME

The trick was the New Process NP203 transfer case. Dodge's 1978 brochure describes five lever positions. Low Loc locks the center differential and gives a 2.0:1 low range for snow, mud and sand. Low keeps the center differential working. Neutral is for running a power take-off. High is for everyday driving, and High Loc is for extra traction. There were no front hubs to lock, because the Spicer 44 front axle was a full-time design with nothing to disengage. Consumer Guide notes that most 4x4s of the period had part-time systems, which couldn't be used on dry pavement.

The rest of the running gear was heavy-duty Dodge truck: leaf springs at both ends, a Chrysler 9¼-inch rear axle, power front disc brakes and a 36,000 psi steel frame. The 225 slant six was available, along with the 318 and 360 small-blocks and the 400 and 440 big-blocks. The 1977 brochure still lists the 440 for the four-wheel-drive AW100. A Ramcharger even won the 1975 Sno*Drift rally.

FROM FLEET SPECIAL TO MACHO

Dodge added a two-wheel-drive AD100 and the slant six for 1975, according to Ramcharger Central's year-by-year notes. From there the truck grew more comfortable every year. The SE package brought cloth-and-vinyl buckets, woodgrain trim, a lockable console with a removable beverage chest and extra gauges. By 1978 you could order a three-passenger front bench, a Sky Lite sunroof, a CB radio, a factory Sno-Commander snowplow package, and towing packages for trailers of up to 6,000 lb.

For 1978 Dodge also leaned into the van-and-decals spirit of the age. The Ramcharger Four by Four came with the Macho package: bold body and tailgate graphics, spoke or chrome wheels, 10-15LT tires and power steering. Top Hand trucks with their own graphics turned up the same year. For 1979 the truck got a new hood, grille and headlights, with four rectangular lamps on the upper trims. The big-blocks were gone, leaving the 318 as standard and the 360 as the option on the 4x4.

THE LAST OPEN-AIR DODGE

In 1980 Dodge dropped full-time four-wheel drive and switched to a part-time system with the chain-drive NP208. It was the generation's last year. The 1981 second-generation Ramcharger followed the redesigned Ram pickup and got a welded-on steel roof and a fiberglass liftgate. That ended the open-air era, and the Trail Duster was dropped after 1981. Rivals kept going in their own directions. The square-body K5 kept a removable rear top, and the full-size Bronco, new for 1978, became the Ramcharger's most direct rival.

Today the first generation is the Ramcharger collectors want. AutoGuide says the earliest and latest Ramchargers generally bring the most money. On Bring a Trailer, Trail Dusters have recently sold for more than equivalent Dodges. Owners on Ramcharger Central still argue about the NP203. Torque King, which sells the parts, calls it troublesome. One long-time member's verdict is 'Just leave it alone! … The 203 is an awesome transfer case.' Keep the front hubs greased, and he may well be right.

SIDEBAR · THE PLYMOUTH VERSION

Trail Duster: Plymouth's only SUV

Plymouth's version arrived alongside the Dodge for 1974 and was sold until 1981. It was Plymouth's only SUV, and Motor1 says it was basically a Ramcharger with a different badge. DrivingLine calls it the much rarer twin. OldCarBrochures holds separate Trail Duster brochures for 1976, 1977 and 1978. The top trim was the Trail Duster Sport, and Motor1 notes its faux-wood trim.

Today the badge matters at auction. Of the 16 first-generation trucks that sold on Bring a Trailer between December 2021 and August 2026, the seven Dodges brought $15,110–26,500. The nine Trail Dusters brought $17,945–57,500, and seven of them sold for $29,000 or more.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

Underneath, the Ramcharger is a short-wheelbase Dodge W100 half-ton: a ladder frame with leaf springs at all four corners, a Spicer 44 front axle and a Chrysler 9¼-inch rear. From 1974 to 1979 every 4x4 had full-time four-wheel drive through the gear-and-chain NP203. The 1980 trucks went part-time with the NP208. Behind the doors you get an optional steel hardtop or nothing at all.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust: wheel wells, floors and the radiator supportRamcharger Central members list the rear wheel wells, lower sills and fenders, the bottom of the radiator support, the floors (especially the driver's side and the rear floor), the rain gutters and the windshield surround. Also look under the battery and at the bottoms of the doors. One desert truck still needed metal in the core support. Bring a magnet.
  2. 02
    Find out which 4WD system it really hasFrom 1974 to 1979 the truck should have a full-time NP203 and a front axle with no hubs. If you see locking hubs, a part-time conversion kit or a different transfer case, ask what was changed. Ramcharger Central's SuperBurban warns that the NP203's park pawl only holds when the front axle is engaged or the case is in Loc. A converted truck with unlocked hubs can roll away in Park.
  3. 03
    Shake the front wheelsOn a forum thread about a full-time 1978 Dodge, one experienced owner calls the full-time front hub bearings 'the weak spot in these trucks from day one'. They were sealed for life until a grease fitting was added in 1976, and few owners ever greased them. Jack up each front wheel and rock it top to bottom. You want zero play. Wide tires on big-offset wheels wear these bearings faster.
  4. 04
    Steering box mount and wanderRamcharger Central members say the stock steering box mounting plate is prone to cracking. Dodge made the plate thicker in 1988, and members recommend the 1977–93 box design as an upgrade for earlier trucks. Check for cracks around the box and the frame rail, and for wandering steering. NHTSA's 1978 recalls covered the intermediate shaft's pot coupling and a misindexed lower shaft, so ask whether the recall work was done.
  5. 05
    Is the engine the one it was born with?These trucks get re-engined constantly. Bring a Trailer has sold first-gen trucks with 340, 383 and 408 V8s, a Cummins 6BT and even a 1974 Trail Duster body on a 1992 Ramcharger chassis. A window sticker or build sheet is gold. The 440 was 4x4-only, the six wasn't sold in California, and the big-blocks were gone after 1978.
  6. 06
    Big-block heatA 440 in a short truck runs hot. The seller of a 1976 Ramcharger on BaT reported overheating at idle in hot weather. A Ramcharger Central member with a 440 trail truck fought constant overheating and low oil pressure at high rpm until he pulled the engine. Look for an upgraded radiator, a working fan shroud and a temperature gauge that stays put in traffic.
  7. 07
    The roof, the doors and their sealsMake sure a removable hardtop is actually there and fits. A truck built before June 10, 1974 with half doors needs its matching roof, because the door pillars are part of it. Hardtop rear-glass weatherstrip is a known chore. Ramcharger Central members use the factory rope trick or work it in with stiff plastic.
  8. 08
    Odometers and titlesFive-digit odometers roll over, and many BaT listings describe true mileage as unknown. Read the title too. Listings have shown Classic and 'Exceeds Limits' brands, and liens. Service records and original paperwork are worth real money in this market.

Spotting the real thing

Seven model years and two badges, and many trucks have since been re-engined or converted. The details below help you work out what a truck actually is.

  1. ID
    First gen vs second gen1974–80 trucks have a removable steel top, or none at all, on the 1972-style Dodge truck body. From 1981 the roof is welded on, the liftgate is fiberglass, and the sheet metal and dash are new (Ramcharger Central).
  2. ID
    Year by nose1977–78 grilles have rectangular parking lamps. For 1979 the hood, grille and headlights are all new, with quad rectangular lamps on the upper trims and two round lamps on the lower ones. The 1980 grille openings are painted black (Ramcharger Central). Grilles swap easily, so check them against the title and the VIN.
  3. ID
    Half doors (early 1974)Trucks built before June 10, 1974 have doors with no frame above the glass. The window frames belong to the removable roof. Later trucks use normal pickup doors (Wikipedia).
  4. ID
    Full-time hardwareA stock 1974–79 4x4 has the NP203 with five lever positions (Low Loc, Low, Neutral, High, High Loc) and a front axle with no locking hubs. Locking hubs, drive flanges or a different transfer case mean the drivetrain has been changed.
  5. ID
    Chassis numbersOn BaT-listed trucks, 4x4 Dodges begin A10 and Trail Dusters AA0. The sixth character is the model-year digit. The fifth character matches the engine on those trucks: E on a 1974 318, F on 1977–78 360s and D on 1976–77 440s. Confirm with a proper decoder or the build paperwork before you trust it.
  6. ID
    Trims and packagesSE (cloth-and-vinyl buckets, woodgrain trim and a lockable console with a removable beverage chest; from 1975 the SE dash adds volt and vacuum gauges). The 1978 Four by Four had the Macho package, with graphics, spoke or chrome wheels, 10-15LT tires and power steering. Also Top Hand, the Sno-Commander factory plow package (4x4 only) and, at Plymouth, the Trail Duster Sport.

Recalls & service bulletins

Open recalls follow the VIN, not the model. Run yours through NHTSA before you buy, and ask the seller for dealer records showing recall work.

Check a VIN at NHTSA ↗
Notable recalls
Steering column misassembled, steering wheel indexed 180° off ↗1978 · NHTSA 77V176000The master serration could be 180 degrees out, and tie-rod adjustments made to compensate could leave too little thread engagement. Dealers replaced the lower steering shaft and checked the tie rods.
Intermediate steering shaft pot coupling may not tighten ↗1978 · NHTSA 78V038000A mismatched coupling with an oversized serration might not clamp properly onto the gear or shaft splines. Dealers inspected it and replaced the coupling if necessary.
Rear step bumper may not protect the fuel tank in a rear impact ↗1980 · NHTSA 84V115000Trucks didn't meet the FMVSS 301 fuel-system integrity impact test, so fuel could leak in a rear collision. The remedy was a free fuel-tank skid plate.

Grease the full-time front hub bearings →
DIFFICULTY · 1 / 5TIME · ≈ 30 minutesSHOP COST · A tube of grease
SYMPTOMSPlay when you rock a raised front wheel top to bottom, or noise from the front hubs. Neglect is the main cause: from 1976 the bearings have a grease fitting that most owners never found.
THE FIXThese are the steps SuperBurban gives on Ramcharger Central. Jack up one front wheel and take it off. Look through the holes around the wheel studs with a flashlight and rotate the hub until you find the grease fitting. Give it several pumps from a grease gun. While the wheel is off the ground, shake it from top to bottom. There should be no play at all. If there is, plan on new bearings or a hub assembly. Grease both sides about once a year. Keep wheel offset close to stock, because big-offset wheels load these closely spaced bearings unevenly.

SYMPTOMSOil dripping from the case or the output seals, a low fluid level, or a chain that has worn noisy. One forum veteran says the 203 is 'perfectly adequate and strong if the chain and sprockets are not worn out'.
THE FIXTorque King's Dodge transfer case chart, compiled from factory shop manuals, calls for SAE 10W-30 engine oil in the 1974–79 Ramcharger's NP203, with a capacity of 9 pints. Drain it, check the magnet and fluid for metal, and refill to the fill plug. If it's weeping, reseal the case with a gasket and seal kit, following the factory shop manual. A bearing kit is available if the case comes apart. If it seeps after a switch to synthetic oil, Torque King points out that old seals may not like the additives, so reseal it first.

Convert the NP203 to part-time →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ a day, including case removalSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSNot a fault, just a choice. Owners convert for less driveline drag and wear on the road. Ramcharger Central is split: some say the fuel savings don't justify it, and others just leave the case in High Loc.
THE FIXMile Marker's 203 Conversion Kit (PN 501) replaces the NP203's internal differential parts with a wedged-gear assembly, so the case works like a part-time unit. It installs with common tools, according to Mile Marker. Pull the case, follow the kit instructions and reseal it as you go. Then decide what to do with the front axle. SuperBurban says a full-time axle can stay as it is and simply turn with the front driveshaft. If you add locking hubs, remember that the 203's park pawl needs the front axle engaged, so fit drive flanges or leave the hubs locked. One forum veteran calls hub conversions for half-ton Dodges expensive and not very strong. Swapping in a 1981-or-later front axle is the alternative.

Reinforce the steering box mount →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ half a daySHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSWandering steering, a steering box that moves when someone turns the wheel with the engine running, or cracks around the box mounting plate or the frame rail.
THE FIXFirst find the actual cause. Ramcharger Central members point out that worn couplers and loose parts cause wander too, and a pot-coupler rebuild kit is available. If the plate is cracked, replace it. Dodge's thicker 1988 plate is discontinued, but Autofab builds a heavy-duty bolt-on version for 1972–93 trucks. Far From Stock's brace works with the stock plate on 1976–93 trucks that keep stock 4x4 steering. On 1974–76 trucks with the early reverse-rotation box, members suggest the 1977–93 box and its parts as a straight swap. Weld any frame cracks before you add a brace.

Replace the Dana 44 inner axle seals →
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ a daySHOP COST · Torque King's seal kits with installer are $189.50–194.50
SYMPTOMSA front differential that keeps running low, or gear oil working its way out toward the steering knuckles.
THE FIXRemove the front hubs and spindles and pull the axle shafts, then remove the old inner seals from the axle tubes. Torque King's application chart lists the seals by build date. Trucks from 1974 to early 1977 (axle BOM 603036, and 603790 on the W100) use seal QU40055. Late-1977 to 1980 Ramchargers and Trail Dusters use QU40159. Use an installer that sets each seal to the correct depth. Torque King's adjustable tool also lets you move the seal lip onto an unworn part of the shaft. Refill the differential and check the knuckle seals and bearings while everything is apart.

Sort out a hot-running big-block →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–4 hoursSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSThe temperature climbs at idle or in traffic on hot days. One BaT seller and one Ramcharger Central member both reported it on 440 trucks.
THE FIXStart with the cheap things. Pressure-test the system and replace the cap, the hoses and the 50-year-old coolant. Make sure the fan shroud is present and the fan clutch actually locks up. If the radiator is original and partly blocked, many owners fit an aluminum radiator. Several of the trucks sold on BaT have them, including a 440 SE and a Top Hand. Check the ignition timing too. If it's still hot, look at the water pump and thermostat before blaming the engine.

Asked on every forum

Dodge Ramcharger (first generation) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

Dodge's largest factory option was a 10-15LT, which the 1978 Macho package used. On Bring a Trailer, listings that mention no lift mostly show 31x10.50R15s. One Top Hand truck ran 32x11.50s, and a 1978 Trail Duster sold on 33x12.50R15s. On a later Ramcharger with the same Dana 44 and leaf-spring layout, an owner found 32x11.50s rubbed when the suspension twisted up. So call 31s safe and 32s the practical limit, and plan on an add-a-leaf or lift before you try 33s off-road.

SOURCE · DODGE BROCHURES · BAT · OFF-ROAD.COM
What's the difference between a Ramcharger and a Trail Duster?

The badge and the trim. Motor1 calls the Trail Duster basically a Ramcharger with a different badge. Plymouth sold it from 1974 to 1981, and it was Plymouth's only SUV. It's the rarer of the two, and on Bring a Trailer Trail Dusters have recently sold for more than comparable Dodges.

SOURCE · MOTOR1 · WIKIPEDIA · BAT
Is the full-time NP203 a problem?

Opinions differ. Torque King calls it 'troublesome', and many trucks were converted to part-time. Ramcharger Central members mostly defend it. They say it's heavy but durable, with a usable low range, and one argues that its poor fuel-economy reputation really comes from the weak late-1970s engines it was paired with. Keep the chain and fluid healthy and grease the front hubs. Mile Marker's PN 501 kit converts it to part-time if you'd rather.

SOURCE · TORQUE KING · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL · MILE MARKER
Does the roof really come off?

Yes, on every 1974–80 truck. You could buy one with no top, a dealer-installed vinyl soft top with roll-up windows, or the removable double-wall steel hardtop. Trucks built before June 10, 1974 have half doors whose window frames are part of the roof. The 1981 second generation got a welded-on steel roof.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · DODGE 1978 BROCHURE
Which engines were offered?

The 225 slant six (2WD only from 1979, and never sold in California) and the 318 and 360 small-blocks. The 400 and 440 big-blocks lasted through 1978, and the 440 was 4x4-only. The 1974 trucks were V8-only, according to Ramcharger Central and Consumer Guide's reading of the launch ad. From 1979 the 318 was standard on the 4x4, with the 360 optional.

SOURCE · DODGE BROCHURES · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL
What does the NP203 take, and how much?

SAE 10W-30 engine oil, 9 pints, for the 1974–79 Ramcharger. That's according to Torque King's chart, compiled from factory shop manuals. A 1980 truck with the part-time NP208 takes Dexron III or ATF+4, 6 pints.

SOURCE · TORQUE KING
How much can it tow?

Dodge's 1978 brochure offered a standard towing package for trailers under 2,000 lb gross, with a 7-inch-section step bumper and a 1⅞-inch ball. The optional heavy-duty package with a load-equalizing hitch covered trailers up to 6,000 lb gross. The 1979 AW100 was rated at 6,050 lb GVW.

SOURCE · DODGE BROCHURES
What axles and gears does it have?

A Spicer 44 front, rated at 3,500 lb on the 4x4, and a Chrysler 9¼-inch rear rated at 3,600 lb. Brochures list 3.2, 3.55 and 3.9 ratios, and Dodge says anti-spin was available with every ratio. A 1978 Macho sold on BaT left the factory with 3.55s, a limited-slip rear and the 35-gallon tank.

SOURCE · DODGE BROCHURES · BAT
How big is the fuel tank?

24 gallons as standard and 35 gallons as an option, according to the 1977 and 1978 brochures.

SOURCE · DODGE BROCHURES
Is the 1980 truck different?

Yes. For 1980 Dodge switched the 4x4s from full-time to part-time four-wheel drive with the chain-drive NP208, and painted the grille openings black. Sources disagree on whether the locking-hub front end with the 5-on-5.5 bolt pattern arrived for 1980 or 1981, so check the truck itself. A 1980 Ramcharger also has an NHTSA campaign (84V115) that adds a fuel-tank skid plate.

SOURCE · RAMCHARGER CENTRAL · TORQUE KING · NHTSA
What's one worth?

On Bring a Trailer, 16 first-generation trucks sold between December 2021 and August 2026 for $15,110 to $57,500, with a median of about $27,750. The seven Dodges sold for $15,110–26,500. Most Trail Dusters brought $29,000–42,250, and a 340-powered Trail Duster Sport reached $57,500.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER
