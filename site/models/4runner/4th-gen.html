TOYOTA 4RUNNER · N210 · 2003–2009

Toyota 4Runner 4th Gen

“The 4Runner in a suit: Prado 120 bones, a third row, cushy X-REAS dampers and — for the only time in its life — a V8. Softer than the trail purists like, but a 4.7 V8 with a lockable Torsen centre diff still hits harder than anything wearing the badge since.”

The story

Car and Driver called the fourth generation the largest, heaviest, most luxurious and most powerful 4Runner yet, and that is the whole story in one sentence. Built on the Land Cruiser Prado 120 platform and launched in October 2002 as a 2003 model, it brought a 4.0-litre V6, the first and only V8 4Runner, a third-row seat and trick suspension options. It kept the things that matter, too: a ladder frame, a solid rear axle and a lockable centre differential. Two decades on, it's the generation that quietly does everything well — and costs less than the cult ones on either side.

PRADO UNDERNEATH

Production ran from August 2002 to August 2009, with the Japanese Hilux Surf twin built until September 2009. Wikipedia describes the recipe: double-wishbone independent front suspension, a solid rear axle 'retained for strength and durability', and the Prado 120's chassis — the same underpinnings as the Lexus GX 470. Launch trims were SR5, Sport Edition and Limited; the SR5 and Sport wore grey cladding, the Limited body colour, and Sport models got a non-functional hood scoop.

The 4.0-litre 1GR-FE V6 made 245 hp and 282 lb-ft; the optional 4.7-litre 2UZ-FE V8 — a sibling of the Land Cruiser 100's engine — launched at 235 hp and 320 lb-ft and gained VVT-i for 2005, rising to 268 hp and 315 lb-ft. Car and Driver clocked a 2005 V8 at 7.2 seconds to 60. Everything was automatic: a four-speed on early V6s, with a five-speed standard on the V6 from the 2005 model year.

THE CLEVER BITS

Four-wheel drive came in two flavours. V6 trucks used Toyota's part-time Multi-Mode system; V8s ran full-time 4WD; both used a lockable Torsen centre differential, with Hill-start Assist and Downhill Assist Control standard on 4WD models. Towing was rated at 5,000 lb for the V6 and up to 7,300 lb for a rear-drive V8 (7,000 lb with 4WD). The 3rd gen's optional rear E-locker, however, did not return until the very end.

Then there was X-REAS — standard on the Sport Edition and optional on SR5 and Limited — which links diagonally opposite dampers through hydraulic lines and a centre valve to cut body roll. Some Limiteds paired it with rear height-control air suspension. Both work well when new and become expensive with age, which is why replacing them with conventional shocks or coil springs is now a common 4th gen job.

YEAR BY YEAR

Toyota kept fiddling. Late 2003 brought standard tyre-pressure monitoring and an optional third-row seat on SR5 and Limited for 2004 — making this, as the Autopian notes, the first 4Runner with a third row. 2005 brought the five-speed V6 and the VVT-i V8; the 2006 refresh added new bumpers, grille, projector headlamps and LED tail lamps; 2007 had the oddball Urban Runner package; and 2008 made curtain and front-side airbags standard. The IIHS rated it Good in frontal offset and side tests but Poor for rear-impact protection.

Sales tell the arc. US buyers took 109,308 in 2003 and a peak of 114,212 in 2004, before demand slid to 47,878 in 2008 and 19,675 in calendar 2009. The fifth generation replaced it for 2010 — on the J150 platform, with a V6 only. No 4Runner has had a V8 since, and the current 6th gen runs four-cylinders. That, more than anything, is why a well-kept V8 4th gen has a following.

SIDEBAR · 2009 ONLY

Trail Edition: the locker comes back

When the 3rd gen dropped its optional rear E-locker after 2000, the 4Runner went most of a decade without one. Toyota fixed that in the 4th gen's final year: Wikipedia records that the 2009 Trail Edition added an electronic locking rear differential, an A-TRAC switch and Bilstein dampers.

As a one-year package on the last model year of the generation, Trail Editions are scarce. If you want a factory-locked 4th gen, it's the only one; check the switch on the dash and test the locker in 4Lo before you pay.

How it’s built

The N210 is a Prado 120 in 4Runner clothing: a ladder frame, double-wishbone front suspension, a four-link solid rear axle on coils, and either Toyota's timing-chain 1GR-FE V6 or the timing-belt 2UZ-FE V8. The engineering story is in the drivetrain — full-time 4WD on the V8, Multi-Mode on the V6, a lockable Torsen centre diff on both — and in optional electronics and hydraulics (X-REAS, rear air suspension, DAC) that were sophisticated in 2003 and are maintenance items now.

Buying one: what to check

  1. 01
    Frame rustNot as notorious as contemporary Tacomas, but salt-belt trucks rot. Inspect the rails, crossmembers and rear suspension link mounts with a hammer and a light — and note the 4Runner wasn't part of Toyota's Tacoma/Tundra/Sequoia frame programmes.
  2. 02
    V8 air-injection pump and manifoldsCarComplaints owners report the V8's secondary air injection system failing around 100,000–150,000 miles, triggering a check-engine light and limp mode, with $2,000–4,000 repair bills. Cold-start ticking can be a leaking exhaust manifold, which on the V8 is integrated with the catalytic converters.
  3. 03
    V8 timing belt recordsThe 2UZ-FE has a timing belt (the V6 has a chain). Toyota's maintenance guide lists replacement at 90,000 miles or 108 months (as summarised by Cherish Your Car); LC Engineering suggests inspecting from about 60,000 and doing the water pump at the same time. No records means budget for it.
  4. 04
    V6 coolant lossSome early 1GR-FE trucks have suffered head-gasket leaks; a cylinder 6 misfire (P0306) plus coolant loss is the red flag. Check the overflow level, watch a cold start, and consider a combustion-gas test.
  5. 05
    X-REAS and rear air springsX-REAS shocks leak and rust with age and TacomaWorld members note they're replaced in pairs at real cost. On Limiteds with rear height control, check the rear doesn't sag overnight. Either can be converted to conventional parts.
  6. 06
    Brake actuatorListen with the engine running: a TacomaWorld member found the ABS accumulator pump cycling every 30 seconds with pedal vibration on one test truck — a sign the actuator is on its way out.
  7. 07
    Steering, CVs and bushingsTurn to full lock and look for a wet inner rack boot (fluid seeping into it damages the inner joint), torn CV boots past 100,000 miles, and clunky front sway-bar bushings.
  8. 08
    Dash and interiorCracked dashboards were common enough that Toyota's ZE6 warranty enhancement covered 2003–2005 4Runners, but that coverage has long since lapsed. Check that the third-row seat, power rear window and interior lights all work.

After you buy it

The 4th gen is young enough that Toyota's owner's-manual data is published — Toyota-Club's US-market tables are used below — but old enough that most examples are on their second or third owner with patchy records. Baseline every fluid, find out whether the truck has X-REAS or rear air springs, and on a V8 assume the timing belt is due until proven otherwise.

For the hands-on owner

Common repairs

The jobs owners actually end up doing, rated for a competent home mechanic with a decent tool kit. Always confirm torque figures and procedures against the factory service manual for your VIN.

Replace X-REAS with conventional shocks
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · A daySHOP COST · Bilstein 5100 four-shock set with front mounts ≈ $650 (ShockWarehouse)
SYMPTOMSLeaking or rusty X-REAS shocks, a floaty or wallowy ride, a quote for four OEM units that's more than the truck feels worth.
THE FIXLift the truck so all four shocks are fully extended, then disconnect the X-REAS lines at the junctions about a foot from each shock — Toyota-4Runner.org members note there are built-in valves, but the system is pressurised, so follow the factory manual or expect a spray of hydraulic fluid. Drain the system and tie off the hose ends, then remove the old front strut assemblies and rear shocks and fit conventional units with new front mounts. Leaving the brackets and tubing in place does no harm.

Rear air suspension to coil conversion (Limited)
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoonSHOP COST · Conversion kit plus coil springs

Differential and transfer case fluid service
DIFFICULTY · 1 / 5TIME · A short afternoonSHOP COST · Gear oil ≈ $5–15 a quart plus crush washers
SYMPTOMSUnknown service history, whining diffs, a 4WD system that's never been exercised.
THE FIXAlways break the fill plug loose first. Rear diff and transfer case use 24 mm plugs; the front diff needs a 10 mm hex after removing the 12 mm-bolted skid plates. Drain each, fit new washers, and pump in 75W-90 until it drips from the fill hole — roughly 1.5 qt front, 1.5 qt transfer case and 3.2 qt rear per the owner's manual figures quoted on 4Runners.com. Cycle 4Hi and 4Lo afterwards.

Front wheel bearing / hub assembly
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoonSHOP COST · Hub assembly plus labor
SYMPTOMSA hum or growl that changes with speed, play in the front wheel, an ABS light.
THE FIXPull the wheel, unbolt the ABS and brake-line bracket, remove the caliper (two 17 mm bolts — support it, don't hang it) and the rotor. Pop off the dust cap, remove cotter pin and nut cap, and break the axle nut with a 35 mm socket. Remove the four 17 mm hub bolts (open-end wrench only), tap the axle free with a soft drift, and pry the hub off evenly. Replace the knuckle dust seal while you're there, then reassemble to factory torque specs.
TOOLS & PARTS35 mm socket · 17 mm sockets and wrench · breaker bar or impact · soft drift · new hub assembly · knuckle dust seal

Radiator replacement and external trans cooler (V6)
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoonSHOP COST · Aftermarket radiator ≈ $134 and trans cooler ≈ $39 (2014 prices quoted on 4Runners.com)
SYMPTOMSCoolant weeping from the bottom of the radiator — the driver's-side bottom is a common failure point on 4th gen V6s — or an old original radiator you don't trust with transmission cooling.
THE FIXRemove the top trim and (optionally) the front bumper for access. Drain the radiator, unbolt the overflow tank, disconnect the transmission cooler lines on the driver's side (keep them upright), unbolt the fan shroud and the four radiator mounts, and lift it out. Mount an external cooler on the condenser, route the transmission lines through it, fit the new radiator, refill and bleed, then check transmission fluid level.
TOOLS & PARTSMetric wrenches to 17 mm · hose-clamp pliers · drain pans · radiator · Hayden-type transmission cooler and hose

2UZ-FE timing belt and water pump (V8)
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · A full day for mostSHOP COST · LC Engineering 2UZ belt kit ≈ $145, plus water pump
SYMPTOMSDue by mileage or age, no records, a weeping water pump. A failed belt can do severe engine damage, per LC Engineering.
THE FIXDrain coolant, remove the fan, shroud and drive belts, then the crank pulley and timing covers. Set the engine to TDC and mark the belt and pulleys. Replace the belt, tensioner pulley, idler pulley and hydraulic belt adjuster, and the water pump — which is driven by the belt — then rotate the crank two full turns to confirm the marks before reassembly.

Asked on every forum

Toyota 4Runner 4th Gen questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

265/70R17 (about 31.6 in) is the size the GX 470 community — same chassis and fitment — says fits with no modification, and owners on both platforms report no rubbing on factory wheels. 255/75R17 may need a front fender-liner bolt repositioned. 285/70R17 (about 33 in) needs a body-mount chop and fender-liner work even with a small lift.

SOURCE · LEXUSGXOR / OVERLAND BOUND
V6 or V8?

The V8 is the reason many buy a 4th gen: 268 hp and 315 lb-ft from 2005, full-time 4WD and up to 7,000 lb of 4WD towing. The V6 has a timing chain instead of a belt, part-time Multi-Mode 4WD and better EPA numbers (17/20 vs 15/19). The V8's air-injection pump and exhaust manifolds are its known expensive faults.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA / CARCOMPLAINTS
Does it have a locker?

All 4WD trucks have a lockable Torsen centre differential. A factory rear locker only came on the 2009 Trail Edition, along with an A-TRAC switch and Bilstein dampers.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What is X-REAS, and should I keep it?

It links diagonally opposite shocks through hydraulic lines and a centre valve to reduce body roll. It was standard on the Sport Edition and optional on SR5 and Limited. It works, but the shocks are costly and replaced in pairs, so many owners swap to conventional shocks such as Bilstein 5100s when they wear out.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA / TACOMAWORLD
How much can it tow?

5,000 lb with the V6; 7,300 lb with a rear-drive V8 and 7,000 lb with a 4WD V8.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What fuel economy should I expect?

Wikipedia lists EPA estimates of 17 city / 20 highway for the V6 and 15/19 for the V8. A 4Runners.com owner reported 17–18 mpg combined and 18–19 on the highway from a stock 2003 V8 4WD over 12 years, less when towing.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA / 4RUNNERS.COM
What fluids do the diffs and transfer case take?

75W-90 gear oil. A 4Runners.com write-up quoting the owner's manual gives roughly 1.5 qt for the front diff, 1.5 qt for the transfer case and 3.2 qt for the rear diff; fill each until it drips from the fill hole.

SOURCE · 4RUNNERS.COM
Why are my VSC and TRAC lights on?

Often nothing is broken: after a battery disconnect, alignment or sensor work the system can need a zero-point calibration, done on level ground with a paper clip across the diagnostic connector. If the lights stay on, scan for codes.

SOURCE · 4RUNNERS.COM
Is there a dash-cracking recall?

Not a safety recall. Toyota's ZE6 warranty enhancement covered cracked or sticky dashboards on 2003–2005 4Runners, but coverage was time-limited and has expired. A dash cover is the usual fix now.

SOURCE · CARSPECMN / ENGINEPATROL
Which years should I look for?

2005-on trucks get the five-speed automatic on the V6 and the VVT-i V8; 2006-on have the refreshed styling and projector headlamps; 2008–09 add standard curtain airbags; and 2009 offers the Trail Edition with a rear locker. Condition, rust and maintenance history matter more than any model year.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Does it have a third row?

It can. A third-row seat became optional on SR5 and Limited from late 2003 (the 2004 model year), making the 4th gen the first 4Runner offered with seven seats.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA / THE AUTOPIAN
