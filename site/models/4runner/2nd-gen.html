TOYOTA 4RUNNER · N120/N130 · 1990–1995

Toyota 4Runner 2nd Gen

“The middle child: four doors, a steel roof and coil springs out back, but still a Hilux where it counts. Overshadowed by the cult 3rd gen, haunted by the V6's head gaskets — and quietly one of the cheapest ways into an old-school Toyota.”

The story

For 1990 the 4Runner grew up a little. The removable fibreglass top went, a one-piece steel body with rear doors arrived, and the rear leaf springs were swapped for coils on a four-link axle. Underneath, though, it was still a Hilux pickup — the same independent front end, the same 22R-E four and 3.0-litre V6. Car and Driver records that the four-door quickly became the volume seller, and US sales climbed to more than 75,000 a year by the end of the run. Today it's the generation people skip on the way to the 3rd gen, which is exactly why it's worth a look.

DOORS, A ROOF, AND COILS

The second generation was released in 1989 for the 1990 model year, built at Toyota's Tahara plant in Japan. Wikipedia notes that most were four-doors; a two-door was built only from 1989 to May 1993, with US sales ending in August 1992 and Canada carrying on through 1993. The first gen's leaf-sprung rear gave way to a four-link solid axle with a Panhard rod and coil springs — better riding, though Wikipedia notes it was also prone to sagging. The torsion-bar independent front suspension carried over from 1986.

Two engines were offered in the US. The 2.4-litre 22R-E four made 116 hp and 139 lb-ft; the 3.0-litre 3VZ-E V6 made 150 hp and 180 lb-ft. Gearboxes were a five-speed manual or the four-speed A340H automatic. One detail matters to anyone building one: Wikipedia records that the gear-driven transfer case was phased out on V6 models in favour of a chain-driven unit, while the four-cylinders kept the gear drive. Car and Driver's test V6 took 15.7 seconds to reach 60 mph.

UPDATES, SAFETY AND A V6 WITH A REPUTATION

The 1992 model year facelift, introduced in 1991, brought one-piece front bumpers, modular headlamps in place of sealed beams, and a new interior pattern. Rear-wheel ABS was standard on the SR5 V6 from 1990 with four-wheel ABS optional, and side-impact beams were added for 1994 and 1995 after criticism of the early doors. Crash performance was still of its era: Wikipedia lists a one-to-two-star frontal rating for the driver.

Then there's the 3VZ-E. Toyota extended head-gasket warranty coverage on 1988–1994 V6 trucks in October 1996 and later ran Special Service Campaign V06, replacing head gaskets on 1/90–10/94 production 'regardless of leakage or not'. That campaign expired in September 2018. Forum theory blames the exhaust crossover pipe cooking the back of the engine — cylinder 6 is the usual casualty — and owners still swear by cooling-system maintenance and crossover deletes to keep them alive.

THE MIDDLE CHILD

Toyota replaced it for 1996 with the third generation, built on the Land Cruiser Prado chassis with coils all round and an optional rear locker — and the N120/N130 slid into obscurity. 4Runners.com members complain that trim and body parts are now harder to find than for both the 3rd gen and, oddly, the 1st gen. Mechanically it's another story: the front end, 22R-E and much of the drivetrain are shared with Toyota's 1986–95 pickups, so suspension, armour and engine parts are still plentiful.

That makes the 2nd gen the bargain of the old-4Runner world, if you can find a straight one. ToyTec, Old Man Emu, Total Chaos and Coastal Offroad all still make parts for it, and Japan's Hilux Surf twin offered diesels that America never got — the 130 hp, 213 lb-ft 1KZ-TE turbodiesel among them — which the US 25-year import rule now makes fair game. Just budget for rust repair and a head-gasket job, and you'll have the most underrated 4Runner on the trail.

SIDEBAR · THE TWO-DOOR

Two doors, one tax

The rarest 2nd gen is the one that looks most like the first: the two-door. Wikipedia records that two-door models were built from 1989 to May 1993, that US sales ended in August 1992 while Canada continued through 1993, and that the two-door was priced higher than the four-door because of the 'chicken tax' — the 25 per cent US tariff on imported light trucks that dates back to 1964.

With a fixed steel roof and rear windows instead of doors, it kept the short, sporty profile of the original without the leaky lid. Few were sold, fewer survived, and IH8MUD and 4Runners.com owners treat a clean one as a find — just don't expect body parts to be any easier to source than they are for the four-door.

How it’s built

The N120/N130 is a halfway house between pickup and SUV. The frame, independent front suspension and engines come straight from Toyota's 1986–95 pickups, but the steel four-door body and coil-sprung four-link rear axle were new. Rear-wheel ABS and a chain-driven transfer case on V6 models show where Toyota was heading; the open differentials (a limited-slip was only offered in Japan and Europe) show where it wasn't yet.

Buying one: what to check

  1. 01
    Head-gasket history (V6)Ask for paperwork. Trucks that went through the V06 campaign got a label on the left front door hinge post near the check strap. Otherwise look for coolant loss, overheating, white exhaust after warm-up or a misfire on cylinder 6.
  2. 02
    Timing belt on the 3VZ-EThe V6 is belt-driven. A 4Runners.com member recalls four years or 96,000 km as the typical interval; owners recommend an Aisin kit with the water pump. No records means do it now.
  3. 03
    Frame and body rustCheck frame rails and crossmembers, the tailgate bottom, rear bumper and wheel arches. A rust-free body and frame is rare outside dry states, and replacement panels are hard to find.
  4. 04
    Sagging rear coilsThe four-link rear was new, but Wikipedia notes it sags too. A nose-high stance usually means tired springs; Old Man Emu sells 2 in replacement coils in two rates for these trucks.
  5. 05
    Power rear window and tailgateThe roll-down rear glass and tailgate lock are known trouble spots — IH8MUD members say the electric rear window 'can be problematic' on both early generations. Make sure it goes up and down.
  6. 06
    Four-cylinder or V6?The 22R-E is slower but simpler and has no head-gasket campaign behind it; the 3VZ-E has more torque but more to go wrong. A four with a gear-driven transfer case is the stronger base for big gearing changes.
  7. 07
    Front-end wearOn the IFS, check idler arm, tie-rod ends and CV boots, and look for a wandering steering feel on the test drive.
  8. 08
    Trim and interior partsMechanical parts are easy; interior and exterior trim isn't. 4Runners.com members report even dealers struggle to supply 2nd gen trim, so a complete truck is worth more than a cheap one missing bits.

Factory capacities for the N120/N130 aren't reliably published online; anything marked below should be checked in the owner's or factory service manual.

For the hands-on owner

Common repairs

The jobs owners actually end up doing, rated for a competent home mechanic with a decent tool kit. Always confirm torque figures and procedures against the factory service manual for your VIN.

3VZ-E head gaskets
DIFFICULTY · 5 / 5TIME · Several days; heads usually go to a machine shopSHOP COST · MLS head gasket set ≈ $116 (LC Engineering), plus machining and a timing belt kit
SYMPTOMSCoolant disappearing with no external leak, white exhaust, overheating, cylinder 6 misfire. 4Runners.com members call blown gaskets almost inevitable on an un-repaired 3VZ-E.
THE FIXDrain coolant and oil, remove the intake plenum, exhaust crossover and timing belt, then the cams and both heads. Have the heads checked for flatness and resurfaced if needed — MLS gaskets want a very smooth deck finish. Refit with new gaskets and head bolts or studs, and replace the timing belt, water pump and thermostat at the same time. Use OEM or reputable Aisin/Denso parts, as owners advise.

3VZ-E timing belt and water pump
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · A full day for mostSHOP COST · Timing belt kit plus water pump; parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSUnknown history, a cracked or glazed belt, a weeping water pump. A snapped belt leaves you stranded.
THE FIXDrain coolant, remove the fan, shroud and drive belts, then the crank pulley and timing covers. Set the engine to TDC and mark the belt and pulleys. Replace the belt, tensioner (and spring), idler and water pump, confirm the timing marks after two full turns of the crank, then reassemble and bleed the cooling system. Forum members suggest Aisin parts over bargain kits.

Exhaust crossover delete (V6)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · An afternoon, plus exhaust shop timeSHOP COST · Kit plus exhaust fabrication
SYMPTOMSNot a failure so much as prevention: the crossover pipe that runs behind the engine puts heat into the back of the heads, which 4Runners.com members link to cylinder 6 head-gasket failures.
THE FIXRemove the factory crossover pipe and blank off or re-route the rear manifold so both banks exit separately or join further back. Yota1's 1988–95 3VZ crossover delete kit supplies the manifold-side parts, but the kit doesn't include the pipe — additional exhaust work is required.

Rear coil spring replacement
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoonSHOP COST · OME 2 in coils ≈ $240 per pair (LC Engineering)
SYMPTOMSRear sitting low, bottoming out loaded, a nose-high stance.
THE FIXSupport the frame on stands, remove the rear wheels and support the axle with a jack. Disconnect the shocks at the lower mounts and make sure brake lines and ABS wiring have slack, then lower the axle until the coils come free. Swap in new springs (OME sells 2 in coils rated for 180 lb or, HD, 330 lb of constant load), raise the axle, refit the shocks and torque with the truck on its wheels.

DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · Most of a weekend for a first-timerSHOP COST · Metal-backed guides ≈ $64 (LC Engineering), plus chain, tensioner and gaskets

THE FIXRemove the timing cover, fit new guides positioned toward the outside of the block, install the crank and cam gears with the chain's bright link on top, tighten the straight guide and tensioner bolts to 9 ft-lb, set the curved guide so the tensioner is mostly retracted, then torque and reassemble with a new gasket set (per LC Engineering's instructions).

DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · An afternoonSHOP COST · Parts plus labor

Asked on every forum

Toyota 4Runner 2nd Gen questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

31s — IH8MUD members call 31s the right size for a stock 2nd gen, and Toyota-4Runner.org owners agree 31x10.50R15 fits without a lift or regear. A narrow 33x10.50R15 can be squeezed in with some pounding and trimming, but expect rubbing at the valance and pinch weld; about 1.5 in of lift is the usual recommendation for 33s.

SOURCE · IH8MUD / T4R.ORG
Was my V6 covered by Toyota's head-gasket campaign?

Special Service Campaign V06 covered 3VZ-E trucks, 4Runners and T100s built from 1/90 to 10/94, replacing head gaskets regardless of whether they leaked. Completed trucks got a label on the left front door hinge post. The campaign expired on 24 September 2018, so any repair now is at your cost.

SOURCE · CARCOMPLAINTS / T4R.ORG
22R-E or 3VZ-E?

The V6 makes 150 hp and 180 lb-ft against the four's 116 hp and 139 lb-ft, which matters in a heavier four-door. The 22R-E is simpler, more durable and outside the head-gasket saga. Owners generally say a maintained 3VZ-E is fine — but it must never overheat.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA / 4RUNNERS.COM
Were there two-door 2nd gens?

Yes, built from 1989 to May 1993. US sales ended in August 1992 and Canada continued through 1993. They cost more than the four-door because of the US chicken tax, so they're rare.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
How often does the timing belt need doing?

A 4Runners.com member recalls four years or 96,000 km (about 60,000 miles) as the typical interval for the 3VZ-E. Do the water pump at the same time and use a quality kit.

SOURCE · 4RUNNERS.COM
What fuel economy does it get?

Not much. Wikipedia lists an EPA city rating of 15 mpg for the V6 with the four-speed automatic, with observed testing at 14.28 mpg. Bigger tyres and a lift make it worse.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Does it have ABS or a locker?

Rear-wheel ABS was standard on the SR5 V6 from 1990 and four-wheel ABS was optional. There was no factory locker in the US — differentials were open, with a limited-slip only offered in Japan and Europe. Aftermarket lockers are the usual fix.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What lift options are there?

The common recipe is a 2 in lift: ToyTec sells a kit with rear coils for 1990–95 4Runners, Old Man Emu sells 2 in rear coils in standard and HD rates, and ball-joint spacers or torsion-bar adjustment lift the front. Total Chaos makes long-travel IFS kits for 1986–95 trucks if you want more.

SOURCE · LCE / TOTAL CHAOS
Should I do a solid-axle swap?

Only if you need it. IH8MUD members describe the 1986–95 Toyota IFS as 'pretty stout' unless you rock-crawl hard; those who break IFS shafts tend to go solid axle. Long-travel IFS and SAS conversions can cost similar money.

SOURCE · IH8MUD
Can I get a diesel one?

Not from a US dealer — US trucks were 22R-E or 3VZ-E only. Japan's Hilux Surf offered the 2L-TE, 3L and 1KZ-TE diesels, and every 2nd gen is now old enough for the US 25-year import exemption.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA / BE FORWARD
How safe is it?

By modern standards, not very. Wikipedia lists a one-to-two-star frontal crash rating for the driver; side-impact door beams were only added for the 1994 and 1995 model years.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
