1st Gen 4Runner years to avoid, and the best years to buy

Every Toyota 4Runner 1st Gen model year: which ones owners and specialists recommend, which have documented problems, which carry recalls, and what they sell for now.

The short answer

1984 and 1985. Toyota switched to independent front suspension with a three-inch-wider track for 1986, so 1986–89 trucks are IFS. Solid-axle trucks are the off-roader's favourite; IFS trucks ride better on the road.

1st Gen 4Runner year by year

YEARVERDICTWHAT CHANGED
1984½—US sales start May 1984: two doors, removable fibreglass top, carbureted 22R, five-speed, solid front axle; base DX has no rear seat
1985Sweet spotFuel-injected 22R-E (116 hp) arrives; rear seat available on all trims. Last year of the solid front axle, which Hagerty says purists still seek out; an Expedition Portal member rates carbureted trucks less desirable
1986—Hi-Trac independent front suspension replaces the solid axle, track 3 in wider; 22R-TE turbo (135 hp) added
1987—No changes
1988—3.0 3VZ-E V6 (145 hp) replaces the turbo on SR5; SR5 gets automatic locking hubs
1988–89 (V6)Beware3.0 3VZ-E V6 trucks: a Hagerty expert says most enthusiasts avoid the six and choose the four, and the 3VZ-E's head-gasket history starts here
1989—Minor cosmetic and option changes before the 1990 replacement

“Sweet spot” means owners and specialists recommend those years; “Beware” means documented problems; “Recall” means check the recall was done rather than walk away.
