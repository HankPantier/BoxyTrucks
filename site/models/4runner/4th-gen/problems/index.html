4th Gen 4Runner problems: 8 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the Toyota 4Runner 4th Gen, the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

4th Gen 4Runner problems to check before you buy

  1. Frame rustNot as notorious as contemporary Tacomas, but salt-belt trucks rot. Inspect the rails, crossmembers and rear suspension link mounts with a hammer and a light — and note the 4Runner wasn't part of Toyota's Tacoma/Tundra/Sequoia frame programmes.
  2. V8 air-injection pump and manifoldsCarComplaints owners report the V8's secondary air injection system failing around 100,000–150,000 miles, triggering a check-engine light and limp mode, with $2,000–4,000 repair bills. Cold-start ticking can be a leaking exhaust manifold, which on the V8 is integrated with the catalytic converters.
  3. V8 timing belt recordsThe 2UZ-FE has a timing belt (the V6 has a chain). Toyota's maintenance guide lists replacement at 90,000 miles or 108 months (as summarised by Cherish Your Car); LC Engineering suggests inspecting from about 60,000 and doing the water pump at the same time. No records means budget for it.
  4. V6 coolant loss2003–05 V6s are the ones Autotrader says report head-gasket failures more than average, and forum owners point to 2003–04 in particular; a cylinder 6 misfire (P0306) plus coolant loss is the red flag. Check the overflow level, watch a cold start, and consider a combustion-gas test.
  5. X-REAS and rear air springsX-REAS shocks leak and rust with age and TacomaWorld members note they're replaced in pairs at real cost. On Limiteds with rear height control, check the rear doesn't sag overnight. Either can be converted to conventional parts.
  6. Brake actuatorListen with the engine running: a TacomaWorld member found the ABS accumulator pump cycling every 30 seconds with pedal vibration on one test truck — a sign the actuator is on its way out.
  7. Steering, CVs and bushingsTurn to full lock and look for a wet inner rack boot (fluid seeping into it damages the inner joint), torn CV boots past 100,000 miles, and clunky front sway-bar bushings.
  8. Dash and interiorCracked dashboards were common enough that Toyota's ZE6 warranty enhancement covered 2003–2005 4Runners, but that coverage has long since lapsed. Check that the third-row seat, power rear window and interior lights all work.

4th Gen 4Runner recalls and service bulletins

  • Fuel pulsation damper leak (V6) ↗ 2003 · NHTSA 03V-146Improperly assembled damper on the fuel rail can tear its diaphragm and leak fuel.
  • Port-installed alloy wheel upgrade ↗ 2003 · NHTSA 02V-339Some trucks fitted with a four-wheel alloy upgrade at the Jacksonville, FL port.
  • Floor mat pedal entrapment ↗ 2003–2009 · NHTSA 11V-113An unsecured or incompatible driver's mat can trap the accelerator pedal wide open.
  • Side curtain airbag inadvertent deployment ↗ 2004–2006 · NHTSA 16V-065Airbag control module may be programmed so curtain airbags deploy unexpectedly.
  • Cracked, sticky or melted dashboard — warranty enhancement ↗ · ZE6Covered certain 2003–2005 4Runners for 10 years from first delivery; long expired.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

What to bring when you look at a 4th Gen 4Runner

Six inexpensive tools that catch most of the problems above before you hand over the money. Or book a specialist: see how to get a pre-purchase inspection.

  • Bluetooth OBD-II scannerReads stored and pending codes, and shows the readiness monitors. If several say "not ready", someone may have cleared the codes just before you arrived.
    Amazon ↗
  • Paint thickness gaugeThick readings on one panel mean body filler or a respray, which usually means an accident or rust repair the seller hasn’t mentioned.
    Amazon ↗
  • Inspection borescope cameraFits through drain holes into frame rails, rockers and sills, where these trucks rust from the inside out.
    Amazon ↗
  • Bright LED flashlightFor the underside: leaks at the axle seals, transfer case and rear main, and the frame behind the rear wheels.
    Amazon ↗
  • Telescoping inspection mirrorShows the back of the engine, the top of the fuel tank and the inner fenders without crawling under.
    Amazon ↗
  • Compression testerOn a high-mileage or long-parked engine, uneven compression is the cheapest way to spot a tired engine before you pay for it. Diesels need a diesel-rated tester.
    Amazon ↗
