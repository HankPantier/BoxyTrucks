PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Twenty-five years after the last full-size Bronco, the name came back because of a factory. In Ford's 2016 contract talks with the UAW, Michigan Assembly in Wayne was set to lose the Focus and C-Max. Instead it was retooled for a new North American Ranger and, since one truck wouldn't fill the plant, a new Bronco. Ford confirmed it at the 2017 Detroit show, showed it on July 13, 2020 after pandemic delays, and built the first customer trucks on June 14, 2021, in the same plant as the previous five generations. Its target was obvious: the Wrangler JL and JL Unlimited, in both two- and four-door form.

HOW IT'S BUILT

Underneath is Ford's T6 body-on-frame platform, shared with the Ranger. Unlike the Jeep, the front suspension is independent, with twin A-arms. The rear has a solid axle on five links and coilovers, and Ford claimed 17 percent more front wheel travel and 10 percent more rear travel than the Wrangler. Styling borrows from the 1966–77 first Bronco; the design chief's own 1976 truck was digitally scanned for reference. The doors are frameless and come off with one bolt per hinge. The mirrors stay behind on the cowl, and the doors can ride in protective bags in the cargo area.

Two engines launched it. The 2.3 EcoBoost four makes 300 hp and 325 lb-ft and the 2.7 twin-turbo V6 330 hp and 415 lb-ft, both on premium fuel and both well above Ford's original estimates. The four is offered with a Getrag seven-speed manual, which is really a six-speed plus a crawler gear marked 'C', or Ford's 10-speed automatic. The V6 is automatic-only. Two transfer cases were offered: a part-time unit with a 2.72:1 low range, and an advanced one with a full-time 4A mode and a 3.06:1 low range. Ford quoted a 95:1 crawl ratio for the manual with the crawler gear.

SASQUATCH, HOSS AND THE RAPTOR

The Sasquatch package is the headline option: 315/70R17 tires (35 inches) on +30 mm-offset wheels, 4.7 axle gears, a mild lift, and the Bilstein dampers and sway-bar disconnect of the off-road trims. On 35s, MotorTrend measured 11.6 inches of ground clearance and approach, departure and breakover angles of 43.2, 37.2 and 29.0 degrees for the two-door. Ford calls its suspension packages HOSS. Badlands trucks get position-sensitive Bilstein dampers and a hydraulic front anti-roll-bar disconnect that reconnects itself at speed. The Wildtrak's HOSS 3.0 uses Fox dampers. For 2025, Badlands buyers could add full HOSS 3.0, with Fox 2.5-inch internal-bypass shocks, the Raptor's severe-duty steering rack and stronger tie rods.

Then there's the Raptor, added for 2022 as a four-door only. It has a 3.0-liter twin-turbo V6 with 418 hp and 440 lb-ft, Fox suspension, 37-inch tires, 13.1 inches of ground clearance, F-150 Raptor brakes and a body widened to 85.7 inches. It gets 'FORD' across the grille instead of 'BRONCO' and is rated to tow 4,500 lb, 1,000 lb more than the standard Bronco. The cost is fuel: the EPA rates a 2023 Raptor at 15 mpg combined.

TWO DOORS OR FOUR

The two-door rides on a 100.4-inch wheelbase and is 174.8 inches long. MotorTrend noted it is 6.9 inches longer than a two-door Wrangler on a 3.6-inch-longer wheelbase, and much roomier inside. The four-door stretches to 116.1 inches between the axles and about 190 inches overall. That costs breakover angle (26.3 degrees against 29.0 on 35s), and the four-door weighs up to about 400 lb more, but it has the bigger fuel tank (21.1 gallons against 17.4) and, at launch, the soft-top option, and the Raptor was built only as a four-door. Both come with removable roofs; the standard hardtop is molded in black or white, and a painted top arrived later.

TEETHING TROUBLES

Demand was huge: Ford sold 117,057 in the US in 2022 and 146,007 in 2025. Early trucks had problems. In August 2021, Ford began replacing molded-in-color hardtops whose finish crazed and delaminated, and a 2026 recall covers 2021–22 roof panels that can separate on the road. Some 2021–22 2.7 V6s can break intake valves. Bilstein rear shocks on 2021–24 trucks can corrode until the reservoir detaches. A service bulletin covers seven-speed manuals that grind. By September 2026, Jalopnik counted about 20 recalls since launch. Buy a sixth-gen Bronco on its VIN history.

SIDEBAR · THE MILLION-DOLLAR FIRST EDITION

VIN 1 and a badly chosen date

Ford unintentionally first scheduled the reveal for July 9, 2020, O.J. Simpson's birthday, then moved it to July 13. On March 27, 2021, the first production Bronco, a First Edition, sold at Barrett-Jackson in Scottsdale for $1,075,000, with the money going to a forest-conservation charity Ford helped set up.

The First Edition combined Badlands hardware with Wildtrak looks and the Outer Banks interior. MotorTrend reported a 3,500-unit run at the reveal, and Ford later built more because of demand. One sold on Bring a Trailer in August 2026 for $40,810.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The Bronco is a body-on-frame Ranger derivative with a removable roof and doors. It has independent front suspension, a coil-sprung solid rear axle and Dana axles, with electronic lockers optional front and rear. Off-road settings are chosen with a dial and the G.O.A.T. ('Goes Over Any Type of Terrain') drive modes, not a second lever.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Run the VIN before anything elseThere are many open-recall possibilities. The big ones are the 2.7 intake valves (24V-635), the rear shock reservoirs (25V-025), 2021–22 molded-in-color roof panels (26V-299, remedy expected November 2026), engine-bay wiring (26V-468), fender flares (26V-403) and, on 2024–25 trucks, loose front control-arm and ball-joint fasteners (24V-770, 25V-260, 25V-308, 25V-310). Get a printout from NHTSA's VIN lookup.
  2. 02
    Look hard at the hardtopMolded-in-color tops were standard on 2021–23 trucks and most 2024s. Crazing or delamination means the top needs replacing: Ford's August 2021 program fitted 'MIC 2.0' tops, and some owners are on their third or fourth. Painted tops arrived for 2025. Check the headliner and seals for leaks and make sure every panel latches.
  3. 03
    2.7 V6: ask about the engine testRecall 24V-635 covers 2021–22 2.7 and 3.0 Nano EcoBoost engines whose intake valves can break. Dealers run an engine cycle test and replace the engine if needed. Bronco Nation names V6s built from May 1 to October 31, 2021 as the bad batch. Get proof the test was done.
  4. 04
    Rear shocks on Badlands and SasquatchBilstein rear shocks on trucks built from September 23, 2020 to July 23, 2024 can corrode until the external reservoir detaches (25V-025). Look at the reservoirs and their mounts and confirm the replacement was done.
  5. 05
    Drive the manual through every gearOn 2021–23 seven-speed trucks, a scraping noise in neutral, first or second is the subject of a Ford service bulletin. Check for clean engagement of the crawler gear and reverse.
  6. 06
    Steering and tie rods on modified trucksOwners have reported tie rods failing during hard off-road use, usually on lifted trucks with bigger tires. Inspect the inner and outer tie rods for bends. The 2023 Wildtrak and 2022 Raptor had a steering-gear recall (23V-155) for internal damage that could lock the wheel.
  7. 07
    Was it flat-towed?2021–22 trucks had a neutral-tow problem: Ford issued a temporary fix in May 2022 and an over-the-air update by March 2023. Bronco Nation warns that flat-tow damage to the transmission can be hard to get covered under warranty, so ask how the truck traveled behind a motorhome.
  8. 08
    Is it a real Sasquatch?Factory Sasquatch trucks have 315/70R17 tires on 17x8.5, +30 mm wheels and 4.7 gears. A truck that only has aftermarket 35s may still have the original gearing and +55 mm wheels. Check the window sticker or the build data.

Spotting the real thing

Sixth-gen trims are mostly about suspension, tires and tops. Aftermarket 35s and badges are easy to add, so check what you're actually buying.

  1. ID
    Real Sasquatch315/70R17 tires on 17x8.5, +30 mm wheels and 4.7 axle gears. Standard wheels are +55 mm. Confirm with the window sticker or build data.
  2. ID
    Bronco RaptorFour-door only. 'FORD' spelled across the grille (other trims say BRONCO), an 85.7-inch-wide body with extra marker lights, 37-inch tires, Fox suspension and a 3.0 V6 with 418 hp.
  3. ID
    Suspension levelBadlands (and Sasquatch) trucks have position-sensitive Bilstein dampers (HOSS 2.0) and a hydraulic front sway-bar disconnect. Wildtrak trucks have Fox dampers (HOSS 3.0). From 2025, Badlands buyers could add the full HOSS 3.0 package.
  4. ID
    First Edition2021 only: Badlands mechanicals with Wildtrak exterior styling and the Outer Banks interior, and 35s standard.
  5. ID
    Which topOriginal molded-in-color, replacement 'MIC 2.0', or painted (2023 Heritage editions, then optional from 2025). A crazed or delaminated MIC top is a recall and program candidate.

THE FIXLook over every panel in daylight for crazing and delamination. SYMPTOMSA bent tie rod after a hard hit on the trail, toe out of spec, the steering wheel off-center, or a tie rod that has failed outright on a lifted truck with big tires.
THE FIXFord's own answer was stronger hardware. The 2024 Badlands and Sasquatch got the Wildtrak's HOSS 3.0 steering gear, and the 2025 HOSS 3.0 option added upgraded inner and outer tie rods with the Raptor's severe-duty rack. To replace a tie rod, mark or count the turns on the old outer end, separate it from the knuckle, unscrew the inner rod from the rack under the boot, fit the new parts to the same length and torque them, then get an alignment.

Fit 35s to a non-Sasquatch Bronco →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · A weekend with a level kitSHOP COST · Wheels, tires and a level kit; regearing extra
SYMPTOMSYou want 35s, but your truck has +55 mm wheels and stock height.
THE FIXPerformance Plus Tire's guide splits it by tire width. A 285/75R17 (about 33.8 in) fits stock wheels and suspension without rubbing. Narrow 35s (35x10.50R17 or 35x11.50R17) fit factory 17-inch wheels once the front crash bars come off, usually with a small 1.25–1.5-inch level. Asked on every forum

Bronco (sixth generation) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

On stock wheels and suspension, a 285/75R17 (about 33.8 in) with no rubbing reported. Narrow 35s (35x10.50R17 or 35x11.50R17) fit factory 17s once the front crash bars are removed, usually with a small level of 1.25–1.5 in. Full-width 35x12.50R17s need about +30 mm wheels, high-clearance flares and roughly 1.5–2 in of lift. The factory way to get 35s is the Sasquatch package (315/70R17); the Raptor runs 37s.

SOURCE · PERFORMANCE PLUS TIRE · MOTORTREND
2.3 or 2.7?

The 2.3 four (300 hp, 325 lb-ft) is the only engine with the manual and the more economical one: the EPA rates a 2021 at 21 mpg combined, against 19 for the 2.7. The 2.7 V6 (330 hp, 415 lb-ft) is automatic-only, and 2021–22 versions are covered by the intake-valve recall. Both ratings are on premium fuel; on regular, Ford quotes 275 and 315 hp.

SOURCE · CAR AND DRIVER · EPA
Is the seven-speed manual really a seven-speed?

Not quite. It's a Getrag six-speed with an extra crawler gear marked 'C', down and left of reverse in a dog-leg. It's for rock crawling and steep ramps, and with the advanced transfer case Ford quoted a 95:1 crawl ratio. About 15% of early buyers chose it.

SOURCE · MOTORTREND · WIKIPEDIA
Is the Sasquatch package worth it?

If you off-road, yes. You get 35-inch tires, 4.7 gears, position-sensitive Bilstein dampers, the sway-bar disconnect, ground clearance up to 11.6 in and much better angles. The EPA cost is real: a 2021 2.3 automatic Sasquatch is rated 18 mpg combined against 21 for the standard truck. Adding the same parts later costs more than the package.

SOURCE · MOTORTREND · EPA
How much can it tow?

3,500 lb for the standard Bronco, the same as a Wrangler. The Bronco Raptor is rated at 4,500 lb.

SOURCE · MOTORTREND · WIKIPEDIA
Two-door or four-door?

The two-door (100.4 in wheelbase) is shorter, lighter and has the better breakover angle (29.0° against 26.3° on 35s). The four-door (116.1 in) has the rear doors, more cargo room, a 21.1-gallon tank instead of 17.4, offered the soft top at launch, and is the only body offered as a Raptor.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · MOTORTREND · BRONCO6G
How do the doors and roof come off?

Lower the window, slip on the door bag, unplug the connector and remove one bolt from each hinge. The doors are frameless and the mirrors stay on the cowl. The two roof panels over the front seats release with quarter-turn latches and stow on board in bags. On four-doors a third panel over the rear seat also comes off but doesn't stow on board, and the remaining rear section is small enough to handle easily.

SOURCE · MOTORTREND
Which years should I buy or avoid?

Avoid 2021–22 unless the paperwork is perfect: CarGurus says to steer clear of both, and they carry the 2.7 intake-valve recall (24V-635) and the 2026 molded-in-color roof-panel recall. CarGurus calls 2023 the best year, with no investigations and fewer complaints, as long as its recalls are fixed. Later trucks get MIC 2.0 or painted tops, the HOSS 3.0 steering gear on 2024 Badlands and Sasquatch, and a full HOSS 3.0 option for 2025, but 2024–25 trucks have their own front-suspension fastener recalls, so check any year's VIN. Jalopnik, citing Bronco Nation, notes relatively little discussion of failures on the 2.3 four.

SOURCE · CARGURUS · NHTSA · JALOPNIK
What oil does it take, and how much?

SAE 5W-30 synthetic blend meeting WSS-M2C961-A1. The 2.3 takes 6.2 qt with the filter (Motorcraft FL-910-S). The 2.7 is listed at 7 qt (FL-2062-A), but owners on Bob Is The Oil Guy report a later Ford bulletin giving 6 qt for a drain and fill, so fill to the dipstick. The 10-speed takes Mercon ULV and the transfer case Mercon LV.

SOURCE · BRONCO6G · BITOG
Is it reliable?

So far Consumer Reports calls it average, according to Jalopnik, which counted about 20 recalls by September 2026. Most problems were in the 2021–22 trucks: hardtops, 2.7 valves, rear shocks, manual-transmission noise. Bronco Nation's view is that tie-rod failures are rarer than the internet suggests and usually involve lifts, big tires or hard driving.

SOURCE · JALOPNIK · BRONCO NATION
What's different about the Bronco Raptor?

It has a 3.0 twin-turbo V6 (418 hp), Fox suspension, 37-inch tires, a body 85.7 in wide, 13.1 in of ground clearance, F-150 Raptor brakes, a reinforced bumper and frame, and 'FORD' on the grille. It's four-door only, tows 4,500 lb and the EPA rates a 2023 at 15 mpg combined.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · EPA
