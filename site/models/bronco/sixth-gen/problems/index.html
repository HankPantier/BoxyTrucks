Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

6th Gen Ford Bronco problems to check before you buy

  1. Run the VIN before anything elseThere are many open-recall possibilities. The big ones are the 2.7 intake valves (24V-635), the rear shock reservoirs (25V-025), 2021–22 molded-in-color roof panels (26V-299, remedy expected November 2026), engine-bay wiring (26V-468), fender flares (26V-403) and, on 2024–25 trucks, loose front control-arm and ball-joint fasteners (24V-770, 25V-260, 25V-308, 25V-310). Get a printout from NHTSA's VIN lookup.
  2. Look hard at the hardtopMolded-in-color tops were standard on 2021–23 trucks and most 2024s. Crazing or delamination means the top needs replacing: Ford's August 2021 program fitted 'MIC 2.0' tops, and some owners are on their third or fourth. Painted tops arrived for 2025. Check the headliner and seals for leaks and make sure every panel latches.
  3. 2.7 V6: ask about the engine testRecall 24V-635 covers 2021–22 2.7 and 3.0 Nano EcoBoost engines whose intake valves can break. Dealers run an engine cycle test and replace the engine if needed. Bronco Nation names V6s built from May 1 to October 31, 2021 as the bad batch. Get proof the test was done.
  4. Rear shocks on Badlands and SasquatchBilstein rear shocks on trucks built from September 23, 2020 to July 23, 2024 can corrode until the external reservoir detaches (25V-025). Look at the reservoirs and their mounts and confirm the replacement was done.
  5. Drive the manual through every gearOn 2021–23 seven-speed trucks, a scraping noise in neutral, first or second is the subject of a Ford service bulletin. Check for clean engagement of the crawler gear and reverse.
  6. Steering and tie rods on modified trucksOwners have reported tie rods failing during hard off-road use, usually on lifted trucks with bigger tires. Inspect the inner and outer tie rods for bends. The 2023 Wildtrak and 2022 Raptor had a steering-gear recall (23V-155) for internal damage that could lock the wheel.
  7. Was it flat-towed?2021–22 trucks had a neutral-tow problem: Ford issued a temporary fix in May 2022 and an over-the-air update by March 2023. Bronco Nation warns that flat-tow damage to the transmission can be hard to get covered under warranty, so ask how the truck traveled behind a motorhome.
  8. Is it a real Sasquatch?Factory Sasquatch trucks have 315/70R17 tires on 17x8.5, +30 mm wheels and 4.7 gears. A truck that only has aftermarket 35s may still have the original gearing and +55 mm wheels. Check the window sticker or the build data.

6th Gen Ford Bronco recalls and service bulletins

  • 2.7/3.0 Nano EcoBoost intake valves may break ↗ 2021–2022 · NHTSA 24V-635Broken intake valves can cause engine failure; dealers run an engine cycle test and replace the engine as needed (Ford 24S55).
  • Rear shock reservoirs can corrode and detach ↗ 2021–2024 · NHTSA 25V-025Dealers replace the rear shocks (Ford 25S01); owner letters mailed August 2025.
  • Molded-in-color hardtop roof panels can separate ↗ 2021–2022 · NHTSA 26V-299Sections of the outer roof panel can detach; dealers will inspect and replace tops, with the remedy expected November 2026 (Ford 26S32).
  • Engine-compartment wiring harness can short ↗ 2021–2026 · NHTSA 26V-468Dealers add protective sheathing; phased campaign, Raptors first, then all other Broncos from about September 2026.
  • Steering gear internal damage ↗ 2022 Raptor · 2023 Wildtrak · NHTSA 23V-155Increased steering effort or wheel lock-up possible; owners told not to drive until the steering gear is replaced.
  • Transmission-to-transfer-case joint misaligned after a previous repair ↗ 2021–2025 · NHTSA 26V-236Can wear the output and input splines; dealers inspect and replace the transmission and transfer case as needed.
  • Front upper control-arm ball-joint nut loose or missing ↗ 2024–2025 · NHTSA 24V-770 · 25V-260 · 25V-310The upper arm can separate from the knuckle; dealers inspect and tighten, or replace the ball joint and knuckle.
  • Front lower control-arm fasteners not tightened ↗ 2024–2025 · NHTSA 25V-308The arm can detach from the frame; owners told not to drive until it has been inspected.
  • Fender flares may detach ↗ 2022–2026 · NHTSA 26V-403Dealers inspect and repair or replace the flares.
  • Windshield not properly bonded ↗ 2022 (and 2025, 25V-683) · NHTSA 22V-451The windshield could detach in a crash; dealers remove and reinstall it.
  • Transmission park pawl damaged in assembly ↗ 2023 · NHTSA 23V-546Rollaway risk; dealers replace the transmission.
  • Low-pressure fuel pump may fail ↗ 2021–2023 · NHTSA 25V-455Can stall the engine; PCM software update on the Bronco.
  • Passenger airbag vent may open early ↗ 2021 · NHTSA 21V-770Dealers replace the passenger airbag.
  • Engine may fail (2025–26) ↗ 2025–2026 · NHTSA 26V-343Dealers will replace the long block; remedy expected November 2026.
  • Seven-speed manual scraping noise in neutral, 1st and 2nd · MC-10237783-0001Covers some 2021–23 manual trucks.
  • Soft-top adhesive issues · MC-10230778-0001Covers some 2021–23 soft tops.
  • Molded-in-color hardtop replacement (customer-satisfaction program) Announced August 2021; crazed or delaminated tops replaced with 'MIC 2.0' tops.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about 6th Gen Ford Bronco reliability

Which years should I buy or avoid?

Avoid 2021–22 unless the paperwork is perfect: CarGurus says to steer clear of both, and they carry the 2.7 intake-valve recall (24V-635) and the 2026 molded-in-color roof-panel recall. CarGurus calls 2023 the best year, with no investigations and fewer complaints, as long as its recalls are fixed. Later trucks get MIC 2.0 or painted tops, the HOSS 3.0 steering gear on 2024 Badlands and Sasquatch, and a full HOSS 3.0 option for 2025, but 2024–25 trucks have their own front-suspension fastener recalls, so check any year's VIN. Jalopnik, citing Bronco Nation, notes relatively little discussion of failures on the 2.3 four.

SOURCE · CARGURUS · NHTSA · JALOPNIK
Is it reliable?

So far Consumer Reports calls it average, according to Jalopnik, which counted about 20 recalls by September 2026. Most problems were in the 2021–22 trucks: hardtops, 2.7 valves, rear shocks, manual-transmission noise. Bronco Nation's view is that tie-rod failures are rarer than the internet suggests and usually involve lifts, big tires or hard driving.
