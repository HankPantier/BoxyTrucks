Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

Unimog 404 problems to check before you buy

  1. Drain the gear oils before you payExpedition Portal owners say to pull the plugs on the hubs, diffs and gearbox and look for tar or metal bits, and to check the tires for oil flung from leaking portal seals. Seals between the axle and the portal hub can blow and, if you keep driving, take the gears with them.
  2. Check the front diff for too much oilRocky Mountain Moggers point out that a leaking transmission output seal doesn't show outside: the oil runs down inside the drive-shaft tube and overfills the differential. A front diff that drains more oil than it should points to the gearbox seal.
  3. Look at the brakes, wheel by wheelBring a Trailer listings regularly mention a leaking wheel cylinder or a recent brake overhaul. Look for fluid on the backing plates and inside the rims, and check the pedal. Rebuilt cylinders and new hoses are a good sign, not a red flag.
  4. Listen to the clutch and throw-out bearingA squeaking throw-out bearing is a warning; caught early it can sometimes be greased in place, and if not it's a big job. Expedition Imports also traced a run of pressure-plate failures to puck-style clutch discs installed the way the old manual shows. One veteran suggests a written promise of a refund if the engine, gearbox, clutch or throw-out bearing turns out bad.
  5. Be suspicious of 'ran when parked'A Steel Soldiers member was offered two parked 404s at $8,500 each and found stale fuel, old tires, leaking pinion seals and portal hubs, a rotted bed and a torn top. Expedition Imports has photographed cylinder-head damage in an engine that sat unused for eight years. Long storage usually means fuel, brakes, seals and canvas.
  6. Find out where it servedMost US trucks are ex-military. One Steel Soldiers member warns against French trucks as abused and poorly maintained, and says Swiss trucks are usually well kept (you can tell them by the taller cab cover). Belgian and Swiss service histories show up often in auction listings.
  7. Inspect the cylinder head and fuel tanksExpedition Imports says the most common M 180 head failure is a water jacket that rots out close to the head-gasket seal, so look for coolant in the oil. Rust or orange flakes in the fuel filters mean the tank lining is peeling.
  8. Know what voltage you're buyingMilitary 404s run 24 volts with shielded, radio-suppressed ignition. That's fine once you know it, but it changes the bulbs, starter, battery and any accessories you add. Check that it charges, and ask whether the ignition has already been converted.

Unimog 404 recalls and service bulletins

  • 404 pressure-plate failures with puck-style clutch discs ↗ · Expedition Imports EICSB101Fit the puck disc with its protruding hub towards the engine, and adjust the pedal so the finger springs can't touch the spinning disc. The workshop manual's diagram only applies to the original organic disc.
  • Hub reduction output seal installed flush ↗ · Expedition Imports tech noteThe 140 mm seal must sit 5 mm inside the housing face so its second lip rides on the wear ring; shops often set it flush.
  • Short-reach vs long-reach spark plugs ↗ · Expedition Imports tech noteOld-style M 180 heads take short-reach plugs and new-style heads long-reach. A long plug in an old head damages the threads.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.
