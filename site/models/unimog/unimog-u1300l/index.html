PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The U1300L is what you get when Mercedes asks its farm-machinery department for an army truck. Under the square cab are coil-sprung portal axles, locking differentials, a cranked frame that twists over ruts, and an eight-speed gearbox that runs from 0.15 to 80 km/h. Gaggenau built 30,726 trucks in the 435 series from 1975 to 1993. More than 21,000 were U 1300 Ls, many of them for the Bundeswehr and German fire brigades. Today they come out of military surplus and into the hands of people building campers that can cross the Sahara. You won't do 70 mph, but you can drive almost anywhere.

THE HEAVY SERIES

Daimler-Benz showed its new heavy series, the SBU, at the 1974 DLG exhibition. Its angular cab won the Federal Design Award, and Mercedes says the look still defines the Unimog today. The bigger change was underneath. More powerful engines needed a new drivetrain, and new noise rules meant the engine and gearbox were no longer bolted together. Gaggenau developed an eight-speed gearbox with double reduction, plus a crawler gear and power take-offs. The cranked U-section frame came from the Unimog S, the 404. Mercedes says it gave excellent torsional flexibility off-road.

Production started in 1975 with the U 1300 and the long-wheelbase U 1300 L. The 435 used the 425's cab but had a longer 3,250 mm wheelbase and bigger engines. The U 1300 L got a 5.7-litre direct-injection six making 96 kW (130 PS) and 363 N·m; Mercedes' operator's manual calls it the OM 352. The turbocharged U 1700 L made 124 kW and 520 N·m and could carry up to 5 tonnes. From 1987 the 6.0-litre OM 366 replaced the older engine. Brakes are air-over-hydraulic discs, and the front axle, transfer case and lockers are all engaged with air.

LKW 2 T TMIL GL

The Bundeswehr adopted the 435 in 1978 as the 'Lkw 2 t tmil gl', its second-generation standard utility truck, replacing the 1.5-tonne Unimog. About 18,000 were delivered. They served as troop and cargo carriers, engineer flatbeds, radio and field-office trucks, repair-crew vehicles and ambulances, and as tractors for 1.5-tonne trailers and towed weapons of up to 2.25 tonnes. Other NATO forces used them too, including US forces stationed in Germany.

Army spec explains a lot of what you'll find today. One Steel Soldiers member selling ex-US Army Europe trucks described them as 24-volt, with no hydraulic system and basic trim. Their contract plates let you trace a truck's service history. Military trucks were rated at 1,500 kg of payload on a 5,760 kg truck, with an 800 km range and a top speed of 80 km/h. Ex-Bundeswehr ambulances and cargo trucks, plus the odd ex-fire-brigade truck, turn up regularly at US auctions.

SAND, CREW CABS AND CAMPERS

Civilian buyers found other uses. From 1983 Wackenhut of Nagold built crew cabs for the U 1300 L that seated up to seven, and Mercedes says they were popular for expeditions and research trips. In 1985 Karl-Friedrich Capito and his sons Jost and Volker won the truck class of the 14,000 km Paris–Dakar with converted U 1300 Ls. The rally trucks made 180 hp and had roll bars and six extra Hella lamps.

The camper scene picked it up from there. The 3,250 mm wheelbase takes a decent box, the portal axles give 440 mm of ground clearance, and the angles are 46 degrees in front and 51 at the rear. Plenty of builds end up too heavy, though. One HUBB contributor warns that many U1300L campers exceed the 7,500 kg gross weight once loaded for travel. Weigh the truck as you'll drive it, not as it's listed.

SUCCESSION AND THE SURPLUS ERA

Mercedes rebuilt the Unimog range in 1988. The new heavy 437 series had the UG 3/65 gearbox and charge-air-cooled OM 366 A and LA engines, and stayed on sale until 2002. The 435 continued alongside it until 1993. Its successors are faster, quieter and more comfortable, and they cost more. They're also further from the simple, mechanical truck that owners like about the 435.

In the US the U1300L now sits between military surplus and a collector truck. Bring a Trailer has sold plain ex-army trucks for around $30,000 in 2026 and finished expedition campers for more than $170,000. It shares its portal-axle thinking with the Hummer H1 and the G 500 4×4², and it was a Cold War contemporary of the Pinzgauer. It's slower than all three.

SIDEBAR · PARIS–DAKAR 1985

The Capito family's desert win

Karl-Friedrich Capito's team just missed the 1984 finish because of a minor defect. For 1985 their U 1300 L was fully overhauled at the Gaggenau factory. The rally truck made 180 hp and had a reinforced cab with roll bars and six extra 750-watt Hella headlamps.

Mercedes credits the coil-sprung axles and low weight for its speed across the scree desert. Its low height also got it through a jungle section faster than some rivals. Capito and his sons Jost and Volker won the truck competition over 14,000 km from Versailles to Dakar.

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The 435 is a heavy-series Unimog. It has a flexible cranked ladder frame and coil-sprung portal axles at both ends, and the eight-speed gearbox is mounted separately from the engine to cut noise and vibration. Compressed air does a lot of the work: it engages the front axle and the lockers, assists the clutch and actuates the hydraulic disc brakes. The engine is a mechanical, direct-injection Mercedes truck six with no electronics.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Portal hub oil levelOwners call the portals the key weak point. Oil can migrate out of a hub, the left front especially, and starve the bearings. Pull the fill and drain plugs on all four, check the level and look for metal on the magnet. Budget for bearings and seals if you find either problem (Iain_U1250, Expedition Portal).
  2. 02
    Breather pumping and diff overfillOn 6-lug trucks like the U1300, the front portal breather hose can pump hub oil into the differential. Owners report the same thing at the rear. If a differential is overfull and a hub is low, that's the cause. Ask whether separate breathers or a vent fix have been fitted (m37charlie, HUBB; Hodakaguy, Expedition Portal).
  3. 03
    GearboxA bad transmission can cost as much as the truck (gdict, Steel Soldiers), though one UK owner quotes gearbox rebuilds at about £5,000 (Pictish, HUBB). Drive it through all eight gears and both directions, cold and hot, and listen for grinding or jumping out of gear. If it ever needs a tow, go gently: one owner notes the gearbox oil pump sits high in the case.
  4. 04
    Which engine is fittedCheck whether it's the naturally aspirated 5.7 OM 352, the turbo OM 352A or the 6.0 OM 366/366A. Filters, injector nozzles, water pumps and manuals all differ. Repowers and replacement turbos are common, so read the engine plate rather than trusting the ad.
  5. 05
    Military spec vs civilianEx-army trucks are 24-volt and basically trimmed, and ex-US Army Europe trucks were described as having no hydraulic system (Steel Soldiers). Check for any PTO, hydraulic or crawler-gear equipment you need. Contract plates help trace a military truck's history.
  6. 06
    Paint and rustBaT listings for 435s have shown bubbling at the windshield base and on tailgates and bed hinges. Look under the cab mats and at the bed floor too. A fresh green respray can hide a lot.
  7. 07
    Air system and brakesThe brakes, front axle engagement and lockers all depend on compressed air. Make sure the system builds pressure quickly and holds it overnight, that both lockers engage and release, and that the air dryer cartridge isn't years old. Soft brake-pad compounds chatter less than the factory pads, according to Expedition Imports.
  8. 08
    Weight and registrationIf it's a camper, weigh it. Many builds exceed the 7,500 kg gross weight. Check how the title describes the truck (truck, motorhome or exempt vehicle) and how your state registers a 7.5-tonne air-braked import before you pay.

Spotting the real thing

Ex-army trucks have been repowered, re-cabbed and converted, and several 435 variants look almost the same. Use the type number, the engine plate and the axles to work out what you're looking at.

  1. ID
    Type number in the chassis numberThe chassis number starts with the type: 435.115 is the U 1300 L, 435.117 the U 1300 L/37, 435.110 the U 1700, 435.111 the U 1700 L and 435.113 the U 1700 L/38. Early trucks read like 43511510…; later ones carry a 17-digit WDB4351151W… VIN.
  2. ID
    Cab number plateThe 435 uses the 425-series cab, which carries a body-number plate reading 425.820. The 435 always has a full cab; a convertible cab points to a different series, such as the Turkish-built 436.
  3. ID
    EngineThe naturally aspirated 5.7 OM 352 (catalogued as OM 353.959/.961) was standard. The turbo OM 352A and the 6.0 OM 366/366A followed, the OM 366 from 1987. Turbo engines take different injector nozzles, and naturally aspirated U1300Ls take their own water pump.
  4. ID
    6-lug vs 10-lug axlesU 1300 Ls run 6-lug disc-brake axles with M18 x 1.5 studs. U 1700 types use 10-lug axles, which owners say don't have the 6-lug trucks' front breather pumping problem.
  5. ID
    Military specBundeswehr and US Army Europe trucks are 24-volt and basically trimmed, and some have no hydraulic system. Military contract plates let you trace a truck's service history.

Importing & legality

Nearly every 435 in the US is a used import, usually ex-Bundeswehr. Military trucks reach the market through German federal surplus sales (VEBEG) and European dealers. One Steel Soldiers member found trucks at VEBEG for around €12,000–15,000, but warned about language, inspection, shipping of $5,000 or more, and customs. RoRo shipping is priced by volume, and a container may mean taking the wheels off.

The 435 was built until 1993, so every one is now past NHTSA's 25-year exemption. In older threads, owners noted that not all U1300Ls were DOT-exempt when younger, and that exemption usually depended on age or a camper conversion. US-titled trucks on Bring a Trailer have carried clean titles from Montana, Washington and Mississippi, but registration of a 7.5-tonne air-braked truck varies by state. Ask your DMV before you buy.

Members of the same thread said US prices for nice trucks ran about $25,000–60,000, and that after fees and paperwork, buying in Europe can cost about the same as buying domestically, with more hassle. Recent BaT results bear that out at the lower end.

After you buy it

Most 435s in the US have spent years in a German army depot and then a container. Expect old fluids, aged tires and seals that have sat dry. Get the operator's manual for your engine first; Mercedes printed it in English. Then change every fluid before you drive it far, as one owner did before a 1,650 km trip home.

Asked on every forum

Unimog U1300L (435) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

Most U1300Ls in the US run 20-inch Continental MPT 81s or Michelin XZLs in 335/80R20 (12.5R20, about 40.6 in tall) or 365/80R20 (14.5R20, about 42.9 in), and several BaT trucks wear 365/80R20 MPT 81s. One builder chose 365s with fast axles to keep first gear low enough for trails. A 395/85R20 is too big for a U1300 without a cab lift, according to one Expedition Portal member, and it also loads the portal bearings, which are expensive.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL · EXPEDITION IMPORTS · BAT
How fast will it go on the highway?

The Bundeswehr quotes 80 km/h (50 mph). Depending on gearbox and portal ratios, top speed runs from 80 to 110 km/h. Owners say fast axles alone aren't enough: one says a fast-axle truck without an overdrive turns about 3,000 rpm at 70 mph, while another says fast axles manage 60 mph. A U1700 camper on 395s held about 55 mph. Cruising at around 65 km/h is more typical with a loaded camper.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · MINIATURES.DE · EXPEDITION PORTAL · HUBB
What fuel economy should I expect?

Wikipedia gives 17.5–19 L/100 km (about 12–13 mpg US) for the U 1300 L. In practice, one owner averages about 11 mpg and another estimates 20–30 L/100 km (8–12 mpg) for a loaded truck, more in sand. Military range was 800 km.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · HUBB · EXPEDITION PORTAL
Why do the hubs lose oil?

At sustained speed the portal gears can pump oil out through the breather, and on 6-lug axles like the U1300's, the front breather hose can feed hub oil into the differential. The fixes owners use are separate breathers or a vent fix, auxiliary reservoirs, new inner seals, and draining and refilling the portals (about 300 ml each) at every oil change. Some owners say the problem is overblown, citing hubs past 300,000 miles with one seal change.

SOURCE · HUBB · EXPEDITION PORTAL
What oil goes in the gearbox?

Mobil's table for U1000–U1700 transmissions lists a synthetic 75W-85 to MB sheet 235.4, and owners stress GL-4, not GL-5. The ex-Bundeswehr 'BW 435' is the exception: MB sheet 231.3 calls for 10W engine oil in that gearbox. Use GL-5 75W-90 in the differentials and portals.

SOURCE · BITOG
U1300L or U1700L?

The U 1700 L has a 124 kW turbo engine with 520 N·m against the base U 1300 L's 96 kW and 363 N·m. It sits on 10-lug axles, which one owner says don't suffer the breather-pumping problem of the 6-lug U1300. Another owner understood the U1700L to have reinforced axles and redesigned hubs, but says that's his reading of the sources rather than a verified fact. The U1300L was built in far greater numbers: 21,775 against 808 U 1700 Ls.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · HUBB · EXPEDITION PORTAL
OM 352 or OM 366?

The OM 366 replaced the older engine from 1987. The two share head bolts and head gaskets. Turbo OM 352A and OM 366A engines use their own injector nozzles. Owners who want 60–65 mph with a camper talk about an OM 366 turbo with an intercooler and fuel tuning, at around 200 hp.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · EXPEDITION IMPORTS · EXPEDITION PORTAL
Can I legally import and register one?

Every 435 is now past the US 25-year exemption: the last were built in 1993. BaT trucks carry clean titles from states including Montana, Washington and Mississippi. Registration rules still vary by state, so check how yours titles a 7.5-tonne truck before you buy. See the import section below for sources.

SOURCE · BAT · STEEL SOLDIERS
How much can it carry and tow?

As the Bundeswehr's Lkw 2 t tmil gl it was rated for 1,500 kg of payload and towed 1.5-tonne trailers or weapons of up to 2.25 tonnes. Gross weight is 7,500 kg, so weigh a camper conversion: many end up over the limit.

SOURCE · MINIATURES.DE · HUBB
Is it a good overland camper base?

For rough country, yes, but owners agree it's slow and noisy on tarmac and struggles in fast traffic. They recommend heavy sound insulation, air conditioning and better seats for ex-military trucks. A Vario or a MAN costs less if you don't need the Unimog's off-road ability.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL · HUBB
