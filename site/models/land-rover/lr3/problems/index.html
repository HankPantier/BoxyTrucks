Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

Land Rover LR3 problems to check before you buy

  1. TDV6 bottom endLand Rover bulletin SSM71816 covers crankshaft and main-bearing failures on the TDV6, caused mainly by bearing shells located incorrectly at assembly and by shells rotating in use. Listen for a rumble or knock that worsens under load, and an oil-pressure light at low revs. Failures are rare but very expensive, and the UK has a whole industry selling reconditioned TDV6 engines.
  2. Air suspensionRun it through every height with the engine on, check it sits level, and leave it overnight to see whether a corner sags. Leaks, height-sensor faults and tired compressors are the usual causes. A compressor that runs for a long time, overheats or slows before cutting out is on its way out.
  3. Electronic parking brakeCheck that it applies and releases cleanly, more than once. PistonHeads says the EPB often clogs with dirt and a replacement unit can cost over £1,000; owners of the closely related LR4 on LandRoverWorld describe $900–2,200 shop quotes for actuator and cable work.
  4. Front lower arms and bushesA 5,400 lb truck wears out its bushes, ball joints, wheel bearings and brakes. Look for split boots and oil weeping from the hydraulic rear bushes of the front lower arms, and budget for seized bolts if they need doing.
  5. ZF six-speed automaticFeel for judder and harsh shifts and ask when the fluid was last changed. Land Rover's long fluid interval was optimistic; PistonHeads suggests changing it about five times as often. Coolant can also reach the gearbox through the heat exchanger in the radiator, so look for milky fluid.
  6. TDV6 belts, flywheel and EGRAsk for proof of the timing belt, tensioner, pulleys and water pump; PistonHeads advises doing them within six years or 60,000 miles. Manual trucks can eat dual-mass flywheels and clutches at low mileage, and EGR valves often fail.
  7. 4.4 V8 coolingThe plastic thermostat housing and its coolant tube can crack and drip. Check around the front of the engine for dried coolant, and make sure the temperature gauge stays steady on a long run.
  8. Electrics, A/C and recallsExpect warning lights and test everything: central locking, windows, rear wiper, sat-nav, A/C (compressors and pipes corrode), battery and alternator. In the US, run the VIN for open recalls, including the 2005–06 steering-wheel clockspring campaign.

Land Rover LR3 recalls and service bulletins

  • Driver airbag clockspring wiring can fracture ↗ 2005–2006 · NHTSA 08V248000Relative motion between the steering-wheel hub, column cowl and clockspring could fracture the airbag wiring; 37,142 LR3 and Range Rover Sport vehicles covered.
  • Fuel tank internal breather pipe ↗ 2005 · NHTSA 05V376000A breather pipe grommet not made to specification can swell, crack and let the pipe detach, allowing fuel into the breather line.
  • Fuel tank breather pipe (follow-up) ↗ 2005–2006 · NHTSA 07V011000A further campaign on the same internal breather pipe and grommet, which can let excess fuel be discharged.
  • Ignition key removable out of Park ↗ 2005–2006 · NHTSA 06V131000After the ignition is switched off, the key could be removed with the shifter out of Park, failing FMVSS 114.
  • Automatic transmission parking pawl guide plate ↗ 2006 · NHTSA 05V502000Some vehicles were built with an incorrectly manufactured parking pawl guide plate.
  • Seat belt buckle mountings ↗ 2006 · NHTSA 05V501000Some vehicles may have incorrectly built seat belt buckle mountings.
  • TDV6 crankshaft and crankshaft bearing concerns ↗ · SSM71816Covers crank and main-bearing failures on the TDV6, caused mainly by incorrectly located bearing shells at assembly; symptoms include a seized engine, a holed block or sump, or a knock with the oil-pressure light.
  • AMK air suspension compressor upgrade ↗ The later AMK compressor (LR072537 kit) is a factory-approved replacement for the original Hitachi unit but needs a software update.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about Land Rover LR3 reliability

Is the TDV6 crank failure real?

Yes, though it's uncommon. Land Rover bulletin SSM71816 covers crankshaft and main-bearing failures, mainly caused by incorrectly located bearing shells at assembly. Symptoms are a knock or rumble that worsens under load, sometimes with the oil-pressure light. Carbuyer calls it rare, unpredictable and very costly.
