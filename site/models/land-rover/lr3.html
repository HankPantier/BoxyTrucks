PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

On 2 April 2004 Land Rover replaced the last Discovery that still rode on 1970 Range Rover bones with something completely new. The Discovery 3, sold in North America as the LR3, kept the family's stepped roof and upright glass but threw away the beam axles, coil springs and ladder-frame simplicity of the Discovery Series I and its successor. In their place came height-adjustable air suspension at all four corners, a body welded to its own chassis, and a rotary knob that told the truck what kind of ground it was on. Purists sneered. Then the reviews arrived.

A CLEAN SHEET FROM THE FORD YEARS

The Discovery 3 was the first Land Rover designed after the company and BMW parted ways in 2000, and it arrived almost four years after Ford bought the brand. It was revealed in April 2004 and made its international debut at the British International Motor Show that May. In North America and the Middle East it was badged LR3, partly because of negative quality associations with the Discovery name and, Land Rover said, an American preference for alphanumeric model names; the second Freelander became the LR2 there too.

Everything under the skin came from the Ford family. American trucks got a 300 hp 4.4-litre Jaguar-derived V8 or, in early years, a 216 hp 4.0-litre Ford V6. In Europe the big seller was meant to be the 2.7-litre TDV6, a Ford/PSA-developed V6 diesel with 195 hp and 440 Nm. The diesel came with a six-speed manual as standard; a ZF six-speed automatic was optional on the diesel and standard on the V8, and every version had a two-speed transfer box and permanent four-wheel drive.

INTEGRATED BODY FRAME AND CROSS-LINKED AIR

Land Rover called the new structure Integrated Body Frame. The engine bay and passenger cell are built as a monocoque, then joined to a basic ladder frame that carries the gearbox and suspension. Land Rover claimed the best of both worlds; critics pointed at a curb weight of 5,426 lb. It is heavier than a pure monocoque but adds strength and toughness, and Land Rover tucked the vital parts up into the body-frame to protect ground clearance.

The suspension is independent double wishbones on air springs, like the Range Rover L322. It can lower for loading, run low at speed and rise for clearance, from 7.3 in at standard height to 9.4 in at off-road height. Off-road, the air springs are cross-linked so that when one wheel drops the other rises, mimicking a beam axle. If the chassis touches the ground at off-road height, the system senses the lost load and lifts another inch. Britain and Europe also got a five-seat, coil-sprung base TDV6 without Terrain Response.

A KNOB THAT DOES THE THINKING

Terrain Response was the headline. Instead of asking the driver to choose gears, ride height, locking strategy and throttle response, the Discovery 3 offered a dial with settings that included Grass-Gravel-Snow, Sand, Mud and Ruts, and Rock Crawl. Land Rover said engineers studied more than 50 off-road surfaces to calibrate it. In Rock Crawl the suspension goes to full height with maximum articulation, the differentials lock and the driver is prompted to select low range; in Sand the traction control is primed for wheelspin and the throttle is sharpened.

Underneath, a computer-controlled, progressively locking centre differential kept torque flowing, and a similar locking differential was available for the rear axle. The press loved it. Richard Hammond called it the 'best 4x4 of all time', Australia's 4WD press named it 4WD of the Year, and Jeremy Clarkson drove one to the summit of Cnoc an Fhreiceadain near Tongue in northern Scotland, a 307 m hill no vehicle had reached before.

LIVING WITH ONE NOW

The bargain is obvious: Bring a Trailer LR3s have recently sold for between about $3,000 and $23,000, with a median near $9,000, for a seven-seat truck that will follow a 100 Series almost anywhere. The catch is everything that makes it clever. Air springs, compressors and height sensors age, the electronic parking brake jams, heavy weight eats front lower-arm bushes, and PistonHeads notes that in 2012 Warranty Direct logged more claims on the Discovery 3 than on any other used car from a European maker.

Diesel buyers outside the US also have to accept the TDV6's crankshaft and bearing failures, which Land Rover's own bulletin blames mainly on bearing shells located incorrectly at assembly. American trucks dodge that bullet with the V8, which drinks fuel at an EPA-rated 12–13 mpg in town but is the engine the US aftermarket knows best. Either way, an IIDTool or similar Land Rover diagnostic tool turns most of the scary warnings into Saturday jobs.

LEGACY

The Discovery 3 platform underpinned the Range Rover Sport, and in 2009 it became the Discovery 4 (LR4 in North America), which kept the Integrated Body Frame and added restyled lights, a new grille and smoother bumpers. Late Discovery 3s from 2008–09 had already gained body-colour bumpers, clear side indicators and a Harman/Kardon stereo upgrade. Today the LR3 is the cheapest way into air-sprung, Terrain Response Land Rover off-roading, with a busy aftermarket of lift rods, sliders and coil conversions behind it.

SIDEBAR · G4, TOP GEAR AND A ROBOT

The family seven-seater that drove itself to fourth place

Land Rover ran the Discovery 3 in its 2006 G4 Challenge alongside the Range Rover Sport, in standard mechanical form apart from added Land Rover off-road equipment. On television, Top Gear sent Jeremy Clarkson up Cnoc an Fhreiceadain in one, and Richard Hammond declared it the best 4x4 of all time.

The oddest entry on its CV is the DARPA Urban Challenge, where a driverless LR3 finished fourth. A plan to assemble Discovery 3s from knock-down kits at Ford's Vsevolozhsk plant in Russia never happened; it was cancelled in 2007.

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The Discovery 3 is built around Land Rover's Integrated Body Frame: a monocoque engine bay and cabin joined to a ladder frame that carries the gearbox and suspension. All four wheels hang on independent double wishbones with air springs, cross-linked off-road to behave like a beam axle. Drive goes through a two-speed transfer box (2.93:1 low) with a progressively locking centre differential, and an electronically locking rear differential was available. Terrain Response, Hill Descent Control, four-wheel electronic traction control and an electronic parking brake round out a truck that is as much a network of control modules as a 4x4.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    TDV6 bottom endLand Rover bulletin SSM71816 covers crankshaft and main-bearing failures on the TDV6, caused mainly by bearing shells located incorrectly at assembly and by shells rotating in use. Listen for a rumble or knock that worsens under load, and an oil-pressure light at low revs. Failures are rare but very expensive, and the UK has a whole industry selling reconditioned TDV6 engines.
  2. 02
    Air suspensionRun it through every height with the engine on, check it sits level, and leave it overnight to see whether a corner sags. Leaks, height-sensor faults and tired compressors are the usual causes. A compressor that runs for a long time, overheats or slows before cutting out is on its way out.
  3. 03
    Electronic parking brakeCheck that it applies and releases cleanly, more than once. PistonHeads says the EPB often clogs with dirt and a replacement unit can cost over £1,000; owners of the closely related LR4 on LandRoverWorld describe $900–2,200 shop quotes for actuator and cable work.
  4. 04
    Front lower arms and bushesA 5,400 lb truck wears out its bushes, ball joints, wheel bearings and brakes. Look for split boots and oil weeping from the hydraulic rear bushes of the front lower arms, and budget for seized bolts if they need doing.
  5. 05
    ZF six-speed automaticFeel for judder and harsh shifts and ask when the fluid was last changed. Land Rover's long fluid interval was optimistic; PistonHeads suggests changing it about five times as often. Coolant can also reach the gearbox through the heat exchanger in the radiator, so look for milky fluid.
  6. 06
    TDV6 belts, flywheel and EGRAsk for proof of the timing belt, tensioner, pulleys and water pump; PistonHeads advises doing them within six years or 60,000 miles. Manual trucks can eat dual-mass flywheels and clutches at low mileage, and EGR valves often fail.
  7. 07
    4.4 V8 coolingThe plastic thermostat housing and its coolant tube can crack and drip. Check around the front of the engine for dried coolant, and make sure the temperature gauge stays steady on a long run.
  8. 08
    Electrics, A/C and recallsExpect warning lights and test everything: central locking, windows, rear wiper, sat-nav, A/C (compressors and pipes corrode), battery and alternator. In the US, run the VIN for open recalls, including the 2005–06 steering-wheel clockspring campaign.

Spotting the real thing

The same truck wears two names and several powertrains depending on market, and a few European trucks lack the air suspension and Terrain Response that define the model.

  1. ID
    LR3 or Discovery 3LR3 is the North American and Middle Eastern badge; elsewhere it is the Discovery 3. US trucks are petrol only: 4.4 V8 throughout, 4.0 V6 for 2005–07.
  2. ID
    Engine badgesTailgate badges such as 'V8 SE' show the engine and trim. A diesel in the US is a private import.
  3. ID
    Coil-sprung baseThe UK/European base model has five seats, coil springs, the 2.7 TDV6 only and no Terrain Response. A missing Terrain Response dial on the console is the giveaway.
  4. ID
    Facelift (2008–09)Body-colour bumpers and clear side indicator lights replace the early trucks' black plastic trim and earlier indicators.
  5. ID
    Discovery 4 / LR4 lookalikesThe 2009-on Discovery 4 shares the body structure but has restyled front and rear lights with LEDs, a new grille and smoother bumpers.
  6. ID
    Compressor typeOriginal trucks have a Hitachi air compressor; a later AMK unit means it has been replaced and the software updated.

Asked on every forum

Land Rover LR3 / Discovery 3 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

On 18-inch wheels, 265/65R18 or 285/60R18 (about 31 in) fit at factory height without modification. 275/65R18 (about 32 in) is about the biggest that works at stock height, and owners report it needs the front-left height-sensor wire zip-tied to the frame rail and a little liner reshaping; one owner's 285/65R18s rubbed slightly at full lock. With lift rods, 275/65R18 is the common choice. One owner advises staying under 32 in overall.

SOURCE · EXPEDITION PORTAL / LANDROVERFORUMS
Is it body-on-frame?

Sort of. Land Rover's Integrated Body Frame builds the engine bay and cabin as a monocoque, then joins them to a basic ladder frame that carries the gearbox and suspension. It is heavier than a pure monocoque but stronger and tougher.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Which engine should I buy?

In the US the choice is the 300 hp 4.4 V8 (all years) or the 216 hp 4.0 V6 (2005–07). The V8 is the common one, shared with the 2006–09 Range Rover Sport and L322 Range Rover. Elsewhere most trucks have the 195 hp, 440 Nm 2.7 TDV6, which is far more economical but carries crank-bearing and timing-belt risks.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA / CARGURUS / JD POWER
Is the TDV6 crank failure real?

Yes, though it's uncommon. Land Rover bulletin SSM71816 covers crankshaft and main-bearing failures, mainly caused by incorrectly located bearing shells at assembly. Symptoms are a knock or rumble that worsens under load, sometimes with the oil-pressure light. Carbuyer calls it rare, unpredictable and very costly.

SOURCE · LAND ROVER SSM71816 / CARBUYER
How much can it tow?

7,716 lb (3,500 kg) braked and 1,654 lb (750 kg) unbraked, according to Land Rover's launch specifications.

SOURCE · LAND ROVER PRESS SPECS
What fuel economy should I expect?

EPA ratings for the V8 are 12–13 mpg city and 17 highway, depending on year; the V6 is rated 13/18 for 2005. In the UK, PistonHeads quotes 27.2 mpg (imperial) for the automatic TDV6 and 18.8 mpg for the V8.

SOURCE · FUELECONOMY.GOV / PISTONHEADS
How do I lift it?

The cheap route is lift rods, which replace the links on the factory height sensors so the air suspension sits about 2–2.5 in higher (Proud Rhino, Terrafirma and Lucky8 make them). A diagnostic tool such as Gap's IIDTool can change the programmed heights instead. If you've had enough of air, Terrafirma sells +2 in coil conversions and Old Man Emu coil springs for the LR3 are sold too.

SOURCE · LUCKY8 / GAP DIAGNOSTIC
Does it have locking differentials?

Every truck has a computer-controlled, progressively locking centre differential. A locking rear differential (Land Rover's electronic torque managed unit) was optional. Terrain Response controls how hard both lock in each mode.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA / LAND ROVER PRESS SPECS
What goes wrong with the air suspension?

Leaking air springs, height sensors, compressors and the electrics. A failing compressor overheats and slows; if ignored, the dryer desiccant can break down and damage the valve blocks. Many owners rebuild the Hitachi compressor; the later AMK unit needs a software update to fit.

SOURCE · CARBUYER / DISCO3.CO.UK
My parking brake is stuck. How do I release it?

PistonHeads says a seized EPB can be released manually using the jack handle through a metal loop behind a plastic panel. The underlying fault is usually the actuator or its cable adjustment, and dirt build-up is a known cause.

SOURCE · PISTONHEADS
What's the difference between an LR3 and a Discovery 3?

Nothing structural: LR3 is the North American and Middle Eastern name. The engines differ by market, with the V8 and V6 petrols in North America and the TDV6 diesel as the main engine elsewhere.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
How deep can it wade?

Land Rover's launch specification quotes a 700 mm (27.6 in) wading depth. Raise the suspension to off-road height before you go in.

SOURCE · LAND ROVER PRESS SPECS
