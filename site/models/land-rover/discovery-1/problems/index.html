Discovery 1 problems: 8 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the Land Rover Discovery Series I, the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Discovery 1 problems to check before you buy

  1. Sills, floors and inner wingsOn LandyZone the standard buying advice is a list of rust: sills, rear floor, boot floor, inner wings, then everything else. Three-doors are reported to be worse. Lift the carpets, check the boot floor and its support strap, and push on the sills where the body mounts sit.
  2. Rear chassis and body mountsLook at the mounts where the large rear crossmember meets the chassis rails, the rearmost body mounts and the box sections around the fuel tank. Galvanised replacement chassis exist, which tells you how often the originals give up.
  3. Sunroofs and headliningMost Disco 1s have two sunroofs, and they leak around the plastic surround and through split drain tubes. A sagging or stained headlining and wet carpets are the clues. A LandyZone member points to the 1998 Anniversary model as a way to dodge the sunroofs, and the US XD was built without them.
  4. Swivels and front endPitted swivel balls weep oil and chew seals; look for oil on the inside of the front wheels. Check the swivel ball part number too: owners report the superseded FTC5366 ball doesn't machine-match the earlier FRC7065.
  5. Driveline backlash (manual Tdi)A jerky take-off on a manual 300Tdi is often the rear propshaft's rubber coupler or worn splines. With the centre diff locked, turn the front and rear props by hand in opposite directions; there should be only a little movement.
  6. V8 cooling and idleOverheating V8s and Tdis can be an airlock, a failed cap or a head gasket. Bleed and test before condemning the engine. On 14CUX trucks, a hunting or high idle points at the idle air control valve or a vacuum leak.
  7. 300Tdi timing beltLike the Defender, the 300Tdi should have the revised timing-belt kit (Land Rover STC4096L-type) fitted. Ask for proof.
  8. Japanese importsJapanese-market re-imports are popular because they rot less, but LandyZone members note they're generally automatics and usually have sunroofs.

Discovery 1 recalls and service bulletins

  • Driver airbag can deploy from overheating rotary coupler ↗ 1994–1998 · NHTSA 98V040000A chafed cruise-control wire can overheat the steering-wheel rotary coupler and fire the driver's airbag; 54,488 Discoveries and Range Rovers covered.
  • Plastic fuel tank stress cracks ↗ 1994–1997 · NHTSA 04V203000Stress cracks can develop in the plastic fuel tank; 45,267 Discovery I and Range Rover Classic trucks covered.
  • Right front door may not latch fully ↗ 1995–1996 · NHTSA 96V247000The right front door can bounce back off its seals instead of latching; 20,889 trucks covered.
  • Driveshaft nuts replaced with wrong size at port ↗ 1995 · NHTSA 95V099000After a port-of-entry pinion-torque check, driveshafts were refitted with the wrong nuts; 155 vehicles covered.
  • Replacement rear driveshaft coupling (service part) ↗ 1995–1998 · NHTSA 12E010000Land Rover service part TVF100010 couplings made April–November 2011 were recalled; check any recently replaced rear coupler.
  • 300Tdi timing-belt modification kit ↗ Like the Defender, the 300Tdi should have the revised timing kit (STC4096L-type) with a flanged crank pulley and spaced idler. Ask for proof.

Owner questions about Discovery 1 reliability

Does it rust?

Yes, badly. The usual places are the sills, rear floor and boot floor, inner wings, body mounts and the rear chassis around the crossmember. Three-doors are reported to be worse.

SOURCE · LANDYZONE
Why do the sunroofs leak?

The plastic surround leaks where it meets the metal roof, the frame seal is flimsy, and the drain tubes and cups split with age. LandyZone owners call the drain installation 'appalling'. Resealing and new drains usually cure it.

SOURCE · LANDYZONE
