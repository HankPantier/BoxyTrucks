Best Land Rover Series & Defender years to buy, and which to avoid

7 generations of the Land Rover Series & Defender, and for each one the model years owners and specialists recommend, the ones with documented problems, and the recalls to check. Four of them have a clear sweet spot; the rest are best bought on condition.

Sweet spot years owners and specialists recommend · Beware years with documented problems · Recall years with a recall to check by VIN. Each one is sourced on the model's own guide.

Land Rover Series I–III

1948–1985

Depends what you want. Series Is are the collectors' trucks. Classic Car Hub calls an 88- or 109-inch Series IIA the sweet spot, with good parts supply and a well-documented spec. Octane says the Series III is by far the easiest to live with and recommends a short-wheelbase 2.25 petrol for most buyers, or a Stage 1 V8 if you want something special; Series123 notes the III's gearbox isn't as strong as the II's. Heritage Parts Centre also rates the 2.25 petrol the preferable engine.

  1. 1961Series IIA: 2.25 diesel; 2.6 six in the 109 from 1967. Classic Car Hub calls an 88 or 109 IIA the sweet spot of the Series rangeSweet spot
  2. 1971Series III: all-synchro gearbox, plastic grille and new dash. Octane calls it by far the easiest Series to live with and suggests a short-wheelbase 2.25 petrolSweet spot

Land Rover 90 / 110 / Defender

1983–2016

For a 25-year-legal import, the Tdi years. ECD rates the 300Tdi (1994–98) the top choice for longevity and the 200Tdi (1990–94) a close second, and Classic Car Hub says if you remember one engine number, make it 300Tdi. Early '10P' Td5s have known head and oil-pump-drive problems, so look for a 15P engine or proof of repair. Among the Puma trucks, PistonHeads points to a 2011. Whatever the engine, the chassis, bulkhead and rear crossmember decide what it's worth.

  1. 1990200Tdi direct-injection diesel (107 hp); 127 becomes the Defender 130. ECD calls the 200Tdi one of the most dependable engines ever fitted to a DefenderSweet spot
  2. 1994300Tdi and R380 gearbox; NAS Defender 90 (3.9 V8, manual) goes on sale. Specialists rate the 300Tdi the most reliable Defender engine; check the revised timing-belt kitSweet spot
  3. 1998–2002Early '10P' Td5s: oil-pump-drive and head-stud failures (fixed within two years) and cylinder heads that crack around the injectors. 15P engines and '52-plate-on trucks are fineBeware
  4. 2011PistonHeads' sweet spot among the Puma Defenders: a 2011 truck, 2.4 or the 2.2 that followed, for the best mix of 'sortedness'Sweet spot

Land Rover Discovery Series I

1989–1998

Condition first: Disco 1s rot in the sills, floors and rear chassis, so a solid truck of any year beats a shiny rotten one. After that, the 1994-on facelift with the 300Tdi is the one guides point to; Classic Car Hub calls it the more sensible choice and says it commands a premium over the V8. One LandyZone member's tip is a late ES (1997–98 model year), or a 1998 Anniversary for most of the kit without the leaky sunroofs. US trucks were all V8s.

  1. 1994Facelift: 300Tdi, 3.9 V8, stronger R380 manual, new dash with airbags. Guides call the 300Tdi the sensible choice, and it commands a premium over the V8Sweet spot

Land Rover Discovery Series II

1998–2004

There isn't an agreed best year. Wikipedia notes early Td5s (1998–2000) suffered oil-pump-drive and head-stud failures that were fixed within two years. In the US, an Expedition Portal buying guide says 2003–04 trucks were the worst for cracked oil pumps and more prone to loose liners, and a LandRoverForums thread gives the 2003–04 4.6 the worst reputation, though other owners there still vote for the 2004 for its facelift and diff-lock lever. Whatever the year, buy the one with head-gasket, cooling and chassis receipts.

  1. 1998–2000Early Td5 oil-pump drive and head-stud ('head shuffle') problems, fixed within two years; ACE pipe and idler-pulley recallsBeware
  2. 2003–04US owners report slipped and cracked liners on late 4.0 and especially 4.6 blocksBeware

Land Rover LR3 / Discovery 3

2004–2009 (US: 2005–2009)

Later is better, within reason. The 2005 LR3 has the most NHTSA complaints (96, against 8 for 2008 and 3 for 2009) and five recalls, and ReRev names 2005 the one to avoid and 2008–09 the most reliable. Raw complaint counts aren't adjusted for how many were sold, though, and PistonHeads still calls any phase-one Discovery 3 a high-risk purchase, so a thick service file beats a late build date.

  1. 2005 MYLR3 launches in North America with the 4.4 V8 or the 216 hp 4.0 V6. First-year trucks carry the most NHTSA complaints (96, against 8 for 2008) and five recalls; ReRev names 2005 the year to avoidBeware
  2. 2005–06 MYClockspring, fuel-breather and ignition-key recalls; check they were doneRecall
  3. 2008 MYUS V8 only; seven seats standard; facelift with body-colour bumpers, clear side indicators and Harman/Kardon upgrade. ReRev rates 2008–09 the most reliable LR3s, and they log the fewest NHTSA complaintsSweet spot
  4. 2009Final year; Discovery 4 / LR4 replaces it in September 2009Sweet spot

Land Rover LR4 / Discovery 4

2009–2016 (US: 2010–2016)

There's no clean answer. The 2010–13 V8s are the ones with standard low range, but treat every one as a timing-chain risk until you see the tensioner and lever invoice; the class-action settlement covered 2012–2014 5.0 trucks. The 2014–16 supercharged V6 avoids that problem and ReRev rates those years best, but low range and the rear locker came only with the Heavy Duty package, and the plastic cooling parts fail on both engines. Buy the records, then the year.

  1. 2010–13 MY5.0 V8 timing-chain tensioners and levers wear: bulletin LTB00474 covers 2010–12 trucks in VIN range AA513326–CA652214, revised parts start at VIN DA696315 (mid-2013), and a US class action covered 2012–14 5.0 trucks. Buy only with the invoiceBeware
