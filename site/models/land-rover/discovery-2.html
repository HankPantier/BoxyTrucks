PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Land Rover said the Series II had 720 differences from the Discovery 1, and every one of them seems to have a wire attached. Underneath it is still the old recipe: a steel ladder frame on a 100-inch wheelbase, a beam axle at each end, permanent four-wheel drive and a two-speed LT230 transfer box. On top sits a longer body, seven forward-facing seats, a new five-cylinder diesel and a stack of systems with acronyms. It was the last Land Rover SUV built on the bones of the 1970 Range Rover, and it is now one of the cheapest ways into a properly capable coil-and-axle Land Rover.

720 DIFFERENCES

The Series II arrived in autumn 1998, four years after BMW bought Rover Group. Every body panel changed except the outer skin of the rear door. The tail grew to make room for adults in all seven seats on the SE7, at the cost of a longer rear overhang and a slightly worse departure angle. The rear lights moved up above the window line, the paddle door handles went, and the wheel pattern changed from the Discovery 1's 5x6.5 in to 5x120 mm, the same as the Range Rover P38.

Under the skin the front axle swapped the old chrome swivel balls for upper and lower ball joints, again shared with the P38, and the rear axle was located by a Watts linkage. Some trucks got self-levelling rear air springs (SLS), and European type approval for the seven-seat version was only granted to air-sprung examples. Production ran at Solihull, with assembly also in Jordan and Malaysia.

TD5, THOR AND A LOT OF SENSORS

Diesel buyers got the Td5, a 2.5-litre five-cylinder with electronic unit injectors, a chain-driven camshaft and two oil filters, one of them centrifugal. It came from the Rover 'Storm' project, not from BMW, and made 136 hp in Discovery tune. Wikipedia records two early mechanical faults, a failing oil-pump drive and 'head shuffle' from weak studs, both fixed within two years. Owners will tell you about the third: oil wicking down the injector harness and into the ECU.

Petrol trucks got the 4.0-litre 'Thor' V8 from the P38, the same 3,946 cc as before but with a different crank, cross-bolted mains, new rods and pistons and Bosch management. American buyers only ever got V8 automatics, and from the 2003 model year the US truck switched to the 4.6. Then there was ACE, Active Cornering Enhancement: hydraulic rams on the anti-roll bars that can counter up to 1 g of lateral force. It made a tall truck corner flat, and it gave owners a whole new set of pipes to corrode.

THE MISSING LEVER AND THE 2003 FACELIFT

Early Series IIs still had a lockable centre differential in the transfer box, but Land Rover left out the linkage to work it, believing traction control and the new Hill Descent Control made it redundant. Owners disagreed. Fitting a Discovery 1 lever, or a kit such as Ashcroft's, became one of the classic Disco 2 upgrades, and the facelift brought the lever back as an option, standard on high-spec trucks.

The facelift (owners call it the D2a) arrived for 2003 with pocketed headlamps like the new Range Rover's, revised rear lamps with the indicators moved up out of the bumper, and the 4.6 V8 for North America. The Td5 had gone to Euro 3 spec in 2002, with an EGR system and smoother low-speed throttle. In 2003, Land Rover prepared 62 Discovery 2s for the first G4 Challenge, where competitors drove them through Las Vegas and Moab. The final trucks left Solihull in late May 2004 to make way for the Discovery 3.

WHY BUY ONE NOW

Because the hard parts are simple. Live axles, coil springs (or air springs that convert to coils in an afternoon), an LT230 that is shared with the Defender, and a chassis that takes 285/75R16s with about three inches of lift. The Td5 is slow in stock form but responds well to tuning, though Famous Four warns it is 'not quite a DIY Land Rover'; you need a diagnostic tool for some jobs. The V8 sounds lovely and drinks accordingly.

The catch is rust. The rear of the frame, around the fuel tank and the rear crossmember, rots from the inside out, and LandyZone members price a replacement chassis and the work at close to £10,000. Fresh under-seal can hide it, so get underneath with a hammer. A rust-free Disco 2 with documented head gaskets or injector loom work is a better buy than a shiny one with every option still fitted.

SIDEBAR · G4 CHALLENGE

Sixty-two Discoverys and a trip to Moab

When Camel Trophy ended, Land Rover launched its own adventure contest. The first G4 Challenge, in 2003, sent competitors from 16 nations across the US, South Africa and Australia over 28 days. Land Rover prepared 62 Discovery 2s for the event, 60 of them used on the main challenge, for the final stage through Las Vegas and the Moab desert.

Belgian fighter pilot Rudi Thoelen won. Offered a new Range Rover as the prize, he asked for two Defenders instead. It was a fitting send-off: the Discovery 2 was the last Discovery built on a separate ladder frame with a live axle at each end.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The Discovery Series II keeps the Range Rover Classic formula of a steel ladder frame on a 100-inch wheelbase, beam axles on coil springs and permanent four-wheel drive through an LT230 transfer box, but changes almost everything bolted to it. The front axle uses ball joints instead of swivel housings, the rear axle is located by a Watts linkage and can carry self-levelling air springs, and optional ACE fights body roll hydraulically. Engines, ABS, traction control, Hill Descent Control and suspension are all managed electronically, so a diagnostic tool such as a Nanocom or Hawkeye is part of the toolkit.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rear chassis and crossmemberThe back of the frame, around the fuel tank and the rear crossmember, rots from the inside out where mud and water collect. Look closely at the rear chassis legs, the crossmember and the suspension hangers, because owners report hangers collapsing. Replacement rear quarter and half chassis sections exist, and so do whole galvanised frames, which tells you how common this is.
  2. 02
    Sills, arches, boot floor and body mountsOne LandyZone member's under-seal inspection turned up rotten footwells, inner and outer sills, all four arches, the boot floor and both rear body mounts, with a £5,040 repair quote. Expect to find at least twice the rust you can see once you start cutting.
  3. 03
    Td5 head, fuel in the oilFamous Four says Td5 cylinder-head failure is now quite common and expensive. Check the dipstick: a level that is too high suggests diesel in the oil. Failed injector sealing washers cause the same symptom and are much cheaper to fix, so diagnose before you condemn the head.
  4. 04
    Oil in the ECU plugLift the ECU out of its tray by the battery and look at the connectors. Oil there means the injector harness seals under the rocker cover have failed, and in time that causes misfires and poor starting. The fix is a cheap harness, but neglected trucks may also have oil in the main engine loom.
  5. 05
    Diesel leak at the back of the Td5Diesel weeping from the rear of the cylinder head on the driver's side is usually the fuel pressure regulator. Fix it quickly: Famous Four warns that leaking fuel can drip onto the starter motor and kill it.
  6. 06
    V8 cooling and head gasketsAsk for head-gasket and cooling records. An Expedition Portal buyer's guide says gaskets often go between 50,000 and 70,000 miles and advises avoiding trucks with a compromised cooling system. Owners also report slipped or cracked cylinder liners, especially on 2003–04 4.6s, which mean an engine rebuild with top-hat liners or a replacement block.
  7. 07
    SLS, ACE and the 'three amigos'A tail that sags overnight means leaking SLS air springs. Oil on the chassis rails or around the anti-roll bars means ACE leaks, which Famous Four calls 'very expensive to rectify'. ABS, TC and HDC lights together (the 'three amigos') are often a dirty or failed wheel sensor, but can be a failing ABS modulator costing thousands.
  8. 08
    Centre diff lock and propshaftsPre-facelift trucks have the lock in the transfer box but often no lever, unless someone has fitted one. On the test drive, a severe shimmy points to worn propshaft joints; the front shaft's double-cardan joint has non-greasable UJs from the factory, and the rear rubber coupling cracks.

Spotting the real thing

A Disco 2 is easy to tell from a Disco 1, but which options it left the factory with (SLS, ACE, CDL lever, engine version) decides what you're buying.

  1. ID
    Series I or Series II?The Series II's rear stop lights sit above the window line (the Series I's are below), the paddle door handles are gone, the tail is longer, and the wheels are 5x120 instead of the Series I's 5x6.5 in.
  2. ID
    Pre-facelift or facelift (D2a)?Facelift trucks (2003 model year on) have 'pocketed' headlamps like the L322 Range Rover and revised rear lamps with the indicators moved up out of the bumper.
  3. ID
    Td5 10P or 15PLater Euro 3 Td5s (15P) have EGR and a fuel pressure regulator with an extra return pipe. Order Td5 parts by chassis number because many changed mid-run.
  4. ID
    SLS and ACESLS trucks have rear air springs instead of coils and a compressor in a plastic box on the left-hand chassis rail, level with the front seats. ACE trucks have a valve block on the right-hand chassis rail with steel lines running to the anti-roll bars.
  5. ID
    Centre diff lockLook for a diff-lock position on the transfer lever. Early trucks have the lock in the LT230 but no linkage unless one has been fitted from a Discovery 1 (matching manual or auto) or a kit.
  6. ID
    US truck or importUS-market Series IIs were all V8 automatics (4.0 for 1999–2002, 4.6 for 2003–04). A Td5 or manual in the US is a private import, so check the VIN, build date and paperwork.

Importing & legality

Land Rover only sold V8 automatics in the US, so a Td5 or a manual Discovery 2 there is a gray-market import. NHTSA lets a vehicle at least 25 years old, counted from its date of manufacture, be imported without meeting FMVSS. Series IIs were built from autumn 1998 to May 2004, so as of October 2026 trucks built before October 2001 qualify and later ones follow month by month. EPA Form 3520-1 code E covers vehicles at least 21 years old in original configuration.

Make sure the VIN, chassis number and build date on the plate agree, because the 25 years run from the build date, not the registration date. Check your state's titling and emissions rules before you buy, especially for a diesel in California.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

After you buy it

Change every fluid, and on a Td5 pull the ECU plugs to look for oil and decide whether to drop the sump for the oil-pump bolt. On a V8, refresh the cooling system before anything else. Do it on axle stands so you can inspect the rear chassis at the same time.

Loosen the top two bolts, unplug the sensor, remove the hidden bottom bolt with a long extension, then release the two quick-release fuel pipes and the last bolt. Don't lose the small gauze filter and O-ring behind the regulator. Fit a new gasket and O-ring, torque the three bolts to 25 Nm (18 lb-ft), reconnect and prime the system. Order the regulator by chassis number: the 2003-on version has an extra return pipe.

SLS air springs, or convert to coils
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–3 hoursSHOP COST · A pair of air springs, or a coil conversion kit
SYMPTOMSThe rear sags overnight or when parked, then pumps back up; the compressor runs for long periods. Ignored leaks burn out the compressor.
THE FIXChock the front wheels, support the chassis by the rear radius-arm mounts and the axle on jacks, and remove the wheels. Open the compressor box on the chassis rail and slowly crack the two outer air unions (not the middle one) to deflate. Pop the retaining clips, collapse the spring, undo its air union, rotate it 90 degrees and lift it out. Replace both sides. To convert instead, fit Discovery 2 (not Discovery 1) spring seats, coils and shocks, and have the SLS switched off with a diagnostic tool.

ACE leaks: pipes, filter or delete
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · Half a day to a weekendSHOP COST · Pipes are cheap; one owner's previous owner spent about $4,000 on the system
SYMPTOMSHydraulic fluid on the chassis or around the anti-roll bars, ACE warning, body roll returning. Pipes rust through and the aluminium valve block pits until its seals fail.
THE FIXTrace the leak: pump banjo, steel lines along the chassis, actuator seals or the valve block. Replace corroded pipes with new Dowty washers (an NHTSA recall had dealers re-torque or replace the pump banjo bolt). Change the high-pressure filter in the bottom of the valve block at 72,000 miles: heat the steel cap, use a 10 mm hex, pull the filter straight out with an M6 bolt, refit at 35 Nm. Refill with Land Rover PAS fluid STC50519, which ACE shares with the steering. Owners who give up fit standard anti-roll bars and a non-ACE belt.

Three amigos: ABS sensors and hubs
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–3 hoursSHOP COST · A sensor, or a front hub assembly (about £160–260, per discovery2.co.uk)
SYMPTOMSABS, TC and HDC warning lights on together, sometimes intermittently.
THE FIXRead the codes with a Nanocom, Hawkeye or similar to find the wheel. Clean the sensor and its tone ring, check the wiring and connector, and replace the sensor if needed. Play in a front wheel bearing can also trigger the lights; the front bearing is part of the hub assembly, and the hub nut is torqued to 490 Nm (360 lb-ft). If the codes point to the modulator, check for the 2004 NHTSA modulator-cover recall and change the brake fluid before replacing the unit.

V8 head gaskets and cooling refresh
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · A weekend, plus machine-shop timeSHOP COST · One US owner paid about $1,250 at an independent shop, including $640 labour and head skimming
SYMPTOMSCoolant loss, overheating at idle, coolant weeping at the head-to-block joint, mayonnaise under the filler cap.
THE FIXStrip the intake, rocker gear and heads; check the heads for warp and have them skimmed (one owner's warped heads needed 0.003–0.004 in taken off after 130,000 miles). Check the bores for a liner standing proud or cracks behind it before you spend money on gaskets. Refit with new composite gaskets and new head bolts. While it's apart, renew the thermostat, radiator, hoses and viscous fan; some owners fit the cooler Td5 thermostat and an OBD gauge, because they don't trust the dash gauge.

Rear chassis and crossmember repair
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · Several weekends, or a week on a rampSHOP COST · LandyZone members put a new chassis plus the work at close to £10,000; patch repairs much less
SYMPTOMSFlaking or holed metal at the rear crossmember, rear chassis legs and around the fuel tank; collapsed suspension hangers; MOT failures.
THE FIXDrop the fuel tank to see the real extent. Small holes can be plated, but most owners cut out the rear quarter or rear half and weld in a replacement section, or swap the whole frame for a galvanised one. Treat the inside of the rails with cavity wax afterwards, because that's where the rot starts.

Asked on every forum

Land Rover Discovery Series II questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

Discoweb owners recommend 245/75R16 for a stock-height truck. Several run 265/75R16 at stock height, but they rub at full lock on the plastic behind the fog lamps or need the front bumper trimmed. With a 2-inch lift such as the OME kit, 265/75R16 is the common choice; 285/75R16 wants nearer 3 inches and raises gearing and halfshaft-strength concerns.

SOURCE · DISCOWEB / LANDROVERFORUMS
Td5 or V8?

The Td5 makes its torque low down and responds well to tuning, but it needs a diagnostic tool for some jobs and has known weak spots: the injector harness, the fuel pressure regulator, the early oil-pump bolt and, increasingly, cylinder heads. The V8 is smoother and quicker than a stock Td5 but thirsty, and head gaskets and liners are its big risks. In the US every Disco 2 is a V8.

SOURCE · AULRO / FAMOUS FOUR
What changed with the 2003 facelift?

Pocketed headlamps, revised rear lamps with the indicators moved up out of the bumper, the centre diff lock lever back as an option (standard on high-spec trucks) and, in North America, the 4.6 V8 instead of the 4.0. Td5s had already gained Euro 3 tune and EGR in 2002.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Does my truck have a centre diff lock?

Every Series II has a lockable centre differential in the LT230, but early trucks were built without the lever. Owners fit a Discovery 1 linkage from a truck with the same transmission (manual and auto parts differ) or a kit such as Ashcroft's. On early trucks, don't cut the CDL input wire to the ABS ECU: the ECU uses it to stop traction control fighting the locked diff.

SOURCE · DISCOVERY2.CO.UK / LANDYZONE
Should I keep ACE?

Opinion is split. ACE fans say the truck corners far better and has more articulation off-road; others fit standard anti-roll bars once the pipes or valve block start leaking. Repairs to a tired system can run to thousands, so price it before you buy.

SOURCE · LANDROVERFORUMS
Can I convert the SLS to coils?

Yes, and it's a common job. Fit Discovery 2 rear spring seats (Discovery 1 seats are shallower), coils and matching shocks, or a ready kit from Terrafirma, Rovers North or Atlantic British. The SLS then needs switching off with dealer software or an aftermarket tool.

SOURCE · DISCOWEB / ATLANTIC BRITISH
What fuel economy should I expect?

The EPA rated a 2002 4.0 V8 automatic at 12 mpg city, 16 highway and 13 combined, and a 2003 4.6 at 11 city, 14 highway and 12 combined. Td5s do much better, though no US figure exists because they were never sold there.

SOURCE · FUELECONOMY.GOV
How much can it tow?

Australian specs for a 2001 Td5 manual list 3,500 kg (7,716 lb) braked and 750 kg unbraked, with a 2,750 kg gross vehicle mass.

SOURCE · CARSALES
Can I import a Td5 Discovery 2 into the US?

Once it is 25 years old by date of manufacture, yes: NHTSA exempts it from FMVSS. Series IIs were built from 1998 to May 2004, so as of October 2026 trucks built before October 2001 qualify, and later Td5s follow month by month. Check EPA and state rules as well.

SOURCE · NHTSA
What fluids does the Td5 take?

discovery2.co.uk's owner uses 5W-30 fully synthetic, the same oil his dealer used, with 7.2 litres including both filters. The transfer box and both axles take 75W-90 gear oil: about 2.0 litres to refill the LT230 (2.3 from dry) and about 3.5 litres for the two axles together.

SOURCE · DISCOVERY2.CO.UK
How is it related to the Discovery 1 and the P38?

It keeps the Discovery 1's 100-inch ladder frame and LT230, but borrows a lot from the P38: the 4.0 'Thor' V8, the 5x120 wheel pattern and the front ball joints. The rear propshaft coupling fits Discovery 1, Discovery 2 and P38.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA / LR DIRECT
