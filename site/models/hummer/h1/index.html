PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The Hummer H1 is what happened when a defense contractor took a call from a movie star. Underneath, it's the Army's M998 HMMWV with gloss paint, carpet and a stereo. The frame, axles and brakes are shared with the military trucks, and so are the hood, tailgate and quarter panels. AM General built both versions on the same Mishawaka, Indiana, line from 1992 to 2006, 11,818 civilian trucks in all. It is 86.5 inches wide, seats four either side of a driveline tunnel the size of a footlocker, and can climb a 22-inch step. Nothing else on this site was built to that brief.

FROM JEEP'S DEFENSE ARM TO THE HUMVEE

AM General began as the General Products Division of Kaiser Jeep, the old Studebaker defense plant in South Bend. American Motors made it a subsidiary in 1971. In 1979 the Army published draft specifications for one truck to replace the M151 jeep, the M561 Gama Goat and the commercial-based CUCV pickups. It had to have a diesel, 16 inches of ground clearance and enough ability to climb a 60 percent grade. AM General developed its entry as a private venture and finished its first prototype in August 1980.

In July 1981 the Army ordered 11 prototypes each from AM General, Chrysler and Teledyne. They were tested at Aberdeen Proving Ground and Yuma. On March 22, 1983, AM General won the production contract for 55,000 HMMWVs. That same year LTV bought AM General: Renault had taken a large stake in AMC, and federal rules barred defense contractors owned by foreign governments, which included partly state-owned Renault. AM General had nicknamed its prototypes 'Hummer' and had the foresight to trademark it. Humvees were in service by 1985.

ARNOLD'S TRUCK

AM General had considered a civilian model since the late 1980s. Gulf War photographs and Arnold Schwarzenegger, who saw an Army convoy while filming Kindergarten Cop in Oregon, made the case. The civilian Hummer went on sale in 1992, and Schwarzenegger bought the first two. At first there were no dealers. You ordered from the factory and could collect the truck in Indiana. Flash Off-Road's year-by-year guide lists the 1992 truck with a mechanical 150-hp 6.2-liter GM diesel, a three-speed 3L80 automatic and a 65-mph top speed. It built 316 that year.

Then came constant change. Because of how the military contracts were written, AM General slipped fixes into production as soon as they were ready, not just at model-year changes. In 1994 the trucks got the 6.5 diesel and a four-speed 4L80-E. For 1995 a 5.7-liter gas V8 was offered to buyers who were wary of diesel, and it was dropped during 1996. In 1996 the 6.5 turbo diesel and a second fuel tank arrived, and by 1997 the turbo made 195 hp and 430 lb-ft.

HOW IT CLEARS 16 INCHES

The H1's clearance doesn't come from a lift. Each wheel has a geared hub, a portal reduction box with a 1.92:1 ratio in the 1999 brochure. It sits the axle shaft above the wheel center, so the halfshafts and differentials ride high in a channel down the middle of the truck. The disc brakes are inboard, next to the differentials, rather than in the wheels. Suspension is independent double A-arms with coil springs at every corner, and the track is 71.6 inches, wide enough to follow tank tracks. Mercedes used the same portal-axle trick decades later on the G 500 4×4².

Wading depth is 30 inches with a diesel, thanks to a high air intake and a radiator mounted high and sloped over the engine. The central tire inflation system lets the driver air the tires down or up from the cab, front and rear pairs together, and runflat inserts were optional. Torsen I differentials handled traction through 1998. In 1999 ABS arrived, along with Torsen II units and electronic TorqTrac4 traction control, and Eaton electric lockers followed from 2003.

GM, THE ALPHA AND THE END

In December 1999 General Motors bought the Hummer brand name and took over marketing. AM General kept building the truck, now called the H1, and also built GM's own H2 under contract. GM's smaller H3 came from a GM plant in Shreveport, Louisiana. In 2001 AM General began building the 6.5 diesels itself, having taken over GM's engine plant, and the 2004 'Optimizer' 6.5 made 205 hp. That set up the last and best H1: the Alpha, shown in Anaheim on October 27, 2004.

The Alpha used GM's 6.6-liter Duramax LLY, rated at 300 hp and 520 lb-ft, with an Allison five-speed automatic. To make room, AM General lifted the body 2 inches and redesigned the cooling. It also fitted helical-cut gears in the hubs, a stronger steering box and 51 gallons of fuel. There was no 2005 model year. Only 729 Alphas were built, at about $128,000 to $140,000 each. In May 2006 GM announced the end, with sales slow and new diesel emissions rules due for 2007, and production stopped in June. The Hummer brand itself closed in 2010. The name returned in 2020 on the electric GMC Hummer EV.

SIDEBAR · BODY STYLES

Wagon, open top, pickup, slant-back

AM General's VIN chart lists a two-door pickup, a four-door pickup, the station wagon and an open-body soft top, plus fleet and 'Recruit' versions. For 1995 it added an extended-cab two-door truck and an open top with full hard doors. The wagon got barn doors instead of a tailgate that year. The two-door short-cab pickup and the half-door open top were dropped for 1997, and the two- and four-door hardtops were dropped after 2001 except for fleet buyers.

In 2000 the factory offered a slant-back with the same body shape as the military Humvee. Flash Off-Road counts just 39 built, which makes it the rarest regular H1. By the end, individual buyers could only choose the soft-top open top or the wagon, and those were the only two bodies offered on the 2006 Alpha.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The H1 is the HMMWV's driveline under a civilian body. A steel ladder frame carries independent double-A-arm suspension at all four corners. The engine sits well back, and the transmission, transfer case and both differentials run down a central tunnel. Halfshafts from the differentials drive geared hubs at the wheels, which drop the wheel center below the axle line. That's where the 16 inches of clearance comes from, along with the inboard brakes mounted at the differentials.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Cold-start the turbo diesel and check the coolant1996 to early-1999 6.5 turbo diesels can crack at the No. 8 cylinder. Flash Off-Road's test: with the engine stone cold, open the radiator cap, run the engine for about a minute, shut it off and open the cap again. Pressure in the coolant, foaming at the bleeder once it's warm, or milky sludge in the CDR valve point to a cracked block or head gasket. A 'Z' in the fourth VIN digit is a GM-built turbo engine; an 'F' is the later AM General engine.
  2. 02
    Front-end playFlash Off-Road calls the front end the H1's biggest weakness: idler arms, pitman arms and steering gears wear out. With the engine running, if the steering wheel moves an inch or two before the front wheels do, suspect the pitman arm. If the steering turns more easily one way than the other, suspect the steering box. Uneven tire wear points to worn front-end parts or a bad alignment.
  3. 03
    Geared-hub and halfshaft leaksA fan of oil sprayed on the inside of a tire means a leaking hub seal. So does a CTIS that won't hold air, through the spindle seal. Look for torn halfshaft boots, and wet spots where the shafts meet the differentials and hubs. Wet calipers can be differential oil, because the brakes sit right next to the differentials.
  4. 04
    Make the CTIS workRun the pump on the fronts, the rears and all four, and watch the cab gauge. Owners do abandon CTIS when the pump dies, and Flash Off-Road says a new pump can cost well over $1,000. Retrofitting CTIS, the four-speed transmission or the second fuel tank to a truck that lacks them is hard and rarely worth it, so buy the features you want.
  5. 05
    Transfer case fluid and the 'vampire'Pull the transfer case fill plug. If fluid pours out, the cooler loop inside the case may be leaking transmission fluid into it, the 'vampire' that AM General addressed with customer-satisfaction campaign R0101 in 2001. Brown, burnt fluid is a sign of long stretches at 80 mph. Flash Off-Road recommends keeping it under about 65 mph (2,400 rpm).
  6. 06
    Heater and A/C: pre- or post-1997.5The HVAC system used from 1994 leaked heater cores and was replaced from VIN 176477 (the '97.5'). Flash Off-Road says the old parts are discontinued and the upgrade runs $3,000 to $4,000. Upgraded systems use a vacuum-controlled heater valve and lack separate recirculate and A/C buttons on the control head.
  7. 07
    Correct wheels and tiresEvery H1 wheel uses an 8x6.5-in bolt pattern with a 7-in backset. Flash Off-Road says only AM General and GT/Cepek wheels have the right offset and load rating. Wrong wheels stress the front end, need custom CTIS lines and throw dirt up the sides. Tires for 16.5-in wheels are scarce, so check the spare.
  8. 08
    Body, rockers and undersideThe body is aluminum but the doors and wagon top are steel. Expect some corrosion where the two meet and around the marker lights, and lift the wheel-well carpet on wagons to check the inner seams. Look for bent rockers and undercarriage protection pushed up against the exhaust, which comes from off-road use.

Spotting the real thing

H1s were updated mid-year, re-engined by owners and sometimes built from military parts, so the VIN and the hardware tell you more than the title's model year.

  1. ID
    VIN engine and transmission codesFourth digit is the engine: X = 6.2 diesel, Y = 6.5 non-turbo diesel, Z = GM 6.5 turbo diesel, F = 6.5 turbo diesel with Caterpillar ECM, D/L = 5.7 L05 gas, W = 350 gas. Fifth digit: E = 3L80 three-speed, A = 4L80-E four-speed. On the 2006 Alpha the fourth digit is P (Duramax) and the fifth H (Allison).
  2. ID
    VIN body and year codesDigits 6–7 give the body: 82 VLC2 two-door truck, 83 HMC4 four-door truck, 84 HMCS wagon, 85 VLCO open top, 86 HMCF fleet, 87/88 Recruit, 89 XLC2 extended cab, 90 HMCO open top with full hard doors. Digit 10 is the model year (N = 1992 … 4 = 2004, 6 = 2006) and digit 11 is the plant (E = Mishawaka).
  3. ID
    Spotting an AlphaA square intake cap in place of the round 'mushroom', a Duramax badge on the hood, a hood hinged at the bottom center, a brush guard without its top bar, and a black block-off strip above the rear bumper filling the 2-in body-lift gap. Wave 2 trucks (from September 2005, first VIN 6E226861) have an all-black interior.
  4. ID
    Torsen type and axle ratioDifferential covers normally carry a tag with the ratio and Torsen type. Otherwise read the ring-gear tooth count: 41/15 is 2.73:1, 41/16 is 2.56:1. All trucks through 1998 have Torsen I; 1998 is the only Torsen I truck with 2.56 gears.
  5. ID
    Old or upgraded HVACPre-'97.5 control heads have extra recirculate and A/C buttons and an electric heater valve. The upgraded system uses a vacuum-controlled heater valve, with small colored vacuum tubes behind the control head.
  6. ID
    6.5 block castingThe definitive check for the improved No. 8-cylinder block is the casting number, readable only with the engine cover off: C-159 (March 15, 1999) and later are the revised design.

Recalls & service bulletins

Open recalls follow the VIN, not the model. Run yours through NHTSA before you buy, and ask the seller for dealer records showing recall work.

Notable recalls
Engine-cover insulation chafing fuel return hose and wiring ↗1992–1993 · NHTSA 94V-162Insulation in the engine cover can wear through the fuel return hose and a wiring harness; dealers fitted steel lines and rerouted the wiring.
Fuel-pump oil pressure switch missing seal ↗1992–1994 · NHTSA 94V-164A missing seal lets contaminants into the switch that powers the fuel pump, so it can fail open or closed.

Replace geared-hub output and spindle seals →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 1.5–2 hours per hub (Flash Off-Road)SHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSOil sprayed on the inside of a tire, oil dripping off the lower ball joint, or a CTIS pair that loses pressure overnight. Pull the hub's breather line and put it under water: bubbles mean CTIS air is leaking past the spindle seal into the hub.
THE FIXJack under the lower A-arm and chock the truck. Disconnect the CTIS line on the opposite wheel so it doesn't deflate. Drain the hub with a 5/16-in hex. Remove the CTIS line and the four 3/4-in steering-arm cover bolts, then the snap ring, and tap out the small spindle seal. Remove the clamp-nut lock screw and the clamp nut, and lay out the nut, keyed washer and bearing in order. Pull the spindle, drive out the output seal, clean the bore and the spindle's sealing surface, and drive the new seal flat. Reassemble following the AM General manual's clamp-nut sequence with red Loctite 272 inside its 10-minute window (lock screw 90 lb-in). Clear the hub vent line, then refill with GL-5 80W-90 or 75W-90 to just under the fill plug, about half a quart.

Replace the idler and pitman arms →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a daySHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSSteering wheel off-center or loose, with an inch or more of free play before the wheels move. Front tires that splay apart over obstacles. A shimmy at around 35 mph that fades by 40. The shop manual's limits are 5/16 in of vertical play at the center link for the idler, 1/8 in for the pitman, and 1/32 in at either ball stud.
THE FIXUnbolt the idler arm from the frame and separate it from the center link with a puller or pickle fork. Flash Off-Road had to cut away the rubber seal first. With the idler off, the center link drops and the pitman arm comes off the steering box shaft. Pack grease around the box output shaft. Fit the new pitman on the splines and the center link together, torque the big nut to 180 lb-ft and the 15/16-in ball-stud nut to 80 lb-ft, and line up the cotter pin. Bolt the new idler to the frame at 60 lb-ft and its ball stud at 80 lb-ft, then grease both. 1993–94 trucks with the 7/8-in center link need spacers; the 1-in link started at VIN 163985.

Relocate the 6.5 TD's pump-mounted driver (PMD/FSD) →

SYMPTOMSA turbo diesel that stalls when hot and restarts once it cools, then gets harder to restart as the fault worsens. Codes P0251 and P0370 point at the injection pump's optical sensor and the PMD. A fuel shutoff solenoid problem is different: it sputters before stalling.
THE FIXFirst rule out fuel: change the filter, check the lift pump and look for a kinked return hose. Fit a new PMD on a remote heat sink outside the engine bay, with thermal paste and the extension harness. If codes persist, have a Stanadyne shop check the pump; Flash Off-Road says to fit a DS4831-5288 or -5521, not the early -5067.

Find and fix a CTIS leak →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ An afternoon to several days of testingSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSOne axle's tires drop pressure overnight, or CTIS won't inflate or deflate at all. The fronts and rears are paired, so a leak can be in either tire, either hub seal, the lines, the manifold solenoids, the over-pressure valve or the pump.
THE FIXSpray soapy water on the tires, rims, lines and fittings. Inflate to normal, pull both quick-disconnects at the wheels and leave it overnight. If the cab gauge drops, the leak is in the lines, manifold or pump; if not, reconnect one wheel at a time to find the leaking tire or hub. Bubble-test each hub vent for a bad spindle seal. Check the braided hose swivel at the hub with the wheels turned both ways. Clean sand out of the manifold solenoid valves. If the pump won't run, jump it from the battery, then check the 5-amp (1G) and 40-amp fuses and the relay, per Gil Herman's checklist on Flash Off-Road.

Rebuild or replace a halfshaft →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day per shaftSHOP COST · Boot kits under $10 locally in Flash Off-Road's report; a shop charged $25 a boot or $50 a joint
SYMPTOMSTorn boots throwing grease, clicking on turns, or a worn seal surface where the shaft enters the hub input seal.
THE FIXThe halfshaft bolts to the inboard brake rotor at one end and splines into the geared hub at the other. On the right rear you must remove the parking brake cable to get it out. Each CV joint has a star, cage and six balls, and the cage is usually what fails. Flash Off-Road says the 1994-on joints share Rockford parts with the 1993–98 Mercury Villager and Ford Explorer. Fit new boots and clips, and a Speedi-Sleeve (99199) if the seal surface is grooved. Reinstall the six M10 class 10.9 bolts with new Nord-Lock washer pairs and red Loctite at 57 lb-ft. Overtightening can warp the rotor.

Test and replace glow plugs →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–3 hoursSHOP COST · Plugs about $10–12 each in Flash Off-Road's report
SYMPTOMSHard cold starts that get worse as temperatures drop, or black smoke and a stall right after starting. Flash Off-Road's 1996 turbo diesel wouldn't start below 50°F when its plugs failed.
THE FIXWith the ignition on, the voltmeter should dip as the plugs draw current. Measure about 50–55 amps per bank and 13–14 amps per plug while they cycle (6.5 non-turbo procedure; military 24-volt trucks are different). Asked on every forum

Hummer H1 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

The factory tire was a 37x12.50R16.5, and later trucks used 37-inch tires on 17-inch wheels. Flash Off-Road says a 38x15.5 is the largest you can run on stock wheels without lifting the truck, though you need to watch for rubbing on the suspension at full lock. Taller tires need a body lift: Flash Off-Road reports owners running 39.5s with a 2-inch body lift and no rubbing.

SOURCE · FLASH OFF-ROAD
Which year should I buy?

Flash Off-Road says many owners consider the 1998 the quintessential pre-Alpha truck. It has the 1997.5 HVAC, better sound insulation, the 2.56 axles and the last of the Torsen I differentials, before ABS and TorqTrac4 arrived in 1999. Its turbo diesel sits right in the No. 8 crack window, though (see the next question), so test the block or look for a replacement engine. Flash Off-Road calls the 2004, with its new interior, Optimizer engine and optional Eaton lockers, the best stock truck yet, and says 2004 is a big step up from 2001 for noise and comfort. The 2006 Alpha is 'an order of magnitude better' on and off road. Autotrader calls it the ultimate H1, and Hagerty found Alphas worth over 40% more than a comparable 2004. The 1992–93 6.2 trucks are simple and a favorite of hard-core off-roaders, but slow.

SOURCE · FLASH OFF-ROAD · AUTOTRADER · HAGERTY
Should I worry about the 'No. 8 cylinder' crack?

On turbo diesels from 1996 to early 1999, yes. One large Hummer dealer told Flash Off-Road that most cracks appear on '96 through early-'99 trucks, with '97s and '98s most common. GM changed the block casting from casting number C-159 (March 15, 1999); AM General started using the new blocks at E-019 (May 1, 1999). An 'F' in the fourth VIN digit means an AM General-built engine, which isn't at risk. Many at-risk engines were replaced under warranty.

SOURCE · FLASH OFF-ROAD
Diesel, turbo diesel or gas?

The turbo diesel. Flash Off-Road owned all three. His 1995 gas truck could barely hold 40 mph over Loveland Pass, while his 1996 turbo diesel held 55 and returned about 11.5 mpg in town and 15 on the road. The gas and non-turbo diesel trucks run out of power at altitude. The gas truck also fords only 24 inches, versus 30 for the diesel, because the diesel's starter is waterproof and it has no ignition to drown.

SOURCE · FLASH OFF-ROAD
What does CTIS do, and do I need it?

The central tire inflation system lets you inflate or deflate the tires from the cab, front and rear pairs together, through seals in the geared hubs. Airing down gives more grip in sand and rock. It was optional on some years (it's on the 1999 options list). Flash Off-Road says retrofitting it is hard and rarely economical, so buy a truck that has it if you want it.

SOURCE · 1999 BROCHURE · FLASH OFF-ROAD
Torsen, TorqTrac4 or lockers?

Through 1998, H1s use Torsen I differentials, which owners can coax into locking with brake-throttle modulation. When ABS became mandatory in 1999, AM General moved to Torsen II with electronic TorqTrac4 traction control, which Flash Off-Road says is good in snow and loose dirt but weaker for rock crawling. The 2002 trucks got a higher torque-bias ratio. From 2003 a rear Eaton electric locker was offered, and front and rear lockers came with the 2004 and 2006 Off-Road Adventure package.

SOURCE · FLASH OFF-ROAD
How fast can I cruise?

Flash Off-Road says not to run it past about 2,400 rpm, which is around 65 mph, if you want it to last. Faster driving burns the transfer case oil and strains a truck designed for unimproved roads. Below 65 he saw 13 to 15 mpg from a turbo diesel. A 1999 turbo diesel took 18 seconds to reach 60 mph according to the brochure; the Alpha took 13.5 according to Edmunds.

SOURCE · FLASH OFF-ROAD · EDMUNDS
How much can it tow?

The 1999 brochure gives a maximum towed load of 7,710 lb for a turbo-diesel wagon and 8,050 lb for an open top. Edmunds says a properly equipped 2006 Alpha open top can tow 9,300 lb.

SOURCE · 1999 BROCHURE · EDMUNDS
Can I run aftermarket or H2 wheels?

H1 wheels use an 8x6.5-inch bolt pattern with a 7-inch backset. Flash Off-Road says only AM General and GT/Cepek wheels have the correct offset and load rating for a truck that weighs 7,000 to 8,000 lb. Owners on Hummer4x4Offroad report that H2 wheels fit with modification, and Moab tour operators have run them. Non-factory wheels also need custom CTIS lines.

SOURCE · FLASH OFF-ROAD · HUMMER4X4OFFROAD
How deep can it wade?

30 inches for a diesel, according to the 1999 brochure and Wikipedia, and 24 inches for the 1995–96 gas trucks. The geared hubs and transfer case vent to the air cleaner, so check that the vent lines and intake seals are intact before deep water.

SOURCE · 1999 BROCHURE · FLASH OFF-ROAD
Is the Alpha just a re-engined H1?

No. AM General told Flash Off-Road that the control arms were the only parts under the truck carried over from 2004. The Alpha has the 12,100-lb GVW upgrade's bigger brakes, halfshafts and springs, a new Sheppard steering box, helical-gear hubs, a longer front driveshaft and reshaped 51-gallon fuel tanks. The downside is that most of those parts are unique to one model year.

SOURCE · FLASH OFF-ROAD
