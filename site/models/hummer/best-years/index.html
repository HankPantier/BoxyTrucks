Best AM General Hummer H1 years to buy, and which to avoid

For the AM General Hummer H1: the model years owners and specialists recommend, the ones with documented problems, and the recalls to check. There is a clear sweet spot.

Sweet spot years owners and specialists recommend · Beware years with documented problems · Recall years with a recall to check by VIN. Each one is sourced on the model's own guide.

Hummer H1

1992–2006

Flash Off-Road says many owners consider the 1998 the quintessential pre-Alpha truck. It has the 1997.5 HVAC, better sound insulation, the 2.56 axles and the last of the Torsen I differentials, before ABS and TorqTrac4 arrived in 1999. Its turbo diesel sits right in the No. 8 crack window, though (see the next question), so test the block or look for a replacement engine. Flash Off-Road calls the 2004, with its new interior, Optimizer engine and optional Eaton lockers, the best stock truck yet, and says 2004 is a big step up from 2001 for noise and comfort. The 2006 Alpha is 'an order of magnitude better' on and off road. Autotrader calls it the ultimate H1, and Hagerty found Alphas worth over 40% more than a comparable 2004. The 1992–93 6.2 trucks are simple and a favorite of hard-core off-roaders, but slow.

  1. 1998Flash Off-Road says many consider it the quintessential pre-Alpha H1: 2.56 axles, cam camber adjustment, quieter hubs, and the last Torsen I year before ABS. But '97–'98 turbo diesels are the most common No. 8 crack years, so test the blockSweet spot
  2. 2004'Optimizer' 6.5 turbo diesel with Caterpillar-built ECM (205 hp, 440 lb-ft); all-new interior; optional front and rear Eaton lockers. Flash Off-Road calls it the best stock truck yet. No 2005 model year followsSweet spot
  3. 2006H1 Alpha: 6.6 Duramax (300 hp, 520 lb-ft), Allison five-speed, 2-in body lift, helical-gear hubs, 12-in brakes, 51 gal of fuel. 729 built; production ends June 2006Sweet spot
