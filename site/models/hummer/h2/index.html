PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The H2 is what happened when General Motors bought the most famous name in off-roading and decided it should sell in volume. AM General built it under contract in a new plant in Mishawaka, Indiana, but GM engineered it from its own truck parts: a shortened Suburban frame, a three-quarter-ton front end and a 6.0-liter small-block V8. It went on sale as a 2003 model for less than half the price of an H1. Car and Driver took one 2,000 miles through Baja and decided it was 'not a Hummer poseur'. It was something else, and that's mostly fine.

FROM SHOW CAR TO SUBURBAN FRAME

In December 1999 GM bought the rights to the Hummer name from AM General, and the H2 was the first product of that deal. GM showed an H2 concept in 2000 with independent rear suspension, then priced it out of the production truck. Wikipedia credits the design to Clay Dean; the Chicago Tribune named Terry Henline as chief designer when the pickup concept appeared at the 2001 New York show. GM's 2003 spec sheet named the H2's rivals as the Range Rover, the Lexus LX 470 and the Lincoln Navigator, so luxury buyers were the target from the start.

The production recipe was pure GM parts bin. Engineers took a Suburban frame, cut 7.2 inches out of its middle for a 122.8-inch wheelbase and trimmed the overhangs, giving a truck nine inches shorter than a Tahoe. The torsion-bar front suspension came from a 2500-series Suburban, the rear became a five-link live axle on coils, and GM said a diesel wouldn't package. The radiator support and engine box are welded to the body, so the big front-hinged hood doesn't shimmy against the rest of the truck.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY DOES IN THE DIRT

Under the costume is a serious drivetrain. A full-time BorgWarner 44-84 transfer case sends 60 percent of the torque to the rear, locks 50/50 at any speed and has a 2.64:1 low range. The 4.10 rear axle carries an Eaton ELocker that engages only in 4LO below 20 mph. Bosch ABS and traction control are tuned for rough ground, and skid plates cover the engine, transfer case and fuel tank. The front chin plate has 'H2' embossed on it, because of course it does.

GM's figures give the coil-sprung truck a 40.4-degree approach and a 39.6-degree departure angle, and the off-road package's LT315/70R17s push the approach to 43.6 degrees. Ground clearance is about ten inches against the H1's sixteen, so in Baja Car and Driver learned to put a tire on the rock rather than straddle it. The optional rear air suspension can lift the tail about two inches and runs an air hose for the tires. The H1's central tire inflation wouldn't work with GM's axles.

THE PICKUP, THE 6.2 AND THE END

The SUT arrived for 2005: an H2 with a short bed and an Avalanche-style Midgate that folds to open the cabin into the bed. US sales peaked at 34,529 in 2003 and slid as fuel prices rose. For 2008 GM gave the H2 a new interior, a 393-hp aluminum 6.2-liter V8 and the six-speed 6L80. Towing went from 6,500 to 8,000 pounds and the crawl ratio rose to about 39:1. Owners rate those last trucks the best H2s, and they're the hardest to find.

The timing couldn't have been worse. GM put Hummer up for sale in 2008, and a deal with China's Tengzhong collapsed in February 2010. H2 production ended in 2009. Avtotor in Kaliningrad, Russia, had also assembled H2s from 2006 to 2009. GM's US sales figures add up to 153,026 H2s. The Mishawaka plant was sold in 2017 to SF Motors, a maker of electric vehicles.

SIDEBAR · THE 8,500-POUND LOOPHOLE

Why there's no EPA sticker

The H2's gross vehicle weight rating is 8,600 pounds. Until 2011 the EPA exempted vehicles over 8,500 pounds GVWR from fuel-economy testing and standards, so GM never had to publish a rating, and H2s didn't count toward its fleet average.

Magazines measured it themselves. Car and Driver and Motor Trend both recorded about 10 mpg, Edmunds 9.2 and Four Wheeler 10.8. With a 32-gallon tank, H2 owners on 37s still stop for fuel 'long before I need to pee', as one forum owner put it.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The H2 sits on a modified GMT820 ladder frame: a GM three-quarter-ton front section and a shortened half-ton Suburban rear. It has torsion-bar independent front suspension, a five-link coil-sprung rear axle with an optional air-spring rear, and full-time 4WD through a BorgWarner 44-84 transfer case. The 6.0-liter LQ4 and four-speed 4L65-E powered it through 2007, then the 6.2-liter L92 and six-speed 6L80 for 2008–09. Wheels are 8-lug, 8 x 165.1 mm.

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

H2s are still affordable, but clean, unmodified, low-mile trucks, especially 2008–09s, now bring real money. Owners say untouched late trucks are getting hard to find.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Find out what the air suspension has been doingIf the rear compressor runs at every stop light, there's a leak somewhere and the compressor is cooking itself. One owner was quoted $2,250 for a GM compressor before labor. Many trucks have already been converted to coils, which owners say costs under $250 in parts. Check which you're buying and whether the onboard air hose still works.
  2. 02
    Shake the front end35-inch tires wear out ball joints, tie rods, pitman and idler arms and hub bearings, even in normal driving. Owners list the front ball joints and tie-rod ends as weak points. Budget roughly $400 in parts for a front-end rebuild before labor, and more if the torsion bars have been cranked.
  3. 03
    Listen for a clunk from the front diffThe front differential hangs on three rubber bushings that soften with age, oil and coolant leaks. A loose diff clunks over bumps. Owners replace them with Outfitter Design's UHMW kit; one paid $400 to have it installed.
  4. 04
    Cycle every 4WD modeShift through 4HI, 4HI Lock and 4LO Lock, and lock the rear diff in 4LO. A SERVICE 4WD message or a slow shift points to the encoder motor, the dash buttons or wiring. A bang followed by no 4WD at all usually means the plastic shift fork inside the transfer case has broken.
  5. 05
    Look up at the roof and the sunroofOwners keep naming water leaks from the roof clearance lights and the sunroof. Check the headliner, the carpet and the rear cargo area for stains or a musty smell.
  6. 06
    Know which truck you want2008–09 trucks have the 6.2, the six-speed and a far better interior, and owners say they're better 'in every way'. They also use some parts specific to those years, and 2009s are rare. 2003–07 trucks are cheaper and have more junkyard parts. Owners call the 4L65-E four-speed the drivetrain's weak link.
  7. 07
    Inspect the chassis, not the chromeDealers can detail out a lot, but not rock gouges in the frame and skid plates. Lifts, tires and wiring added by previous owners are common, so trace any extra switches or lights, and run the VIN for open recalls.

Spotting the real thing

Every H2 is a GM-engineered, AM General-built truck, but 2008–09 trucks have a different engine, transmission, interior and some chassis parts. Many trucks have also been lifted, converted from air springs to coils or re-engined, so check what you're looking at.

  1. ID
    VINUS H2 VINs begin 5GR (for example 5GRGN23U73H135443, a 2003 sold on Bring a Trailer). The owner's manual says the 8th character is the engine code: U is the 6.0 VORTEC 6000 (2004 manual) and 8 is the 6.2 (2008 manual). The 10th character is the model year.
  2. ID
    2003–07 vs 2008–092008–09 trucks have larger grille openings, a bigger lower grille and silver-painted lower bumpers (earlier trucks' are black). Inside, the dash and gauges are new and a rotary dial replaces the 4WD push buttons.
  3. ID
    SUTThe pickup has a short bed, a power Midgate window and a folding Midgate, an outside swing-away spare, and extra taillights in the lower rear bumper.
  4. ID
    Options labelThe Service Parts Identification label in the glovebox lists the VIN, paint and every production option. Look for ZM6, the GM option code for the self-leveling rear air suspension.
  5. ID
    Parts that changed in 2008Front hub bearings differ between 2003–07 and 2008–09 trucks (Kryptonite lists separate parts). Owners say the transfer case input spline also changed, because the 6L80's output shaft differs from the four-speed's.

Post-purchase baseline

After you buy it

Most H2s are now 17 to 23 years old, and few were serviced off-road. Treat every gear oil as unknown. The rear diff carries the E-locker, the transfer case is easy to neglect, and the front end needs grease. Fluids changed in 2008: the owner's manual lists DEXRON-III for the transmission and transfer case on a 2004, and DEXRON-VI on a 2008.

DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day (much less if the diff is already out for gears)SHOP COST · One owner: $400 to install, about $600 all in with the kit
SYMPTOMSA clunk from the front over bumps, or the diff visibly rocking when someone shifts between Park, Reverse and Drive. Oil or coolant dripping onto the old rubber bushings speeds up the wear.
THE FIXSupport the front diff, disconnect the front driveshaft and CV axles as needed, and unbolt the three mounts: the crossmember bushing, the top mount and the side mount. Press out the old rubber and fit all three replacement bushings, since Outfitter Design sells them only as a kit so the mounts share the same hardness. Fix the oil leaks first if you can; one shop wouldn't install new bushings until the leaks were repaired.
TOOLS & PARTSJack and transmission or diff jack · press or bushing tools · torque wrench. Outfitter Design's kit is UHMW (one crossmember bushing plus top and side mount bushings with steel sleeves).

Rear air suspension: compressor, air springs or coil conversion →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–4 hours for a coil conversionSHOP COST · One owner was quoted $2,250 for a GM compressor before labor; another converted to coils for under $250 in parts

THE FIXTo repair it, find the leak first (lines, fittings or the air springs) before replacing the compressor. Owners say later dual-stage compressors are less likely to overheat, and switching to one needs two new hoses. To convert, support the frame, lower the axle (one owner didn't need to remove the shocks), remove the air springs and fit coil springs with upper and lower isolators.

Steering linkage rebuild: tie rods, pitman arm, idler arm and support →

THE FIXSeparate the tie-rod ends, pitman arm and idler arm with a puller, count the turns on the tie-rod sleeves to keep a rough toe setting, and replace the worn parts. Owners recommend heavier tie rods and an idler and pitman support kit if you run 37s. Grease every fitting afterwards. The idler support's fitting can't be reached without removing it, so a grease-relocation kit helps. Get the truck aligned.

Front lower ball joints →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 3–6 hours (one shop quoted 6 hours for both sides)SHOP COST · $720 with parts at one shop; ball joints $20–40 each for a DIYer
SYMPTOMSAn alignment that won't hold, play at the bottom of the knuckle when you rock the tire top to bottom, and clunks. In one owner's 2006 the upper ball joint pulled out of the control arm while driving.
THE FIXUnload the torsion bar first. Then remove the wheel, brake caliper and rotor, separate the lower ball joint from the knuckle, and press the old joint out of the control arm with a rented ball-joint press. One owner needed a torch and 'a lot of patience' with a breaker bar. Press in the new joint, torque it, grease it (the manual says not below 10°F) and get an alignment.

Front wheel hub bearing →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hours per sideSHOP COST · Parts plus labor

THE FIXRemove the wheel, caliper, rotor and CV axle nut, unplug the ABS sensor, then unbolt the hub from the knuckle and fit the new unit. Owners prefer Timken, which they say is the OE supplier, or the ACDelco FW314. Torque the axle nut to the factory figure; owners disagree about the number, so check the service manual.

Transfer case won't shift: encoder motor or shift fork →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 1 hour for the motor, an afternoon to drop the case for a forkSHOP COST · Owners quote about $200–400 for an encoder motor
SYMPTOMSA SERVICE 4WD message, mode lights that keep flashing, shifts that take minutes, or a bang followed by no 4HI or 4LO at all.
THE FIXScan the codes first. Clean or check the dash buttons, then check the encoder motor on the transfer case and its wiring. Owners warn that remanufactured and non-OEM motors are 'rubbish'. One owner says the usual failure is the contact ring inside the encoder, sold separately. If the case banged and stopped working, the plastic shift fork has probably broken: drop the transfer case, fit an aluminum fork and refill with fresh fluid.

Asked on every forum

Hummer H2 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

Owners agree on 37x12.50R17 on stock wheels, or wheels with the same offset. One owner runs Cooper STTs at stock height with a slight torsion-bar crank and doesn't rub at full stuff, apart from a light scrub on the sway bar at full lock, which the stock 35s do too. 37x14.50s didn't fit. Wider tires or different offsets need a lift, spacers or both.

SOURCE · HUMMER4X4OFFROAD
Is it really just a Tahoe underneath?

Partly. Car and Driver reported a Suburban frame cut down by 7.2 inches, a 2500-series front suspension and a Suburban five-link rear. The body, transfer-case calibration, rear E-locker, skid plates and angles are H2-specific, and AM General built the trucks in Mishawaka. Parts for the engine and front end cross over with GM trucks; body and trim parts mostly don't.

SOURCE · CAR AND DRIVER · OWNERS
What fuel economy does it get?

No official rating exists, because its 8,600-lb GVWR exempted it from EPA testing. Magazines measured 9 to 10.8 mpg. Owners on 37s report about 10.5 mpg on the stock 4.10 gears, and one got 12.5 after regearing to 4.56.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · HUMMER4X4OFFROAD
Does it have lockers?

Every H2 has a lockable center differential and an Eaton ELocker in the rear axle. The rear locker only works in 4LO Lock, engages below 2 mph and drops out above 20 mph. There's no front locker from the factory; one owner added an Eaton E-locker to the front diff while regearing.

SOURCE · OWNER'S MANUAL · OWNERS
Which years should I buy?

If budget allows, 2008–09 for the 6.2, the six-speed and the much better interior; owners say they're better in every way. They're also the rarest and most expensive, and some of their parts are specific to those years. Among 2003–07 trucks, one owner would pick a mid-2005 to 2007 truck over a 2003 for the stronger front diff and better build.

SOURCE · HUMMER4X4OFFROAD
Should I keep the air suspension?

Opinion is split. Owners who tow love being able to raise and lower the rear, and the onboard air hose. Others have replaced compressors more than once at around $2,000 installed and switched to coils, saying the ride hardly changed. If the compressor runs constantly, find the leak before replacing it.

SOURCE · HUMMER4X4OFFROAD
How much can it tow?

GM's 2003 spec sheet said 7,000 lb. GM's 2008 announcement said the new powertrain raised capacity from 6,500 to 8,000 lb. Use tow/haul mode, and check your door sticker and hitch rating.

SOURCE · GM VIA OFF-ROAD.COM · ROCKCRAWLER
Do I need to regear for 37s?

Owners say the stock 4.10s with 37s feel gutless on the highway and the transmission keeps hunting for gears. 4.56 restores roughly stock feel on 37s; 4.88 suits 38s or bigger and towing. Expect to retune the speedometer and shift points afterwards. Owners were quoted $1,900 to $4,800 for both axles.

SOURCE · HUMMER4X4OFFROAD
Can I level it with the torsion bars?

Yes. Crank the stock keys or fit GM's re-indexed 'green' keys; one owner gained about an inch with the green keys alone. Owners warn that cranked torsion bars ride harsher and wear tie rods, pitman and idler arms much faster, and the extra height leaves a gap above the bump stops.

SOURCE · HUMMER4X4OFFROAD
What does 'SERVICE 4WD' mean?

The transfer-case control system has a fault. Common causes are sticking dash buttons or a mode dial, a failing encoder motor on the transfer case, damaged wiring, or a bad wheel-speed sensor or hub bearing. Have it scanned with a proper tool, because a cheap code reader won't show the module's codes.

SOURCE · OWNER'S MANUAL · OWNERS
How do I flat-tow it behind an RV?

The transfer case has a NEUTRAL position for towing the H2 with all four wheels on the ground; the owner's manual explains the procedure. Set the parking brake before you select NEUTRAL, because the truck can roll even with the transmission in Park.

SOURCE · OWNER'S MANUAL
