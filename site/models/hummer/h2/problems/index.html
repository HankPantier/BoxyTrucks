Hummer H2 problems: 7 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the AM General Hummer H2, the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Hummer H2 problems to check before you buy

  1. Find out what the air suspension has been doingIf the rear compressor runs at every stop light, there's a leak somewhere and the compressor is cooking itself. One owner was quoted $2,250 for a GM compressor before labor. Many trucks have already been converted to coils, which owners say costs under $250 in parts. Check which you're buying and whether the onboard air hose still works.
  2. Shake the front end35-inch tires wear out ball joints, tie rods, pitman and idler arms and hub bearings, even in normal driving. Owners list the front ball joints and tie-rod ends as weak points. Budget roughly $400 in parts for a front-end rebuild before labor, and more if the torsion bars have been cranked.
  3. Listen for a clunk from the front diffThe front differential hangs on three rubber bushings that soften with age, oil and coolant leaks. A loose diff clunks over bumps. Owners replace them with Outfitter Design's UHMW kit; one paid $400 to have it installed.
  4. Cycle every 4WD modeShift through 4HI, 4HI Lock and 4LO Lock, and lock the rear diff in 4LO. A SERVICE 4WD message or a slow shift points to the encoder motor, the dash buttons or wiring. A bang followed by no 4WD at all usually means the plastic shift fork inside the transfer case has broken.
  5. Look up at the roof and the sunroofOwners keep naming water leaks from the roof clearance lights and the sunroof. Check the headliner, the carpet and the rear cargo area for stains or a musty smell.
  6. Know which truck you want2008–09 trucks have the 6.2, the six-speed and a far better interior, and owners say they're better 'in every way'. They also use some parts specific to those years, and 2009s are rare. 2003–07 trucks are cheaper and have more junkyard parts. Owners call the 4L65-E four-speed the drivetrain's weak link.
  7. Inspect the chassis, not the chromeDealers can detail out a lot, but not rock gouges in the frame and skid plates. Lifts, tires and wiring added by previous owners are common, so trace any extra switches or lights, and run the VIN for open recalls.

Hummer H2 recalls and service bulletins

  • Windshield wiper motor failure ↗ 2003 · NHTSA 02V283000Improperly manufactured wiper motors could fail, reducing visibility in bad weather.
  • Ignition key / shift lever override ↗ 2003 · NHTSA 03V075000Mechanical overrides let the key come out or the shifter leave Park in ways FMVSS 114 doesn't allow without a locking column.
  • Hydro-boost relief valve bore ↗ 2003–2004 · NHTSA 04V045000An out-of-spec relief valve bore in the Hydro-boost brake housing could fracture its O-ring seal, slightly increasing steering and brake effort.
  • Hydro-boost accumulator cracking ↗ 2004–2005 · NHTSA 05V043000The Bosch Hydro-boost pressure accumulator could crack and separate; fragments could injure people near the engine bay with the hood open.
  • Second-row center seat belt routing ↗ 2004–2005 · NHTSA 05V163000The center rear lap belt may not sit low across the hips; owner's manual guidance was updated.
  • Power steering hose ↗ 2006 · NHTSA 05V455000An out-of-spec hose could fracture at full lock while braking, losing power steering and, with Hydro-boost, power brake assist.
  • Tire and loading label ↗ 2006 · NHTSA 05V552000Labels listed an incorrect vehicle capacity weight (FMVSS 110).
  • ABS wheel-speed sensor wiring ↗ 2008 · NHTSA 07V373000The front or rear ABS sensor harness wires may be reversed, which can lengthen stopping distances.
  • Heated washer fluid module (first campaign) ↗ 2006–2008 · NHTSA 08V441000A short on the washer-heater circuit board could overheat its ground wire; the fix added a fuse.
  • Water in the wiper motor module ↗ 2003 · NHTSA 08V456000On 13,358 model-year 2003 H2s, water could get into the wiper motor module and make the wipers intermittent or stop them working.
  • Heated washer fluid module (second campaign) ↗ 2006–2009 · NHTSA 10V240000New thermal incidents after the 2008 fix; the heated washer module could melt or catch fire.
  • Fabtech aftermarket pitman arms ↗ 2003–2006 (aftermarket part) · NHTSA 06E010000An equipment recall for Fabtech pitman arms FTS71000 and FTS71001: the internal pin can shear and cause total loss of steering. Check any lifted truck that has them.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.
