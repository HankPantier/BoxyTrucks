PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

In December 1997 Nissan replaced the Y60 'GQ' with a Patrol that looked new but thought the same way. The Y61, sold as the GU in Australia and the Safari in Japan, kept a separate chassis, coil springs and a live axle at each end on the wagon, at a time when Toyota was giving the 100 Series independent front suspension. Nissan Australia sold 104,510 GU wagons and 37,112 cab-chassis between 1997 and October 2016, according to figures quoted by JDM Buy Sell, and Middle East production carried on into 2024. Few 4x4s have had a run that long.

SAME RECIPE, SQUARER BOX

Underneath, the GU is an evolution of the GQ. Wikipedia notes larger CV joints, manual gearboxes with fewer synchros, and wider axle housings to suit the new body, with the same H233 and H260 differential centres. The five-door wagon rides on a 2,970 mm wheelbase, and some markets also got a 2,400 mm three-door. Body styles were the seven-seat wagon, a cab-chassis (the UY61 in Australia), sold with either a leaf-sprung or a coil-sprung rear, and a two-door pickup. Japan also got a two-door hardtop and a four-door van.

The engine list at launch covered every taste. Australian Series I trucks offered the 4.5-litre TB45E petrol six, the 2.8-litre RD28ETi turbo-diesel six, and the 4.2-litre TD42 diesel. The TD42T turbo-diesel joined the ST in 1999. CarsGuide notes that the RD28 had a timing belt, the TD42 timing gears, and the ZD30 and petrol sixes chains. Manuals were five-speeds: the FS5R30A behind the RD28 and the FS5R50B behind everything else.

THE ZD30 PROBLEM

Series II arrived in 2000 with the engine that defines the GU's reputation: the 3.0-litre ZD30DDTi, a four-cylinder turbo-diesel with a Bosch VP44 pump, rated at 116 kW. It was cheaper than the 4.2 six and could be had with an automatic. CarsGuide says it 'has a history of breaking pistons due to poor engine management and boost spikes'. Too many engines died early, usually through a holed crown or a cracked ring land on the number three or four piston, and the fix was a bottom-end rebuild.

Nobody has published a definitive cause. The suspects are the EGR system upsetting the mixture and creating hot spots, oil from the crankcase vent fouling the mass-airflow sensor, and boost spikes from a slow-reacting turbo control. Nissan also originally specified 6 litres of oil. After reports of oil starvation it raised the capacity to 8.3 litres and told dealers to shorten and recalibrate the dipsticks. In September 2004 a Nissan service bulletin, quoted by owners on ExplorOz, also tightened the oil spec to ACEA B3 or JASO DH-1 with 10W-40 strongly recommended. Owners responded with what forums call 'engine saver' mods: boost and EGT gauges, catch cans, and in many cases a blanked EGR.

SERIES BY SERIES

Series III, from late 2001, brought the 4.8-litre TB48DE petrol six, a DOHC 24-valve engine rated at 185 kW in Australia, and later the intercooled TD42Ti. The 2003 Japanese facelift is credited to Junichi Sakai. The big visual change came with Series IV in October 2004: boxy wheel-arch flares, new headlights, grille and tail-lights, a new interior, and 17-inch alloys on the ST and Ti. There was no Series V; the next update was GU VI in September 2007.

By 2007 the TD42 was gone (4X4 Australia says 2006; Wikipedia says the TD42Ti couldn't meet Euro IV for 2007), and a common-rail ZD30 became the only diesel, at 118 kW and up to 380 Nm. CarsGuide suggests that the common-rail system may have helped with the boost and mixture problems. Dual front airbags only became standard across the range that year. By the end in Australia, the 3.0 was the only engine left.

THE LONG GOODBYE

The petrol-only Y62 reached Australia in 2013, so the GU stayed on sale beside it until 2016. It finished with the 300-unit Legend Edition: an ST with the common-rail 3.0, a rear diff lock, and $10,000 of genuine accessories including a steel bull bar with a Warn winch, a snorkel, a roof rack and a tow bar. Because the newer truck had no diesel, late GUs held their value. Wikipedia lists a worldwide phase-out in 2014, but sales continued in South Africa, the Middle East and parts of Africa, Asia and South America. Production reportedly ended in 2024.

Its rivals were Toyota's IFS 100 Series and its solid-axle 105, with the 70 Series as the cab-chassis alternative. The GU's case is simple: coil-sprung solid axles, a huge aftermarket and a truck that UN agencies and armies still drive. Pick the engine carefully and it can last for decades.

SIDEBAR · THE DESERT PATROL

Super Safari: the Y61 that refused to retire

Outside Australia the Y61 lived on as the Patrol Safari and Super Safari, built in Japan until 2024 for markets such as the Gulf. A 2017 Middle East brochure lists the TB48DE at 280 hp (SAE gross), a five-speed automatic, a rear differential lock, VDC, an electric winch and tan leather. Wikipedia notes that the TB48DE has a following among Middle Eastern tuners, and that the Gulf had special editions with names like Falcon, Gazelle and Al Ostoura.

Japan's version was the Safari, and it took a different route: the 245 PS TB48DE in the Granroad Limited from November 2002, and a rear limited-slip diff on every model from September 1999. The Safari name disappeared from Japanese showrooms in the mid-2000s, while the Gulf trucks kept going for most of another twenty years.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The GU is a traditional ladder-frame 4x4 with a beam axle and coil springs at each end on the wagon. The front axle is located by radius arms and a panhard rod, which is why lifted GUs need caster correction. Drive is part-time 4WD with low range and auto-locking free-wheel hubs as standard on many trucks. The engine range is the main thing that separates good GUs from expensive ones.

Buying one

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Identify the engine and series firstMake sure the engine and gearbox match the build date and series. A 'Series III ZD30' and a 'Series IV TB48' are different buys. A ZD30 sold as 'overhauled' should come with an itemised list: head, bores, pistons, bearings and rings, not just new pistons and a turbo.
  2. 02
    ZD30: demand oil history and check the dipstickLook for regular oil changes and the right grade: a September 2004 Nissan bulletin, quoted by owners, calls for ACEA B3 or JASO DH-1 oil with 10W-40 strongly recommended. Nissan Europe's Y61 service manual schedules ZD30 oil and filter every 15,000 km or 12 months, or every 7,500 km or 6 months in severe use such as towing, dust or short trips. Check your own market's service book; many owners change it more often. Fuel in the oil is bad news. One forum veteran says heavy soot within 5,000 km points to an open EGR, and another warns that factory intercoolers eventually leak, so check for oil staining. Listen for knocks on a cold start.
  3. 03
    Cooling under loadEvery GU engine can overheat when towing, and the TD42 has a particular reputation for it. Check the radiator core, the foam seals between condenser and radiator, and the fan clutch (it should drag and turn only about half a revolution when cold). Watch the temperature gauge on a long climb.
  4. 04
    Manual gearboxDrive through every gear. Owners report fifth-gear spline and hub problems and worn 2nd-to-3rd synchros on the five-speed.
  5. 05
    Rear axle seals, diffs and underbodyPut it on a hoist. Look for wet rear backing plates from leaking axle seals, bash plates pushed up into what they're meant to protect, and damage to sills and bumpers. Find out whether the rear diff is an H233B or an H260 if you plan to fit a locker.
  6. 06
    Cab-chassis: look behind the cabExplorOz members report cracks in GU cab-chassis frames just behind the cab and near the rear axle on heavily loaded trays, repaired by plating. Check under any big service body.
  7. 07
    Rear barn door and dashOwners report cracks in the big rear door that carries the spare, down the side of the window on the left-hand side. Dashboard tops can blister; Nissan Japan extended the warranty to seven years on specified wagons built August 2004 to June 2007.
  8. 08
    Previous lift workA lifted GU that wanders or steers vaguely probably needs caster correction. Lifts of about 3 in or more also need longer panhard rods, drag-link changes and longer brake lines. Ask what was fitted and whether it was certified.

Spotting the real thing

The Y61 spans more than two decades, five engine families and several plants, and the US never sold one new. Pin down the series, the engine and the build date. The build date decides whether the truck can be imported at all.

  1. ID
    Names and codesY61 is Nissan's series code. Australia calls it the GU, Japan sold it as the Safari, and Europe called it the Patrol GR. Known codes include WRGY61 (1997 Japanese TD42T wagon) and UY61 (Australian cab-chassis).
  2. ID
    Which seriesGU I runs from 1997 and GU II from April 2000. GU III (October 2001) brought a new nose. GU IV (October 2004) is the easy one to spot, with new headlights, boxy wheel-arch flares and larger tail lights. Later series are GU VI (September 2007) and GU VII (2010).
  3. ID
    Which enginePetrol trucks have the 4.5 L TB45 (1997–2001) or 4.8 L TB48DE (late 2001 on). Diesels are the 2.8 L RD28ETi, the 4.2 L TD42 family (naturally aspirated, then TD42T from 1999, then intercooled TD42Ti from 2003) or the 3.0 L four-cylinder ZD30. Every ZD30 carries the ZD30DDTi name, but before 2007 it used a VP44 distributor pump and from 2007 it's common-rail, so check which one you're looking at.
  4. ID
    Coil or leaf rearWagons have coil springs at both ends. Cab-chassis utes were also sold with a leaf-spring rear axle, such as the 2011 'DX (Leaf Spring) Wide Wheel' in Australia.
  5. ID
    Build date for US importUS eligibility runs 25 years from the vehicle's own manufacture date, not its model year. Read the build plate before money changes hands. A late-2001 GU III with a TB48 may not be legal to import yet even though the model launched that year.

Importing & legality

Nissan never sold the Y61 new in the US. Its successor platform reached Americans only as the Infiniti QX56/QX80 and, from 2017, the Armada. Every US Y61 is a gray import.

NHTSA's exemption covers vehicles at least 25 years old, counted from each truck's manufacture date. As of October 2026, that means trucks built up to about October 2001: GU I and GU II wagons with the TB45, RD28, TD42, TD42T and early VP44 ZD30. The first TB48s (late 2001) are only just becoming eligible. The intercooled TD42Ti (2003) won't qualify until 2028, and GU IV facelift trucks (October 2004) not until late 2029. The EPA's separate exemption (Form 3520-1, code E) requires the vehicle to be at least 21 years old and in original configuration; a replacement engine must be the same model as originally installed.

California's emissions rules make registering an overseas vehicle difficult there even when the federal rules are met, and other states vary. Canada's 15-year rule means newer Y61s are already legal north of the border, which doesn't make them legal to bring into the US. Check your state's titling and emissions rules before you buy.

After you buy it

Assume nothing has been done without receipts. On a GU, the job that matters most depends on the engine. A ZD30 needs proof of frequent oil changes and the revised oil level. A TD42 needs a cooling system that can cope with towing. Every GU needs the diff, transfer and hub servicing that bush trucks skip. Fluids and capacities below come from Nissan's Y61 service manual unless marked. Its maintenance schedules are the European ones, so check your own market's service book, and fill to the plug rather than by volume.

ZD30 'engine saver' setup (gauges, oil level, breather) →
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day to a daySHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSNone until a piston fails, usually number three or four. Warning signs include high EGTs when towing, boost spikes or drops under load, sooty oil, and oil consumption.
THE FIXMake sure the dipstick and service fill reflect Nissan's revised 8.3-litre capacity (a Nissan representative quoted on ExplorOz advised 9 litres at a change), use ACEA B3 or JASO DH-1 10W-40 oil, then check the oil daily on hard trips. Fit a boost gauge and a pyrometer (EGT gauge) and learn what's normal. One owner saw 300–450 °C at highway speed with the EGR working and about 600 °C on a steep climb at full throttle. Back off before EGTs climb. Fit a catch can so crankcase oil doesn't reach the intake and MAF sensor, and keep the MAF clean. Blanking the EGR is common but disputed: one owner saw lower highway boost and higher EGTs after blanking it and refitted the valve. It is also an emissions device, so check your local rules.

Front wheel bearing service and hub seals →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–3 hours per sideSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSPlay in the wheel when rocked top to bottom, grinding, grease on the inside of the rim, or an auto hub that runs hot after a drive.
THE FIXPut the axle on stands and remove the wheel. Set the auto-locking hub to LOCK, remove its six 8 mm Allen bolts and pull the hub. Undo the two 22 mm caliper (torque member) bolts and tie the caliper out of the way. Remove the snap ring, washer and thrust ring, the lock-washer screws and lock washer, then the bearing lock nut. Pull the hub, catching the outer bearing, then lever out the seal and remove the inner bearing. Clean and repack both bearings, and fit a new seal. On reassembly, the Gregory's manual quoted on ExplorOz says to torque the lock nut to 167–196 Nm while rotating the hub, back it off, retighten to 3–5 Nm, then check the preload with a spring gauge (0–18.6 N starting force). Nissan's own Y61 service manual (FA section) gives the same figures, with zero axial end play. A Lonard's procedure gives different figures, so follow the Nissan ones.

Cure overheating under load (TD42 and others) →

THE FIXClean the radiator core thoroughly. Owners note that blocked internal tubes won't clear with a flush, so a recore or new radiator may be needed. Caster correction after a lift →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day (bushes) to a full day (arms)SHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSLight, wandering steering that won't self-centre after a suspension lift.
THE FIXLifting a radius-arm front axle rotates it and reduces caster. One owner with a nominal 2 in kit (closer to 3 in in practice) fitted 2° caster-correction bushes and reported a clear improvement. Press the old radius-arm bushes out at the chassis end and press in offset bushes, set to add caster. For lifts of about 3 in or more, forum members also recommend an adjustable or longer panhard rod to re-centre the axle, drag-link changes and longer brake lines. Get a wheel alignment afterwards. Series 2-on wagons use a different front panhard rod from GQ and Series 1 trucks, so order for your build.

Rear axle seal and bearing leak →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day per sideSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSWet backing plate or inner rim, oily brake shoes or pads, a falling diff oil level.
THE FIXRear axle oil seals are a known GU leak. Drain or lower the diff oil, remove the wheel and brake, and undo the axle retainer. Pull the axle with a slide hammer, then replace the inner oil seal in the housing. If the bearing is rough, have the bearing and collar pressed off and on at a shop. Clean contaminated brake parts or replace them, refit the axle and refill with LSD-rated gear oil if the diff is a limited-slip. Nissan's Y61 service manual specifies Nissan Hypoid LSD gear oil (KLD31-14002), or API GL-5 SAE 140 with 10% by volume of LSD friction modifier (38469-C6000), which matches what an owner quoted on ExplorOz. Fit a rear locker →
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A full day (setup is critical)SHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSNot a fault. The rear LSD on many GUs isn't enough on rocky climbs and cross-axle ruts.
THE FIXFirst find out which rear diff you have. Harrop's GU ELocker and the Detroit H233 unit suit the H233B only, and the GU used both H233B and H260 rear centres. Asked on every forum

Patrol Y61 (GU) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

265/75R16 is the popular stock-height size on ExplorOz, and some owners run 285/75R16 (about 33 in) on 16-inch rims. One owner runs 305/70R16 on a 2004 GU with only a 'tiny' scrub at full lock in reverse. Bigger sizes such as 305/70R17 and 35s are generally reported with at least a 2 in lift and the mud flaps removed. Many GUs won't take 15-inch rims over their brakes. In Australia, check your state's current limit on tyre-diameter increases; owners note Nissan sold a replacement tyre placard for 265/75R16.

SOURCE · EXPLOROZ / NISSANPATROL.COM.AU
Which engine should I buy?

CarsGuide says it would be 'very leery of any RD28 or ZD30-powered example, especially if it has covered a lot of kilometres', and that the 4.2 diesel and petrol sixes 'seem to offer a better package'. It names injection-pump failures and blown head gaskets as the RD28's problems, and calls the petrols 'guzzlers'. Owners describe the TD42T as very reliable if it's kept cool and full of oil.

SOURCE · CARSGUIDE
Which years are the best to buy?

For a touring diesel, the TD42 years. CarsGuide calls the TD42 turbo-diesel 'the pick of the Patrol engines from this era', and Westside Auto names the 2005–07 TD42T ST and ST-L as the GU's sweet spot for reliability. The TD42T arrived in 1999 and the intercooled TD42Ti in 2003, and Australian sources date the end of the TD42 to 2006 or 2007. Be wary of 2000–2003 ZD30s. In the Gulf, where petrol GUs dominate, CarSwitch rates the 2008–10 Safaris as the most refined Y61s.

SOURCE · CARSGUIDE · WESTSIDE AUTO · CARSWITCH
Is the ZD30 really that bad, and which years were fixed?

Nobody agrees on a cut-off and no failure rate has been published. Typical failures are a holed or cracked number three or four piston, and Nissan raised the oil capacity to 8.3 L and recalibrated the dipsticks. Perth dealer Westside Auto says the 2000–2003 engines earned the reputation for 'grenading' and calls the 2007-on common-rail engine improved. One forum veteran says most bugs were ironed out by 2003, but CarsGuide was still answering piston-failure questions about 2008 and 2009 ZD30s in 2022 and 2025. Many high-mileage ZD30s run fine. Buy on service history and condition, not on the year alone.

SOURCE · WESTSIDE AUTO · CARSGUIDE / FORUMS
Should I blank the EGR on a ZD30?

It's the most argued-about ZD30 mod. Some owners blank it to keep soot out of the intake and report quicker spool. Others have seen highway boost drop and EGTs rise after blanking it, and refitted it. If you do it, fit boost and EGT gauges so you can see the effect. The EGR is an emissions control device, so check your local roadworthiness rules.

SOURCE · EXPLOROZ
How much can a GU tow?

CarsGuide lists braked towing limits of 3,500 kg for the 4.2 turbo-diesel and 4.8 petrol wagons, 3,200 kg for the 3.0 diesel wagon, 3,140 kg for the 4.5 petrol, 2,800 kg for the 2.8 diesel, and up to 2,500 kg for the cab-chassis and ute, all with a suitable heavy-duty towbar. The 2016 Legend Edition is rated at 3,200 kg as a manual and 2,500 kg as an auto.

SOURCE · CARSGUIDE / 4X4 AUSTRALIA
What fuel economy should I expect?

Nissan Australia quoted 17.2 L/100 km (ADR combined) for the 4.8-litre petrol, and CarsGuide expects at least 15 L/100 km on a highway cruise from the petrols and 20-plus in town. A 2010 common-rail ZD30 DX cab-chassis is listed at 12.5 L/100 km combined (15.9 urban, 10.5 highway), and the 2012 ST manual wagon at 10.9 L/100 km. A loaded touring truck on big tyres will use more.

SOURCE · JDM BUY SELL / CARSGUIDE / NISSANPATROL.COM.AU / CAREXPERT
Does it have lockers?

Japanese Safaris had a rear LSD as standard from September 1999, and later Australian cab-chassis list an LSD as standard. A factory rear diff lock is documented on the 2016 Legend Edition and the Middle East Super Safari. For everything else, ARB Air Lockers, Harrop ELockers and Detroits are the usual upgrades. Check whether your rear diff is an H233B or an H260 before you order.

SOURCE · JDM BUY SELL / 4X4 AUSTRALIA / HARROP
What oil does a ZD30 take, and how much?

Nissan's September 2004 service bulletin, quoted by owners on ExplorOz, requires ACEA B3 or JASO DH-1 oil and strongly recommends 10W-40. The capacity was raised from 6 L to 8.3 L, and dealers recalibrated the dipsticks, so make sure yours has been done and fill to the revised mark. A Nissan representative quoted on ExplorOz advised using 9 L at changes and checking the oil daily when working hard. Confirm the change interval in your handbook.

SOURCE · CARSGUIDE / EXPLOROZ
How do I tell the series apart?

Series I–III (1997–2004) have the original smooth arches and smaller tail-lights; GU III (October 2001) got a new nose. The October 2004 Series IV facelift added box-style flares, new headlights, grille and tail-lights, and 17-inch alloys on the ST and Ti. There was no Series V; GU VI arrived in September 2007 and GU VII in 2010. A diesel GU built from 2007 has the common-rail ZD30, because the TD42 had gone by then.

SOURCE · GOAUTO / 4X4 AUSTRALIA / WIKIPEDIA
Can I import one to the US or Canada?

The US 25-year exemption counts from each vehicle's month of manufacture, so early GUs from 1997 to 2001 are now eligible. EPA rules also require the original engine configuration or a certified replacement. Canada's limit is 15 years. Bring a Trailer has sold several Y61-era Safaris and Patrols.

SOURCE · JDMBUYSELL / BAT
Why does the front drive drop out when I'm stuck?

CarsGuide says the standard auto-locking hubs 'have the potential to disengage when, for instance, rocking back and forth to free a stuck vehicle'. That's why many owners fit manual hubs and lock them whenever they're in 4WD. The GU's transfer case is engaged mechanically, so follow the owner's-manual procedure for shifting into 4WD.

SOURCE · CARSGUIDE
