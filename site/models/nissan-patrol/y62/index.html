PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

Nissan launched the sixth-generation Patrol, the Y62, in Abu Dhabi on 13 February 2010, which tells you who it was built for. It broke with six decades of Patrol tradition. The Y61 it replaced kept solid axles at both ends, as had every Patrol before it, from the leaf-sprung 160/MQ to the coil-sprung Y60. The Y62 got independent suspension at all four corners, a direct-injection 5.6-litre V8 and an optional hydraulic system whose rams do the work of both dampers and anti-roll bars. In North America the same truck became the Infiniti QX56 in 2010 and the Nissan Armada for 2017–2024.

A V8 AND FOUR INDEPENDENT CORNERS

The engine is the VK56VD, rated at 298 kW at 5,800 rpm and 560 Nm at 4,000 rpm in the Patrol, according to 4X4 Australia. Wikipedia says entry-level trims first used a 236 kW VK56DE V8 and, from 2017, a 4.0-litre VQ40DE V6 with 205 kW. All models have had a seven-speed automatic since 2017. Early entry trims offered a five-speed automatic or a six-speed manual. There's no diesel, which South Africa's Cars.co.za lists as one of the truck's weaknesses.

Underneath is a separate frame with a two-speed transfer case. Patrolapart describes the Y62's 4WD as an electronic multi-plate clutch unit that needs Nissan's ATX 90A fluid. The driver picks Auto, 4H or 4L, plus Sand, Rock and Snow modes. Non-US trucks have a rear differential lock and hill descent control, and Cars.co.za lists a helical limited-slip rear diff as well. 4X4 Australia's tester praised the wheel travel, low range and traction control, and grumbled that the traction control switches off when the rear locker is engaged.

HYDRAULIC BODY MOTION CONTROL

HBMC is the Y62's party trick. Nissan's description: the hydraulic cylinders at each wheel are cross-linked by pipes in two circuits, so when the truck leans into a corner, fluid from the outside cylinders flows into an accumulator and raises roll stiffness. Off-road, each wheel is free to rise or drop with the ground. Nissan says it needs no power source, so it costs nothing in fuel. 4X4 Australia puts it more simply: the rams act as both the shock absorbers and the sway bars. Wikipedia says Nissan developed it and tested it on the Patrol in the Middle East.

The catch arrives when you want to lift it. On NissanPatrol.com.au, one owner's research found that HBMC front springs can't be swapped on their own, only as complete shock-and-spring units, and another watched his rear end sink three inches under 280 kg of ball weight. Ontrack's 2-inch lift kits for HBMC trucks are designed to fit without disturbing the system's pressure. When Nissan Australia and Premcar built the 2023 Patrol Warrior, they kept HBMC and re-valved it. Not every Y62 has it: Wikipedia says the Gulf-market Desert Edition used conventional suspension, and the US Armada never offered it.

BUILT FOR THE GULF

The Y62 went on sale in Dubai on 1 April 2010. By mid-May, dealer Arabian Automobiles had delivered more than 450 and was taking desert-safari operators dune-bashing in them. Nissan claimed more than 13,000 hours of testing in local deserts. It paid off. In The National's 2013 report, Nissan Middle East said Patrol sales had grown 66 per cent in a year, to more than 14,000 across the region, giving it 22 per cent of the Gulf SUV segment.

The love has lasted. Bestselling Cars Blog reports the Patrol was the UAE's best-selling vehicle of 2024, its first win since 2020. Gulf Business's 2024 interview with Nissan's Middle East chief calls it 'a cultural symbol'. Gulf buyers got grades from XE to LE Platinum City, plus a Nismo with 319 kW, Bilstein dampers and 22-inch forged wheels, launched in Dubai. Japan, oddly, never got the Y62 new. Wikipedia says it was export-only, and most Y62s in Japan are reverse-imported US Armadas.

THE ARMADA YEARS AND WHAT CAME NEXT

Nissan showed the second-generation Armada in Chicago in 2016, and it went on sale that August as a 2017 model, built in Kyushu alongside the Patrol. It had a 390 hp VK56VD and a seven-speed automatic, and a 2021 refresh added 10 hp and 19 lb-ft. AutoGuide reported that US buyers didn't get the Patrol's locking differentials, disconnecting sway bars or HBMC, and that the US truck had its own suspension tuning. What it kept was the frame, low range and 8,500 lb of towing. It was the first Patrol-based Nissan sold in America since the 60 Series left in 1969.

The Y62 had a 2014 facelift, a heavier one in 2019 (the Armada's came for 2021) and limited editions such as the 2021 Middle East 70th Anniversary. Australia got the Warrior in 2023, with a 50 mm lift, 295/70R18 Yokohama all-terrains and 323 mm of ground clearance. Its successor, the Y63, was unveiled in Abu Dhabi on 3 September 2024 with V6 engines, including a 425 hp twin-turbo, and a nine-speed automatic. Its Armada twin went on sale in the US that December. Wikipedia says the Y63 reaches Australia in November 2026, so the Y62 is still on sale in some markets as this is written.

SIDEBAR · ARMADA VS PATROL

Same truck, different toolkit

Under the skin, the 2017–2024 Armada is a Y62. Autoblog called it the same 'rugged body-on-frame chassis and powertrain' the rest of the world calls the Patrol. AutoGuide listed what was left out for the US: front and rear locking differentials, the disconnecting sway bars and HBMC. The US truck got unique suspension tuning and just over 9 inches of ground clearance instead.

So an Armada is a Patrol with an open rear diff and conventional sway bars. That makes it the easiest Y62 to buy and the hardest to turn into a desert truck. Only the next generation, the Y63, brought an Armada Pro-4X with an electronic locking differential and air suspension.

How it’s built

The Y62 is still a body-on-frame truck with a real low range, but it's the first Patrol with independent suspension at both ends. The big V8, the seven-speed automatic and a clutch-type transfer case are shared across the Patrol, Armada and Infiniti QX56/QX80. The rear locker and the HBMC hydraulics are what separate a Gulf or Australian Patrol from a US Armada.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Shift qualityCarSwitch says the RE7R01A seven-speed in early Y62s (2010–2014) has a reputation for valve-body problems, costing AED 5,000–12,000 to fix in the UAE. Test for harsh shifts, delayed engagement and shudder between second and third. One Australian owner reported his Y62 holding gears under hard acceleration while towing, even after a warranty transmission.
  2. 02
    Signs of a hard desert lifeCarSwitch's desert checklist: gouged skid plates and crossmembers, compacted sand in the frame rails and behind the inner guard liners, fine sand in the air filter, and ABS, stability or TPMS warnings on a cold start.
  3. 03
    Patrol or Armada?A US Armada has no rear locker, no disconnecting sway bars and no HBMC (AutoGuide). If you want a locker, you're shopping for a Patrol, and a Y62 Patrol isn't old enough to import into the US under the 25-year rule.
  4. 04
    HBMC and lift kitsIf the truck has HBMC, ask what's been done to it. One owner found HBMC front springs only come as complete shock-and-spring assemblies, and HBMC-specific kits such as Ontrack's are designed to fit without disturbing the system. Look for wet hydraulic lines and fittings at each corner and a truck that sits level.
  5. 05
    Modifications and warrantyCars.co.za advises avoiding heavily modified examples. One NissanPatrol.com.au member was told by his dealer that drivetrain problems wouldn't be covered after a lift.
  6. 06
    Towing historyOwners report the rear of an HBMC truck sagging under heavy ball weights. Many towing rigs have airbag kits designed for HBMC. A transmission cooler or deep pan is a good sign on a truck that has towed.
  7. 07
    Armada recallsCheck the VIN with NHTSA. The 2020–2021 Armada fuel-pump recall (21V373) replaces a pump whose impeller can swell and stall the engine. The 2026 campaign 26V455 corrects the axle-weight label on Armadas with 22-inch wheels.
  8. 08
    Brakes and tyresIt's a heavy truck (a Warrior weighs 2,884 kg). Cars.co.za notes the weight wears brakes and tyres quickly, so check pad and rotor thickness and tyre wear before you price the truck.

Spotting the real thing

Patrols, Armadas, Nismos, Warriors and the Infiniti QX56/QX80 share a body and engine but differ in what they can do off-road. Check what's actually fitted.

  1. ID
    Armada vs PatrolA US Armada has no locking differentials, no disconnecting sway bars and no HBMC (AutoGuide).
  2. ID
    HBMC or notHBMC trucks have hydraulic lines running to each damper. The Desert Edition used regular suspension (Wikipedia), and so did every Armada.
  3. ID
    Series and partsParts change by series. Patrolapart lists different thermostats for Series 1–2 and Series 3-on trucks (from February 2016) and two different timing chains for some 2016–2017 Series 3 trucks. Give your parts supplier the VIN.
  4. ID
    WarriorPremcar's 2023 Warrior has 323 mm of ground clearance, 295/70R18 Yokohama all-terrains, a 2.5 mm steel front bash plate and a 3,620 kg GVM (4X4 Australia).
  5. ID
    NismoThe Gulf-market Nismo has a 319 kW (428 hp) V8, Bilstein dampers and 22-inch forged wheels (Wikipedia).

Importing & legality

The Y62 Patrol was never sold in the US. Its US version is the 2017–2024 Nissan Armada (and the Infiniti QX56/QX80). NHTSA's 25-year exemption counts from the date of manufacture, so the earliest 2010 Patrols become eligible around 2035, and a non-US Patrol can't be imported before then unless it meets US standards.

Japan is the reverse case: Wikipedia says the Y62 was export-only there, and most Y62s registered in Japan are reverse-imported left-hand-drive US Armadas.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy. How importing works: the 25-year rule, duty and registration →

Asked on every forum

Patrol Y62 / Armada questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

We couldn't find a solid owner consensus for a stock Y62. Stock is 265/70R18 on Australian Patrols and the Armada SV, or 275/60R20 on higher grades. 6061 Works lists 275/70R18 as a mild upgrade, about 15 mm taller than stock. For 295/70R18, Nissan's own Warrior needed a 29 mm suspension lift, and 6061 Works recommends that size after a 2-inch lift. Nissan sells re-gear sets for trucks on 35s.

SOURCE · 4X4 AUSTRALIA · 6061 WORKS · PATROLAPART
Is the Armada the same truck as the Patrol?

The 2017–2024 Armada shares the Y62's frame and powertrain and is built alongside it in Japan. AutoGuide reported that the US version lacked the Patrol's locking differentials, disconnecting sway bars and HBMC, and had its own suspension tuning.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · AUTOGUIDE
Can I import a Y62 Patrol into the US?

Not yet, unless it was built to US standards. NHTSA's 25-year exemption counts from the date of manufacture, so the earliest 2010 Y62 Patrols become eligible around 2035. Until then the Armada is the US-legal Y62.

SOURCE · NHTSA
What is HBMC, and does it stop me lifting the truck?

Hydraulic Body Motion Control cross-links hydraulic rams at each wheel through accumulators, so they act as both dampers and anti-roll bars. You can lift an HBMC truck, but you need a kit designed for it. One owner says HBMC front springs only come as complete assemblies, and Ontrack's 2-inch kits are designed to keep the system's pressure intact.

SOURCE · NISSAN · 4X4 AUSTRALIA · NISSANPATROL.COM.AU
Which years should I buy or avoid?

CarSwitch, a UAE used-car marketplace, rates 2015–2017 trucks as the value sweet spot and the 2020–2021 facelifts as the best recent buys. Perth dealer Westside Auto calls the 2020-on facelift Patrols the most desirable on the used market. CarSwitch suggests caution with 2010–2012 trucks because of transmission valve-body problems and early fit-and-finish issues. In the US, run any 2020–2021 Armada's VIN for the fuel-pump recall.

SOURCE · CARSWITCH · WESTSIDE AUTO · NHTSA
What fuel economy should I expect?

The EPA rates every 2017–2024 Armada 4WD at 13 mpg city, 18 highway and 15 combined. In Australia the official Patrol figure is 14.4 L/100 km, and 4X4 Australia averaged 15.2 L/100 km on test, or about 12 to 13 on the freeway.

SOURCE · FUELECONOMY.GOV · 4X4 AUSTRALIA
How much can it tow?

3,500 kg braked for the Patrol (Cars.co.za, 4X4 Australia) and 8,500 lb for the 2017 Armada (AutoGuide). Owners of HBMC trucks report rear sag under heavy ball weights, and airbag kits are made specifically for HBMC suspension.

SOURCE · CARS.CO.ZA · AUTOGUIDE · NISSANPATROL.COM.AU
Does it need premium fuel?

The EPA lists regular fuel for 2017–2020 Armadas and premium for the 2021–2024 trucks with the 400 hp tune.

SOURCE · FUELECONOMY.GOV
Was there a diesel?

No. The Y62 was sold with petrol V8s and, in some markets, a 4.0 V6. Cars.co.za lists the lack of a turbo-diesel among its weaknesses.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · CARS.CO.ZA
What transmission fluid does it take?

Nissan Matic-S in the seven-speed automatic, and Nissan ATX 90A in the transfer case. Patrolapart gives the transfer-case service refill as 1.5 litres.

SOURCE · PATROLAPART
What replaced it?

The Y63 Patrol, unveiled in Abu Dhabi on 3 September 2024, with a 3.8 V6 or a 3.5 twin-turbo V6 (425 hp, 700 Nm) and a nine-speed automatic. The matching Armada went on sale in the US in December 2024, and Wikipedia says Australia gets the Y63 in November 2026.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
