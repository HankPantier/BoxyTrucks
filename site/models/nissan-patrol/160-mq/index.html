PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

In 1980 Nissan replaced the long-running 60 Series Patrol with the 160, sold in Australia and New Zealand as the MQ and, after a 1983 update, unofficially called the MK. In Japan it became the Safari. Underneath it was still a 1960s recipe: a separate chassis, live axles and leaf springs at both ends, and a two-speed offset transfer case. GoAuto called it 'a decisive step towards civility': more space, a less truck-like cabin, better ride and braking, and a long list of variants aimed at Toyota's 40 Series and 60 Series Land Cruisers.

FOUR ENGINES AND A 24-VOLT DIESEL

GoAuto says Australia's MQ launched with the 3.2-litre (3,246 cc) SD33 diesel six, rated at 70 kW and 215 Nm, with the 2.8-litre L28 petrol six (88 kW, 201 Nm) arriving in April 1981 and a short-lived 4.0-litre P40 petrol rated at 90 kW and 280 Nm. Owners describe the P40 as rare and very torquey, and the L28 as the one that will rev all day. A four-speed manual was standard. From late 1981 the seven-seat Deluxe wagon could have a three-speed automatic, which Wikipedia says was limited to long-wheelbase L28 trucks.

The SD33 is the engine with a story. It's a slow overhead-valve diesel six built for trucks and forklifts. Wikipedia notes the same engine powered International Scout II diesels in the late 1970s, and a turbo version went into 6,400 Scouts in 1980. In the MQ it ran 24-volt electrics with twin batteries, one on each side of the engine bay. 4X4 Australia notes the later switch to 12 volts made the truck much easier to fit with accessories.

THE MK UPDATE

The 1983 facelift turned the 160 into the 161. Wikipedia says the Australian 'MK' label never appeared in Nissan literature or parts catalogues. Rectangular headlights replaced round ones, the front suspension was upgraded and most models got a fifth gear. A high-roof wagon arrived, along with the SD33T turbo-diesel, which 4X4 Australia lists at 81 kW and 255 Nm. Wikipedia says the turbo engines got a larger clutch and oil cooler, and that the naturally aspirated SD33 switched to three-ring pistons, oil squirters and a spin-on oil filter. The Datsun badge disappeared in 1984 in most markets.

By 1985, GoAuto counts 21 Australian variants, from a two-seat rural hardtop to a seven-seat Deluxe wagon. Power steering and a limited-slip rear differential were standard by then. 4X4 Australia remembers the bright colours and 'loud' graphics of the era. GoAuto also lists an updated P40 petrol rated at 84 kW and 270 Nm by 1985. Owners also list the MK's extra inner-guard bracing, stepped rear shackles and bigger front shackle bolts among the differences.

SPAIN, IRAN AND THE LONG TAIL

Nissan bought into Spain's Motor Ibérica in 1980, and the first Spanish-built Patrol left the Zona Franca plant near Barcelona in early 1983, according to Wikipedia. To meet local-content rules it used a Spanish Perkins MD27 four-cylinder diesel, later replaced by the Nissan-built 2.8-litre A428 and its turbocharged version. Spain's facelifted version became the 260, built into the 2000s. Wikipedia also lists 160 production in Iran by Pars Khodro (1984–2003), South Africa (1983–1991) and the Philippines.

The coil-sprung Y60 'GQ' replaced the 160 from 1987, and forum regulars still tell would-be MQ buyers to 'buy a GQ' instead. Owners on NissanPatrol.com.au report MKs passing 780,000 and even 900,000 km on their original engines, along with rust bad enough that the trucks can't be driven without serious welding. Both stories are typical of the 160. Today it's the cheapest way into an old-school leaf-sprung Patrol, and the parts that matter most (swivel-hub kits, springs and bearings) are still sold for it in Australia.

SIDEBAR · PARIS–DAKAR 1987

Car 211 and the diesel class win

Nissan says its 1987 Paris–Dakar Patrol, car 211, driven by Miguel Prieto with Ramon Termens, finished ninth overall and won the diesel category. It was the first diesel to finish the event inside the top ten. The sister car, 212, crewed by Jorge and Hansi Babler, retired after rolling down a dune.

Nissan's restoration release lists a 2,800 cc four-cylinder turbodiesel making 146 hp in a 1,600 kg truck. Nissan doesn't name the engine. A 2.8-litre four matches the Perkins-derived A428T that Wikipedia says Spanish-built Patrols used, rather than the Japanese and Australian SD33 sixes. The car was later restored at Nissan's technical centre in Barcelona.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The 160 is a leaf-sprung, live-axle Patrol of the old school: a ladder frame, semi-elliptic leaf springs at both ends, kingpin swivel hubs, a two-speed transfer case and a choice of engines that all trade speed for torque. Underneath the MQ and MK are much the same truck. The differences are in the front suspension, shackles, electrics and gearbox.

PART 03

Buying one

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

What they cost

160 Patrols are still cheap in the US, where they're gray-market curiosities. Japanese fire trucks and imported Latin American and South African wagons make up most of the sales.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust: where to lookNissanPatrol.com.au members list roof gutters, the plenum under the windscreen (a leak there rots the floor and the low-set body mounts), front floor pans, the radiator support, the inner guard under the battery tray, door bottoms, rear lower quarter panels and the top of the rear wheel arch. Chassis-to-body mount points are where the worst rust lives.
  2. 02
    Turbo or not?A turbo SD33T has 'Turbo' on the rocker cover and a turbocharger on the exhaust side. Owners warn that some non-turbo engines wear turbo rocker covers, so look for the turbo itself.
  3. 03
    24 or 12 voltsMQ SD33 diesels ran 24-volt electrics with twin batteries; MKs are 12-volt. The engine number won't tell you. Owners say the starter, injection-pump solenoid and glow plugs are the voltage-sensitive parts, so a half-converted truck can be a headache.
  4. 04
    Which diff is under itRear axles were C200, H233 or H260 (utes) depending on market and model. The ratio is stamped on a tin plate at the front of the driver's-side inner guard (for example HG43 means H233, 4.3:1). Members warn that MQ H233 parts and lockers aren't interchangeable with the later H233B in GQs and GUs.
  5. 05
    Can you undo the plugs and grease it?One member's advice: make sure you can crack the drain and fill plugs on both diffs, the gearbox and the transfer case, and buy a good grease gun. The leaf-sprung Patrol has far more grease nipples than a coil-sprung GQ, including greasable shackles.
  6. 06
    Suspension historyOriginal springs are 40 years old and usually sagged. Ask what's been replaced. Owners say a rebuild with new leaves, U-bolts, shocks and shackles quickly costs more than an MQ is worth, and shackle extensions can invert.
  7. 07
    Overheating and the head gasketAn SD33T that keeps losing coolant isn't automatically a head-gasket job. Forum diagnosis starts with the radiator, cap, hoses, thermostat and fan clutch. If the head does come off, the SD33T needs its own head gasket; the non-turbo SD33 gasket doesn't fit it correctly.
  8. 08
    Import paperwork (US)Nissan never sold the 160 Patrol new in the US. US examples on Bring a Trailer came from Japan (Safari fire trucks), South Africa and Colombia. Check the title, the import documents and that the chassis number matches.

Spotting the real thing

MQs, MKs, Japanese Safaris and Spanish-built trucks look alike but differ in electrics, engines and parts, so identify the truck before you order parts.

  1. ID
    MQ vs MKMQ: round headlights, two-spoke wheel, flat radio pod, four holes in the stone tray. MK: rectangular headlights, three-spoke wheel, tapered pod with clock, six slots in the stone tray, rear indicators in the bumper, extra inner-guard bracing (NissanPatrol.com.au).
  2. ID
    VIN and model codeA Japanese-built truck's VIN carries the model code; one owner's reads JN10KR160U0… Parts sellers list codes such as KR160 (SD33), KM160/WMG160 (L28), VG160 (P40) and URG/VRG/WRG160 (SD33 and SD33T). Spanish-built trucks may show 260.
  3. ID
    Turbo or notAn SD33T has a turbocharger on the exhaust side and 'Turbo' on the rocker cover, but owners warn that turbo covers turn up on non-turbo engines.
  4. ID
    Diff plateA tin plate at the front of the driver's-side inner guard gives the rear axle code and ratio, e.g. HG43 for an H233 at 4.3:1. Crown wheels are also stamped with tooth counts, such as 37:8 (4.625).
  5. ID
    24 vs 12 voltsMQ SD33 diesels are 24-volt, with batteries on both sides of the engine bay; MKs are 12-volt with one battery on the passenger side. The engine number won't tell you.

Importing & legality

Nissan never sold the 160 Patrol new in the US. US examples are gray imports: right-hand-drive Japanese Safaris (often ex-fire trucks), Australian MQs and MKs, South African trucks, and left-hand-drive examples from Spain and Latin America.

Every 160 is past the 25-year mark, so NHTSA's exemption from federal safety standards applies, counted from the vehicle's date of manufacture. Titling, inspection and emissions rules vary by state, and California is the hardest. Check them before you buy, and make sure the chassis number matches the import paperwork.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

Asked on every forum

Patrol 160 / MQ questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

About 31x10.50. In an ExplorOz thread, an owner on stock, saggy springs was running 31x10.5s, and another suggested 32x11.5s might fit once springs were reset. The consensus for 33s is a 2-inch spring lift. For 35s, owners add a 2-inch body lift, and 38s took a 3-inch body lift with longer shackles. MQs don't have much wheel travel, so guard clearance is tight but workable.

SOURCE · EXPLOROZ
MQ or MK: how do I tell them apart?

MQs have round headlights, a two-spoke wheel and a flat radio pod. MKs have rectangular headlights, a three-spoke wheel, a tapered pod with a clock, separate rear indicators in the bumper and extra inner-guard bracing. Owners add that MQs use straight shackles and MKs stepped ones, MQ diesels are 24-volt, and MK petrols could have a factory five-speed.

SOURCE · NISSANPATROL.COM.AU
Which engine should I buy?

For touring, most owners want the SD33T turbo-diesel. The non-turbo SD33 is slow but long-lived: members report MKs past 780,000 and 900,000 km on original engines. The L28 petrol revs freely but has less torque, and the P40 4.0 is rare and the torque king. On a steep riverbed, one owner says the L28 'has to scream' where the turbo diesel is just above idle.

SOURCE · NISSANPATROL.COM.AU · GOAUTO
Is my SD33 24-volt?

If it's an MQ diesel with batteries on both sides of the engine bay, almost certainly. MKs are 12-volt. You can't tell from the engine number. Owners say the starter, injection-pump solenoid and glow plugs are the parts that differ by voltage.

SOURCE · NISSANPATROL.COM.AU · 4X4 AUSTRALIA
Can I bolt a turbo onto a non-turbo SD33?

People have, but owners advise against it for a remote-area truck. The SD33T differs in its head gasket, pistons and rings, piston oil squirters, injection-pump delivery valves and injectors. Using an SD33 head gasket on a turbo engine can block water and oil passages.

SOURCE · NISSANPATROL.COM.AU · WIKIPEDIA
What diff ratio do I have?

Read the tin plate at the front of the driver's-side inner guard (HG43 means H233, 4.3:1) or the tooth count stamped on the crown wheel. Owners list roughly 4.625 for MQ L28/SD33, 4.11 for the P40, 4.33 for MK and 4.88 for H260 utes, and say all except the H260 centres interchange.

SOURCE · NISSANPATROL.COM.AU
Can I fit a locker?

Yes, but buy for the right diff. Members say the MQ's H233 isn't the GQ/GU's H233B, so H233B lockers such as ARB's RD135/RD136 won't fit. One member gives ARB's MQ H233 part as RD04. Check ARB's current application chart before ordering.

SOURCE · NISSANPATROL.COM.AU · ARB
Should I convert it to coil springs?

Forum regulars mostly say no: Nissan already did the conversion, and it's called the GQ. If you love the MQ/MK body, one member suggests putting it on a GQ rolling chassis rather than engineering coils into a leaf-spring frame.

SOURCE · NISSANPATROL.COM.AU
Was it sold in the US, and can I import one?

Not new. Every 160 is past the 25-year mark, so NHTSA's safety-standard exemption applies. Bring a Trailer has sold Japanese Safari fire trucks, a South African L28 wagon and a Colombian P40 hardtop in the US. State titling and emissions rules still apply.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER · NHTSA
What's one worth?

Not much, by classic-4x4 standards. Bring a Trailer sales of 160-series trucks from 2021 to 2026 ran from $7,500 (a 1981 Safari fire truck) to $12,750 (a 1982 P40 two-door from Colombia), with a South African L28 wagon at $12,000.
