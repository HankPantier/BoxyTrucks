Patrol 60 Series

60 · G60 · H60K60 / KG60 HARDTOP · WG60 WAGONP 4.0 L OHV I63-SPEED · TWIN-STICK TRANSFERUS 1962–1969

“The 60 is the Patrol Americans could actually buy, for seven years, from a Datsun dealer, with a cowboy on the TV ads. It's a 4.0-litre truck-engined six, a three-speed and a twin-stick transfer case in a body that looks like a Jeep that went to finishing school. Most survivors stateside now arrive by ship from Colombia.”

PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

In October 1960 Nissan replaced the Jeep-like 4W66 Patrol with the 60 Series, and then built it for twenty years. Wikipedia lists the soft-top 60 on a 2,200 mm wheelbase and the G60 on 2,500 mm, with an extra-long H60 that could seat eight. Hardtops (KG60) followed in 1963. Under every one sat Nissan's P-series straight-six, a three-speed gearbox and a two-speed transfer case with part-time four-wheel drive. It was Nissan's answer to Toyota's FJ40, and from 1962 to 1969 it was also the only Nissan-badged vehicle sold in America.

THE P-SERIES SIX

The engine is the 60's whole personality. Wikipedia traces the P back to Nissan's pre-war Type A, a licence-built Graham-Paige design, by way of the sidevalve NC six it replaced. The P kept the same 3,956 cc and 85.7 x 114.3 mm bore and stroke but moved the valves overhead. It launched at 125 PS at 3,400 rpm, rose to 135 PS and reached 145 PS with the 1965 changes. It has bathtub-shaped combustion chambers and a fully balanced seven-bearing crank. Later versions were badged P40, and a fire-service PF version got a stronger alternator and a block heater.

It's a long-stroke truck engine, and it sounds like one. Australian 60s were rated at 93 kW, according to GoAuto, and Bring a Trailer's listings quote 145 hp for 1960s US trucks, 130 hp for 1971 Colombian trucks and 128 hp with 217 lb-ft for the last four-speed trucks of 1980–82. The same basic six carried on in the 160/MQ as the P40 and in Spain's 260 until 1993, which is why Patrol people still recognise its rocker cover.

DATSUN'S COWBOY TRUCK

Canadian and American buyers could get a Patrol from 1962 until 1969, sold through Datsun dealers. Nissan's US heritage page says the Patrol was the only vehicle sold and marketed in the US as a Nissan before 1983. Datsun's first American TV commercial was shot in 1963 for the Patrol. When the company heard that Roy Rogers liked the truck, it offered him a Patrol and two pickups instead of a fee, and Goodwood says the singing cowboy went on to front Datsun's low-budget TV ads.

Restorer SNLC, who cites twenty years with these trucks, wrote on IH8MUD that America got short-wheelbase L60 soft-tops and KL60 hardtops, all with a three-speed, a twin-stick transfer case and a light-duty C216 rear axle. Dealers fitted Warn free-wheeling hubs and a Koenig PTO winch driven from a Nissan PTO box. Then Nissan stopped. Curbside Classic says it's unclear why imports ended after 1969. The next Patrol-based vehicle sold in North America was the Infiniti QX56 of 2010, a cousin of the Y62.

SIMPSON DESERT, JONGAS AND COLOMBIA

Nissan Australia claims the 60 was the first vehicle to drive across the Simpson Desert, in 1962, and re-enacted the trip in 2012 to promote the Y62. Goodwood notes that a Toyota Land Cruiser support vehicle may have got there first, so treat the claim as marketing history. In India, the Vehicle Factory Jabalpur began assembling the Patrol as the Jonga around 1969. Wikipedia says it served the Indian Army until the late 1990s. In Bangladesh's Chittagong Hill Tracts, Patrol 60s and Jongas still work as Chander Gari, carrying passengers and produce. They're the trucks in this page's photos.

The 60 outlived its own replacement in some places. Bring a Trailer has sold Colombian-market Patrols titled as 1980 and 1982 'LG-61' trucks that keep the 60's body but use a four-speed. Colombia also keeps restorations going: SNLC's 1964 got new floor pans and an OEM steering-box seal from there. By the time the coil-sprung Y60 arrived in 1987, Japanese production of the 60 had been over for seven years, and the Y61 that followed shared nothing with it but the name. Nissan's Heritage Collection in Nashville keeps a 1967 found as a barn find on an Iowa farm.

SIDEBAR · RARITY

How many came to America?

Nissan hasn't published a US sales total that we could find. Nissan's 2024 heritage release on its 1967 Patrol and Curbside Classic's histories both say only that the truck was sold from 1962 to 1969, and Curbside's Rich Baron calls it 'small numbers'.

The most specific figure comes from restorer SNLC on IH8MUD: 2,616 Patrols sold in the USA from 1962 to 1969, of which about 950 were soft-tops. He adds that nobody knows whether that number includes Canada. A Curbside Classic reader estimated 'about 4000'. Treat both as enthusiast figures, not factory data. Either way, US-delivered trucks are scarce: six of the ten 60-series trucks Bring a Trailer sold in 2025–26 were recent imports from Colombia.

PART 02

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The 60 is a 1950s recipe done carefully: a ladder frame, leaf springs and live axles at both ends, closed-knuckle front swivels, drum brakes all round, and a big, slow overhead-valve six. A transmission-mounted drum handbrake, a PTO take-off and dealer-fitted hubs and winches tell you it was built for work, not commuting.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust in the usual placesSNLC's restoration needed new rear floor steel, fender kick-vent repairs and new metal under the gearbox crossmember. A 1965 on BaT had rust through the driver's footwell. He also found that Nissan didn't seam-seal the body seams on early trucks, so rust starts inside the joints. Check floors, footwells, kick vents, door bottoms and the crossmember under the transmission mounts.
  2. 02
    US-delivered or imported?US trucks were short-wheelbase L60 and KL60s with 80-mph speedometers. A 1965 on BaT with a US-market chassis number (4-L60-02697) had one, while Colombian trucks show km/h. Many 60s now for sale in the US came from Colombia in the past few years. Check the import papers and title, and that the chassis plate matches.
  3. 03
    Transfer-case seals and the handbrakeSNLC says it's common for old Patrols to blow the front or rear output seals on the transfer case, and a leaking rear seal ruins the transmission-mounted drum handbrake. Look for oil on the handbrake drum and ask whether the parking brake actually holds. One 1967 on BaT had no working parking brake.
  4. 04
    Brakes that work on all four wheelsThese are four-wheel drum trucks. One BaT 1967 had its rear brakes disabled. SNLC had none of four master cylinders good enough to use without boring and sleeving. Budget for wheel cylinders, hoses and a master cylinder unless the seller has receipts.
  5. 05
    Front swivels and axle jointsEarly trucks used Tracta joints and later ones Birfields, according to SNLC. Pitted knuckle balls are the big problem: without a smooth surface for the seal, he warns, keeping the knuckle dry is very difficult. Look for weeping swivel seals and play at the top and bottom of each wheel.
  6. 06
    Original engine and fuel systemThe P six used a single-barrel Hitachi carburettor. Some BaT trucks now run electric fuel pumps that bypass the mechanical pump, and one has an aluminium radiator and a plastic tank. Those changes are reasonable on a driver but worth knowing on a 'correct' restoration.
  7. 07
    Dealer options worth moneyA front PTO winch (Koenig, with Nissan's PTO box) and 1960s Warn hubs are period US dealer options. SNLC says a complete PTO assembly can sell for $1,000 among Patrol people, and that only about a quarter of US trucks had a PTO winch.
  8. 08
    Mileage and paperworkFive-digit odometers have rolled or been replaced on many trucks. Several recent BaT listings were sold with 'true mileage unknown'. Judge on condition and documented work, not the odometer.

Spotting the real thing

US-delivered trucks, Colombian imports, Japanese fire trucks and Indian Jongas all look alike, and many US 60s for sale today are recent imports. Know which one you're looking at.

  1. ID
    US-market trucksRestorer SNLC says the US got short-wheelbase L60 soft-tops and KL60 hardtops, all with a three-speed and a C216 rear axle. A BaT US-market 1965 had chassis 4-L60-02697 and an 80-mph speedometer.
  2. ID
    ImportsColombian trucks on BaT show km/h speedometers and chassis numbers such as L60…, KL60… and, on 1980–82 LG-61 trucks, G61…, with four-speed gearboxes. Ask for the import paperwork.
  3. ID
    Front axle jointsSNLC says early front axles (before about 1968) use Tracta joints and later ones Birfields.
  4. ID
    Speedometer drivePre-1966 trucks drive the speedometer from the front of the transfer case; later trucks have a splined pinion behind the 4WD flange.
  5. ID
    Fire PatrolsWikipedia says the two-wheel-drive Fire Patrol lacks the 4x4 badge, transfer lever and front differential, but every Fire Patrol has a PTO lever for the pump.

Importing & legality

Nissan sold the 60 new in the US only from 1962 to 1969, so many trucks for sale today are imports, especially from Colombia. Every 60 is well past the 25-year mark, so NHTSA's exemption from federal safety standards applies, counted from the date of manufacture.

State titling and emissions rules still apply. Several recent BaT imports were titled in Florida. Make sure the chassis number matches the import documents.

Rules change and vary by state and country. Confirm with your DMV and a licensed importer before you buy.

THE FIXSNLC's rebuild of a 1964's front axle: strip the hub, spindle and axle shaft (Tracta or Birfield joint), then the knuckle cap and kingpin or trunnion bearings. Check the knuckle balls. Rear axle shaft bearing
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a day per sideSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSGear oil on the rear brake shoes, a rumble from one rear wheel, or axle end-play.
THE FIXSNLC says the rear wheel bearing on the 60 isn't shimmed for preload. It's preset by the spacer under the bearing and bearing cage, which is why the nut must be torqued properly: 157 ft-lb in the early manual and over 200 ft-lb in late-1970s manuals. Pull the shaft (it needs the right puller to leave the bearing cage), press the bearing off and the new one on with a drift or pipe, with the wheel studs fitted first. Then shim the shaft for factory end-play. He made his own socket for the bearing nut.

Asked on every forum

Patrol 60 Series questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

We couldn't find a forum consensus for the 60, so here's what's documented. Stock was a narrow 16-inch bias-ply, 7.00-16 or 7.50x16 depending on the truck. One 1969 on Bring a Trailer runs 30x9.50 tyres on 15-inch wheels. A 1982 Colombian LG-61 runs 33x12.50 tyres on 15s, with longer rear shackles. Narrow tall tyres such as a 7.50x16 suit the truck's look, gearing and steering best.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER · IH8MUD
How many were sold in the US?

Nissan hasn't published a figure we could find. The most specific number is a restorer's on IH8MUD: 2,616 from 1962 to 1969, about 950 of them soft-tops. He's unsure whether that includes Canada. A Curbside Classic reader estimated about 4,000.

SOURCE · IH8MUD · CURBSIDE CLASSIC
What engine does it have?

Nissan's P-series 3,956 cc overhead-valve straight-six, rated at 125 PS at first and 145 PS from 1965. It's the same family as the P40 in the 160/MQ. Wikipedia traces it back to the pre-war Graham-Paige-based Type A.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Three-speed or four-speed?

Wikipedia says the F3B83L gearbox started with three speeds and later had four. Restorer SNLC says every US truck was a three-speed with a twin-stick transfer case. The Colombian LG-61 trucks sold on BaT have four-speeds.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · IH8MUD · BAT
What's a 60 worth?

On Bring a Trailer in 2025–26, ten 60-series trucks sold for $6,000 to $23,000. Most fell between about $10,000 and $18,000. The top sale was a refurbished 1965 from Colombia; the cheapest was a rusty US-market 1965 with a patina repaint.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER
Are parts available?

More than you'd think. RockAuto lists gasket sets, spark plugs, hoses and drum brake hardware for the 1967 Patrol 4.0. SNLC says bearings and seals aren't a problem, that engine rebuild kits and body parts are sold in the US, and that OEM seals and floor pans can still be found in Colombia. Trim rubber and NOS parts are the hard ones.

SOURCE · ROCKAUTO · IH8MUD
Can I import one from Colombia or Australia?

Yes. Every 60 is well past the 25-year mark, so NHTSA's exemption from federal safety standards applies. Many US trucks on Bring a Trailer are recent Colombian imports titled in Florida. State titling and emissions rules still apply.

SOURCE · NHTSA · BRING A TRAILER
Did a Patrol really cross the Simpson Desert first?

Nissan Australia says the 60 Series was the first vehicle to cross the Simpson Desert, and re-enacted the 1962 trip in 2012. Goodwood notes that a Toyota Land Cruiser support vehicle may have arrived first, so the claim is disputed.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · GOODWOOD
Is a Jonga a Patrol?

Essentially. Wikipedia says India's Vehicle Factory Jabalpur began assembling the quarter-ton Patrol as the Jonga around 1969. Indian Army Jongas served until the late 1990s, and a civilian diesel version sold fewer than 200 in 1996.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Free-wheeling hubs and a winch: original or not?

Both can be correct for a US truck. SNLC says 1960s Warn hubs were sold by US dealers, and dealers fitted Koenig winches driven from Nissan's PTO box. He puts the share of US trucks with a PTO winch at about a quarter.
