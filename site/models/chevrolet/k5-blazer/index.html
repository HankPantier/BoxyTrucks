PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

For 1973 General Motors redesigned its whole light-truck line. GM called it the 'Rounded Line', but everyone else calls it the square body, and the K5 Blazer came along with it. The formula stayed the same: take a four-wheel-drive half-ton pickup, shorten it, and make the roof removable. The new Blazer was 7 inches longer than the first generation on a wheelbase 2.5 inches longer. It stayed in production for 19 model years, through two facelifts, a diesel and an Army contract. GMC sold the same truck as the Jimmy, and the long-wheelbase Suburban as the GMC Suburban.

A PICKUP WITH THE ROOF OPTIONAL

The K5 was built as an answer to the International Scout and the Ford Bronco. Because it shared its platform with the C/K pickups, it was bigger, roomier and more stable on the road than either. It also cost less to build, and buyers could add pickup options such as automatic transmissions, air conditioning and radios. By 1970 it was outselling its rivals. Rivals soon copied the idea. Jeep shortened its Gladiator into the Cherokee for 1974, and Ford moved the Bronco onto the F-100 platform for 1978.

From 1973 to 1975 the Blazer was a fully open truck behind the windshield, with a choice of a removable hardtop or a convertible top. In 1976 GM made it a half-cab: a fixed steel roof over the front seats, which improved rollover protection and shared more parts with the pickups, and a removable fiberglass top over the back. That rear top still came off in 1991. The tailgate had a glass window that wound down inside it, either by hand crank or by an electric motor. The motor is a known weak point, so many of these windows no longer work.

SMALL BLOCKS, ONE DIESEL AND FOUR TRANSFER CASES

The 250 straight-six was the base engine until 1982. Most buyers chose a small-block V8: a 307, 305 or 350, or from 1975 a 400. In 1973 Popular Science tested a 175 hp 350 automatic with full-time 4WD. It took 22.5 seconds to reach 60 mph, and the testers recorded 9.6 mpg at a steady 45 mph. For 1982 GM added a 6.2-liter diesel V8 developed with Detroit Diesel. It made 130 hp and 240 lb-ft at launch and fit the same mounts as the gas V8. In 1987 throttle-body injection arrived, and after that the TBI 350 was the standard engine.

Transfer cases tell you the truck's age. From 1973 to 1980 you got either the gear-driven, part-time NP205 or the chain-driven, full-time NP203 with a lockable center differential. GM dropped full-time 4WD after 1979. For 1981 the aluminum NP208 arrived, a part-time case you could shift into 4WD at up to 25 mph, along with automatic locking hubs. From 1989 the NP241 replaced it. Up front, early trucks had a Dana 44, and GM switched to its own corporate 10-bolt from 1977. The rear axle was a 12-bolt, then a 10-bolt. A full-floating 14-bolt from a three-quarter-ton truck is the swap owners usually make.

SILVERADO, CHEYENNE AND THE ARMY

Trim levels followed the pickups. Custom Deluxe was the base model. Cheyenne added carpet, a headliner and extra insulation, and from 1975 Silverado was the top trim. Cheyenne trucks are usually 1970s models, because the name was dropped after 1981 and came back as a lower trim on the 1989–91 R/V trucks. Silverado ran to the end. GMC's Jimmy used Sierra names. In 1976 and 1977 a camper conversion was also offered: about 1,800 Blazer Chalet and Jimmy Casa Grande trucks with a pop-up camper body built by Chinook.

Then the Army bought some. Between 1983 and 1987 GM built the M1009, a K5 rated at three-quarter-ton capacity, as part of its Commercial Utility Cargo Vehicle program. It used the 6.2 diesel, a TH400 automatic and an NP208 transfer case. GM built about 70,000 CUCVs of all types, and many have since gone through government surplus sales. Some were replaced by the Humvees they had been bought to supplement. Ex-military M1009s now come up for sale regularly, and one sold on Bring a Trailer for $20,500 in 2026.

HOW IT ENDED

When the smaller S-10 Blazer appeared in 1983, GM officially renamed the big one the 'Full-Size Blazer' and the GMC version the 'K-Jimmy'. For 1987 the old trucks were renamed the R/V series, because the new GMT400 pickups took over the C/K name, and the Jimmy became the V-Jimmy. The Blazer, Suburban and crew cabs stayed on the old chassis. In 1988 the K5 badges disappeared. The 1989 trucks got a flatter grille in the style of the GMT400, plus the NP241 and 30-spline 10-bolt axle shafts. Rear anti-lock brakes and double-sided galvanized panels arrived for 1990.

1991 was the last year. The 1992 replacement moved to the GMT400 platform with a fixed roof, the Jimmy became the GMC Yukon, and in 1995 the full-size Blazer was renamed Tahoe. The square-body K5 is now an affordable classic that's very easy to modify. On Bring a Trailer, stock trucks often sell for less than $30,000, while LS-swapped restomods have reached six figures.

SIDEBAR · THE ARMY BLAZER

M1009 CUCV: a K5 with a 24-volt system

Around 1976 Vic Hickey, known as the father of the Humvee, used a K5 as a test vehicle for what became the CUCV program. The production M1009 had no air conditioning, an extra leaf in the springs, blackout lights, a brush guard, a rifle rack and paint in olive drab, woodland camouflage or desert tan. It ran on 10.00-15 tires and 3.08 gears, with open 10-bolt axles front and rear except for an Eaton automatic locker in the rear. It was rated for 1,200 lb of payload and a 3,000 lb towed load.

The odd part is the electrics. Two 12-volt alternators and two batteries are wired in series. The starter, the injection pump, the glow-plug circuit and the NATO slave receptacle use 24 volts, and everything else uses 12. The glow plugs are actually 12-volt plugs fed through a resistor pack on the firewall. Many civilian owners bypass the pack, fit a 12-volt starter and remove the second alternator.

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

Mechanically, the square-body K5 is a shortened K10 pickup. It has a ladder frame, leaf springs and live axles at both ends, a transfer case bolted straight to the transmission, and the driveline mounted high to clear obstacles. Most parts are shared with GM's light trucks, which is why they're still easy to find. The details changed a lot over 19 years, so identify the axles and transfer case before you buy parts.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Frame cracks at the steering boxThis is the classic K5 fault. On CK5 and GMSquareBody, owners say the frame cracks around and behind the steering-box bolts, often on the inside of the rail. You may not see the cracks with the box bolted on. Unbolt it if you can, or have someone rock the steering wheel while you watch for the frame moving. A properly welded reinforcement plate is fine; a bolt-on brace over an old crack isn't a fix.
  2. 02
    Rust in the usual placesCK5 members list the rockers, the front floor and the front seat-belt anchors, the rear wheel arches, the tail pan, the bottoms of the doors and tailgate, the cab mounts and the rear floor supports by the tailgate hinges. Pull up the carpet and smell for mildew. If the paint is bubbling from outside, the rust underneath is usually already serious.
  3. 03
    Tailgate window and top sealTry the rear window. It winds down inside the tailgate by crank or motor, the motor and its drive cable are known to fail, and a safety switch stops the glass going up if the tailgate isn't latched. Ask whether the top has been removed. Owners disagree on whether that always causes leaks, but the seal between the top and the body is the usual leak point.
  4. 04
    Is it really a 350?One CK5 member warns about 305s being sold as 350s, engines described as 'rebuilt' that never were, and a TH350 in a truck that should have a 700R4. Look for clean grease between the engine and transmission, which suggests nothing has been apart. Check the freeze plugs for weeping.
  5. 05
    Identify the axles and transfer caseOn the front axle, a 10-bolt cover is nearly round with no flat sides, while the Dana 44 has the familiar Dana shape. Mismatched gear ratios front and rear, or a missing front driveshaft, mean someone has swapped parts. NP205 and NP203 cases carry ID tags above the front output. On 1989–91 NP241s, a leaking slip-yoke rear output is common.
  6. 06
    Hubs and 4WD engagement1981 and later trucks came with automatic locking hubs, which seize with age. One King of the Hammers K5 owner bought a truck with a 'blown transmission' that turned out to be a transfer case stuck in neutral and seized automatic hubs. Engage 4WD on the test drive, and budget for manual hubs.
  7. 07
    Previous owners' workLook for hacked-in wiring, stereos and lights, cheap lift blocks and poor welding. One CK5 member says to pay extra for a properly done one-ton axle swap (8-lug axles) rather than a cheaply lifted 6-lug truck. Another suggests 1987–91 trucks for fuel injection, with 1989–91 the favorites.
  8. 08
    M1009 specificsCheck whether the 24/12-volt system is original or has been converted. If the resistor pack has been bypassed for 12-volt glow plugs, the starter, the alternator and the NATO slave receptacle in the grille also need dealing with. Expect 3.08 gears, 10.00-15 tires and a governed 55 mph top speed as delivered.

Spotting the real thing

After 35 to 50 years, many K5s have had engine, axle and transfer case swaps, and plenty of GMC Jimmys have been re-badged. These checks help you work out what a truck started out as.

  1. ID
    Front axleTorque King says the GM 10-bolt front cover is an oval about 11 in high by 10-1/4 in wide with no flat sides, unlike the Dana 44 cover. GMSquareBody members also note the two small ears on the 10-bolt housing. Dana 44s are typical of 1973–76, either could be fitted in 1977–78, and the 10-bolt is typical from 1979. Dana 44 and 10-bolt shafts don't interchange.
  2. ID
    Transfer caseNP205: a one-piece cast-iron case with a small three-bolt idler cover on the back and an ID tag above the front output. NP203: about 22–23 in long, in four bolted sections with a range box on the front and its tag on the chain case. NP208 (1981–88) and NP241 (1989–91) are aluminum, chain-driven cases, and the NP241's tag is on the rear of the case.
  3. ID
    305 or 350?CK5 members warn that 305s get sold as 350s. Wikipedia notes that 1981-on 305s used smaller 56 cc combustion chambers, against 76 cc. Check the casting numbers and the engine code before you pay a 350 price.
  4. ID
    Year by the front end1973–80 trucks mostly have round headlamps (Silverado-trim C/K trucks got square ones for 1980). From 1981 there's a new front end with available stacked square headlamps. The 1988 trucks lose the K5 badge and get a fender-mounted antenna. The 1989–91 trucks have the flatter, GMT400-style grille.
  5. ID
    A real M1009Genuine M1009s have the 6.2 diesel, a TH400 and an NP208, 10-bolt axles with 3.08 gears and an Eaton automatic rear locker, 10.00-15 tires, and a 24/12-volt system with two batteries, two alternators, a firewall glow-plug resistor pack and a NATO slave receptacle in the grille. Blackout lights, a brush guard and a rifle rack were standard.
  6. ID
    Model namingChevrolet badges read K5 until 1988. GM officially called it the Full-Size Blazer from 1983 and the V10 / V1500 Blazer from 1987. GMC's twin was the Jimmy, then the K-Jimmy, then from 1987 the V-Jimmy.

Swap a worn or leaking steering box
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 3–4 hoursSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSPlay at the wheel, wandering on the highway, fluid weeping from the box's seals, or a box that has been loosened by a cracking frame.
THE FIXInspect the frame first, because a new box on a cracked rail will soon loosen. Disconnect the steering shaft coupler and the pressure and return lines, pull the pitman arm with a puller, and unbolt the box. Fit the new box with new hardware and a new or rebuilt coupler. Refill and bleed the power steering by turning lock to lock with the front end raised. Lifted trucks often get a dropped pitman arm at the same time to correct drag-link angle.

Reseal an NP208 or NP241 and fix a leaking slip yoke
DIFFICULTY · 4 / 5TIME · ≈ A day (case out of the truck)SHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSATF dripping from the rear output, a driveline clunk, or a slip yoke pulling out as the suspension droops on a lifted truck. A worn chain can also skip or slap.
THE FIXBoth cases are aluminum, chain-driven and filled with ATF. Drain the case, drop both driveshafts and unbolt the case from the transmission adapter. Split the halves, check the chain for stretch and the bearings for wear, and replace all seals and gaskets. On a lifted NP241, a slip-yoke eliminator gives you a fixed rear yoke and a shorter shaft. Offroad Design's kit uses a common 32-spline yoke shortened by 1/4 inch. Refill with ATF.

Rebuild the front axle ends (ball joints, U-joints, bearings)
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ A weekendSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSDeath-grip steering, clunks over bumps, uneven tire wear, a front end that won't hold alignment, or clicking from the axle U-joints in tight turns in 4WD.
THE FIXIdentify the axle first. A Dana 44 and a GM 10-bolt look alike, but CK5 members warn that the inner parts and shafts don't interchange. Strip the hubs, calipers and spindles, pull the axle shafts, press out the old U-joints and ball joints, then press in new ones. Replace the spindle bearings and seals, repack the wheel bearings and set the preload. Finish with new hub seals and a front-end alignment.

THE FIXSupport the frame and take the weight off one spring at a time. 6.2 diesel glow plugs and controller
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–3 hoursSHOP COST · Parts only for DIY
SYMPTOMSHard cold starts, long cranking, white smoke until warm, or a wait light that misbehaves. On an M1009, a bad resistor pack or a mixed 12/24-volt conversion can cook new plugs.
THE FIXTest each glow plug's resistance and check that power reaches each one during the glow cycle. Replace failed plugs (or all eight) and the controller or relay if it isn't cycling. On M1009s, confirm whether the truck still has the 24-volt starting system and the firewall resistor pack before you buy plugs. Asked on every forum

Chevrolet K5 Blazer (1973–1991) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

About 32 inches. Hogback Fabrication says on CK5 that a stock K5 fits 32s without trimming and 33s with minor trimming. Other owners report 33s rubbing the inner fender at full lock and full bump, but fine on the road. On 16-inch wheels, 285/75R16s are commonly said not to rub. Wheel width and backspacing matter: one member suggests 15x8 wheels at about 3.5 inches of backspacing as the easy fit for 33x12.50s.

SOURCE · CK5 FORUMS
Which years should I look for?

It depends on what you want. The 1973–75 trucks have the fully open body, and 1976–80 trucks are the classic half-cab with the old grille, NP205 or NP203 and simpler mechanicals. CK5 members tend to recommend 1987–91 for throttle-body injection, with 1989–91 the favorites for the final front end, the NP241 and 30-spline axles. One member names 1988 for its TBI 350 and revised 700R4.

SOURCE · CK5 FORUMS · WIKIPEDIA
Does mine have a Dana 44 or a 10-bolt front axle?

Look at the cover. Torque King describes the GM 10-bolt cover as an oval about 11 by 10-1/4 inches with no flat sides, unlike the Dana 44's. 1973–76 half-tons used the Dana 44, 1977–78 trucks could have either, and from 1979 Blazers used the 10-bolt through 1991, according to one GMSquareBody member's list (another CK5 member says GMC used up its last Dana 44s through 1982). Thirty-spline 10-bolt shafts came in around 1989.

SOURCE · TORQUE KING · GMSQUAREBODY · CK5
Which transfer case do I have?

Up to 1980 you'll find the gear-driven NP205, a one-piece iron case with a small three-bolt idler cover on the back, or the full-time NP203, a long four-section case with a separate range box on the front. From 1981 the aluminum, chain-driven NP208 took over, and the NP241 replaced it from 1989. The 208 and 241 both take ATF.

SOURCE · OFFROAD DESIGN · DIESEL HUB · WIKIPEDIA
Is the 6.2 diesel any good?

It's slow but simple. At launch it made 130 hp and 240 lb-ft, with a mechanical injection pump and indirect injection, and it fits the same mounts as the gas V8. Known problems include cracked main-bearing webs, and crank failures linked to worn harmonic balancers. Inspect the balancer and find out the engine's history before you buy.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
What's different about an M1009?

It's a 1983–87 Army K5 with the 6.2 diesel, a TH400 and an NP208, 10-bolt axles with 3.08 gears and an Eaton automatic rear locker, heavier springs and 10.00-15 tires. It has a 24/12-volt electrical system and is rated for 1,200 lb of payload and 3,000 lb towed. GM built about 70,000 CUCVs of all types.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
How does the removable top work?

On 1973–75 trucks the whole roof behind the windshield came off, with a hardtop or a convertible top. From 1976 the front roof is steel and fixed, and only the fiberglass rear top unbolts. That stayed true through 1991. Offroad Design still sells a soft top for 1969–91 K5s and Jimmys.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · OFFROAD DESIGN
How good is the fuel economy?

Bad with a gas V8. Popular Science's 1973 test of a 350 automatic with full-time 4WD recorded 9.6 mpg at a steady 45 mph. A 1974 250 six with an automatic managed 13.7 mpg at the same speed. Offroad Design got 11–12 mpg on a road trip in its lifted 1988 TBI Suburban, which has the same drivetrain.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · OFFROAD DESIGN
What gears do I need for bigger tires?

More than you'd think. Offroad Design put 4.56 one-ton axles under its 1988 Suburban, with a TBI 350, a 700R4 and 35-inch tires, and found it had enough rpm but still not enough power. On CK5, one member recommends at least 4.56 for 38s and 5.13s for 35-inch-and-up tires on one-ton axles. M1009s left the factory with tall 3.08s.

SOURCE · OFFROAD DESIGN · CK5
Can I put one-ton axles under it?

Yes. A Dana 60 front and 14-bolt rear from a K30 is the classic upgrade. CK5 members note that the K30's rear spring pads sit about 2 inches narrower than the Blazer's, and the perches and shock tabs need moving. Driveshaft lengths change too. The bigger brakes may also need a larger master cylinder.

SOURCE · CK5 FORUMS
What is it worth?

Prices vary a lot. Bring a Trailer's 15 most recent K5 sales (August–October 2026) ran from $4,600 for a 1986 project to $130,000 for an LSA-powered 1973, with a median of $29,500. Stock trucks mostly sold for $15,000–38,500, and a 1984 M1009 sold for $20,500. Hagerty noted in 2019 that the Blazer's median #2-condition value had risen 164% since 2009.

SOURCE · BRING A TRAILER · HAGERTY
Can it tow?

The Army rated the M1009 for a 3,000 lb towed load. Wikipedia says the six-cylinder Blazer wasn't recommended for towing. For a civilian truck, go by the GVWR, axle ratio and hitch it actually has, and check the factory trailering guide for your year.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
