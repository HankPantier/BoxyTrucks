PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

For 1973 GM rebuilt its whole truck line, and the Suburban came out the other side as something new: a full-size wagon with four passenger doors. Chevrolet's 1973 brochure called it a 'Superwagon' and, counting the standard double rear doors, a 'six-door wagon.' Underneath it was still all truck, with a pickup frame, leaf springs and, on the K-series, a solid front axle and a two-speed transfer case. Its short-wheelbase sibling was the K5 Blazer. That mix of wagon body and truck hardware ran for 19 model years, and it's why these Suburbans still tow trailers, haul families and turn up at trailheads.

FROM CARRYALL TO 'SIX-DOOR WAGON'

The 1967–72 Suburban had an odd door count: one door on the driver's side and two on the passenger side. The 1973 truck switched to four side doors, more than a decade after the Jeep Wagoneer and International Travelall, according to Wikipedia. From the front doors back it shared its body with the new crew-cab pickup. Double panel-type rear doors were standard, and Chevrolet quoted an opening 40 inches high and 59.6 inches wide. A tailgate with a wind-down window was optional, cranked by hand or by an electric motor.

Chevrolet sold it in two series: the half-ton 10 and the three-quarter-ton 20, with C for two-wheel drive and K for 4x4. GMC used 15 and 25. The 1973 brochure lists GVWs of 5,400 to 8,200 lb, up to 144 cubic feet of load space and seating for nine with the second and third seats fitted. It also claims that a properly equipped Series 20 could move up to 14,000 lb of trailer, truck, passengers and luggage combined.

WHAT'S UNDER A 4x4 SUBURBAN

The engine you most often find in a K-series Suburban is the 350 small-block, rated at 175 net horsepower in four-barrel form at launch. The 400 V8 was a K-series-only option from 1975 to 1980, and the 6.2-liter Detroit Diesel V8 arrived for 1982. The 454 big-block is the exception. Hagerty and Chevrolet's own 1982 and 1987 spec sheets list it only in two-wheel-drive Suburbans, so a 454 4x4 'Burb is a swap.

Four-wheel-drive hardware changed several times. Early trucks used the gear-driven NP205 part-time case or, with a V8 and automatic, the NP203 full-time case, which had a lockable center differential. Full-time 4WD was dropped for 1980. For 1981 the aluminum, chain-drive NP208 arrived with automatic locking hubs that let you shift into 4WD at up to 25 mph. Its low range is 2.61:1, against 1.96:1 for the 205. Behind it sat a TH350 or TH400 automatic, an SM465 four-speed manual, or, from 1981, the 700R4 overdrive automatic.

NINETEEN YEARS OF RUNNING CHANGES

Hagerty's quick spotter's guide: 1973–80 trucks have round headlamps, a styled hood and vertical side markers; 1981–88 trucks have rectangular lamps, a flat hood and horizontal markers; and 1989 brought four small rectangular headlamps. For 1987, gasoline engines switched to throttle-body fuel injection and, because the new GMT400 pickups had taken the C/K name, the Suburban became the R (2WD) or V (4x4). Badging moved to 1500 and 2500.

The 1989 facelift gave it a flush, mostly black grille and a new steering wheel. For 1990 came power mirrors and optional rear-wheel ABS, and Wikipedia says the electronically controlled 4L80-E replaced the 700R4 for 1991. That was the last year: for 1992 the Suburban moved to the GMT400 platform, after 19 model years on the square body, and the K5 Blazer moved over with it. Late V1500s are the most refined of the breed, with fuel injection and an overdrive automatic, but they share the same frame, axles and rust problems as the early trucks.

WHY OVERLANDERS AND TOWERS STILL BUY THEM

Its appeal today is what Chevrolet sold it on in 1973: space and pulling power. Chevrolet's 1982 spec sheet lists gross combination ratings of 11,000 lb for a gas K10 and 12,000 lb for a K20. Behind the second row there's room for a fridge, a drawer system and a sleeping platform. When Offroad Design needed a family camping and four-wheeling truck, it bought a fuel-injected 1988 Suburban, gave it a modest lift and 33s, and drove it on Moab's Hell's Revenge, Kane Creek and Steel Bender trails without a winch or strap.

Plenty of dealers saw the same appeal when these trucks were new. Bring a Trailer has sold a 1990 GMC V1500 that Trail Wagons of Yakima, Washington, converted when new with its Chinook Trail Boss package, and a 1990 V1500 modified when new by Truk-Mate of Minnesota. The price for that ability is fuel and rust. Hagerty warns about rockers, floors and wheel wells, and says lifted trucks on big tires need frame bracing at the steering box or the frame will likely crack.

SIDEBAR · A SHOP TRUCK GOES TO MOAB

Offroad Design's 'Burly Burb'

Colorado's Offroad Design builds suspension for these trucks, and its own family hauler was an '88 Suburban. It started with a stock TBI 350, a TH700, an NP208, 10-bolt axles front and rear and 235/75 tires. The shop added 3 inches of lift at the front and 4 at the rear (the rear with a shackle flip), greasable bushings, braided brake lines, sway-bar disconnects and 33x12.50R15s on 15x8 military steel wheels. ORD put the suspension cost at $1,003.

Then it loaded the family and went to Moab for Memorial Day 2002. ORD wrote that the 'Burb was never winched or strapped and needed only a few stacked rocks, and returned 11–12 mpg on the road trip with the air conditioning running. ORD called that 'Not bad for a truck the size of a whale with open diffs.'

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The square-body Suburban uses GM's 1973–91 Rounded Line truck frame on a 129.5-inch wheelbase. K/V-series 4x4s have a live front axle on leaf springs, a GM Dana 44 or corporate 10-bolt depending on year and series, and a semi-floating rear axle, also on leaves. Chevrolet's spec sheets reserve the full-floating rear axle for the two-wheel-drive 454 trucks. The body has four side doors, with double rear doors as standard and a tailgate with a wind-down window as an option.

What to pay, what to check, and how to tell a good one from a clone.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Rust: rockers, floors and wheel wellsHagerty names these as the main problem areas, especially on early trucks. Also check the bottoms of all four doors and the rear doors or tailgate, and look under the carpet. Mill Supply and Auto Metal Direct sell the panels, but a structurally rotten 'Burb is a big job.
  2. 02
    Frame at the steering boxHagerty says lifted 4x4s on bigger tires need frame bracing at the steering box or cracking is likely. Offroad Design calls frame cracks there notorious on straight-axle GM trucks. Look for cracks, fresh welds or a brace that's already been fitted.
  3. 03
    Is it really a 4x4, and which case?Look for a front driveshaft and a V or K in the model badging. BaT has sold a C2500 with a 4x4 swap. Then identify the case: the NP203 (full-time, 1973–79) is long and in four sections, the NP205 is one piece of iron, and the NP208 is aluminum.
  4. 04
    Hubs on 1981-and-later trucksFrom 1981 GM fitted automatic locking hubs with the NP208, and manual hubs were optional. Make sure the front engages in 4WD and frees up in 2WD. Many owners have already swapped to manual hubs.
  5. 05
    Check rear HVAC and the dashHagerty notes that the Suburban-only rear heater and air-conditioning parts can be hard to fix, and that dash pads often crack down the center. Run both front and rear air and heat before you buy.
  6. 06
    Rear window and tailgateWikipedia notes that the wind-down rear glass is heavy, that hand-crank gears and electric motors wear, and that a drive cable links the motor to the regulator. A safety switch stops the glass rising if the tailgate isn't latched, so check that the tailgate is latched before you blame the motor.
  7. 07
    Check modifications carefullyHagerty's advice is to inspect any modifications carefully. Lifts, engine swaps and wiring jobs vary widely in quality. A stock, rust-free V1500 often makes a better starting point than someone else's half-finished build.
  8. 08
    6.2 diesel trucksWikipedia lists crankshaft failures related to age-worn harmonic balancers, plus main-bearing web cracks, as known 6.2/6.5 problems. Ask about the balancer and listen for knocks. A diesel is slow, but it gives you range.

Spotting the real thing

Nineteen years, two brands, three transfer cases, half- and three-quarter-ton versions, and plenty of 2WD-to-4WD conversions: work out exactly what you're looking at before you price it.

  1. ID
    Year by front endPer Hagerty: 1973–80 trucks have round headlamps, a styled hood and vertical side markers; 1981–88 trucks have rectangular lamps, a flat hood and horizontal markers; 1989–91 trucks have four small rectangular headlamps (two on economy trims, per Wikipedia).
  2. ID
    Model codesC = 2WD and K = 4x4, with 10/20 for Chevrolet half- and three-quarter-ton and 15/25 for GMC until 1980. For 1987–91 the trucks are R (2WD) and V (4x4), badged 1500/2500.
  3. ID
    True 4x4 or conversion?Check for a factory front axle, transfer case and K/V designation. BaT has sold a C2500 Suburban with a 4x4 swap. Also, no factory 4x4 Suburban had a 454 per Chevrolet's 1982 and 1987 spec sheets.
  4. ID
    Transfer caseNP203 (full-time, 1973–79): about 22–23 in long, four bolted sections, with a lock position. NP205: one-piece iron case, three-bolt idler cover, tag above the front output. NP208 (1981+): aluminum, chain-drive (Offroad Design, Wikipedia).
  5. ID
    Diesel trucksFrom 1982, 6.2 diesel trucks got their own fender and tailgate badging (Wikipedia). Diesels needed the B3J diesel equipment package per the 1982 spec sheet.

Repack front wheel bearings and replace spindle seals →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ Half a daySHOP COST · Bearing and seal kits
SYMPTOMSPlay at the top and bottom of the tire, a growl that changes on turns, or water in the hubs after crossings.
THE FIXRemove the wheel, caliper, hub and spindle nuts and slide the hub off. Clean and inspect the bearings and races, and replace them as a set if they're pitted. Pack the new bearings, fit a new hub seal and spindle seal, and reassemble, setting the bearing preload to the shop-manual spec. Reuse nothing that looks tired.

Fix a power tailgate window that won't move →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 2–4 hoursSHOP COST · Motor, cable or regulator, plus switch if needed
SYMPTOMSThe rear glass won't go up or down from the key switch or the dash switch, the motor runs but the glass doesn't move, or the motor gets hot and stops.
THE FIXFirst make sure the tailgate is fully latched. Wikipedia notes that a safety switch stops the glass rising if it isn't. Check for power at the motor from both the key switch and the dash switch. If the motor spins but the glass doesn't move, the cable between the motor and the regulator (similar to a speedometer cable) has probably failed. If the motor doesn't run, or overheats, replace it. Remove the inner tailgate panel, support the heavy glass, and replace the cable, motor or regulator. Lubricate the tracks before you refit the panel.

Rebush the leaf springs and cure the sag →
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ A daySHOP COST · Bushing kit; springs if they're flat
SYMPTOMSClunks, wandering, and a 'Burb that sits low at the rear or leans to one side.
THE FIXReplace the spring-eye and shackle bushings with the truck supported on the frame, one corner at a time. Offroad Design's leaf-spring bushing chart lists the right bushing for each position. If the springs have lost their arch, replace them. ORD describes the Suburban as 'typically saggy' and built its own '88 with 3 inches at the front and 4 at the rear so it would sit level. On a lean, ORD suggests swapping the springs side to side before assuming one is bad.

Asked on every forum

Chevrolet/GMC Suburban (1973–1991 square body) questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

On a half-ton, 31x10.50R15s are the safe choice: GMSquarebody owners of a 1987 Suburban 4x4 and a 1987 short-box report no rubbing at all. 33x10.50s on factory rims also work. A moderator ran 33x12.50s on a stock three-quarter-ton Suburban with no rubbing, but says a half-ton will almost certainly need trimming. On CK5, owners of the related half-ton K5 Blazer call 32s safe and say 33s rub at full lock.

SOURCE · GMSQUAREBODY · CK5
How many doors does it have?

Four side doors, plus double rear panel doors as standard or an optional tailgate with a wind-down window. Chevrolet called it a 'six-door wagon' in 1973. The three-door layout (one door on the driver's side, two on the passenger side) belongs to the 1967–72 Suburban.

SOURCE · CHEVROLET 1973 BROCHURE · WIKIPEDIA
Was there a factory 454 4x4 Suburban?

No. Chevrolet's 1982 and 1987 spec sheets list the 7.4-liter 454 only for the two-wheel-drive C20/R20, and Hagerty agrees it was 2WD only. The 4x4s got the 350 (plus the 400 from 1975 to 1980) or the 6.2 diesel. A 454 K-series Suburban has had an engine swap.

SOURCE · CHEVROLET SPEC SHEETS · HAGERTY
What's the 6.2 diesel like?

It's a naturally aspirated, indirect-injection 379 cu in V8 offered in Suburbans from 1982 to 1991. The 1987 spec sheet rates it at 130 hp and 240 lb-ft in 10-series trucks and 148 hp and 246 lb-ft in 20-series trucks. Wikipedia says it was built for fuel economy, and lists main-bearing web cracks and balancer-related crank failures as known problems. It's the same engine family as the Hummer H1's.

SOURCE · CHEVROLET 1987 SPEC SHEET · WIKIPEDIA
What's the difference between the NP203, NP205 and NP208?

The NP203 (1973–79 option) is full-time, with a center differential you lock for off-road use. The NP205 is a gear-driven, part-time cast-iron case with a 1.96:1 low range. The NP208 (from 1981) is aluminum and chain-driven, has a 2.61:1 low range, and with automatic hubs can shift into 4WD at up to 25 mph.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · OFFROAD DESIGN
How much can it tow?

Chevrolet's 1982 spec sheet gives gross combination ratings of 11,000 lb for a gas K10 and 12,000 lb for a K20 with the trailering package. The 1973 brochure says a properly equipped Series 20 could move up to 14,000 lb combined. A trailer's weight counts toward that total, along with the truck, its passengers and cargo.

SOURCE · CHEVROLET BROCHURES
Half-ton or three-quarter-ton?

In 1982 a K10 rode on P215/75R15 tires with 6,100–6,800 lb GVW, while a K20 had LT235/85R16s and 8,600 lb. The 20 series has heavier front and rear axle ratings (3,800 and 6,000 lb against 3,600 and 4,000). Half-tons ride better and use the common 6-on-5.5-inch wheel pattern.

SOURCE · CHEVROLET 1982 SPEC SHEET
Which years have fuel injection?

1987 to 1991. For 1987 all the gasoline engines switched to throttle-body injection, and the 5.7-liter V8 was rated at 210 hp and 300 lb-ft in 10-series trucks. The diesel already had mechanical injection.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · CHEVROLET 1987 SPEC SHEET
What does R/V mean?

For 1987, the new GMT400 pickups took the C/K name, so the old-body trucks became R (2WD) and V (4x4) to avoid confusion. A V1500 is a half-ton 4x4 and a V2500 a three-quarter-ton.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
Which years should I look for?

It depends on what you want. 1973–80 trucks have round headlamps and the NP203 or NP205, and Hagerty notes that the earlier trucks tend to have more rust. 1981–86 trucks have the NP208, overdrive and the 6.2 diesel option, which is slow and has known crank and balancer problems. For a truck to use, owners tend to recommend the 1987–91 trucks: one GMSquareBody member says TBI brings 'better mileage and more power stock than the carbed version', and on CK5 one owner says to stick with 1989–91 for their transmission and transfer-case setups. There's no harder consensus than that, so buy the straightest one.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · GMSQUAREBODY · CK5
What's one worth?

Hagerty says a usable example can be had for under $10,000 and an excellent one for $25,000–30,000. On Bring a Trailer, mostly original 4x4 Suburbans sold for about $7,900–37,250 between 2024 and 2026, with a median near $16,000. An exceptional 1973 Cheyenne Super Estate 4x4 reached $70,000.

SOURCE · HAGERTY · BRING A TRAILER
