Exercise your lockers

Engage the lockers every six months or so, on almost any boxy truck. Both of my locker problems, on the 80 Series and the G500, came from systems that weren’t used often enough.

Range Rover L322 problems to check before you buy

  1. Demand timing-chain paperworkOn 2003–2005 BMW-engined trucks, look for receipts for chains and guides, or budget for them. The nylon guides go brittle and break, and owners report failures clustering around 130,000–170,000 miles. On 2010–2012 5.0s, check for tensioner and guide updates. Listen for a rattle on a cold start.
  2. Cycle the air suspension through every heightThe compressor should be barely audible. Grinding means it's on its way out. Watch for slow rises, a lean, or warnings. On 2003–05 trucks, corroded or seized height sensors can stop the truck adjusting without leaving any fault codes.
  3. Test low range and the range-change motorSelect low and back again with the truck stationary. A range-change motor that has seized from lack of use can cost about £1,000 to fix. Make sure Hill Descent Control works too.
  4. Treat 'sealed for life' gearboxes as unsealedHesitant shifts or warning lights point to gearbox trouble. Ask whether the fluid and filter have ever been changed. Atlantic British recommends a service every 60,000 miles on later ZF six-speeds, including the leak-prone valve-body sleeve. A rebuild costs about £2,000 in the UK.
  5. Listen at full lockKnocking at low speed on full lock can mean a front differential problem, and many were recalled. Clonks over bumps from the rear point to perished hub bushes.
  6. Read the codes before and after the driveA pre-purchase diagnostic scan is cheap insurance. Read the codes before and after a long test drive, because sellers can clear faults just before you arrive. Check that every warning light comes on with the ignition and then goes out.
  7. Hunt for water and oilCheck for water in the rear fuse box and under the carpets. Check the M62 for valve-cover and other oil leaks, and look for white coolant streaks around the expansion tank. Expansion tanks fail.
  8. Check the brakes and wheelsL322s are hard on brakes; expect discs and pads about every 30,000 miles. Avoid 22-inch wheels with low-profile tyres, which ride badly and pinch-puncture easily.

Range Rover L322 recalls and service bulletins

  • Front differential coupling / propshaft spline wear ↗ 2003–2005 · NHTSA 08V-635The front diff coupling sleeve and propshaft may be misaligned and wear the splines, causing noise and vibration. Land Rover recalled 19,168 trucks.
  • Transmission parking pawl guide plate ↗ 2006 · NHTSA 05V-502Some trucks were built with an incorrectly made parking-pawl guide plate.
  • Front brake hose rupture ↗ 2006–2012 · NHTSA 15V-039One or both flexible front brake hoses may rupture and lose brake fluid (built 15 April 2005 to 4 September 2012).
  • Takata passenger airbag inflator (Zone A) ↗ 2007–2011 · NHTSA 16V-374Passenger frontal airbag inflator replacement, prioritised by state of registration.
  • Takata passenger airbag inflator (expansion) ↗ 2007–2012 · NHTSA 17V-015Further Takata inflator replacements by zone; see also 18V-011 and 18V-015.
  • Occupant classification system ↗ 2010 · NHTSA 09V-467An OCS fault can light the airbag warning and disable the passenger airbag.
  • Windshield installation ↗ 2012 · NHTSA 12V-485Windshields may have been installed incorrectly at the factory.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about Range Rover L322 reliability

Which years and engines should I buy or avoid?

Owners and buyers' guides generally rate the 2007–2009 Jaguar-engined trucks best: Terrain Response, a locking centre diff and the six-speed ZF. Buy a 2003–2005 BMW 4.4 only with receipts for timing guides (or if you'll do them yourself). Buy a 2010–2012 5.0 only with evidence that the chain tensioners and guides have been updated.

SOURCE · THE AUTOPIAN · REREV · FULLFATRR
Is the air suspension a deal-breaker?

No, but budget for it. Springs leak, compressors fail slowly and noisily, and 2003–05 height sensors seize. Genuine parts are expensive (a full air-ride restoration kit for 2003–05 trucks lists at about $2,450), but each part can be replaced on its own. Some long-term owners convert to coils after spring failures.

SOURCE · CLUB 4X4 · ATLANTIC BRITISH · EXPEDITION PORTAL
