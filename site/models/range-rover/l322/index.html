PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The third Range Rover was a clean break. The P38 had wrapped new electronics around old Land Rover hardware. The L322 threw out the ladder frame and the beam axles and started again: a unitary body, independent air suspension at all four corners and, at launch, BMW engines and electronics. Purists called it heresy. Then they drove it off-road and found that its cross-linked air springs let the wheels droop and stuff much like the old live axles did. Twenty years later it's the cheapest way into a properly capable luxury 4x4, and a minefield for anyone who buys one without receipts.

THE BMW RANGE ROVER FORD LAUNCHED

Under BMW ownership the project was codenamed L30. Joint Rover and BMW teams developed it, first at BMW's FIZ research centre in Munich and then at Solihull for the final stages. The plan was to share electronics and core engines with the E38 7 Series. Then BMW sold Land Rover to Ford about two years before production started, though the contract kept BMW involved until volume production was under way. Ford renamed the project L322, and the truck went on sale in 2002, arriving in North America as a 2003 model.

That handover explains the L322's three engine eras. Early petrol trucks used BMW's 4.4-litre M62 V8 (286 PS), and outside North America there was a BMW 2.9-litre Td6 diesel. For the 2006 model year Ford switched to Jaguar's AJ-V8 in 4.4-litre (305 PS) and 4.2-litre supercharged (400 PS) forms, behind a six-speed ZF. The 2010 facelift brought Jaguar Land Rover's 5.0-litre V8s: 375 PS naturally aspirated, 510 PS supercharged.

A LIVE AXLE IN DISGUISE

The off-road trick was in the plumbing. MacPherson struts at the front and double wishbones at the rear ride on air springs, and the valves linking each side's springs can make the suspension behave like a live axle when the truck is crossed up. Permanent four-wheel drive runs through a chain-driven two-speed transfer case that can shift between high and low on the move. Early trucks used a Torsen centre differential with a 50:50 split that could send up to 65% of torque to either axle.

From the 2007 model year the L322 got the Terrain Response system from the LR3 and Range Rover Sport, a lockable centre differential and, on Supercharged models (optional elsewhere), an electronically locking rear diff. That year also brought stiffer anti-roll bars and air springs, and the TDV8 twin-turbo diesel replaced the BMW six outside North America. Later updates revised the sand, rock-crawl and hill-descent programs, added hill-start assist, and the 2011 4.4 TDV8 with ZF's eight-speed automatic.

LIVING WITH ONE NOW

The L322's reputation is mostly about three failures. The BMW M62's nylon timing-chain guides go brittle with age and break, and a broken guide can wreck the engine. The 5.0-litre V8 has its own chain-tensioner and guide wear, and replacing the chains is a big, specialist job. The air suspension, as on every Range Rover since, eventually needs compressors, springs and height sensors. Owners who keep L322s running long-term replace the guides before they fail, service the 'sealed for life' gearboxes anyway, and treat a diagnostic tool as standard equipment.

Get past that and the truck rewards you. Running parts are cheap from aftermarket suppliers, Atlantic British publishes free how-to videos for most routine jobs, and the aftermarket offers sump guards, bush kits and sensor-rod lifts. A well-sorted 2007–2009 or a documented 2011–2012 is one of the most comfortable ways to reach a trailhead. With a sensor-rod lift and all-terrain tyres, it will keep going after the trailhead too.

LEGACY

Land Rover built 293,494 L322s before the all-aluminium L405 replaced it in 2012. It set the template every Range Rover since has followed: monocoque body, four-corner air suspension, and electronics deciding how the power reaches the ground. Atlantic British's ROVERLOG blog says it ranks second only to the Classic in Range Rover fans' affections. In its day it was the truck that bridged the gap between a Land Cruiser 100 or LX 470 and a W463 G-Class — plusher than the Toyotas, more road-friendly than the Mercedes.

SIDEBAR · SPECIAL EDITIONS

Anniversaries, Autobiographies and an armoured Vogue

Late L322s were sold with plenty of special editions. North America got a 20th Anniversary edition of 40 supercharged trucks in pearl white. The UK had 30 Claret-painted supercharged 35th Anniversary cars. The 40th Anniversary Autobiography Black ran to 700 cars, and the run-out Autobiography Ultimate Edition (500 cars) came with rear-seat iPads, a refrigerator and reclining climate-controlled rear seats.

The oddest one was built for people with enemies. In 2007 Land Rover offered an Armoured Range Rover Vogue, developed with Armour Holdings and certified to the European B6 ballistic standard. In 2010 Overfinch built a Holland & Holland edition, with the gunmaker acting as design consultant.

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The L322 swapped Land Rover's traditional ladder frame for a stiff steel monocoque and replaced live axles with four-corner independent air suspension. Off-road ability comes from software and plumbing instead: cross-linked air springs that imitate axle articulation, a two-speed chain-drive transfer case, a centre diff that was a Torsen until 2006 and lockable from 2007, and traction control on every corner. Engines came from three owners: BMW (2002–05), Jaguar (2006–09) and JLR's own 5.0 V8s (2010–12).

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Demand timing-chain paperworkOn 2003–2005 BMW-engined trucks, look for receipts for chains and guides, or budget for them. The nylon guides go brittle and break, and owners report failures clustering around 130,000–170,000 miles. On 2010–2012 5.0s, check for tensioner and guide updates. Listen for a rattle on a cold start.
  2. 02
    Cycle the air suspension through every heightThe compressor should be barely audible. Grinding means it's on its way out. Watch for slow rises, a lean, or warnings. On 2003–05 trucks, corroded or seized height sensors can stop the truck adjusting without leaving any fault codes.
  3. 03
    Test low range and the range-change motorSelect low and back again with the truck stationary. A range-change motor that has seized from lack of use can cost about £1,000 to fix. Make sure Hill Descent Control works too.
  4. 04
    Treat 'sealed for life' gearboxes as unsealedHesitant shifts or warning lights point to gearbox trouble. Ask whether the fluid and filter have ever been changed. Atlantic British recommends a service every 60,000 miles on later ZF six-speeds, including the leak-prone valve-body sleeve. A rebuild costs about £2,000 in the UK.
  5. 05
    Listen at full lockKnocking at low speed on full lock can mean a front differential problem, and many were recalled. Clonks over bumps from the rear point to perished hub bushes.
  6. 06
    Read the codes before and after the driveA pre-purchase diagnostic scan is cheap insurance. Read the codes before and after a long test drive, because sellers can clear faults just before you arrive. Check that every warning light comes on with the ignition and then goes out.
  7. 07
    Hunt for water and oilCheck for water in the rear fuse box and under the carpets. Check the M62 for valve-cover and other oil leaks, and look for white coolant streaks around the expansion tank. Expansion tanks fail.
  8. 08
    Check the brakes and wheelsL322s are hard on brakes; expect discs and pads about every 30,000 miles. Avoid 22-inch wheels with low-profile tyres, which ride badly and pinch-puncture easily.

Spotting the real thing

Over 10 years the L322 changed engines twice and its 4x4 hardware once, and those changes decide what the truck needs and what it's worth.

  1. ID
    Engine era2003–05 US trucks have BMW's M62 4.4. For 2006–09 it's Jaguar's AJ-V8, as a 4.4 or a 4.2 supercharged. 2010–12 trucks have the 5.0 or 5.0 supercharged. The diesels (Td6, 3.6 TDV8, 4.4 TDV8) were never sold in the US.
  2. ID
    4x4 hardwareBefore 2007 the truck has a Torsen centre diff and no Terrain Response. From 2007 it has Terrain Response, a locking centre diff and an electronic rear diff (standard on Supercharged, optional on others).
  3. ID
    2010 facelift tellsLED-accented bi-xenon headlights, LED tail lights with a three-bar design, fog lights moved lower in the bumper, and a 12-in TFT virtual instrument panel instead of analogue gauges.
  4. ID
    KeyAround January 2007 production the BMW-style key gave way to Land Rover's sealed 'switchblade' key, which recharges from a coil in the ignition switch.
  5. ID
    Holland & HollandNot an official Land Rover limited edition. Overfinch built them from standard trucks, on the 4.4 TDV8 or 5.0 supercharged, with a gun box, a drinks cabinet and the 'Aero 3' body kit.

Assume nothing was done. Ride height sensor replacement (2003–2005)
DIFFICULTY · 2 / 5TIME · VariesSHOP COST · Parts plus labor
SYMPTOMSThe air suspension won't change height or adjusts erratically, often with no stored fault codes. The sensor pivots seize, and cracked rear sensor bodies let water in, which corrupts the readings.
THE FIXRear: the sensor sits against the rear subframe on the lower control arm. Clean and unplug the connector. Undo the 13 mm-head bolt and nut that hold the link to the arm, then remove the two screws holding the sensor to its bracket (they're often rusted, so use penetrating oil and locking pliers). Fit the new rubber-bushed sensor with its new screws, check that the arm moves freely, refit the nut while holding it with a 10 mm spanner, and pack the connector with dielectric grease. The front sensors are done the same way.

Cold-start rattle: VANOS seals and non-return valves
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · VariesSHOP COST · Valves cost a few pounds each. A VANOS seal kit and tool run about £120
SYMPTOMSA brief rattle on a cold start that goes away on a quick restart, on a BMW 4.4 whose guides may still be fine.
THE FIXBefore condemning the chains, change to a good fully synthetic 5W-30 or 5W-40 with a quality (Mann or genuine) filter. Owners report the rattle stopping after the filter change alone. Next, replace the VANOS solenoid seals and the oil non-return valves behind the solenoids. Some say the real culprits are two non-return valves in the block under the head gasket. If it still rattles, move on to the tensioners and then the guides, which means dropping the sump.

Transfer case range-change faults
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · VariesSHOP COST · About £1,000 if the motor has to be replaced
SYMPTOMSA chime and a warning message, then the transfer case drops into neutral. Low range engages but high won't, or a 'trans failsafe' message appears.
THE FIXRarely used range-change motors seize, and their contacts wear. Check that the connections under the transfer case are clean and plugged in. Try the reset owners use: fit a spare 5A fuse in position 37 of the main fuse box, cycle the ignition, remove the fuse, then cycle the hi/lo selector. One owner fixed worn contacts by taking off the contact cover under the case and adjusting them. Replace the motor if it has seized.

Asked on every forum

Range Rover L322 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

On 18-inch wheels, owners say 265/60R18 (about 30.5 in) fits with no problems, including off-road. 265/65R18 has been warned against because it can rub when the air suspension drops. On 20s, 275/55R20 clears the bump stops but rubs the liners at full steering lock. Check that a taller spare still fits in the well.

SOURCE · AULRO
What fuel economy should I expect?

Thirsty. The EPA rated a 2004 BMW 4.4 at 11 mpg city, 15 highway and 12 combined; a 2008 4.4 or 4.2 Supercharged at 12/18/14; a 2011 5.0 at 12/19/15; and a 2011 5.0 Supercharged at 12/18/14. The tank holds 100 litres.

SOURCE · FUELECONOMY.GOV · CARSALES
How much can it tow?

3,500 kg (7,716 lb) braked and 750 kg unbraked. A 2002 4.4 HSE weighs about 2,430 kg at the curb.

SOURCE · CARSALES
Which years and engines should I buy or avoid?

Owners and buyers' guides generally rate the 2007–2009 Jaguar-engined trucks best: Terrain Response, a locking centre diff and the six-speed ZF. Buy a 2003–2005 BMW 4.4 only with receipts for timing guides (or if you'll do them yourself). Buy a 2010–2012 5.0 only with evidence that the chain tensioners and guides have been updated.

SOURCE · THE AUTOPIAN · REREV · FULLFATRR
Does it have low range and lockers?

Every L322 has full-time four-wheel drive with a two-speed chain-drive transfer case you can shift on the move. 2003–2006 trucks use a Torsen centre diff and open axles with traction control. From 2007 the centre diff can lock under Terrain Response, and an electronic rear locker was standard on Supercharged models and optional on others.

SOURCE · AUSTRALIAN CAR REVIEWS · WIKIPEDIA
How do I lift an L322?

The usual way is longer ride-height sensor links. Atlantic British's RHSRAL322 rods make the air suspension think it's low, so it holds about 2.5 in of extra height all the time, enough for bigger all-terrains. Its listed fitment is 2005–2012 trucks.

SOURCE · ATLANTIC BRITISH
Is the air suspension a deal-breaker?

No, but budget for it. Springs leak, compressors fail slowly and noisily, and 2003–05 height sensors seize. Genuine parts are expensive (a full air-ride restoration kit for 2003–05 trucks lists at about $2,450), but each part can be replaced on its own. Some long-term owners convert to coils after spring failures.

SOURCE · CLUB 4X4 · ATLANTIC BRITISH · EXPEDITION PORTAL
Are the gearboxes really 'sealed for life'?

No. Early petrol trucks use a ZF 5HP24, and Td6 diesels use a GM 5L40-E that Australian testers call so bad owners treat it as a service item. Both left the factory with 'sealed for life' stickers. Atlantic British recommends a fluid and filter service every 60,000 miles on the later ZF six-speed.

SOURCE · CLUB 4X4 · ATLANTIC BRITISH
How do I tell which engine I'm looking at?

For US trucks, go by model year: 2003–2005 is the BMW 4.4, 2006–2009 the Jaguar 4.4 or 4.2 supercharged, and 2010–2012 the 5.0 or 5.0 supercharged. Parts suppliers split their catalogues the same way, so order by engine as well as year.

SOURCE · ATLANTIC BRITISH · WIKIPEDIA
My BMW 4.4 rattles on cold starts — are the guides gone?

Not necessarily. Owners trace many short cold-start rattles to VANOS seals, solenoid non-return valves or a poor oil filter. One owner's rattle stopped after switching to a Mann filter. A rattle that keeps getting worse, or that shows up with low oil pressure, is a different matter. Stop driving and check the guides.

SOURCE · FULLFATRR
Is it actually good off-road?

Yes. Club 4X4 calls it great off-road, and its cross-linked air springs are designed to mimic live-axle articulation. On any truck you're considering, check that low range engages and that Hill Descent Control works. The usual first mods for trail use are a sump guard, a sensor-rod lift and all-terrain tyres.

SOURCE · CLUB 4X4 · AUSTRALIAN CAR REVIEWS
