Range Rover L405 problems: 8 things to check

Range Rover L405 problems to check before you buy

  1. Check every recall is closedNHTSA lists keyless door-latch recalls for 2013–2016 trucks (doors may unlatch) and front-suspension knuckle recalls for 2014–2017 trucks (the upper knuckle joint can crack). AutoTrader.ca counted at least 19 safety recalls on this generation in Canada. Trucks that left the dealer network early are the most likely to have open campaigns. Run the VIN.
  2. Be extra careful with pre-2015 trucksPistonHeads' L405 guide says early cars had the most electrical and body problems. It advises a warranty, saying Land Rover's own cover for early cars costs around £1,400 a year after a free inspection. Panel fit on the rear doors, tailgate and bonnet is a known weak spot, and so are door seals.
  3. Listen to the front end on a rough roadCoast and brake moderately over broken tarmac. Clunks or pops point to worn front track control arms or rear lower arms (around £700 fitted as a pair, per PistonHeads) — or, on 2014–2017 trucks, to the knuckle recall. AutoTrader.ca says not to take delivery of one with unusual front-end noise.
  4. Watch the ride height overnightCompressors fail and air springs split, and PistonHeads calls both 'known things.' Look for a corner that sags after standing, a compressor that runs constantly, or suspension faults on the dash. Check that every height mode works: access, normal and off-road.
  5. Cold-start a 5.0 V8 yourselfA rattle lasting a second or two on a cold start is the classic sign of tired timing-chain guides and tensioners, particularly on 2010–2014 engines. Fault codes P0016–P0019 come later. A metallic rattle at idle can instead be the supercharger coupler or isolator. Look for coolant loss too: the plastic crossover pipes crack.
  6. Ask how the diesel was servicedTDV6/SDV6 crank-bearing failures are documented in Land Rover bulletin SSM71816 and in owner threads. Owners suspect oil sludging under long intervals and the wrong oil grade play a part. Ask for regular servicing with the correct oil. On the 4.4 SDV8, check for EGR faults and oily turbo breather pipes (up to £850 to replace).
  7. Test the screens and switchesInfotainment freezes, dead cameras and Bluetooth faults are common and often fixed by software. If the head unit goes black or won't boot, assume an expensive repair until proved otherwise. Check the start/stop and steering-wheel buttons, the interior door releases, and the rotary gear selector, which can fail to rise.
  8. Look at the wheels, and the transfer-case historyPistonHeads says the 22-inch wheels are soft and buckle on potholes, so check them for bends. AutoTrader.ca advises that any non-factory wheels come from a reputable brand and be rated for the truck's weight. Transfer-box and differential oil should be changed at around 30,000 miles, so ask for proof.

Range Rover L405 recalls and service bulletins

  • Seat SRS connector may disconnect ↗ 2013–2014 · NHTSA 13V-607The side-airbag connector under the front seats may disconnect because of insufficient clearance.
  • Brake vacuum hose routing ↗ 2013–2014 · NHTSA 15V-042The brake vacuum hose may be misrouted and chafe through.
  • Occupant classification (passenger airbag) ↗ 2013–2015 · NHTSA 15V-093A lightweight adult passenger may be misclassified, so the passenger airbag is switched off.
  • Doors may unlatch ↗ 2013–2016 · NHTSA 15V-385Doors may unlatch unexpectedly once closed.
  • Keyless door latch (KV Latch) ↗ 2013–2016 · NHTSA 19V-392Doors can look closed but still be unlatched. 20V-387 re-checks earlier repairs that may not have been done properly.
  • Wheel lug nuts may crack ↗ 2015 · NHTSA 15V-092Lug nuts on some trucks built in January 2015 may crack and come off the stud.
  • Front upper knuckle may crack ↗ 2014–2017 · NHTSA 25V-514The front suspension's upper knuckle joint can crack and let a suspension arm detach. See also 24V-840 (2014–2016) and 26V-527 (wrong replacement knuckles).
  • Driver seat-belt retractor ↗ 2016–2017 · NHTSA 22V-219The driver's emergency-locking retractor may not lock (see also 19V-350).
  • Surround camera water ingress ↗ 2018–2022 · NHTSA 24V-023Water can get into the rear-view camera, giving no picture or a distorted one.
  • Cam carrier oil channel ↗ 2020–2023 · NHTSA 23V-044The cam-carrier oil channel may be blocked, which can cause an oil leak (Ingenium six).
  • TDV6 / SDV6 crankshaft and bearing failure ↗ · SSM71816Land Rover technical bulletin on crank-bearing failures in the 3.0 V6 diesel family. Long oil-change intervals and the wrong oil grade are suspected contributors.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about Range Rover L405 reliability

Is the TDV6 crankshaft failure real?

Yes, but it isn't universal. Land Rover service bulletin SSM71816 (May 2014) blamed a 'low incidence' of crank failures mainly on wrongly located or rotating main-bearing shells. In a FullFatRR poll of L405 3.0 owners, 88% reported no issues. The usual advice is the correct oil, shorter oil-change intervals than the maximum, and a warranty that covers the engine.
