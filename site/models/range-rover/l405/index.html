PART 01

Overview

Where it came from, why it matters, and how it changed year to year.

The story

The fourth-generation Range Rover broke cover at the 2012 Paris Motor Show and did something no full-size 4x4 had done before: it threw away the steel. Land Rover calls the L405 the first production SUV with an all-aluminium monocoque, and the diet was dramatic — the body-in-white was 39 percent lighter and the whole truck shed about 420 kg (926 lb) against the steel L322. It still had a two-speed transfer box, cross-linked air springs and a 900 mm wading depth. It was simply built from the same stuff as a beer can.

LIGHTER, STIFFER, POSHER

Gerry McGovern's design team kept the floating roof, the clamshell bonnet and the upright glasshouse, then smoothed everything else. Underneath, the aluminium shell was built in a new all-aluminium facility at Solihull, using a high-strength alloy with up to 50 percent recycled content. The weight saving went straight into performance and economy: launch engines were a supercharged 5.0 V8, a 3.0 TDV6 and a 4.4 SDV8, all driving through a ZF eight-speed automatic and a Magna Steyr DD295 two-speed transfer case with a 50/50 torque split.

Off-road, the numbers moved the right way. In a UK road test of the launch TDV6, Land Rover quoted 303 mm of ground clearance (up 17 mm), a 900 mm wading depth (up 200 mm), and approach and departure angles of 34.7 and 29.6 degrees. Terrain Response 2 could now read the surface itself and choose between grass/gravel/snow, mud, sand and rock crawl. It would also back off the active anti-roll bars to let the wheels droop when it sensed the truck was off the tarmac.

STRETCHED, HYBRIDISED, FACELIFTED

The range grew sideways for nine years. A long-wheelbase version, 200 mm longer between the axles at 3,122 mm, arrived at the 2013 Los Angeles show. A supercharged 3.0 V6 joined in 2014, an SDV6 diesel-electric hybrid followed, and Special Vehicle Operations turned the 5.0 V8 up to 550 PS, then 565 PS, for the SVAutobiography Dynamic. America finally got a diesel, the 254 hp Td6, for the 2016 model year. It was rated at 22 mpg city and 28 highway, against 14 and 19 for the supercharged V8.

The 2018 facelift brought a four-bar grille, new LED lights, thicker glass, and a second touchscreen in place of the climate knobs. The P400e plug-in hybrid followed, pairing a 2.0-litre Ingenium four with a 13.1 kWh battery and an 85 kW motor for 404 PS. Late trucks gained Ingenium straight-sixes, including a 3.0 mild-hybrid petrol for the 2020 model year. One planned derivative never made it: the three-door, 999-unit SV Coupé, shown at Geneva in 2018, was cancelled in January 2019 for financial reasons.

THE CATCH

All that cleverness has a price, and second owners pay it. Specialist guides single out pre-2015 cars for the most electrical and body faults. The 3.0 TDV6 family is known for crankshaft and bearing failures, which a 2014 Land Rover bulletin blamed mainly on bearing shells being located wrongly or rotating. The supercharged 5.0 V8 eats timing-chain guides and cracks its plastic coolant pipes, and the air suspension wants compressors and struts. NHTSA lists keyless door-latch recalls for 2013–2016 trucks and front-knuckle cracking recalls for 2014–2017 trucks. None of that stops a sorted L405 being a superb, genuinely capable expedition limo — exactly why Lucky8 built a lifted, winched, rock-slidered 'Project L405' out of a supercharged one.

LEGACY

The L405 ran from 2012 to 2021, which Top Gear noted is an age in motoring terms, and the Velar, named after the 1967 prototypes, was spun off during its lifetime. It was replaced by the L460 in late 2021. For off-roaders it is now the newest full-size Range Rover with real aftermarket support: lift rods, rock sliders, hidden winch mounts and 285-section all-terrains. Treat it like the 2.3-tonne computer it is, and it will go most places a Land Cruiser 200 goes — just more quietly, and with more dashboard messages.

SIDEBAR · 2015 ROYAL COMMISSION

The topless Range Rover built for the Queen

In June 2015 Land Rover delivered a one-off 'State Review' Range Rover for Queen Elizabeth II. It was based on the long-wheelbase L405 Hybrid, with a 3.0 SDV6 diesel and an electric motor making 335 hp and 700 Nm, and it could run in pure electric mode. The rear passenger compartment had no roof, so the monarch could stand and wave. Land Rover Design built it with engineering input from Special Vehicle Operations.

It made its royal debut in Cardiff, when the Queen presented new Colours to the Royal Welsh Regiment. It was the fourth State Review vehicle from Land Rover: the first was a 1953 Series I, and this one replaced the State Review Range Rover in service since 2002.

PART 02

Specs & capability

How it’s built, what it can do, and what fits under it.

How it’s built

The L405 is a unibody truck with no separate frame. The structure is a riveted and bonded aluminium monocoque, sprung on cross-linked air springs at all four corners and driven full-time through a two-speed transfer case. It is light for its size, and it was properly engineered for off-road work. It is also completely dependent on its electronics: ride height, roll control, traction and even the transfer-case logic all go through modules that want a diagnostic tool, not a spanner.

What to check before you buy

  1. 01
    Check every recall is closedNHTSA lists keyless door-latch recalls for 2013–2016 trucks (doors may unlatch) and front-suspension knuckle recalls for 2014–2017 trucks (the upper knuckle joint can crack). AutoTrader.ca counted at least 19 safety recalls on this generation in Canada. Trucks that left the dealer network early are the most likely to have open campaigns. Run the VIN.
  2. 02
    Be extra careful with pre-2015 trucksPistonHeads' L405 guide says early cars had the most electrical and body problems. It advises a warranty, saying Land Rover's own cover for early cars costs around £1,400 a year after a free inspection. Panel fit on the rear doors, tailgate and bonnet is a known weak spot, and so are door seals.
  3. 03
    Listen to the front end on a rough roadCoast and brake moderately over broken tarmac. Clunks or pops point to worn front track control arms or rear lower arms (around £700 fitted as a pair, per PistonHeads) — or, on 2014–2017 trucks, to the knuckle recall. AutoTrader.ca says not to take delivery of one with unusual front-end noise.
  4. 04
    Watch the ride height overnightCompressors fail and air springs split, and PistonHeads calls both 'known things.' Look for a corner that sags after standing, a compressor that runs constantly, or suspension faults on the dash. Check that every height mode works: access, normal and off-road.
  5. 05
    Cold-start a 5.0 V8 yourselfA rattle lasting a second or two on a cold start is the classic sign of tired timing-chain guides and tensioners, particularly on 2010–2014 engines. Fault codes P0016–P0019 come later. A metallic rattle at idle can instead be the supercharger coupler or isolator. Look for coolant loss too: the plastic crossover pipes crack.
  6. 06
    Ask how the diesel was servicedTDV6/SDV6 crank-bearing failures are documented in Land Rover bulletin SSM71816 and in owner threads. Owners suspect oil sludging under long intervals and the wrong oil grade play a part. Ask for regular servicing with the correct oil. On the 4.4 SDV8, check for EGR faults and oily turbo breather pipes (up to £850 to replace).
  7. 07
    Test the screens and switchesInfotainment freezes, dead cameras and Bluetooth faults are common and often fixed by software. If the head unit goes black or won't boot, assume an expensive repair until proved otherwise. Check the start/stop and steering-wheel buttons, the interior door releases, and the rotary gear selector, which can fail to rise.
  8. 08
    Look at the wheels, and the transfer-case historyPistonHeads says the 22-inch wheels are soft and buckle on potholes, so check them for bends. AutoTrader.ca advises that any non-factory wheels come from a reputable brand and be rated for the truck's weight. Transfer-box and differential oil should be changed at around 30,000 miles, so ask for proof.

Spotting the real thing

Nine model years, two wheelbases and seven powertrains look much alike, and the differences matter for parts, reliability and value.

  1. ID
    Pre- or post-faceliftTrucks from 2013 to 2017 have a three-bar grille, physical climate knobs and seat switches on the seat. From the 2018 model year they have a four-bar grille, new LED DRLs and twin touchscreens, and the seat controls are on the doors.
  2. ID
    WheelbaseThe SWB is 2,922 mm (4,999 mm long). The LWB, from 2014, adds 200 mm, giving 3,122 mm (5,199 mm long), all of it in rear legroom.
  3. ID
    US engines5.0 supercharged V8 (510 PS, 525 PS from 2017); 3.0 supercharged V6 from 2014; Td6 diesel from 2016; P400e plug-in hybrid from the 2019 model year; 3.0 straight-six mild hybrid from 2020. The 4.4 SDV8 and the SDV6 Hybrid were never sold in the US.
  4. ID
    Rear lockerLand Rover's owner guide lists separate fluid specs and fill volumes for the non-locking and electronic locking rear differentials. Check the build spec or the diff before you assume you have a locker.
  5. ID
    SVAutobiographyThe SVO-built flagship. The SVAutobiography Dynamic uses a 550 PS version of the supercharged 5.0. These trucks are worth far more than an HSE, so check the VIN-decoded build spec before paying SV money.

Common repairs

The jobs owners actually end up doing, rated for a competent home mechanic with a decent tool kit. Always confirm torque figures, fluids and procedures against the factory service manual for your VIN, and work safely — support the truck on stands, never just a jack. Front air strut replacement
DIFFICULTY · 3 / 5TIME · ≈ 1–2 hours per side (forum labour estimate for full-size Range Rover struts)SHOP COST · Front strut about $730–$830 (Lucky8 BWI/OE listings), plus labor
SYMPTOMSOne front corner sitting low after standing, a hiss when the truck lowers to access height, the compressor working overtime, or air-suspension faults that come back after recalibration.
THE FIXRelieve the system pressure (it runs up to 10 bar) and disconnect the battery. With the wheels straight, raise the truck, support it safely and remove the wheel. Unplug the strut's electrical connector, release the air line and remove its fitting, then remove the sensor-wire and brake-line bracket and the lower strut bolt. Unhook the anti-roll bar link from the strut clevis and separate the upper control arm ball joint from the knuckle. Remove the top mount nuts, swing the knuckle out and lift the strut out. To fit: leave the air fitting on the new strut, install the strut and lower bolt, reconnect the upper arm, clevis, sway-bar link and brackets, fit the top bolts, then remove the shipping pin and push the air line home until it locks. Plug in the connector, torque everything to factory spec, refit the wheel and recalibrate the ride heights.

Air-suspension lift rods (height-sensor links)
DIFFICULTY · 1 / 5TIME · ≈ 20–30 minutesSHOP COST · About $99 per set (Lucky8)
SYMPTOMSNot a fault — it's the cheapest lift on the planet. The truck sits too low for bigger tyres or rough tracks, or scrapes its sills in ruts.
THE FIXRemove the factory ride-height sensor rods at each corner and fit the longer links in their place: short rods at the front, long rods at the rear. The links fool the sensors into reading the truck as lower than it is, so the air springs raise every height mode by 1, 1.5 or 2 inches depending on the kit. No coding is needed and Terrain Response keeps working. Get a wheel alignment afterwards.

THE FIXFirst run the VIN for the 2014–2017 front-knuckle recall, because a cracking upper knuckle joint can also make noises and is fixed free by dealers. Recalibrate the ride height if a height sensor was disturbed, then align.

THE FIXLock the engine at the correct position with the JLR timing tools before anything comes apart. Replace both cam chains, the tensioners and blades, both guides, the auxiliary drive chain with its tensioner and guide, and the cam lubricating jets, then refit with new rocker-cover gaskets and spark-plug seals. THE FIXRule out the timing chain first with a cold-start listen and a scan for cam-timing codes. Asked on every forum

Range Rover L405 questions, answered

What's the biggest tire I can fit without a lift?

On 20-inch wheels, the FullFatRR consensus is 285/50R20 (about 31.2 in). Two owners run them, one calls them probably the largest that won't rub in any height mode, and the spare still fits the well. 275/55R20 (about 31.9 in) is also fitted by 'quite a few' owners, though one member predicted it might just catch at full lock. Twenty inches is the smallest rim owners trust to clear the brakes, and 255/60R20 tyres on new-Defender wheels also fit. Want bigger? Lucky8 has a video on trimming the bumper for larger tyres on its lifted Project L405.

SOURCE · FULLFATRR · LUCKY8
What fuel economy should I expect?

For the 2016 model year, the EPA rated the supercharged 5.0 V8 at 14 mpg city, 19 highway and 16 combined, the 3.0 supercharged V6 at 17/23/19, and the Td6 diesel at 22/28/24. PistonHeads reports about 30 mpg (UK) in real-world driving for the 4.4 SDV8.

SOURCE · FUELECONOMY.GOV · PISTONHEADS
How much can it tow?

Australian-market figures give 3,500 kg (7,716 lb) braked for the V8s, petrol or diesel, and 3,000 kg for the 3.0 V6s. Check your owner's manual and plate; the figure varies by engine and market.

SOURCE · CARSGUIDE
Which engine should I buy?

What Car? calls the 3.0 TDV6 the sensible choice. PistonHeads rates the 4.4 SDV8 as 'a good and reliable engine' without the TDV6's crank issues or timing belt. In the US, the choice is the 5.0 V8 (thirsty, chain and coolant-pipe risks), the 3.0 V6, the Td6 diesel from 2016, or the later hybrids. AutoTrader.ca recommends a 2020-or-newer truck with the straight-six if budget allows.

SOURCE · WHAT CAR? · PISTONHEADS · AUTOTRADER.CA
Is the TDV6 crankshaft failure real?

Yes, but it isn't universal. Land Rover service bulletin SSM71816 (May 2014) blamed a 'low incidence' of crank failures mainly on wrongly located or rotating main-bearing shells. In a FullFatRR poll of L405 3.0 owners, 88% reported no issues. The usual advice is the correct oil, shorter oil-change intervals than the maximum, and a warranty that covers the engine.

SOURCE · AULRO · FULLFATRR
Does it have low range?

Yes. Every L405 has permanent four-wheel drive with a Magna Steyr DD295 two-speed transfer case, a 50/50 split and a low range, managed by Terrain Response 2 with hill descent control. Change the transfer-box and diff oil at around 30,000 miles.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · PISTONHEADS
How can I lift it?

The easy way is height-sensor lift rods, which raise every air-suspension mode by 1, 1.5 or 2 inches with no electronics. Proud Rhino's rods take about 20–30 minutes to fit, and Lucky8 recommends an alignment afterwards.

SOURCE · LUCKY8 / PROUD RHINO
Can I work on the air suspension at home?

Mostly, with the right scan tool. GAP's IID G4 tool lists air-suspension height adjustment and guided calibration for the L405. 2017–2022 trucks work with the standard tool, but 2013–2016 trucks need the optional adapter version. Struts and the compressor unbolt with hand tools, but the system runs at up to 10 bar, so depressurise it first, and the calibration afterwards needs the tool.

SOURCE · LUCKY8 · ARNOTT
Pre-facelift or facelift?

The 2018 facelift added the four-bar grille, new LED lights, thicker glass, Touch Pro Duo screens and the P400e plug-in hybrid. More importantly, PistonHeads says pre-2015 trucks had the most electrical and body faults, ReRev rates 2018–21 the most reliable years (and flags 2017 for windscreen-seal leaks), and AutoTrader.ca suggests a 2020-on straight-six if the budget allows. Later is better.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA · PISTONHEADS · REREV · AUTOTRADER.CA
How deep can it wade?

Land Rover quoted 900 mm (35.4 in), 200 mm more than the L322, with up to 303 mm of ground clearance at off-road height.

SOURCE · WHEELWORLD REVIEWS
Was there a long-wheelbase version?

Yes. Revealed at the 2013 LA Auto Show with deliveries from early 2014, it has a 3,122 mm (122.9 in) wheelbase and is 5,199 mm long — 200 mm more in each than the standard truck.

SOURCE · WIKIPEDIA
