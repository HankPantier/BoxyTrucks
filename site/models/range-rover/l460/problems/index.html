Range Rover L460 problems: 8 things to check

The problems owners and specialists flag most on the Land Rover Range Rover L460, the repairs they end up doing and what those cost, plus the years and recalls to watch. Sourced, not scraped.

Range Rover L460 problems to check before you buy

  1. Run the VIN against every recallNHTSA lists a stack of campaigns: a cam-carrier oil channel (2022–2023), 7-seat second-row frame welds (2022–2023), tail-light links (2022–2023), rear-camera water ingress (2022–2023) and tailgate camera drain holes (2022–2026), body panels not bonded properly (2024), transmission oil pressure (2024), a cracking oil filter housing (2024), the driver's airbag clockspring connector (2022–2026), and the DC-DC converter (26V248000 and 26V599000, 2022–2024 L460s). Get written confirmation that they're closed.
  2. Ask about the DC-DC converterOn mild hybrids, a failing 48-volt to 12-volt converter shows 'Stop – electrical fault' with a red battery light and stops charging the 12-volt battery. Owners say it may log no fault and clear itself, only to return. Dealers have had cars waiting weeks for back-ordered modules. NHTSA recalls 26V248000 and 26V599000 are interim software updates, with a final remedy to follow.
  3. Check that it goes to sleepFlat 12-volt batteries after a few days parked are a recurring forum topic. Dealers have fixed some with door-module and other software updates, and one owner traced the same symptom to a brake-pedal security lock that kept the car from sleeping. Ask whether it has needed jump-starts, and whether any aftermarket immobiliser or tracker is fitted.
  4. Check the build spec on early trucksOwners say many early (2022) trucks were delivered with downgraded audio and other options during the parts shortage. Check the car against its original order or a parts catalogue before paying for options it doesn't have.
  5. Know which V8 you're gettingP530s built for 2022–2023 use BMW's N63 twin-turbo V8, while 2024-on trucks got the newer S68. Forum owners rate the S68 as significantly more refined. It's also the engine in the SV P615.
  6. Look at the service history, not just the stampsService intervals were up to 21,000 miles or 24 months, and owners report the 2024 model year D350 dropped to 10,500 miles or 12 months. Many owners change the oil more often than the schedule asks. A truck run to the maximum on short urban trips deserves extra scrutiny.
  7. Check software and connectivityUpdates arrive over the air, but one 2022 owner whose truck was stuck on an old version found reports of a telematics (TCU) problem on some 2022–23 trucks. Check the software version against Land Rover's published releases, and confirm the remote app works.
  8. Mind the wheel bolts and wheel sizeThe L460 uses wheel bolts, not the studs and nuts of the L405, so older Range Rover wheels need the correct flat-seat bolts. Owners say 22- and 23-inch wheels ride harshly on broken roads. For trail use, a smaller wheel with more sidewall is the better bet.

Range Rover L460 recalls and service bulletins

  • Cam carrier oil channel ↗ 2022–2023 · NHTSA 23V-044A blocked oil channel in the cam carrier can cause an oil leak.
  • 7-seat second-row seat frame welds ↗ 2022–2023 · NHTSA 23V-252On seven-seat trucks, the second-row left seat frame may be insufficiently welded.
  • Second-row armrest latch ↗ 2022–2023 · NHTSA 23V-324The armrest storage latch may fail in a crash (FMVSS 201).
  • Tail-light link ↗ 2022–2023 · NHTSA 23V-394A damaged link can stop the tail, brake and turn lights working.
  • Rear camera water ingress ↗ 2022–2023 · NHTSA 24V-947Water can get into the rear-view camera, giving no image or a distorted one.
  • Rear camera drain holes ↗ 2022–2026 · NHTSA 26V-678Water may not drain away from the rear-view camera and can damage it. Dealers will drill extra drain holes in the tailgate trim and replace the camera if needed; interim owner letters were due in December 2026.
  • Body panels not bonded properly ↗ 2024 · NHTSA 23V-872Body panels may not be properly bonded, which reduces structural strength.
  • Oil cooler hoses ↗ 2024 · NHTSA 23V-790Engine-mounted oil-cooler hoses may not be properly secured and can leak oil onto the road.
  • Oil filter housing ↗ 2024 · NHTSA 24V-450The oil filter housing can crack and leak oil into the engine bay.
  • Transmission oil pressure loss ↗ 2024 · NHTSA 24V-380Transmission oil pressure can drop and shift the clutch into neutral, cutting drive.
  • MHEV DC-DC converter ↗ 2022–2024 · NHTSA 26V-599The mild-hybrid DC-DC converter can fail from electrical overload or a start-up software fault. This follows campaign 26V-248.
  • Panoramic roof side finisher ↗ 2023–2026 · NHTSA 26V-097The sunroof's side trim may not be properly secured and can detach.
  • Driver airbag clockspring connector ↗ 2022–2026 · NHTSA 26V-389The clockspring connector can corrode, so the driver's airbag may not deploy as intended.
  • Second-row centre buckle ↗ 2025 · NHTSA 25V-155The second-row centre seat-belt buckle may not latch correctly.

Check any VIN for open recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls ↗, or see the recall tracker.

Owner questions about Range Rover L460 reliability

Is it reliable?

The early record is mixed. NHTSA lists more than a dozen recalls across the 2023 and 2024 model years, and the DC-DC converter, 12-volt battery drain and software glitches dominate the FullFatRR technical board. Buy one with a warranty, a clean recall record and connected software that actually updates.
