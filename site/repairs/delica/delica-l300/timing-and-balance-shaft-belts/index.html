DIY repair · Mitsubishi Delica Star Wagon (L300 4WD)

Timing and balance-shaft belts (4D56)

DIFFICULTY 3 / 5TIME A day for a first-timerCOST Parts plus labor

Symptoms

Usually none before it fails. A belt that has jumped a tooth gives a loud, clattery 'tractor' noise at idle and on the move, black smoke, low power and sometimes oil consumption. A broken balance-shaft belt can knock the timing belt off a tooth and then hide in the bottom of the cover.

The fix, step by step

  1. Remove the top timing cover and look for bits of belt.
  2. Turn the engine until the camshaft and injection-pump sprocket marks line up, then check that the crank pulley notch points at the leftmost 'T' mark on the lower cover (pages 11B-14 and 11B-15 of Mitsubishi's '4D5 & 4G7 Engine Manual', as quoted on Delica.ca).
  3. Replace the timing belt and the balance-shaft belt together, with their tensioners, and the water pump while you're there.
  4. You'll need to hold the crank pulley to undo its bolt and a puller to remove it.
  5. Owners say the factory 100,000 km interval suits a new, dry engine, but 70,000–90,000 km is safer on an old engine with oily, worn pulleys.

Tools & part numbers

  • Workshop manual
  • crank pulley holding tool and puller
  • torque wrench
  • belt tension gauge or the manual's method
  • balancer belt MD310484 (engines from May 1993) or MD363481 (earlier), per a Delica.ca member; confirm by frame number.
