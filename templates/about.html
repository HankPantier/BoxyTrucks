About BoxyTrucks

For the love of right angles

An independent guide to the square-shouldered 4x4s people actually take off-road: {{ n_gens }} generations across {{ n_fams }} families, from the FJ25 to the Grenadier.

BoxyTrucks started with a simple frustration: the best knowledge about these trucks is scattered across twenty-year-old forum threads, specialist shops’ blogs, factory brochures and owners’ heads. We pull it together, one generation at a time, and show our sources.

Who’s behind it

I’m Hank Pantier, and I’ve been buying, fixing and driving boxy 4x4s for most of my adult life, these days from Upstate South Carolina.

The garage so far: two Jeep LJs, a 60 Series and an 80 Series Land Cruiser, an FJ Cruiser, and four 4Runners: a 2002, a 2007, a 2016 and the 2024 in the driveway now. Next to it is a 2007 G500, the first US year with the 7-speed. I bought that one knowing exactly which years to avoid, which is the point of this site. The one I regret selling is the 60.

Every one of them taught me something the brochure didn’t: which year to buy, what breaks first, and which forum thread has the real answer. That knowledge is out there, but it’s buried in twenty-year-old threads and the heads of owners who’ve done the job three times. BoxyTrucks is my attempt to dig it out and pull it together in one place, with sources, for the next person standing in a seller’s driveway with a flashlight.

I’ve built websites for a living since 2001, so this is where the two halves of my life meet.

What you’ll find here

Every generation gets a full guide: where it came from and how it’s built, the best years to buy and the ones to avoid, a pre-purchase checklist, the {{ n_repairs }} repairs owners actually end up doing, a printable specs and fluids sheet, wheel and tire fitment, recalls, values, and the suppliers, builders, forums and channels worth knowing. Around them sit tools: the tire fitment tool, head-to-head comparisons and best years to buy for every family.

How we research

Everything is sourced. Each guide lists its sources at the bottom: factory press releases and brochures, owner’s and service manuals cited by name, NHTSA recall records, specialist shops, parts suppliers and long-running owner forums. Where owners and the factory disagree, we say so.

“Best years” means buyer consensus, not spec sheets. A year earns the Sweet spot tag because owners and specialists recommend it, and the Beware tag because of documented problems. Years whose only issue is a recall are tagged Recall, so you know to check it was done rather than to walk away.

We flag what we haven’t verified. Fluid specs, capacities and intervals marked confirm in manual come from owners or suppliers and haven’t been checked against a factory source. Confirm them against the manual for your VIN before you rely on them; see the disclaimer.

Off-road photos only, always credited. We use photography of trucks doing what they were built for, licensed from Unsplash, Pexels, Wikimedia Commons and similar sources, with the photographer credited on every page. We never use photos of wrecks or breakdowns.
